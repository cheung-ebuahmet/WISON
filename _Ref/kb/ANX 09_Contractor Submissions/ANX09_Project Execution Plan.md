1 PROJECT DESCRIPTION
1.1 INTRODUCTION
The Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 2 (RSHT-2) is owned by ADNOC and operated by
ADNOC Gas (AG) and is located approximately 240 km west-southwest of Abu Dhabi City in
the Ruwais Industrial Area. RSHT-2 facility is designed to handle bulk granulated sulphur
coming from Shah and Habshan plant via railcars and transported via new federal railway
(Union Railway) to Ruwais Terminal (SHT-2).
Based on ADNOC recent directives, the Sulphur Handling Scenario at Ruwais SHT-2 (Ruwais
Sulphur Granulation Plant-RSGP) will be developed further due to consideration of receiving
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur from Hail & Ghasha to the new plant, via pipeline. This new plant
will be located to the East of the BOROUGE terminal-3 & to the west of RSHT-2 as depicted
in Fig.1.1 below.

1.2 PROJECT OBJECTIVE AND SCOPE
The primary objective of the RSGP Development project at RSHT-2 is to accommodate
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur received via pipeline from the Hail & Ghasha OPP. This involves
constructing new infrastructure for liquid sulphur receiving storage and transfer, sulphur
forming (Granulation), Granulated sulphur transfer and storage, stacking and reclaiming and
export to existing ship loaders. The project also includes implementing dust control systems,
sulphur remelting unit, utility systems and integration with existing facilities. The developed
facility aims to ensure efficient and environmentally sound sulphur processing, support
ADNOC's sulphur production, and maintain operational safety and reliability.

1.3 PURPOSES OF THIS DOCUMENT
The Project Execution Plan (PEP) serves as the authoritative blueprint to ensure the
| successful delivery of the project in alignment with the Company·s strategic objectives and
expectations. Its primary purpose is to translate agreed-upon goals into a structured
framework, defining clear scope boundaries, deliverables, and milestones while coordinating
resources (personnel, budget, equipment) and timelines via tools such as Gantt charts and
Work Breakdown Structures (WBS). The document proactively addresses risks through
mitigation strategies and contingency plans, embeds quality control protocols to ensure
compliance with standards, and establishes communication mechanisms to maintain
stakeholder alignment. By clarifying roles, responsibilities, and decision-making pathways, the
PEP fosters accountability, minimizes uncertainties, and provides a measurable roadmap for
adaptive management, enabling timely adjustments to deviations while ensuring transparent,
coordinated execution across all project phases.

8.9.1 Engineering Risk MANAGEMENT
Engineering risk management focuses on identifying and mitigating risks during the design, planning,
and technical execution phases of a project. This includes addressing potential design flaws, such as
miscalculations in structural integrity or material specifications, which could lead to failures during
construction or operation. It also involves assessing technological risks, such as compatibility issues
between systems or the reliability of innovative engineering solutions. Regulatory compliance is
another key aspect, ensuring adherence to building codes, environmental regulations, and industry
standards to avoid legal or financial penalties. Additionally, engineering risk management evaluates
long-term performance uncertainties, such as the durability of materials under extreme conditions or
the efficiency of integrated systems. Proactive strategies like Failure Modes and Effects Analysis
(FMEA), peer design reviews, and advanced simulation modeling are employed to minimize these
risks while ensuring project feasibility and sustainability.
8.9.2 Construction risk management
Construction risk management is primarily focused on mitigating risks during the physical execution
of projects. This involves identifying and addressing potential hazards that could impact worker safety,
such as falls, equipment malfunctions, or exposure to hazardous materials. It also encompasses
monitoring contractor performance to ensure compliance with project specifications, safety protocols,
and quality standards. Additionally, this risk management approach evaluates unpredictable site
conditions like unstable soil, underground utilities, or adverse weather that may cause delays or
structural issues. Schedule-related risks are another critical component, including labor shortages,
supply chain disruptions, or inefficient workflow coordination that could derail project timelines.
