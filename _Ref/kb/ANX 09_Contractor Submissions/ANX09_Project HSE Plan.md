1 PROJECT DESCRIPTION
1.1 Introduction
The Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 2 (RSHT-2) is owned by ADNOC and operated by
ADNOC Gas (AG) and is located approximately 240 km west-southwest of Abu Dhabi City in
the Ruwais Industrial Area. RSHT-2 facility is designed to handle bulk granulated sulphur |
coming from Shah and Habshan plant via railcars and transported via new federal railway
(Union Railway) to Ruwais Terminal (SHT-2).
Based on ADNOC recent directives, the Sulphur Handling Scenario at Ruwais SHT-2 (Ruwais
Sulphur Granulation Plant-RSGP) will be developed further due to consideration of receiving
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur from Hail & Ghasha to the new plant, via pipeline. This new plant
will be located to the East of the BOROUGE terminal-3 & to the west of RSHT-2 as depicted
in Fig.1.1 below.

1.2 Project Objective and Scope
The primary objective of the RSGP Development project at RSHT-2 is to accommodate
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur received via pipeline from the Hail & Ghasha OPP. This involves
constructing new infrastructure for liquid sulphur receiving storage and transfer, sulphur
forming (Granulation), Granulated sulphur transfer and storage, stacking and reclaiming and
export to existing ship loaders. The project also includes implementing dust control systems,
sulphur remelting unit, utility systems and integration with existing facilities. The developed

10.28 Best Available Technique (BAT) Review
The use of the best available techniques shall emphasize the use of non-waste technology, if
available. The term ·best available techniques· means the latest stage of development (state
of the art) of processes, of facilities or of methods of operation which indicate the practical
suitability of a particular measure for limiting discharges, emissions and waste. In determining
whether a set of processes, facilities and methods of operation constitute the best available
techniques in general or individual cases, special consideration shall be given to:
> Comparable processes, facilities or methods of operation which have recently been
successfully tried out;
>» Technological advances and changes in scientific knowledge and understanding;
> The economic feasibility of such techniques;
> Time limits for installation in both new and existing plants; and
> The nature and volume of the discharges and emissions concerned.
It therefore follows that what is ·best available techniques· for a particular process will change
with time in the light of technological advances, economic and social factors, as well as
changes in scientific knowledge and understanding. If the reduction of discharges and
emissions resulting from the use of best available techniques does not lead to environmentally
acceptable results, additional measures have to be applied.
·Techniques· include both the technology used and the way in which the installation is
designed, built, maintained, operated and dismantled.

are fully aware of the required control measures to be implemented.
To support a robust Job Safety Analysis (JSA) process, a clear and detailed method statement
must be prepared, outlining how the work will be executed. This document must be approved
by the relevant authorities.
The Performing Authority is responsible for developing the JSA, ensuring that the work scope
content and location are clearly defined. The JSA must identify the potential hazards
associated with the worksite and each task or activity, assess the severity of those hazards,
and establish adequate control measures to reduce risks to as low as reasonably practicable
(ALARP).

that all relevant risks and control measures associated with the job scope are properly
identified and evaluated. The risk assessment team is collectively responsible for validating
the completeness and accuracy of this process. CONTRACTOR shall utilize the COMPANY-
approved 6x6 Risk Assessment Matrix and comply with the requirements of ADNOC HSE
Standard HSE-OS-ST03.

type. This chart will also indicate the level of JSA required based on the nature of the task.

determined by IWAP Team
