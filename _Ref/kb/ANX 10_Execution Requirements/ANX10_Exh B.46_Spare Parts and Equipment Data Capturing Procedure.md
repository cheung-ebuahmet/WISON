B.46.1 
INTRODUCTION 
This procedure is intended to define the COMPANY's requirement to be 
performed by CONTRACTOR for the following activities.   
• 
Equipment Data capturing & providing to Company  
• 
Ordering of Construction, Pre-commissioning and Commissioning Spares 
• 
Ordering of Insurance spares  
• 
Ordering of Special Tools 
• 
Ordering of Operating spares  
• 
Field receiving of all types of Spares and Special Tools 
• 
Deliver of Operating spares,  Insurance spares and Special Tools to 
Company  
B.46.1.1 
PURPOSE 
This procedure defines the scope, requirements, responsibilities, work flow, and 
procedures to be followed for the selection, purchase, field receiving, and 
handover of spares for the Project.  Spares included in this procedure are 
construction, pre-commissioning and commissioning, two years operating, 
insurance (mandatory) spares, and SPECIAL TOOLS as defined in Section 
B.46.1.2. 
This procedure applies to all material / equipment including but not limited to 
electrical, mechanical, instruments and Sub-Contractor furnished equipment

B.46.2 
OVERVIEW OF SPARE PARTS ACTIVITIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES 
The scope of spare parts activities on the PROJECT includes the following: 
B.46.2.1 
CONTRACTOR ACTIVITIES (INCLUDED IN LUMP SUM COST OF AGREEMENT)  
• 
Purchase of INSURANCES SPARES with the original equipment Purchase 
Order.  
• 
Typical list of equipment for which insurance and warehouse spares are 
required is included in Appendix 3.  
CONTRACTOR shall assign a full time Spare Parts Coordinator (SPC) who shall 
report to Project Procurement Manager. SPC should be having experience in plant 
maintenance, knowledge of materials and strong in integrated maintenance system, 
for equipment and spare parts data input in MAXIMO / SAP.  This SPC will receive 
training (training may be provided to more than one person of CONTRACTOR on 
approval from COMPANY) in COMPANY Head Office – Abu Dhabi (for a period of 
maximum 2 weeks in one go).  It is intended that this SPC becomes the leader for 
CONTRACTOR and will provide further training to vendor’s personnel to input the 
required data properly.  COMPANY will not charge CONTRACTOR for this training, 
however all travelling, boarding & lodging etc. to be to arranged and paid by 
CONTRACTOR. 
• 
Assess price level as quoted by VENDOR and negotiate acceptable prices prior 
to submit G-SPIR package to COMPANY.  
• 
Ensure that estimated prices during tender stage or equipment commitment 
stage, are compatible with G-SPIR prices and there is no unjustified price 
increase.  
• 
After submit G-SPIR package to COMPANY for review and when required by 
COMPANY, CONTRACTOR shall negotiate with VENDOR the prices, which are 
not acceptable to COMPANY.  
• 
Status reporting on a line item basis for all INSURANCE SPARES, and 
SPECIAL TOOLS. 
• 
Purchase of CONSTRUCTION SPARES and SPECIAL TOOLS with the original 
equipment Purchase Order. CONSTRUCTION SPARES and SPECIAL TOOLS 
which are unknown at the time of equipment purchase shall be purchased via an 
order supplement.  
• 
Obtain G-SPIR Package from VENDOR and subsequent verification of data.  
Guidelines are detailed in Appendix 2 (G-SPIR Procedure) and Appendix 8 (G-
SPIR Process). 
• 
Obtain from VENDORS, OPERATING SPARES recommended list and prices 
with applicable discount along with offers for main equipment/material, and 
submit to COMPANY along with Procurement Award recommendation.

consequences of equipment downtime to accurately determine whether or 
not an OPERATING SPARE should be recommended for purchase. EPC 
stage RAM study Model shall be utilized to support to evaluate the availability 
improvement with respect to spares quantity. 
* 
Minimize spares cost based on normal delivery time of spares.  Non-critical 
spares with less than 2 week deliveries should not be recommended for 
purchase. 
* 
Professional judgment and experience should be exercised when 
determining quantity recommendations.

STANDARDIZATION OF EQUIPMENT

and direct impact on spares stock investment and promotes a more efficient

standardization should be fully taken into account.

agreed to keep in stock complete units, these should be kept for the purpose 
intended, i.e. replacing a unit, which has failed.

1.0  PRIMARY HIERARCHY  
 
The Locations Hierarchy in G-SPIR shall be reflecting the Assets physical location 
(Primary Hierarchy). The “Primary “ Hierarchy will cater for all location types (i.e. for 
Rotating/ Static equipment, Piping, Electrical, Instrumentation and Civil). 
The Primary Hierarchy reflects the geographical location of all Plant Assets (e.g., 
Equipment, Buildings, Workshops, Packages, Structures, Sub-stations, etc.).

in order to simplify the drill down and search for any particular Asset, and to be able to 
roll up the respective Assets costs. 
The Primary Hierarchy can also be used to group work to be performed across all 
Engineering Disciplines when the Area, Units, Package or main Equipment is shut down.   
Structured Tagging of the Primary Locations is essential for consistency and search 
flexibility. Therefore, business rules have been established by keeping the same 
Tagging used on sites, and identical matching to the DCS Tagging Structure.

1.2.2    Define Sub-equipment Tag: 
 
The sub-equipment is any secondary equipment that has a function to assist 
the main equipment to deliver its role.  The Tag number is also defined as 
per the one used in design, unless a Tag does not exist.  In this latter case, a 
new Tag number is created and structured in a similar consistent way. 
 
The lowest level of a sub-equipment Tag is limited to the equipment that 
need their failure and maintenance costs tracked.  Therefore, general 
isolation valves of smaller sizes are not recorded, since any failure or 
maintenance costs tracking could be done at work order and Area / unit 
levels.  However, the Pressure / Temperature Safety Valves are recorded as 
sub-equipment of the main equipment or the pipeline they protect.

Introduction 
This specification is intended to assist VENDORS supplying equipment Data and 
spares by giving recommendations on both: 
a. The selection and quantities of spares in general

component parts of any piece of equipment. 
1.2 
PURPOSE 
This specification is to advise the VENDOR of his Equipment Data and spares 
obligations and responsibilities: 
a. When preparing the equipment quotation. 
b. After receipt of the equipment Purchase Order. 
This specification covers all Equipment as well as the spares required for 
construction and for two years continuous operation.  Spares are required for all 
mechanical equipment, non-bulk electrical equipment, and instruments supplied 
for the Project.  INSURANCE SPARES will be purchased along with the original 
equipment wherever practical.  Delivery of spares ordered with the original 
equipment such as CONSTRUCTION and INSURANCE SPARES are required 
concurrent with the original equipment.  All other spares shall be delivered prior 
to equipment commissioning.

materials, components or equipment already in progress or completely 
manufactured but which does not meet or comply with COMPANY requirements.  
A concession request is subject to COMPANY approval.
