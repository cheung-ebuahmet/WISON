B.39 REVIEWS, COMMENTS AND APPROVALS
B.39.1 SCOPE
This EXHIBIT B.39 outlines COMPANY·s minimum requirements for the
handling of documentation on the PROJECT and the action to be taken by the
CONTRACTOR.
The attached Guideline Document Classification Table provides guidelines to
be adopted for classification of documents on the PROJECT. CONTRACTOR
shall, within three (3) weeks of the EFFECTIVE DATE, submit for COMPANY·s
review and approval a detailed Document Classification Table which shall be
followed on the PROJECT.
B.39.2 DOCUMENT CLASS
All drawings, documents and manuals to be submitted by CONTRACTOR will
be classified in one of the following three categories for the purpose of review,
comment and approval.
Class 1 All documentation in Class 1 shall require formal COMPANY
approval.
Class 1 documents shall be further categorized as follows:
Cat-1:
Documents requiring Specialist / Independent Reviews
Cat-2:
Documents not requiring Specialist / Independent Reviews
CONTRACTOR shall categorise ALL Class 1 documents as per
above and submit for COMPANY approval accordingly
Class 2 Documentation in Class 2 shall be issued to COMPANY for
comment, if comments are not received within stipulated period.
CONTRACTOR shall inform COMPANY in writing of its intention
to proceed with work.
Class 3 Documentation in Class 3 shall be issued to COMPANY for
information only. However, comments made on Class 3
documents shall also be considered when received in a timely
manner or advised that they are being developed.

6.1.2. FEED
Once the number of options have been selected, a more detailed RAM analysis shall be carried out to
choose the major equipment configuration. From a RAM analysis point of view, the FEED stage is
where the application of analysis becomes most effective · as changes can be easily incorporated in

6.1.3 EPC
| Once the FEED has been finalized, a much more detailed design of the system is specified.
RAM analysis at this stage shall ensure that the system design meets COMPANY·s required

optimum balance of cost and reliability performance. The critical elements and bottlenecks in the

Inspection (RBI) and Reliability Centered Maintenance (RCM). The output from the RBI and RCM
processes can subsequently be fed back into the model to arrive at a final picture of system
performance. For further details refer to Section 8.11.3 of the DGS.
6.1.4 Operate
In the Operate stage, it is not cost-effective to make decisions regarding the design. However, RAM
studies should be used to assess the impact of planned modifications. The most common evaluation
carried out during this stage is related to the maintenance philosophy which, basically, refers to the
number of spares, re-stocking time and available personnel as there is always a trade-off between the
costs of lost production versus the cost of maintenance. Additionally, a performance trend is created
based on the output of ongoing updates to analysis. The impact on availability and reliability for any
·Plant Change Proposal· can also be evaluated.
6.1.5 Disposal / Life Extension
Due to the high number of variables in oil and gas development, it is not easy to identify where the
operational expenditure (OPEX) exceeds the revenue, making the system no longer economically
viable. At this stage, RAM studies, by modelling all dynamic behavior of the system, allow to evaluate
various disposal strategies.
For mature systems/ageing assets, RAM analysis allows to find potential areas for rejuvenation or life
extension of facilities. Many sensitivity (what · if) cases are applied to a mature system model, which
will indicate various life extension options and the potential quantified gains.

modelling the asset while considering all the factors that can potentially impact production.
The most powerful approach to this type of analysis is to use simulation techniques. This means
applying a structured process to mathematically evaluate the behavior of a system. A common

6.3.1 Event Data
Events comprise of any occurrences within a system's life which affect the behavior of the system.
A system can incur a wide range of events from routine (expected) events such as failure and repair
of equipment, routine inspection and overhaul, etc., to abnormal (undesirable) events such as
catastrophic failure, loss of feedstock, loss of manpower, etc. There are six important parameters
which fully define an event:
e Time to Failure, which describes when an event occurs;
e Time to Repair, which describes the overall duration of the event;
e Capacity Loss at Failure, which describes the immediate consequences of the event upon loss
of functionality of the system;
e Capacity Loss at Repair, which describes the consequences of the event during the ·repair·
stage of the event;
e Delay in commencing the repair stage of the event, i.e. time to diagnose what has happened
and decide upon an action to rectify the situation if necessary;
e Actual Repair Time, which describes the duration of the repair stage.

Events can be formally classified depending on their mechanism of initiation, i.e., Unscheduled Events,
Scheduled Events and Conditional Events.
Unscheduled Events are unplanned and occur at random (i.e., their time to failure is irregular).
However, their occurrence usually corresponds to a particular mathematical distribution. The most
common group of unscheduled events are those associated with the failure of equipment within a
system.
Scheduled Events are planned activities (i.e., their time to failure occurs at a known time), typically
routine inspection and overhaul of equipment, phasing-in (or out) of parts of a system etc.
Conditional Events are a special group of events which are initiated by the occurrence of other events.
They offer a very powerful mechanism for representing dependencies between individual events and
the net effect of a group of events upon system performance and system operability.

6.3.4 Cost Data
RAM analysis can be used to estimate expenditures and revenue from product delivery to give an
overall financial picture. This provides a powerful tool to optimize design configuration and
maintenance strategies. It also helps to find a balance between CAPEX and OPEX, and identify the
changes that will be effective by ranking the options with higher return on investment.
Benefits/improvements expressed in terms of cost are always easily understood by all.
The financial calculations can be extended to incorporate product pricing which enables estimation of
the revenue produced. With the revenue, the Net Present Value (NPV) can be calculated. NPV is a
financial figure that allows comparisons between different projects by using all the cash flows from
the project and adjusting them to their present values by applying the appropriate discount factor.
The projects then become directly comparable. Should the present value of the capital inflows exceed
those of the outflows after the selected discount rate has been applied, the project shows a positive
cash flow return · the greater the better. However, if the NPV is negative, the returns from the
project are less than the outflows, and attempts should be made here to minimize the NPV. There are
two options while calculating the NPV · ·Negative NPV· and the ·Standard NPV·. The negative NPV
accounts for the potential loss of revenue whereas the standard NPV accounts for the profit.
The NPV calculation should account for:
e Annual Discount Rate: An annual discount rate in percentage form must be ascertained. In
financial terms, this will relate the worth of a future sum to its present value.

6. RISK AND RELIABILITY MANAGEMENT
6.1. RCM/REM Process Boundary and Interaction with Other RRM Processes
Risk based approach to maintenance strategy development involves identifying the functions and
functional failures, analyzing the failure modes, evaluating the effect of these failures on economics,
health safety and environment and deciding what must be done to reduce these risks as low as
reasonably practicable (ALARP). There are three main methodologies, namely Risk Based inspection
(RBI), Instrument Protective Function (IPF) and Reliability Centered Maintenance (RCM) which looks
into specific type of equipment based on the type of function it is doing.
RBI focusses on pressure containments. In this process the degradation mechanisms, which depend
on the process and the material being used, are analyzed and the risk level is assessed. The outcome
of RBI exercise is mainly inspection tasks and the Integrity Operating Windows (IOW).
IPF is a specific process that is applied for instrument protective equipment. In this process the
instrument loops are analyzed and the risk level in terms Safety Integrity Level (SIL) is assessed.
Outcome of the exercise is mainly the instrument testing task and the frequency of testing so as to
ensure the functioning of the protective action when the demand arises. RCM process boundary in

| 7.1. Introduction
RCM methodology deals with some key issues not dealt with by other maintenance programs. It
recognizes that all equipment in a facility is not of equal importance to either the process or facility
safety. It recognizes that equipment design and operation differs and the operating condition,
resulting in different failures mechanisms. It also recognizes that a facility does not have unlimited
financial and personnel resources and that the use of both need to be prioritized and optimized. RCM
is a systematic approach to evaluate an equipment functional requirement and ensure its reliability
through cost-effective measures.
The process described in this document will be based on RCM 2 approach and complies with RCM

7.2. RCM Premises
Maintenance has evolved over last several years in response to changing world of maintenance.
Expectation on maintenance is much more than just fix it when breaks down. Some of these key
expectations are:
e Greater plant availability and reliability
e Better product quality
e Greater safety
e No damage to environment
e Longer equipment life
e Cost effectiveness
Additional important factors that have contributed to evolution of maintenance is the understanding
of failure mode characteristics and availability of advanced condition monitoring techniques. Earlier
understanding was that as the equipment get older it is likely to fail and the failure management
philosophy was to arrange for scheduled restoration or discard towards the end of its life. The failure
pattern assumed at that time was simple bath-tub curve. The new findings have revealed there are six
failure patterns, categorized into two groups namely age related and non-age related failure patterns.
Only a few percentage of failure modes are likely to be age related whereas a large number of failure
modes are non-age related. Having too many time based maintenance is not going to be effective as
it addresses relatively small percentage of failure modes.

8.6. Failure Mode
After listing all the functional failures, the next step in RCM is to find out what causes these functional
failures. The reason or event that is likely to cause functional failure is called Failure Mode. The failure
mode must be identified at appropriate details so that the causes of functional failure addressed and
not the symptoms. The failure mode should be defined in enough details for it to be possible to select
a suitable failure management policy. As per RCM Standard SAE JA 1011 / SAE JA 1012A the definition
of failure mode is a single event which causes a functional failure.
As a minimum, the description of the failure mode consists of a noun and a verb. Example bearing
failure, impulse line blockage. However, more precise description would be to mention the cause of
failure as well. For example, ·bearing failure due to lack of lube oil· is more meaningful than just saying
·bearing failure·.
8.7. Failure Effect
Failure effect basically describes what happens when a failure mode occurs. It is a short description of
the sequence of failure including whether the failure becomes evident to operating crew under normal
operating conditions. The description should state whether there will be any warning sign or alarm
and whether the machine shuts down or it continues to operate until there is secondary failure. The
failure effect could include effects on HSE and production.
8.8. Failure Consequence (Risk Assessment)
The purpose of assessing failure consequence is to understand the risk associated with the failure
mode and generate a cost effective strategy to address the failure mode. Consequences are of two
types; evident function and hidden function.
e Evident Function: Failure of evident function becomes evident to the operating crew under
normal operating conditions.

8.9. Maintenance Task Selection and Cost Optimization
8.9.1. Task Selection
The actions to address the failure mode can be of two types:
i. Proactive Task: These tasks are done before reaching the failed state. Proactive Maintenance
include Predictive Maintenance (PdM) and Preventive Maintenance (PM). Predictive
maintenance is basically the ·On-Condition· task in RCM language whereas the Preventive
Maintenance includes ·Scheduled Restoration· and ·Scheduled Discard· tasks.
ii. Default Action: This deals with the failed state and this option is selected when there is no
effective proactive task available.
The order of selecting the task is as follows:
i. | On-Condition task (Condition Monitoring an Condition Based Tasks)
ii, | Scheduled Restoration task (Scheduled repair, it is a Time Based maintenance or Scheduled
Maintenance)
iii. | Scheduled Discard task (Scheduled replacement, it is also a Time Based maintenance or
Scheduled Maintenance)
iv. Default Options
On-condition task is based on the premise that the failure of an equipment is preceded by a sufficient
indications and there is sufficient time between the detection of abnormal condition and the
functional failure. Frequency of carrying out condition monitoring task depends on the rate at which
degradation progresses.

9. REVIEW EXISTING MAINTENANCE (REM)
| REM is a simplified process which uses the RCM thinking without actually rigorously going through
every RCM step. In this process the existing proactive maintenance plans and failure history are
downloaded from the Maximo for a selected functional unit. The maintenance plans are reviewed one
by one using brain storming approach by a multi-discipline team facilitated by an RCM facilitator. First
step is to check whether the task is mandatory and necessary to meet the regulatory authority
requirements. Exclude those tasks from which are generated from structured processes like RBI and
IPF from review. Mandatory tasks and the tasks generated from RBI and IPF are retained. The non-
mandatory tasks are subjected to review using REM process. For HSECES related tasks (Performance
Standards) only the frequency can be challenged and not the task itself. Next question to ask is
whether the task is addressing any known or potential failure mode. If the answer is no, then the task
can be deleted. Otherwise the task is further analyzed for its economic values; whether the cost of
doing the task is reasonable as compared to the risk it is reducing. If the task adds value then it is
retained. If the task is not cost effective then the task is modified or a new task is added to address
the failure mode and cost effectiveness is verified again. Flow chart for the REM process is shown

5.2  Criticality Ranking Levels
Criticality ranking indicated using a character assigned to an asset to reflect its importance to safety,
environment, economics consequences, and probability of failure occurrence. There are six levels of
equipment criticality: Non-critical (N), Low (L), Medium (M), Medium High (MH), High (H) and Extreme
(E). ·Extreme· criticality would require measures to be put in place to minimise the risk. The number of
equipment in this category is expected to be very low and hence, for all practical purpose, there are five
levels of criticality. Possible interventions to reduce the risk depends on the criticality ranking of the
asset. Examples of such intervention could be RCM for E, MH and H category, PM Optimisation for M
and L category and ·Run-to-Failure· approach for N category. However, this strategy on preventive
maintenance is elaborated in the RCM guideline document.

6. CRITICALITY ASSESSMENT MATRIX
The Criticality Assessment Matrix is the tool which provides a standard approach to qualitative
assessment of risk. Criticality Ranking is used for identifying a suitable course of action to manage the
assessed risk.
This matrix has two types of Potential Consequences; Economic and HSE consequences, Economic
impact includes loss of processing capacity and asset loss (repair and material cost). HSE consequence
consists of ·harm to People·, ·Environment· and ·Reputation·.
The Probability of failure is assessed using Mean Time Between Failure (MTBF). Source of MTBF data
shall preferable be based on site experience. However, in case there is no sufficient failure history
available to assess MTBF, generic data from external sources such as OREDA can be referred to for
deciding the MTBF value.

7.3 HSE Critical Equipment and Systems
If the location or tag has a MAH management role then it will be categorized as HSE critical equipment
and systems (HSECES). Equipment that are critical to safety are identified based on the HSECES
Identification Guide (IA_HSECES-GU-016).
7.4 Production Critical Equipment (PCE)
Failure of a PCE will result in economic impact to the organisation, either in terms of production loss or
equipment repair cost. Criticality assessment for production critical equipment is carried out at two
levels; functional systems or package level and equipment level.
7.4.1 Functional System Criticality
A Functional System consists of a set of equipment configured in such a way that it performs
desired function in a given operating condition. For example, a compressor package can be
considered as a functional system. There can be several pieces of equipment within this
functional system such as compressor, motor, pump, valve and transmitter.
Criticality shall be assessed at functional system level by analysing the consequence of failure
and probability of occurrence of such failure.
