B.55 
OPERATIONS ASSURANCE REQUIREMENTS 
B.55.1 
INTRODUCTION 
The OA (Operations Assurance) requirements defined within this EXHIBIT shall be 
implemented by EPC Contractor alongside and integral with, all of EPC Contractor’s 
other discipline activities, and its Sub-Contractors, and Company’s other EPC 
Contractors. 
COMPANY recognizes that all Installations must be designed and constructed with 
the proactive participation of suitably qualified and experienced Operations and 
Maintenance (O&M) personnel under circumstances where participation materially 
improves the design and meets Company’s safety, integrity, operability, and 
maintainability objectives. EPC Contractor is responsible for ensuring the Facilities 
provided meet optimum standards of safety, integrity, operability, reliability and 
maintainability and to provide all the documents accordingly. 
All aspects of EPC Contractor engineering, procurement and support services 
activities shall consider the protection of O&M personnel during normal operations 
and during start-up, shutdown and emergency situations. 
All document deliverables required shall be subject to review cycles involving 
Company’s OA Team and incorporation of all comments by EPC Contractor into the 
facility design and construction. 
B.55.2 
OBJECTIVE 
The objective is to: 
• Deliver completed design and procurement packages for safe, operable, 
maintainable and reliable facilities 
• Support the construction, transition to operations and start–up of the facilities 
meeting Company’s HSE and Right First Time Start-up requirements.  
B.55.3 
SCOPE 
This document provides Company’s OA requirements that shall be implemented by 
EPC Contractor as part of the Agreement. These requirements shall be progressed 
alongside, and be an integral part of, the engineering, procurement and support 
services provided by the EPC Contractor during the development of the facilities. A 
summary of EPC Contractor’s Operations Deliverables is provided as Appendix I – 
Operability Assurance Deliverables. 
B.55.4 
ORGANIZATION AND RESPONSIBILITIES 
In order to achieve the objective, EPC Contractor shall provide the proactive and 
auditable participation of suitably qualified and experienced production, maintenance 
and operations supervisory personnel employed within EPC Contractor organization 
and dedicated to the Project. These dedicated EPC Contractor resources shall 
engage with EPC Contractor’s other disciplines and other parties providing a 
comprehensive, knowledgeable, experienced and competent approach to OA.

B.55.5 
PLANNING FOR OA 
Within sixty (60) calendar days after Effective Date, EPC Contractor shall submit a 
comprehensive Operability Assurance (OA) Plan for Company’s review and EPC 
Contractor’s incorporation of Company’s comments. The OA Plan shall define all 
resources, and actions EPC Contractor shall implement to meet the requirements of 
this EXHIBIT. The OA Plan shall be integral and sequential with the progress of all 
aspects of the Work. This Plan shall be continually reviewed by EPC Contractor and 
its status shall be reported and reviewed monthly with Company, and updated as 
necessary to maintain alignment between the facilities development and OA 
requirements..

B.55.15.4 REGISTERS 
Registers are classified under the following two categories: 
i. To manage temporary situations (such as ESD/USD passing valves, temporary 
equipment, temporary repairs, isolations, and temporary overrides); and 
ii. To ensure appropriate state and use of equipment (such as locked valves and 
hoses). 
Registers for Temporary Situations 
Registers which are used to manage temporary situations provide an overview of the 
risk mitigations in place to cover situations that are not as per design. These 
documented items must be verified on asset, agreed on a monthly basis and further 
changes to these items must be managed by the PCM process. 
Registers for Ensuring Appropriate State 
Registers which are used to ensure that the equipment is in an appropriate state and 
use should be checked on asset to ensure they reflect the true status through periodic 
reviews. The locked valve register reflects those valves that should, by design, be 
locked open or locked closed during normal operations. The hose register ensures 
proper management of temporary hoses which are pressure rated and/or contain 
hazardous fluids and are not included in the scope of the maintenance management 
system. Hoses that are permanently fixed to the installations and are included in the 
scope of the maintenance management system do not have to be included in the hose 
register. 
Registers shall be in place for: Passing ESD/USD valves, Temporary repairs, 
Temporary equipment, Locked open/locked closed valves, Hoses, Isolations, 
Temporary overrides. As a minimum, registers shall contain the following: 
• Tag number and location, Date applied, End date 
• Reason for application 
• Risk assessment, Mitigation of risk (only passing valves, temporary repairs, and 
overrides) 
• Approval, Last date verified/physically checked 
• Specific for locked valves: 
o O/C by design (P&ID) 
o Temporary change (either deviation from design or temporarily added LO/LC valves 
to the design list)

B.55.15.6 OPERATING WINDOWS 
There are three different types of limits defined in Company. These limits are referred 
to as Operating Windows, Integrity Windows and Design Windows. 
 
i. 
Operating Windows: are a set of operating ranges and limits (e.g., pressure, 
temperature, flow rates, level, product quality, etc.) that are established for 
process variables within which operation will achieve the desired results. 
Operating within the safe operating windows will ensure safe operation. It is 
important to note that safe operating windows will be defined for each individual 
asset, and each individual piece of major equipment. The Operations staff should 
ensure that each asset/equipment is operated within those limits. 
ii. 
Integrity Windows: are limits beyond which asset integrity deteriorates more 
rapidly leading to unsafe conditions. Operating in this zone shall be avoided to 
minimize the deterioration of the asset. 
iii. 
Design Windows: are a set of limits which, if exceeded, can cause immediate 
unsafe conditions. Relief devices will generally be activated under such 
conditions.  
 
EPC Contractor shall ensure limits are defined and documented covering all 
parameters/checks for assets and major individual equipment, for physical checks by 
the field operators.
