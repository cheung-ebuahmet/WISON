1.0... INTRODUCTION
ADNOC Group Operating Integrity Management Standard (AGAI-STD-0003)" mandates that
·ADNOC Group Companies shall have a controlled set of critical operating documents (including As
built Engineering and Process flow drawings) available at every operating site for use by the site
operating team. Site Operations staff shall refer to controlled documentation prior to undertaking
operations activities.·
Further, Sub-element 12.4 of ADNOC Group Health, Safety and Environment Management System
(HSEMS) Standard(HSE-GA-ST02)®*!, sets expectation that,
·Operating procedures shall be developed, maintained and implemented for operations involving
manufacturing, storage, handling or processing materials. The Group Companies / Affiliates
Management shall supervise and evaluate adherence to the operating procedures·
Operating Procedures and Standard Instructions have been included in ADNOC Gas Processing as
one of the fundamental Operating Integrity elements. Availability of well-written Operating Procedures
(OPs) and Standing Instructions (SIs), and their easy accessibility to all the staff involved in asset
operations sets the foundation for minimizing risks through safe conduct of process-related activities
and supports the strong drive in ADNOC Gas Processing to Ensure Safe Production (ESP).
1.1. Purpose
This document provides detailed guidelines on managing OPs and Sls, and the roles and
responsibilities in each process step to ensure a consistent approach across all ADNOC Gas
Processing assets.
It fulfils the minimum expectations set in AGAI-STD-0003'", HSE-GA-ST02"9 and OE-MAN-008 '7)
and addresses the requirement of risk assessment in each activity.
1.2. Scope
This document applies to all ADNOC Gas Processing existing and new operating assets (including
pipelines). It provides guidance for developing OPs and the roles and responsibilities in each of the
process steps to ensure a consistent approach for Operations related activities.
1.3. Interrelationships and Customers
This guideline applies to all ADNOC Gas Processing operating assets (including pipelines) and
Projects. The interrelationships are described in subsequent sections.
This guideline has been developed as part of risk management in ADNOC Gas Processing's
operations.

2.0. GUIDELINES FOR MANAGING OPERATING PROCEDURES
An Operating Procedure (OP) is a set of written step-by-step instructions and associated information
(notes, cautions, warnings, etc.) for safely performing a task, from start to finish within operating limits.
A few examples for OPs are instructions for carrying out tasks/activities such as collecting a sample,
start/stop equipment, data processing and evaluation, monitoring operating equipment, risk
assessment, and auditing equipment operation.
Further, OPs are used for auditing/verification, determining training needs, determining and
maintaining manning levels, assuring competence, developing records maintenance procedures, or
describing communication procedures.
The development and use of OPs minimizes variation and promotes quality through consistent
implementation of a process or procedure within the organization, even if there are temporary or
permanent personnel changes. OPs can indicate compliance with organizational and governmental
requirements and can be used as part of a personnel training program, as they provide detailed work
instructions.
OPs minimize opportunities for miscommunication and can address safety concerns. When historical
data is evaluated for current use, OPs can also be valuable for reconstructing project activities when
no other references are available. The benefits of a valid OP are reduced work effort, along with
improved comparability, credibility, and legal defensibility.
2.1. OPs Classification
The OPs can typically be classified as:
1. Normal startup and shutdown procedures,
2. Normal OPs,
3. Emergency OPs,
4. Commissioning/decommissioning procedures, and
5. Other OPs such as Test/Trial OPs, etc. that do not fall under the categories described above
and procedures having more than one of above activities
2.2. Requirements for Developing OPs
The following activities shall require approved OPs and NA is not required:
1. Activities identified as HSE critical in the COMAH report;
2. All activities which require procedures as per regulatory requirements;
3. Activities with risk ranking RAM red and yellow 4A, 4B and 5A;
4. Commissioning/decommissioning activities;
5. Startup and shutdown activities;
6. Emergency procedures;

3.0. GUIDELINES FOR MANAGING STANDING INSTRUCTIONS
Standing Instructions (SIs) outline the steps for dealing with situations that are not included in the OPs.
They are primarily intended to address temporary situations and provide sufficient mitigations to
operate assets safely.
Sls can be initiated also for any deviations from OPs, recorded and accessible in the control room. Ifa
change is permanent, the related OP can be updated immediately or in the next revision based on the
criticality.
3.1. Analyze the Process and Initiate SI
The Operations Engineer/SMEs should monitor and initiate the SI as required based on the process
and various other requirements that include logistics, availability of feed/chemicals and HSE.
Any deviation from the OP that affects or might affect the plant·s safeguarding system shall be
managed through the MOC process. Refer to the Override Control Procedure(OE-PR-008Ré),
Management of change (MOC) procedure for Operate phase(OE-PR-026!"*] and Downgraded
Situations Management (DGS) Procedure(OE-PR-052)*· for further details.
For example: Let us assume that the recommended minimum level in a storage tank is 1.5 m.
Due to logistics or any other issues, revision of the minimum tank level (for reordering)
to more than 1.5 m can be managed through the SI process.
On the other hand, revision of the minimum tank level to less than 1.5m should be
handled through the MOC process.
At any point in time, the S! process should not be used as a shortcut for bypassing safe operating
practices.
3.2. Draft/Update and Review
After confirming the requirement, the Operations Engineer/SMEs should draft a SI to mitigate the
temporary situation while defining its validity period.
The Operations Team Leader in the respective site should review the draft and other SMEs and the
writer should incorporate the comments and shall update the SI.
3.3. Approve
Team Leader of the respective plant shall approve the SI clearly capturing its effective date and validity.
The SI will come into force or become ·Active· from the effective date.
3.4. Record
If the drafted SI deviates from an existing OP, this shall be recorded and accessible in the control room
by operation team, this deviation should also be communicated to the operations team.
