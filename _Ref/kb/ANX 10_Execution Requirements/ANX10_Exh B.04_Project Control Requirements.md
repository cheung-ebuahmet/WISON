PLANNING AND SCHEDULING PROCEDURE FOR EPC CONTRACTS
1.0 OBJECTIVE
To provide in a structured format all the information necessary for the CONTRACTOR·S
and COMPANY'S project teams to monitor the project successfully, identify the problem
areas early, initiate timely corrective actions and to capitalize on the opportunities for
schedule improvement.
2.0 SCOPE
This Exhibit B.4.1 covers the methodology to be followed and the requirements to be
satisfied by the EPC Contractor with regard to the development, updating and statusing of
all Engineering, Procurement, Construction and Commissioning activities relevant to the
EPC Contract to ensure effective planning and scheduling during various phases of project
implementation.
3.0 DEFINITIONS / ABBREVIATIONS
a) AR : Award Recommendation
b) COMPANY : ADNOC GAS PROCESSING
c) AGREEMENT : The contract between COMPANY and EPC CONTRACTOR.
d) EDDR : Engineering Document Deliverable Register
e) EPC : Engineering, Procurement, Construction and Commissioning
f) FAT : Factory Acceptance Test
g) FOB : Freight on Board
h) IFR : Issued for Internal Review
i) IFA : Issued for Approval
j) IFD : Issued for Design
k) ICR : Issued for Client Review / Comments
\) IF : Issued for Information
m) IFC : Issued for Construction

CONTRACTOR shall develop the Manpower Development Schedule (MDS) for Field
Services, Consistent with the following requirements:
a) Designations indicated in the sample format (ATTACHMENT-6) are only indicative
and may be changed as required, to suit to CONTRACTOR'S own Organization
Structure. However, CONTRACTOR shall not change the grouping of personnel
identified in the sample format.

Manpower Deployment Schedule and all other related documents shall be identical.

against each subcontract) and Vendor Engineers if any, for the entire duration of

personnel), who are directly involved in the supervision of Construction, Pre-

Construction Manager, Commissioning Manager, Engineers, Superintendents and
other Engineers connected with Construction, Pre-commissioning & Commissioning,
Survey, HSE, QA/QC etc.) Manpower details shall be furnished separately against
each discipline (viz. Civil, Piping, Mechanical, Electrical, Instrumentation etc.)

personnel below the level of Foremen (including Foremen) who are directly involved
in the field activities (viz. Foremen and below including Skilled / Semi-Skilled /
Unskilled workmen, Equipment Operators, Heavy Equipment Drivers etc.).
f) Indirect Manpower (Field Management) shall include all personnel who are
essentially office based and are not directly involved in the construction supervision
activities (viz. Project Manager, Project Control Engineers, Procurement/ Material
Control/ Expediting Engineers, Contract / Subcontract Administrators / Engineers,
Field Engineering Personnel, Warehousing personnel etc.).
g) Other Office Staff including Administration / Personnel, Finance / Accounts and
Camp staff (Viz. catering, maintenance, janitorial services etc.) shall not be included
in the Manpower Deployment Schedule.
h) Subcontractor manpower shall be included separately against each of the proposed
subcontracts.

6.7.1 Engineering Document Deliverable Register (EDDR)
Engineering Document Deliverable Register (EDDR) shall include detail listing of all
engineering deliverables covering all Drawings, Data Sheets, Standards, Specifications,
Method statements, MTO·s, Material Requisitions, Technical Reports, Studies etc. and
shall be prepared separately for each area / unit / discipline, clearly identifying the
category of each document within each discipline.
Engineering Document Deliverable Register (EDDR) shall also include all the engineering
deliverables to be prepared / submitted for the project separately against each subcontract.
Engineering Document Deliverable Register (EDDR) shall not however include the effort
required for technical evaluation of offers received from Vendors and the effort required for
Vendor drawing approvals, since the progress against these activities shall be covered
under Procurement Services. Similarly, any other effort which is not directly related to the
deliverables identified in the EDDR (Viz. Site Visits, Meetings, General/Engineering
Co-ordination, 3-D Model Review etc.) shall be excluded from the weighting system for the
purpose of computation of progress against detail engineering activities.
Each document in EDDR shall be cross referenced to its relevant activity in the Baseline
Schedule through appropriate activity coding structure and will show Estimated Man-
hours/ Weighting against each deliverable. Engineering Document Deliverable Register
shall also include details of Plan, Forecast and Actual dates for submission/approval, as
well as Plan / Earned man-hours (weighting) against each deliverable, based on agreed %
completion at each intermediate Milestone completion stage (Control Point).
Planned completion dates against each deliverable shall be in accordance with the
approved Baseline Schedule. Estimated / Earned Man-hours (Weighting) for each
deliverable will form the basis for computation of progress (Plan / Actual) against each
Area / Phase / Discipline etc. and consequently for the entire Engineering Services.
While developing the Engineering Document Deliverable Register (EDDR), a provision of
minimum 5% of total man-hours shall be allocated towards ·Miscellaneous Documents·
against each Discipline / Area / Phase, as a contingency provision for the likely addition of
documents during detail engineering stage. During the period of implementation, any
unused portion of the contingency will be progressed and equivalent progress claimed in
consultation with COMPANY.
Progress against miscellaneous deliverable shall be planned during later period of the
Project after about 80% of planned progress of Detail engineering for their respective
disciplines. Actual progress shall match the Plan profile of miscellaneous document
activity and shall be claimed in consultation with COMPANY as per following guideline:
* 80% on completion of 80% Detailed Engineering Progress
* 90% on completion of 90% Detailed Engineering Progress

6.1 Progress measurement shall be based upon the physical progress against each of the
activities / sub-activities / deliverables according to a set of weighted values, allocated
according to the effort required to complete. For working out the weightings of various
activities / deliverables within each area and within each project phase, the following basis
will be used:
a) Home Office Services - Man-hours
i) Detail Engineering Services - Estimated Man-hours· for each

each subcontract.
b) Manufacturing & Delivery (Civil - Cost
items and other site procured (Budgeted cost of each Purchase
materials excluded) Order)
c) Construction and Commissioning - Man-hours
(Estimated Man-hours for each activity
based on Physical Quantity of work
involved)
6.2 Relative weightings for developing the overall S-curves against the above three phases
and for each area and / or discipline shall be in accordance with the weightings given in
the PROJECT WBS.
6.3. WEIGHTED VALUE SYSTEM
6.3.1 Each activity or deliverable listed in the respective Planning Register will be assigned
estimated man-hours / cost depending on the estimated work content / cost, as applicable.
6.3.2 Activity or deliverable weight factors shall be computed based on the estimated man-
hours/cost as a fraction of the total man-hours / cost for each Area / Phase / Discipline etc.
and for the whole project.
6.3.3 The weight factors thus computed from Planning Registers shall be rolled upto Level-2
and Level-1 by Area / Phase / Discipline etc. in line with the PROJECT Work Breakdown
Structure (WBS).

1.0 OBJECTIVE
Main Objectives of Schedule Risk Analysis include the following:
a) To identify activity duration uncertainties and risk events that may cause project
delays.
b) To evaluate above Uncertainties/ Risk Events Qualitatively & Quantitatively and to
access the probability of meeting the schedule deterministic completion date.
c) Toplan and implement mitigation measures for the identified risks.
d) To monitor and control the risks that may cause Project delays.

·Schedule Risk Analysis· and includes the specific details to be submitted by the EPC
Contractor as a part of the ·Schedule Risk Analysis· report.

Developing a risk model involves estimating the probability and potential impacts of risks
on the Project and then mapping those impacts to the Project Schedule in the following
sequence:
i) Risk Model based on Duration Uncertainties
ii) Risk Model based on Risk Events
6.3.1 Risk Model based on Duration Uncertainties
·Templated Quick Risk· is used initially for applying the duration uncertainties at the WBS
Level / Summary Level to determine the impact on the project duration. Probability %
range (minimum and maximum) to be followed for risk analysis for each summary level
activity shall be discussed and agreed in advance after discussion with Company.
EPC Contractor shall apply the agreed Correlation Factor (generally 80%) while carrying
out the Schedule Risk Analysis.
After running the Risk Analysis based on the agreed duration uncertainties, thorough
review of Distribution Graph (based on Finish Date) and Tornado Graph (based on
Duration Cruciality) shall be undertaken and duration uncertainties for all major/ critical
activities shall be adjusted as required, to reflect the extent of uncertainties as accurately
as possible.
Results of final Templated Quick Risk shall be submitted by the Contractor in the form of
·Distribution Graph· & ·Tornado Graph· as per formats given at ATTACHMENTS · 4 & 5.
Distribution Graph shall be based on the ·Finish Date· while the Tornado Graph shall be
based on the ·Duration Cruciality·.
6.3.2 Risk Model based on Risk Events
a) Identification of Risks and Development of Risk Register
EPC CONTRACTOR shall identify risks (external and within the Project Schedule) for the
entire Project scope covering all phases of the Project, that may impact the Project
completion date. The risks identified by the EPC Contractor shall be listed in the Schedule
Risk Register as per format given at ATTACHMENT-6. EPC Contractor shall ensure that
Risk Register shall contain only the unknown risks (unknown unknowns) which may
happen only rarely but will have adverse impact on the completion Schedule in the event
of such risks happening. Inclusion of known uncertainties and / or Assumptions /
Constraints in the Risk Register will be avoided since all such risks will form part of the
duration uncertainties. Similarly, known ·Issues· will not find a place in the Risk Register.
Risk events that could disrupt the project shall also be identified and included in the Risk
Register alongwith their likelihood and potential impact on the project duration.

ADNOC CONTRACT TRENDING PROCEDURE

1. PURPOSE
The purpose of the EPC Contract Trending Procedure is to establish a
methodology for early identification, authorization and control of potential
changes from the AGREEMENT, through continuous monitoring of
developments. The desired outcome of such a procedure is communication
resulting in corrective action/resolution prior to problems; expediency in
addressing issues of cost and schedule; and successful settlement of changes
during the course of the AGREEMENT.
2. SCOPE
This procedure outlines requirements for the identification of potential changes,
preparation of the trend notices, responsibilities and administration of trends.
The scope of the procedure covers those changes to the AGREEMENT
requirements that could potentially impact the cost, schedule and / or have
detrimental impact on quality.
For the sake of clarity it shall be noted that those design Deviations and
Concession Requests that do not impact the cost, schedule and / or have
detrimental impact on quality and HSE are excluded from the scope of this
procedure and shall be dealt with in accordance with Project MOC Procedure
OE-PR-048 section 2.5.
Non-Conformance Reports (NCR) are not covered by this procedure. Only in
case CONTRACTOR·s remedial action is not acceptable shall the NCR be
considered for a CTN.
3. PROCEDURE
3.1 Identification of Trends
Only those potential changes that fall outside the AGREEMENT and could be
construed to impact cost, schedule or do not have detrimental impact on quality
and HSE should be considered for a trend notice.
3.2 Timing
After a potential change is identified, the Contract Trend Notice (CTN) should be
initiated promptly. Within ten (10) calendar days after the event which causes a
potential change, a CTN should be prepared and issued by the CONTRACTOR.

This Invoicing Procedure describes and details various aspects of the invoicing
requirements to be followed by the CONTRACTOR during the execution of the WORK.
