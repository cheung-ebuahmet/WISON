1 GENERAL
1.1 Introduction
This specification describes the minimum technical requirements for the design, construction and testing
aspects of Piling and covers the following:
a. Design, construction/installation, protection, inspection and repair of the following onshore piling
works:
i. RCC bored cast-in-situ concrete pile including pile caps.
ii. Driven pile including pile caps
iii. Sheet piles
iv. Micro piles
b. Design, construction/ installation, inspection and repair of the following piling works for marine and
coastal structures such as loading platforms, breasting dolphins, mooring dolphins, pipe/access
trestles, ship loader support structure, etc.
i. RCC bored cast-in-situ concrete marine/coastal piles
ii. Driven steel marine and coastal piles
iii. Drilled and grouted piles
iv. Special piles
c. Load testing of piles.
This specification excludes piling for offshore steel structures such as well head towers, process platforms,
offshore complexes, offshore flares, riser structures, bridge structures, etc. for which relevant COMPANY
specifications shall be referred.
1.2 Purpose
This specification provides the minimum requirements for standards, design criteria, products, materials,
procurement and execution to accomplish the piling works described herein, as outlined on the drawings
and Contract documents.

7.1.3 Geotechnical Design
This section applies to the design of bored, driven, and raked piles, based on geotechnical considerations
and covers requirements related to group effect of piles, effect of negative skin friction on piles, and pile
subjected to lateral and dynamic loading.
a. Bored cast-in-situ reinforced concrete pile is suitable for almost all type of soils. These piles are
particularly advantageous for non-uniform soil strata, boulder or rocky strata where driving of pile
becomes difficult.
AGES-SP-01-008 Rev. No: 1

ADNOC Classification: Internal
b. The design load carrying capacity of pile shall be determined considering soil parameters as per
approved soil investigation report and due consideration to the recommendations provided by the
geotechnical consultant and in accordance with BS 8004 and BS EN 1997. Geotechnical investigation
and soil testing shall be carried out in accordance with respective AGES specifications. Initial pile
loads test shall be carried out to establish the pile capacities at site and to finalize the method of
installation of pile to be followed at site.
c. When a pile is installed through a recent fill or very loose strata which undergoes settlement/
consolidation after the pile is installed, the downward movement of the soil will cause a drag on the
pile. This drag is termed as negative drag and its effect shall be considered while calculating the
design capacity of pile.
d. Piles are generally designed for compression, tension and lateral loading and their applicable
combination. Loads and load combinations shall be as per AGES-SP-01-003.
e. When a pile is installed at an angle to the ground surface, the pile is called as raking pile. Raking
piles are generally provided where the structure subjected to a tremendous lateral load such as water
front structures, high retaining wall, towers etc. When raking piles penetrate through soft soil strata,
the effect of bending moments shall be taken into account in the design. Effects of the rake on the
stresses during pile driving shall be considered.
f. Tension pile is the pile which is mainly designed to resist the uplift forces. Wherever possible the axis
of the pile shall be coinciding with the direction of loading.
g. The lateral capacity of a pile is defined as the load corresponding to specified displacement as per
the performance or design requirements of the structure. The following parameters shall be taken
into account for design of laterally loaded piles:
i. Stiffness of the pile
ii. Stiffness of the surrounding soil
iii. Passive (yield) earth pressure and residual soil strength
iv. Bending stresses in the pile
v. Pile deformations
vi. The pile head displacement and rotation that can be tolerated by the superstructure
vii. Pile caps and tie-beams as applicable
viii. Effects of cyclic loading
ix. Interaction with axial loads.
x. For short piles, rotation & translation as a rigid body
xi. For longer pile bending failure of the pile, along with the local yielding and displacement of soil
near the pile head.
h. When the pile is subjected to vibratory loads, the analysis of pile foundation shall be carried out
considering the effect of soil pile interaction, dynamic properties of pile by P-Y method or any other
universally accepted method. For analysis of pile, the dynamic parameters of soil shall be obtained
by geotechnical investigation in accordance with respective AGES specification and pile dynamic
parameters shall be obtained from dynamic pile load test.
i. Pile interaction factors shall be considered for design of the pile group capacity. Pile group effect to
be considered for assessing pile resistance to lateral loading. Lateral loading to a pile may result in
combination of compression, tension and lateral load in individual pile which shall be considered in
the pile design. Settlement analysis of group effects shall be checked. Possible reduction of the
negative friction on the inner piles of a group shall be taken into account.

7.3.5 Negative Skin Friction
a. Negative skin friction refers to the downward drag exerted on piles by surrounding soil due to its
downward movement or settlement relative to the pile. This may produce additional load on piles
which is transferred by shearing stress that develops at soil-shaft interface.
b. As per FHWA guidelines, soil relative movement of the order of 10 to 12 mm is all that is necessary
for negative friction to mobilize.
c. In a normal loading case, pile settles relative to the ground and in this process mobilizes a positive
frictional resistance.
d. The depth where the shear stress along the pile changes over from negative skin friction into positive
shaft resistance is called as neutral axis / plane. This axis is where there is no relative displacement
between pile and soil. In cases where piles are subjected to negative friction (in loose soil, artificial
Island), neutral axis / plane may be considered at bedrock elevation (i.e. interface between
compressible material, i.e. dredged sand fill/existing silty sand layer and bedrock).
e. Creep settlements of compacted reclaimed/dredged fill material shall be assessed by CONTRACTOR
based on the findings from the geotechnical investigation and if it is estimated that the relative
movement between soil and pile will be sufficient to induce significant negative skin friction over and

7.3.6 Liquefaction Analysis
a. Verification of the liquefaction effects on seismic design of pile foundations shall be considered for
saturated soil fills in accordance with geotechnical findings and recommendations.
b. Liquefaction is defined as a phenomenon where in saturated cohesion-less soil under undrained
conditions loses a large percentage of its shear resistance when subjected to monotonic cyclic or
shock loading and flows in a manner resembling a liquid until the shear stresses acting on the mass
are as low as the reduced shear resistance. There is a sudden increase in pore water pressure
resulting in loss of shear strength, which takes place when the cyclic stresses on a soil layer (CSR;
cyclic stress ratio) exceeds the cyclic resistance ratio CRR.
c. Factor of safety (FOS) of liquefaction shall be evaluated based on Project liquefaction hazard
assessment and compared with the allowable limits. Considerable judgement is needed while
weighing the critical factors involved in the determination of Factor of Safety against liquefaction. Few
of these factors include structural vulnerability, acceptable levels of risk, magnitude of earthquake
event considered, SPT blow counts/ CPT values and the uncertainties involved and levels of design
ground motion.
d. The total earthquake induced loads on piles shall take into consideration of inertial loads imposed by
the superstructure to the pile head and the Kinematic forces acting along the embedded length of the
pile due to movement of soil. Natural Frequency of the superstructure, change in stiffness of the soil,
loss of soil support, etc. shall be considered.
e. Any additional loading on the piles and the pile cap that arise due to lateral spreading, particularly in
the presence of non-liquefied soil strata overlying liquefiable soil shall be considered.
f. COMPANY approved software shall be utilized for detail pile capacity evaluation wherein both inertial
and kinematic effects shall be modelled.
g. Unless specified by Project documents / geotechnical investigation recommendations, factor of safety
(FOS) of liquefaction computed for all granular soil layers as a ratio between CRR and CSR shall not
be less than 1.5 for which further analysis of liquefaction & lateral ground displacement is generally
not required. Effects of lateral spreading of soil, settlement, etc. shall be evaluated in detail for FOS
between 1.0 to 1.5. FOS less than 1.0 shall be considered to be susceptible to liquefaction and
requires Project specific mitigation measures including Site Class verification. Seismic design of Pile
foundations and applicable reduction factors for pile axial load/ lateral load capacities shall be
considered accordingly.

7.4 Driven Piles
7.4.1 General
This section describes the general principles of design, execution of driven piles which are installed in
ground using impact, vibration, pressing, screwing or a combination of these methods, Excavation is not
carried out unless for removal of obstructions or to assist penetration. Such piles are generally preferred
in areas with loose soils wherein the driving of pile itself results in densification and hence improvement
of the soil. Such piles are not recommended in soils where there is a possibility of development of high
pore pressure due to the driving of the pile. Driven pile is not suitable in rocky or boulder strata due to
diameter and length restrictions and uncertainty in concrete quality (Improper cleaning of base, soil
inclusion etc.).
7.4.2 Design Considerations
7.4.2.1 Structural Design
a. The piles shall have necessary structural strength to transmit the loads imposed on it to soil. BS EN
12699 and BS EN 1991 shall be considered for assessing the structural capacity of piles. All
calculations shall be performed by specialist and submitted for COMPANY approval.
b. One or more driving tests are to be conducted in absence of comparable experience.
c. Drivability of piles shall be investigated taking into account any specific requirement in design and
techniques used for assisting pile driving.
d. The minimum longitudinal reinforcement shall be 1.0% of the gross cross sectional of pile.
e. Structural design of concrete piles shall be as per BS 1997 or BS EN 1992.
f. Structural design of composite piles shall be as per BS EN 1994.

account for determining the driving criteria.
b. Driving stresses calculated shall be within permissible limits as per BS EN 12699.

In soft rock the pile toe can be strengthened using bands, extra reinforcement and plates.
d. The joints in the piles shall be capable of maintaining the alignment during installation and safely
resist the stresses generated.

7.6 Micro Piles
7.6.1 General
a. This section describes the general principles of design, execution, testing of micro piles. Such piles
are generally drilled or driven micro pile having diameter not greater than 300mm. Micro piles are
structural members to transfer loads to the ground and limit deformation of structures.
b. Micro piles are generally used for the structures where there are space restrictions, reinforcing or
strengthening of existing structures and underpinning works for reducing settlement of the structures.
7.6.2 Design Considerations
7.6.2.1 Structural Design
a. The piles shall have necessary structural strength to transmit the loads imposed it to the soil. Relevant
BS codes (BS 8081, BS 8004 & BS EN 14199) or ACI 543R and specific requirements shall be
considered to apply for assessing the structural capacity of piles.
b. The cased and uncased length of each micro pile shall be designed to resist the forces distributed to
the micro pile based on the micro pile inclination and spacing.
7.6.2.2 Reinforcement
a. The amount of steel reinforcement placed in a micro pile is determined by the loading it supports and
the stiffness required to limit the elastic displacement. Reinforcement may consist of a single
reinforcing bar, a group of reinforcing bars, a steel pipe casing or rolled structural steel. For a group,
the individual bars can be separated by the use of spacers or tied to the helical reinforcement, to
provide area for grout to flow between the bars and ensure adequate bonding between the bars and
grout or bars can be bundled, provided adequate development length for bundle is provided.
Generally bar sizes used in micro pile range in diameter from 25 mm to 63 mm. Minimum grout cover
to reinforcement is 20mm.
b. In aggressive ground conditions, corrosion protection system such as an additional grout cover as
per site conditions, encapsulation, and cathodic protection may be considered.
7.6.2.3 Grout
In general, grout mixture consists of cement, water and admixtures such as plasticizers, sand to achieve
the required working conditions. Grout, have a major impact on the carrying capacity of the micro pile.
The grout needs to have adequate property of fluidity, strength, stability and durability. Of all the factors
fluidity and set properties, water/cement ratio of the grout is the most important. The water cement ratio
of the grout material shall be limited to 0.50. The allowable bleed after 2 hours shall be less than 3%.
Grout shall be tested in accordance with BS 1881 & BS EN 196-2.
7.6.2.4 Permanent casing
For permanent casing that will be shop or field welded and all welded seams and splices shall be complete
penetration welds. Threaded casing joints shall develop at least the required compressive, tensile, and/or
bending strength used in the design of the micro pile.
7.6.2.5 Spacing of micro piles
The spacing of micro piles shall be considered in relation to type of micro pile, diameter of pile, length of
pile, site conditions, and the group performance. The interference effect of one micro pile with another
micro pile during installation shall be considered while determining the spacing, orientation and installation
sequence.

c. One of the following three categories of the reaction system shall be utilized based on the site

i. Reaction Piles

2. A number of reaction (anchor) piles can be placed surrounding the test pile and will provide

ii. Kentledge
1. Should the ground conditions or site constraints preclude the use of reaction piles, the
alternative is to use kentledge. A frame is assembled over the pile to be tested on top of
which an amount of weight (a minimum 110 to 120% of maximum test load) is safely stacked.

deflection of the supports.

volume of the constituent materials an adequate factor of safety against error shall be allowed.
iii. Bi-directional method (O-cell)
This system is usually only applicable to conventional auger bored piles, and involves a load cell
or cells placed in the pile bore either at the pile base or part of the way up the pile shaft during
the concreting operation. During the test, the cell is hydraulically expanded so the upper part of
the pile reacts against the lower part. Where the cell is at the base of the pile, the soil at the base
provides the reaction. More than one cell can be provided to test different sections. There must
however always be sufficient shaft resistance from the pile section above the load cell to provide
the necessary reaction force to stop the pile being forced out of the ground. As there is no reaction
frame assembly only a small working area is required, dictated by the independent method of

f) Pile Group Effect & Efficiency
Pile Group Effect
a. Distance between center to center of piles in a pile group shall be minimum 3 times the pile diameter
(3D) except for any case(s) which requires optimization due to constructability or to mitigate clash
with underground facility. In cases where it is not possible to maintain minimum spacing of 3D, a
reduction coefficient will have to be accounted for in the calculation of total axial / vertical load capacity.
b. According to FHWA NHI-10-016 predominantly friction piles having center to center spacing greater
than or equal to 3D (D is the pile diameter) are considered to have the same axial /vertical capacity
as individual piles.
c. However, piles subjected to lateral loads may suffer under the effect of “shadowing”. This effect is
described as the overlapping of failure zones, where the front row of piles pushes into
virgin(undisturbed) soil while the trailing piles push into the “shadow” of the front row piles or the yield
zones created at the back of the leading piles. Thus the total lateral load resisted by a Pile group will
be less than the sum of the resistances of the individual Piles. Further, the front row of Piles will have
higher soil resistance than the trailing piles and will accordingly carry a greater percentage of the total
lateral load.

h) Liquefaction Analysis
The procedure described below essentially compares the cyclic resistance ratio (CRR) [the cyclic stress
ratio required to induce liquefaction for a cohesion less soil stratum at a given depth] with the earthquake-
induced cyclic stress ratio (CSR) at that depth from a specified design earthquake (defined by a peak
ground surface acceleration and an associated earthquake moment magnitude).
Liquefaction Factor Of Safety = Cyclic Resistance Ratio (CRR)/Cyclic Stress Ratio (CSR)
Where,
CRR = f(CPT / Shear Wave Velocity)
CSR = f(Earthquake PGA)
CSR is estimated as
CSR fs = 0.65 x (amax /g) x (sv0 / s’v0) x rd x fs
Where:
CSRfs = cyclic stress ratio induced by an earthquake magnified by a safety factor ‘fs’
amax = peak ground horizontal surface acceleration
sv0 = total vertical overburden pressure
s’v0 = effective overburden pressure
rd = is the stress reduction coefficient determined by NCEER 1997.
0.65 = weighing factor introduced by Seed to calculate the number of uniform stress cycles required
to produce the same pore water pressure increment as an irregular earthquake ground motion.
The CRR, seen as the soil strength in resisting cyclic motion, can be derived using different methods
incorporating SPT (standard penetration test) or CPT (cone penetration test)).
The Robertson and Wride’s modified method (published after the NCEER 1997 workshop) may be
utilized and the theoretical approach followed in this software is explained below:
CRRM = CRRV x MSF
Where:
CRRM = CRR corrected to a magnitude M
CRRV = CRR7.5 x ka x kβ
CRR5 = CRR based on an earthquake of M
Ka = correction factor for initial shear stress = 1
Kβ = correction factor for overburden stress ranging from 0.6 to 1.0
MSF = magnitude scaling factor = 102.24 / M2.56
CRR5 is calculated as follows:
CRR2 = 0.833 [(qc1N)f / 1000] +0.05 If (qc1N)f< 50
CRR5 = 0.833 [(qc1N)f / 1000] +0.05 If 50 ≤ (qc1N)f ≤160
Where;
(qc1N)f = qc1N + Dqc1N
(qc1N)f = measured tip resistance corrected for fines content
