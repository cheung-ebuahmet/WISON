GENERAL
Introduction
This Specification establishes the minimum requirements for the design and engineering of gravity-
based drainage systems for onshore facilities. The drainage systems addressed are Clean Water
System (CWS), Accidently Oil Contaminated (AOC), Continuously Oil Contaminated (COC) and
Sanitary Sewer (SS). It also includes the minimum material and installation requirements for these
drainage systems.
This specification should be read in conjunction with the project specific drainage philosophy.
Purpose
This Specification contains the minimum criteria for the classification, design and engineering of site
drainage runoff and collection systems including all hydraulic calculations for rainfall and firefighting
capabilities. It also prescribes the requirements for the materials and installation of underground sewer
and drainage pipes, with the necessary fittings, manholes, catch basins, sumps, pits, traps and lined
ditches.
Excluded from this Specification are the design and construction requirements for pressurised drainage
systems, closed drain systems, cryogenic drainage systems and offshore platform drainage systems.
Also excluded from this Specification are requirements related to the secondary treatment facilities such
as sewage treatment facilities, waste water treatment facilities, etc.

Effluent Classification
Atmospheric drainage effluents are classified as belonging to one of the following categories:
8.3.1 Clean Water System (CWS)
Clean rainwater draining to the Clean Water System (CWS) drain. This is applicable exclusively to
areas where surface rainwater cannot be contaminated by hydrocarbons or chemicals.
8.3.2 Accidentally Oil Contaminated System (AOC)
Effluents from areas where spills or leakages of hydrocarbons or chemicals may be anticipated but
not expected. This system collects storm water / fire water run-off from paved equipment areas
divided into AOC catchment areas. These effluent streams normally meet the required water
discharge specifications but on occasion may be contaminated.
8.3.3 Continuously Oil Contaminated System (COC)
Effluent collected underneath the oil process equipment such as pumps, compressors, vessels and
manifolds that are likely to produce drips and spillage in routine operation. Such effluent shall be
collected from a drip tray or bunded area. This also includes water collected underneath oil loading
areas including loading arms, hose handling areas at jetties and filling points.
This system collects effluent water with possible hydrocarbon content that exceeds the required
water discharge specifications.
8.3.4 Sanitary Sewer System (SS)
Domestic sewage draining to the sanitary sewer (SS). Sanitary sewer system mainly includes waste
streams from toilets, showers, sinks, kitchens and canteen facilities. No hydrocarbons shall be
allowed to enter the sanitary sewer.
8.3.5 Closed Drains (CD)
Additional underground hazardous Process Effluent Streams, comprising largely of pure
hydrocarbons or chemicals which are routed to closed systems. The Closed Drain (CD) is defined
as a fully contained drain – a hard pipe connecting the equipment to be drained to the collection
system. The system connects to a collection vessel for recovery. Examples of Closed Drains include:
i. Process water effluents such as Sour Water (SW) draining to a closed drain.
ii. Hydrocarbon vessel drain - draining to a dedicated plant Closed Drain (CD) system.
iii. Amine discharge draining to a closed Amine drain (SDM)
iv. Glycol discharge draining to a closed Glycol drain (GD)
v. Boiler blowdown and emergency water dumping (BWS)

piping specifications. The collected discharges are pumped via above ground drain headers to the

Floating hydrocarbons from effluents at gravity separators shall be skimmed. The selection of type

i. type of treatment facility (e.g., Oily Water Separator, first flush interceptor), and requirements

ii. the quantity of floating hydrocarbon expected;
iii. the economics (costs versus recovery);
iv. operability (manual versus automatic, inclusive of maintainability).

Fixed pipe skimmers shall only be used when the water level in an interceptor is always constant.

10.11.5 Operational Instructions and Requirements
It is important that regular skimming of the free oil is carried out, recommended to do at least twice
a week. Setting the adjustable skimmer in a continuous skimming position is an alternative but is
not recommended as it often results in skimming more water than oil.
Pump start and stop functions (level switches and manual) to be tested once a week.
Since the interceptor configuration is recommended to be an open structure, dirt and sand will find
its way into the sump and will settle at the bottom. The bottom profile is to be designed with a slope
towards a cleaning channel, where the collected dirt can be removed by means of a vacuum truck
/ gulley sucker.

The design of drainage networks shall consider the maximum use of indirect flows to reduce peak

Leak Test for Drainage Systems
The bedding and back fill shall be sufficiently applied to support the pipe. The joints shall be left
exposed until the completion and acceptance of the test by COMPANY.
The section of pipe to be tested shall be sealed between the upstream and downstream manholes
of the pipe section.
The pipe shall be filled with water to a head of not less than 1.5 m above the maximum head which
the pipe will normally sustain or to the top of the sump, whichever is lower.
After topping up and allowing for water absorption by the pipes and joints, the head shall be held
for 24 hours to the satisfaction of COMPANY. Any visible leaks detected at the joints shall be
repaired and the section shall be retested until compliance is achieved.
Leakage shall not be permitted in any circumstances. CONTRACTOR shall demonstrate that all
underground piping systems are watertight.
All sumps, manholes and basins shall be hydraulically tested, by filling up to the top of the structure
with water and visually show no leakage for at least 24 hours.

SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS FOR ADNOC ONSHORE
A2.1. The AOC First Flush Interceptor (AOC Retention basin)
The AOC First Flush Interceptor shall be sized to retain greater of:
i. 30 minute of firefighting water run-off, or
ii. one hour of the design storm water run-off collected via the AOC drainage system
Beyond specified duration or peak flow, the overflow will be diverted to evaporation pond.
A2.2. Evaporation Pond
The Evaporation Pond shall be provided with an inlet facility (Inlet Oil Separator) which shall be
provided with a baffled outlet, a V-notch weir which is designed to clear free floating oil and facilities
to split the flows between the two halves of the Evaporation Pond. The inlet facility shall be provided
with narrow weiring penstocks which shall cause the level to rise when the pump is operating such
that floating oil is automatically skimmed off via a V-notch weir. The residence time of the inlet
facility shall be a minimum of one hour of the capacity of the pump. Skimmed oil shall be returned
to the AOC First Flush Interceptor (AOC Retention basin) via the AOC drainage system.
Beyond specified peak flow, the overflow will be diverted to desert / low lying area.
