trowelled, and finally hand trowelled. The first trowelling after power floating shall produce a
smooth surface that is somewhat free of defects, but which may still show some trowel marks.
Additional trowelling shall be done by hand after the surface has hardened sufficiently. The final
trowelling shall be done when a ringing sound is produced as the trowel is moved over the surface.
The surface shall be thoroughly consolidated by the hand trowelling operations. The finished
surface shall be essentially free of trowel marks, uniform in texture and appearance and shall be
plane to a Class A tolerance, except tolerance for concrete on metal deck shall be Class B as per
Table 9-6.
On surfaces intended to support floor coverings, any defects of sufficient magnitude to show
through the floor covering shall be removed by grinding.
c) Broom and Belt Finish
Immediately after the concrete has received a float finish as specified in section 9.12.1.1 (a), it
shall be given a coarse transverse scored texture by drawing a broom or burlap belt across the
surface.
d) Scratched Finish
After the concrete has been placed, consolidated, struck off, and levelled to a Class C tolerance
as per Table 9-6, the surface shall be roughened with stiff brushes or rakes before the final set.
e) Nonslip Finish
Where the contract documents require a nonslip finish, the surface shall be given a Dry-Shake
application, as specified in section 9.12.1.1 (f), using crushed ceramic bonded aluminium oxide or
other specified selected abrasive particles. The rate of application of such material shall not be
less than 1.25 Kg per m2.

9.13 Curing
9.13.1 General
Curing and protection shall be in accordance with ACI 301, ACI 305.1 and ACI 308R or BS EN 1992-
2 and BS 8500-2. The following shall apply:
a) Water used for concrete curing shall comply in all respects with the requirements of BS EN 1008
or ACI 308R and this specification.
b) Minimum curing period for concrete with ggbs/micro silica shall not be less than 10 days.
c) The use of curing compounds shall be permitted subject to COMPANY approval and shall comply
with the requirements of ASTM C309-19. However, it shall be applied after minimum 7 days of
wet curing, for concrete with ggbs/micro silica.
d) Hessian shall be in accordance with AASHTO M182 and shall weigh a minimum of 0.3 kg/m2.
e) Impervious sheeting shall be minimum 0.10mm thick, white opaque, polyethylene in accordance
with ASTM C 171.
f) Efficient monitoring to ensure that curing is continuous and effective throughout the curing period
shall be properly scheduled and supervised.

9.13.2 Horizontal Surfaces
Every part of the exposed surface shall be covered with a layer of sealed polyethylene per ASTM
C171 or similar impermeable sheeting immediately after finishing. The sheeting shall be in close
contact with the concrete, unless some initial separation is necessary to obtain a particular surface
finish. The sheets shall overlap adjacent sheets at the edges by at least 400mm, and they shall be
fixed down at the ends and edges to prevent air circulation over the concrete. The following shall apply:
a) As soon as the concrete has stiffened sufficiently and after not more than 6 hours, the sheeting
layer shall be completely replaced with a layer of wet hessian or absorptive mats covered with a
layer of sealed polyethylene or similar impermeable sheeting.
b) Additional water shall frequently be introduced under the sheeting in order to keep the hessian
thoroughly and permanently wet.
c) When it is impossible to cover the whole of the surface closely with wet hessian (e.g. where a lot
of starter bars protrude from it), pounding with water of the quality recommended for mixing is an
acceptable alternative. Water shall be renewed regularly to ensure that salt concentration in the
water is not increased by evaporation.
9.13.3 Vertical Surfaces
Immediately after concrete placement, metal formwork shall be kept continuously wet until formwork
is removed, and normal curing can commence. The following shall apply:
a) Directly upon removal of the formwork, the concrete shall be closely wrapped with wet hessian
and enclosed with sealed polyethylene sheeting. Battening shall be used to keep both hessian
and polyethylene lightly in contact with the concrete surface. Care shall be taken to ensure that
pressure from the battens does not impede the downward flow of water within the hessian layer.
b) Additional water shall continuously be introduced on the hessian by means of trickle water hoses
in order to keep the hessian thoroughly and permanently wet.
c) If striking operation of large vertical areas is planned to take longer than 4 hours, all exposed
concrete must be continuously sprayed with water until the curing system is installed. Alternatively,
if practicable the curing system shall be installed as the formwork striking operation proceeds.
9.13.4 Inaccessible Areas
Curing compound may be used on slab soffits, elevated beams sides and soffits, inaccessible column
faces and high shear walls. The following shall apply:
a) Curing compound shall be sprayed on the surfaces immediately upon stripping the formwork in
accordance with the MANUFACTURER’s recommendations, with care to be taken to ensure the
required rate of spread is achieved. Brushing is generally better than spraying.
b) The curing compound must be compatible with any future finishes, where required, or otherwise
to be totally removable.

9.15 Liquid Retaining Structures
9.15.1 General
Construction of liquid retaining structures shall conform to ACI 301 or BS EN1992-3 in conjunction
with BS EN1992-1-1. Any structure designated as a hydraulic (liquid containing) structure on the
Engineering drawings shall comply with the following:
a) Hydraulic structures shall be cured by a wet-cure procedure only (e.g., use of ponding, sprinkling,
or a moisture-retaining fabric).
b) Moist wood forms in contact with concrete shall not be considered as curing for hydraulic
structures.
c) Immediately after placement, concrete shall be protected from premature drying, excessive high
or low temperatures, rain, flowing water, or mechanical damage.

9.16 Fibre Reinforced Concrete
9.16.1 Methods of Preparing Fibre Reinforced Concrete
The preparation method for fibre reinforced concrete depends on the quantity of work and nature of
the application such ground bearing concrete slabs. Fibres can be used in various ways such as
manual mixing, and site mixing using batch plants.
a) Manual Mixing
In this method, initially the required quantity of fibres is added to mixing water. The water shall be
stirred continuously to ensure fibres have been separated from each other and dispersed evenly.

The water in which fibres are dispersed shall then be added immediately to the dry mix ingredients
to prepare the concrete depot.
b) Batch Plant
When large amount of concrete is required, usually batch plants are used. In this procedure, the
required mixing water is added to mixer vessel and the required quantity of fibres is added to the
rotating drum. All other ingredients such as sand, gravels, and cement shall then be added and
rotated until a homogenous concrete is prepared and ready for use.
9.16.2 During the mixing process the reinforcing fibres shall be uniformly and rapidly dispersed without balling
or agglomeration.

9.18 Concrete Repair
The concrete surfaces shall be sound, solid and free from defects. All corners (except underside of
foundation) shall have a 25 x 25 mm chamfer. Concrete surfaces shall not be repaired or touched up
after the removal of formwork until the surfaces have been inspected and released in accordance with
the quality procedures. The following shall apply:
a) Before starting any concrete work, a repair method statement shall be developed and submitted
to the COMPANY for review and approval. The following shall apply:
1) The repair materials shall be based on cement types.
2) The cured mortar colour shall be approximate to the colour of cured parent material.
3) No rendering of imperfect concrete faces shall be allowed and any concrete that is defective
in any way (including colour) is to be cut out and replaced to such depths or be made good in
such manner as the COMPANY may direct.
4) Any concrete that is damaged or unsound shall be demolished if necessary and shall be
repaired and re-instated.
5) Repair shall not proceed until concrete has been fully cured.
6) Only the highest quality materials compatible with the parent concrete shall be used for the
repair.
b) For any cracks observed post formwork removal (other than minor hairline & shrinkage cracks),
CONTRACTOR shall furnish his corrective proposals for COMPANY review and approval, prior
to proceeding with any repair works. The cracks are deemed as hairline/shrinkage cracks if the
crack width is less than 0.1mm
c) Tie holes shall be patched with waterproofing materials in accordance with MANUFACTURER’s
recommendations.
d) Repair of surface defects other than tie holes shall comply with the requirements of ACI 301 clause
5.3.7.3 and BS EN 1504-9, or an approved equivalent standard.
