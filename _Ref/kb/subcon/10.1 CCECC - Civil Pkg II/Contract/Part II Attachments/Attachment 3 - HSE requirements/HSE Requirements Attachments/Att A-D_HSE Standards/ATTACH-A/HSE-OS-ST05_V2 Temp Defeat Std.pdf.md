1.

INTRODUCTION

A Temporary Defeat or bypass occurs when a HSE Critical Equipment and System (HSECES) is taken
out of service (isolated or shut down) while the equipment or facility being protected remains in service.

The use of the term Defeat should be taken to mean any of the following - to temporarily bypass, block
out,  isolate,  override,  inhibit,  force,  jumper,  or  disable  a  safety  /  protection  device  or  an  instrumented
system or any parts thereof which would interrupt the normal design functionality of the equipment and
system and its inherent ability to perform its protective or safety functions.

Temporary  Defeats  are  an  integral  part  of  Permit  to  Work.  A  Temporary  Defeat  Certificate  is  used  to
manage critical protective devices defeated on equipment which remains in service. Temporary Defeat
Certificate can act as a stand-alone Certificate to temporarily defeat a HSECES.

2.

PURPOSE

The purpose of the Temporary Defeat of HSECES Standard is to provide instruction on the evaluation
and  mitigation  of  risk  for  all  occurrences  where  HSE  Critical  Equipment  &  Systems  (HSECES)  are
temporarily  defeated,  and  to  ensure  that  any  such  bypasses  of  HSECES  are  authorized,  applied,
monitored and removed in a correct and controlled manner.

This Standard sets out a safe, standardized and robust system for temporarily defeating the HSECES
required to ensure risks associated with exposure to hazardous energy sources are as low as reasonably
practicable (ALARP).

Temporary Defeats are an integral part of Permit to Work.  This Standard should be used in conjunction
with the WMS Framework and all WMS standards.

If anybody involved in the Temporary Defeat process is unsure of a course of action, a higher authority
or management shall be consulted rather than taking risks.

3.

SCOPE

The  requirements  of  this  Standard  shall  be  applicable  to  all  ADNOC  group  companies,  employees,
contractors, facilities (including interface areas), activities and services without exception for managing
temporary defeat of HSECES.

Contractors  shall  be  responsible  for  ensuring  that  all  activities  performed  within  ADNOC  operating
facilities in the course of carrying out their contractual requirements are managed in accordance with the
requirements of this document and reported, when applicable, to the contract holder.

When applying or implementing the Temporary Defeat Standard, adherence shall be required with all
other  ADNOC  Standards  and  Recommended  Practices  and  other  Work  Management  System  (WMS)
Standards.

This  standard  shall  be  used  and  read  in  conjunction  with  other  WMS  standards.  WMS  Framework
standard contains governing principles such as custodianship, deviations, document retention, training
and competence assurance which are applicable to all WMS standards.

4.

LAWS AND REGULATIONS

There are no specific laws and regulations applicable to this standard.

In this section, all roles within the Temporary Defeat process are described together with their specific
responsibilities and accountabilities.

For proper harmonization of the Temporary Defeat process across ADNOC Group, all Businesses should
adopt  the  common  terminology  for  naming  of  roles  and  understanding  of  their  responsibilities  and
accountabilities.

The ultimate success of Temporary Defeat process depends on the competence and awareness of the
people carrying out the activities.  If they do not have a sound  understanding of permit or Temporary
Defeat requirements and their own responsibilities, the system will have a limited effect on maintaining a
safe operation and preventing accidents.

7.2.  APPLYING DEFEATS TO HSECES

The circumstances, under which temporary defeats (including bypass, over-ride or inhibition of function)
on equipment or systems identified as HSECES are permitted, are strictly limited to certain clearly defined
situations.

A HSECES shall only be defeated if it is  necessary to prevent a greater  risk or  to perform an activity
related  to  HSECES.  Defeats  of  HSECES  shall  be  allowed  in  the  following  situations  and  under  strict
control:

(i)  When required for continued safe operations of plant and equipment;

(ii)

For testing of the device (during normal plant conditions or construction/hook-up);

(iii)  When required for maintenance or repair of the device or connected equipment;

(iv)  When  its  function  may  be  adversely  affected  by  certain  work  activities  and  where  those
activities span a very short timeframe (Example  - a fire detection sensor activated by hot
work thereby causing false equipment shut-downs or false alarms).

Only the minimum number of HSECES or systems that can be adequately monitored shall be taken out
of service within the same zone and, as soon as the task is completed, the safety device or devices shall
be placed back in service.

Defeating  the  HSECES  should  be  permitted  for  the  shortest  duration  possible,  are  subject  to  risk
assessment and only to be used when the facility is manned or remotely monitored. All other safeguarding
instrumentation  on  equipment  should  be  functional  and  high  priority  given  to  any  repairs  required  to
normalize a system.

7.3.  CATEGORIES OF DEFEAT

Basis the type and duration, Temporary Defeats of HSECES can be categorized into various categories.

7.3.1.  CATEGORIZATION BASIS TYPE:

(a)  Process Defeats:

Any process-related bypass required / enacted by the DCS operator during start-up / shut down (unless
specifically designed for start-up or shut-down or controlled by OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer)
approved Standard Operating Procedure, during process upset or in the course of handling emergency
conditions.

(b)  Maintenance Defeats:

Any bypass related to enabling the undertaking of corrective or preventive maintenance.  The following
are typical means employed as temporary override devices on instrumented functions:

(i)

Maintenance Override Switch (MOS) -  installed by design for the purpose of testing and
maintenance;

(ii)

Force: An imposed healthy signal within an Instrumented Protective System;

(iii)  Mechanical (Hardware force) - physical override (examples include wire bridges, pneumatic

bypasses, physical removal of protective device such as a minimum stop); and

(IV)  Wire jumper.

7.3.2.  CATEGORIZATION BASIS DURATION:

(a)

Temporary Bypasses / Defeats

Temporary Defeats or bypasses of HSECES are enacted only to allow certain work to proceed without
causing unnecessary process shutdown or interruption or fire alarm. HSECES may only be temporarily
bypassed for the purposes of safe start-up (unless controlled by Standard Operating Procedures), testing,
maintenance or repair. In the case of fire detection sensors, these may also be temporarily bypassed
during hot work etc. where such work may cause false equipment shut-down.

(b)  Extended Period Defeats

Extended  Period  Defeats  include  temporary  defeat  which  is  to  remain  in  place  beyond  the  planned
duration  of  approval.  If  extension  is  required,  the  respective  Approver  decides  whether  a  separate
Temporary Defeat Risk Assessment is required or not and provides a separate validity and approval for
the extension.

7.3.3.  CONDITIONS FOR EXCLUSION OF DEFEATS:

There are certain conditions as listed below that do not require temporary defeat standard workflow to be
followed,  such  as  cases  of  equipment  that  is  isolated  (Extended  Period  or  Permanent)  provided  the
positive isolation status remains unchanged. However, the details of such defeats shall be updated in the
Defeat Register.

(a)  Defeats on Isolated Equipment

In some cases, HSECES bypasses are applied to process equipment that may be positively isolated for
long periods of time. In these cases, the bypasses are applied to prevent the shutdown of the equipment
caused by process parameters that are normal during isolation but undesired when operational (e.g. low
pressures, low levels, low flows).

During  this  period  of  equipment  isolation,  the  hazard  that  the  safeguard  is  protecting  against  may  no
longer exist, or the risk is significantly reduced. It is not necessary to provide alternate safeguards for the
bypassed safety critical equipment providing positive isolation is in place and no additional hazards have
been identified.

The bypass shall be reinstated and the equipment safeguarding functionality tested before the isolation
is removed. Reinstatement and testing of each affected safeguard must be recorded in the defeat register.

(b)  Equipment temporarily / permanently out of service

Isolated equipment may also be temporarily / permanently out-of-service whereby it is not being used as
part of the production process and it is positively isolated from all other production equipment or energy
sources. Bypassed HSECES for out-of-service equipment must be labelled "Out-of-Service". Additional
safeguards  may  not  be  required  if  bypassed  HSECES  providing  positive  isolation  is  in  place  and  all
hazards have been removed.

7.3.4.  NON-PERMITTED OR PROHIBITED DEFEATS

Non-permitted or prohibited temporary defeats should have been identified by regulatory codes, as part
of equipment standards or vendor specifications for safe operation and shall NOT be bypassed, tampered
with,  over-ridden  or  removed  unless  the  equipment  is  undergoing  maintenance.  Examples  include
processes  specific  to  DCS  (Distributed  Control  Systems)  and  PLC  (Programmable  Logic  Controller)
shutdowns, fire and gas detection systems and car seal management. In these cases, Lockout/Tag-out
shall be used to ensure positive isolation of hazardous energy and all equipment vendor requirements
shall be followed.

Such  prohibited  defeats  should  be  identified  and  included  as  part  of  HSEIA  or  SIMOPS  Manuals  of
Permitted Operations (MOPOs) where such manuals are used.

APPENDIX NO. 4.

REVIEW OF AUDITS AND DEFEATS

Formal review of ‘override’ status including audit of the Fire & Gas and Shut down override logbooks,
procedures  and  permits,  etc.  shall  be  carried  out  as  below.    Each  site  /  field  /  asset  to  maintain  two
separate register for monitoring / registering short term overrides (< 12 Hrs.) and long term overrides (>
12 Hrs.):

(i)

Daily: Shift Supervisor/Team Leader shall review all active Override / Bypasses and ensure
any required remedial work has a Work Notification and is given the appropriate priority. He
shall also ensure that any Override / Bypass which has been active for an extended period
has authorization or an MOC as required.

(ii)  Weekly:  The  Area  Authority  (Manager-Operations  /Process)  shall  review  the  risks,  check

and maintain the condition and clear visibility of all overrides in place on a weekly basis.

(iii)  Monthly: The Area Authority (Manager-Operations/Process) shall report any overrides that
are  in  place  (with  reasons)  to  the  Site  Manager  (Vice  President-Site)  at  the  end  of  each
month using the OVERRIDE LOG BOOK/ Register.

(iv)  Site has to ensure the procedure is adhered to with periodic audits to compliance.

(v)  On  a  Yearly  basis,  the  Compliance  Assurance Division  with  Subject  Matter  Experts shall
conduct  an  audit  on  all  ‘critical’  override  activities.  (Emphasis  shall  be  also  on  the
assessment of risk and justification for long term overrides).
