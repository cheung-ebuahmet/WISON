2 PURPOSE
Inspection is one of the prime tools and a proactive measure to keep the vehicle roadworthy and safe to use
on roads.
The risk associated with road transport will vary according to the vehicle type used, where and how it is
operated, and how well it is managed, consideration shall be taken to situations that result in an exposure
to road transport hazards and how significant are these.
The ADNOC Road Transport Operations Safety Standard (HSE-OS-ST25) establishes the mandatory
requirements for verification of vehicle safety of all vehicles operating for the ADNOC Group (Directorates &
Functions at HQ, Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors stipulating that “the vehicles shall be
subject to regular roadworthiness inspections, correctly fitted with the appropriate safety equipment for the
intended work, regularly cleaned and maintained taking in consideration regulatory requirements,
manufacturer recommendations, vehicle age, service history, distance travelled and operating conditions”.
This document describes ADNOC’s minimum requirements to ADNOC Group Companies & Functions for
managing the inspection of company and contractor vehicles intended to work for ADNOC with the purpose
of keeping them roadworthy and ensuring health & safety requirements of ADNOC HSE Standards are met.
The vehicle user, operator or owner is responsible for its roadworthiness. The vehicle itself may belong to
ADNOC or any Group Company, or be in their possession under agreement for hire, loan or lease.

6.1 VAAI - Vehicle Acceptance and Assurance Inspection
The VAAI provides minimum requirements to ADNOC Group Companies to ensure Vehicle’s roadworthiness
before the vehicle is handed over to the Vehicle user. Each Group Company is responsible for its vehicle’s
roadworthiness. The vehicle itself may belong to the Group Company, or be in its possession under
agreement for hire, loan, or lease.
All vehicles operating for any ADNOC Group Company shall be inspected and issued with an inspection
sticker (please refer to annexure 9 & 10) that confirms compliance with this document.
All ADNOC Group Companies shall comply with the requirements of the VAAI. Associated Contractors, sub-
contractors and applicable vendors/suppliers are responsible for ensuring, via a structured VAAI inspection
scheme, that their own and sub-contracted vehicles are always maintained in a roadworthy condition.
ADNOC Group Companies shall ensure that the requirements of these guidelines are included in the
contracts.
All Vehicles must pass the Vehicle Acceptance & Assurance inspection (VAAI) before being used on
Company related work and must have a valid VAAI inspection sticker which shall be issued only after the
vehicle successfully passed the VAAI.
The Group Companies are also responsible for ensuring that all periodic safety maintenance, inspection,
and certification is conducted, to keep their vehicles in compliance with this document’s requirement at all
times.
The VAAI for the vehicle shall be conducted in designated areas assigned by each Group Company, by a
designated competent Inspector in line with the requirements of this document, please refer to section 6.1.3
for inspector requirements.
Records of VAAI must be kept and maintained by all Group Companies for a minimum of 3 years. An ADNOC
Unified Vehicle Inspection Electronic Database shall be developed and implemented across the group
to cover all vehicles approved under the VAAI process, that database shall also maintain an updated list of
ADNOC Group Company designated Vehicle Inspectors.

6.1.2 VAAI – Vehicle Acceptance & Assurance Inspection Process:
These inspections follow set pattern of items to be inspected based on the type.
1. As a pre-requisite before any vehicle can be inspected under the VAAI process, the vehicle
maintenance must be carried out in compliance with the vehicle manufacturers’ recommended
specifications and standards, i.e., it must be serviced in accordance with the manufacturers stated
specifications and intervals. Maintenance must be carried out by, or directly supervised by, trained
and qualified vehicle mechanics.
2. The process starts by the provision of a Request for Inspection (RFI) form filled out by the Vehicle
Contractor or Supplier to initiate the VAAI process, it shall be sent to the designated ADNOC Group

Company VAAI Focal Point together with relevant supportive documentation please refer to
annexure 8 of this document.
3. The ADNOC Group Company VAAI Focal Point then determine the Vehicle Inspection
Requirements based on the Risk Criteria and schedule the inspection at the most convenient
location based on inspector availability.
4. Then the VAAI is conducted, inspector shall determine if vehicle complies with the requirements
requested in the checklists based on the provision of supportive documentation and visual
verification for each type of vehicle, safety equipment fitted and operational area.
5. If the vehicle successfully passes the inspection, the inspector shall provide a Sticker with Inspection
details used for verification purposes by ADNOC Group Companies.
6. All VAAI shall be completed by ADNOC Group Company approved/designated inspector, otherwise
the inspection is considered invalid.
7. Stickers shall be displayed inside each vehicle facing outward indicating the date of VAAI inspection
and the next due date of VAAI inspection.
8. The Sticker and report shall be signed the designated Inspector, please refer to section 6.1.3 for
inspector requirements.
9. The Validity of the VAAI is one year from the date of inspection
10. If the vehicle does not pass the VAAI, it shall be sent back to Contractor / Supplier to correct all
identified issues before the vehicle is re-inspected for approval.
11. ADNOC Group Companies shall ensure yearly periodic VAAI for the vehicles operating in their fleet
and schedule the VAAI well in advance to prevent system delays.
12. Previous inspection report must be available, clearly indicating that previous findings (if any) are
properly closed-out.
13. VAAI inspections must be given a higher priority than operational needs. Care must be taken by
the Group Company Transportation Division to plan the use of vehicles so that they are available
for VAAI inspections.
14. ADNOC Group Companies shall make sure that VAAI are covering all the applicable items listed in
the appropriate checklists (Annexure - 1,2,3,4 & 5 of this document).
15. Vehicles must be compliant with any UAE traffic regulations, including valid vehicle registration at
the time of inspection. Hazardous chemical license and pressure vessel or vacuum tanker inspection
certificates must be valid at the time of VAAI Inspection. Reference should be made to the relevant
vehicle specification and the manufacturer’s recommended tolerances to ensure that each item
addressed in the checklist is inspected properly.
16. ADNOC Group Companies might be required to conduct a Site Assurance Inspection to verify that
the vehicle complies with any Site-Specific requirement before it is being allowed to enter Vehicle
Controlled areas.
17. Supplementary VAAI inspection of the vehicle outside the scheduled frequency shall be required if:
- Vehicle has been involved in an Incident (MVC)
- Vehicle has been operating in off-roads (desert) for long period of time and additional resources
are deployed to release the vehicle from the stuck area.
- Major breakdown of the vehicle is recorded.
- Suspected any significant condition that may affect vehicle roadworthiness.
18. Each ADNOC Group Company may develop internal procedures to support the implementation of
VAAI process.

6.1.3 VAAI Inspectors
1. The VAAI Inspection team shall be designated personnel assigned by a Group Company/Function.
2. All Inspectors shall be adequately trained in the VAAI Inspection process and conducting the VAAI
inspections and issuing the report.
3. ADNOC Group Companies shall ensure that Inspectors are competent and experienced enough to
carry out the VAAI Inspection process.
4. ADNOC Group Companies shall maintain an updated list of designated Vehicle Inspectors in the
ADNOC Unified Vehicle Inspection Electronic Database.
5. The Inspectors could have different background discipline such as but not limited to HSE,
Mechanical and/or any technical discipline, General Services/Transportation, etc. VAAI Inspectors
could be from site or city based.
6. There shall be no Conflict of Interest in the designation of Vehicle Inspectors made by the ADNOC
Group Company VAAI Focal Point.
7. ADNOC Group Company VAAI focal point shall support site HSE teams in the provision of any
clarifications regarding a vehicle VAAI inspection status and the process.
8. Due to the nature of the cargo and the risk associated with cargo handling activities the inspection
of tankers carrying flammable / hazardous substances shall be conducted by inspectors with the
following qualifications:
 Diploma or Bachelor’s Degree in Mechanical/Automotive Engineering with minimum 2 years
of proven experience working with tankers carrying flammable/hazardous substances.
6.1.4 VAAI Inspection Reports & Records
1. Each VAAI inspection must be recorded in writing or electronically and filed chronologically into the
ADNOC Unified Vehicle Inspection Electronic Database.
2. VAAI inspection report forms/checklists are included as Annexure – 1, 2, 3, 4 & 5.
3. The VAAI inspection report form must have notes of repair work done to remedy defects identified
and details of any work to be carried forward.
4. A list of all major parts replaced.
5. VAAI inspection report forms must be kept for at least 24 months as part of the maintenance history
of the vehicle by the Group Company.
6. Each VAAI report must be in writing and/or electronic form. Defects must be highlighted by the
inspector on the inspection report/given checklist. A vehicle with an identified defect will fail the
inspection until remedial work has been completed and has satisfactorily been re-inspected.
7. The original signed checklist shall be given to the owner of the vehicle.
8. The master copy of all VAAI inspection checklists must be available at the ADNOC Unified Vehicle
Inspection Electronic Database. The unified database shall be available to all ADNOC Group
Companies for vehicle verification purposes at operational sites, and to prevent access to those
vehicles not approved through the VAAI process.

6.1.5 Visual Evidence of VAAI completion
A certificate or sticker shall be issued to vehicles which pass the VAAI inspection, to be attached or kept in
the vehicle until the following inspection.
The sticker must include, as a minimum:
1. Serial number of inspection
2. Vehicle identification
3. Contract Number (if applicable)
4. Name of Owner / Contractor
5. Date of inspection
6. Identification Code
7. Name of the inspector
8. VAAI location.
9. Type of Road approval.
10. Operation Area approval (Zone A, B or C)
Stickers must be fixed to the vehicle after the inspection, by the Inspector – they must not be handed to the
vehicle driver/user.
Stickers must be colour coded in blue and white. Blue stickers are for Blacktop operation only, white stickers
are for blacktop & off-road operation. The sticker must be approximately 9 cm x 6 cm. The position of the
sticker must be such that fading due to the sun is avoided (e.g. on the side of the dashboard at the driver
side door). A typical format and design are given in illustration, refer Annexure – 9 & 10.
Blank stickers must be kept in a secure location prior to use.
Note: Provision of blank/counterfeit VAAI stickers can put the personnel safety at serious risk, hence such
violations will be treated as serious non-compliance. All ADNOC Group Companies and functions shall
investigate such violations, which shall be subject to assessment by JCAC (Just Culture and Assessment
Committee) as part of Accountability Framework.
Any ADNOC Group Company employee or Contractor that comes into possession of counterfeit VAAI
stickers must immediately notify their Line Manager, HSE focal point and/or Transportation Focal point
(VP/Manager) and action taken accordingly by the relevant ADNOC Group Company/Function.
6.1.6 Inspection Scheduling and Monitoring
ADNOC Group Companies shall develop a VAAI schedule covering all vehicles & trailers in order to prevent
operational constrains, a year planner, flowchart or a computer-based system may be used, it should be
kept in a simple format and displayed prominently (as applicable).
Close monitoring is essential to maintain effectiveness in ensuring that vehicles are safe for use on the road,
especially at early stages of the vehicle life.

Safety requirements not covered under the scope of the VAAI i.e. Compressed Air Cascade Systems before
entering into H2S Red Zones. Additionally, Operational sites shall ensure that drivers and cargo are verified
before allowing them to enter into certain operational controlled areas.

6.3 Daily Checks/Inspection
Daily inspection as a minimum shall be conducted on all vehicles prior starting the journey. It’s not meant to
be completed more than once a day. If vehicle is not used on continuous basis, then in such cases, daily
checks shall be performed prior commencing the journey.
1. This inspection shall be performed by the vehicle driver to whom the vehicle is assigned to or by the
person who is assigned the vehicle for the day, the inspection could also be completed by other
authorised person e.g. mechanic or inspection team
2. This inspection targets mainly HSE and basic vehicle aspects ensuring vehicle is safe for operating.
3. Failure to conduct Daily inspection shall render the vehicle as unauthorized to operate.
4. The Vehicle Daily Safety Checklist Form is mentioned in Annexure - 6
5. Other non-safety related items may be added to this checklist but the items on the list shall be
checked daily. Check lists may be pictorial to aid their use by non-literate drivers.
6. Failure of a driver to ensure that a daily check is conducted, recorded, and made available on
request is deemed a disciplinary matter.
7. Any faults identified during daily checks, whilst the vehicle is in use, or on its return to base shall be
reported by the driver to the site HSE Supervisor or to Transport focal point.
8. There shall be a system of reporting faults or defects that may affect the roadworthiness of the
vehicle, including:
a. Not using the vehicle until the faults or defects are fixed, especially those affect the safety of the
vehicle and passengers.
b. Recording the faults in an electronic action monitoring and follow-up database module to ensure
efficient tracking & close-out by evidence.
9. Defect report could be part of the maintenance record of the vehicle and could be kept, together
with details of the remedial action taken, for at least 24 months with the vehicle maintenance records.
10. Please refer to Section 7 of this document for more information on the different types of defects for
Daily Inspections.
6.4 Spot Checks of Vehicles
Random Spot checks shall be regularly carried out, either at the roadside (where the vehicle is safely parked)
or at a suitable time during an operation.
Random spot checks are normally be carried out by HSE Personnel, Senior Personnel at the site or by the
personnel from corporate office on the Group Company’s behalf.
The Vehicle Spot Checklist (Annexure – 7) provides inspection guidance for non-professional inspectors
wishing to carry out random checks.
 These inspections have no frequency, but it is recommended that Group Company HSE and Line
Management encourage conducting spot checks and maintain record of the same.
 Applicable for all type of vehicles (LV & HV)
Any faults identified during daily checks, whilst the vehicle is in use, or on its return to base shall be reported
by the driver to the site HSE Supervisor or to Transport HSE focal point.

7 DEFECT TYPES/CLASSIFICATIONS
There are two types of Defect classifications used for only daily inspections of vehicles – Class A & Class B.
Class A: Class A defects are those defects found on a vehicle during a daily inspection, that can put vehicle
occupants at an intolerable Risk to suffer a Motor Vehicle Crash if the vehicle is used, also includes those
defects that might impair critical safety features of the vehicle. Therefore, the Vehicle should not be used
once Class A Defect is identified.
Class B: Class B Defects are those defects that if found during daily inspection, allows the vehicle to move
to the workshop at the earliest opportunity for repair.
The following lists gives examples of Class A defects.
 Missing or defective seat belt
 Malfunctioning Braking system
 VAAI inspection out of date.
 A roll over cage not being fitted in high-risk area for rollover.
 Tyre conditions substandard to cause a risk to the vehicle and other road users
 Load security and restraint standards not compliant with Manufacturer specifications or ADNOC
Road Transport Operations Safety standard.
 Malfunctioning safety control such as airbags (SRS)
Where any of the above defects are identified on a vehicle, the following action must be taken:
 The person inspecting the vehicle during (daily checks) will have the authority to impound the vehicle
where the inspection has taken place. It must be parked safely so as not to present a risk to other
road users. The journey will not be allowed to continue until such time as the Class A defects have
been rectified.
 The inspector will contact the concerned Transportation focal point of the Group Company or Site
Manager/Supervisor or Site HSE and inform them of the identification of Class A defects on the
vehicle. The inspector will ensure that the concerned focal points of Transportation clearly
understand the nature of the class A defects.
 The ADNOC Group Company must accept responsibility for the defect and to arrange for mobile
mechanics to conduct repairs to rectify the Class A defect(s) at the location where the vehicle has
been impounded.
 Where it is not possible to complete the repairs or it would be unsafe to do so, then the Group
Company must arrange for the vehicle to be transported back to its base workshop. Vehicles will
only be allowed to continue on to the company workshop with an escorting vehicle and with the
permission of responsible Group Company HSE Manager, Site Asset Owner and Asset Approval
Authority who will take responsibility for the journey by means of approved Risk Assessment and
relevant Permit to Work as part of ADNOC Work Management System.
 An authorized person shall remain with the vehicle until the repairs are completed. If this is not
possible (e.g. due to impending darkness) then arrangements should be in place to secure the
vehicle and keep informed the inspector on the vehicle status.
 Unless the Group Company Asset Owner has given specific approval, the vehicle will not be allowed
to be driven until it has been formally confirmed that the repairs have been conducted and released
the vehicle.
 Following the satisfactory completion of repairs the vehicle will be allowed to continue its journey.

The following list examples of Class B defects.
 Vehicle lights malfunctioning
 High intensity rear lights missing or not functioning
 Air conditioning not available or not functioning
 Sufficient and suitable spare wheels not available on the vehicle
 Basic Safety equipment such as the First aid kit
Where any of the above defects are identified on a vehicle, the following action must be taken:
 The inspector/driver shall notify site HSE and the management of the company owning the vehicle
and inform them of the identification of class B defects on the vehicle.
 The Group Company shall ensure that the defects will be repaired immediately the vehicle returns
to the company/contractor workshop and that the vehicle will not be allowed to return to the road
until such repairs have been completed.
 Group Company are responsible for creation and approval of Risk assessment/JSA for vehicle with
Class B defect prior to be driven to the workshop for repair.
 Where the vehicle is closer to its journey end point than the nearest company workshop, it will be
allowed to complete its journey provided it is immediately taken to the workshop after being off
loaded. It is not allowed to load any other material until the Class B defects have been repaired.
 The driver shall keep inspection records within the vehicle for at least 2 weeks.
 The Group Company will keep a copy of the inspection report on file. Inspection records shall be
retained for the period of minimum 3 months by the Group Companies in their system.
