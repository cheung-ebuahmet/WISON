1. INTRODUCTION
Emergency Systems Survivability Assessment (ESSA) is a technique to assess the survivability of
HSECES to perform its design intent during major accident event.
The overall aim of an ESSA is to:
 Identify HSECES for given facility / installation which contributes to emergency management;
 Determine the vulnerability of these identified emergency HSECES systems and its sub-
components against each identified Major Accident Hazards (MAH);
 If vulnerable, determine whether emergency systems are fail-safe or not;
 If not fail safe, determine whether redundancies are available for emergency systems; and
 Consider additional risk reduction measures for emergency systems that are vulnerable, not fail-
safe and have no redundancies.
2. PURPOSE
The purpose of this Standard is to set out the requirements and provide an overall understanding of the
ESSA process and its application within ADNOC Group. This Standard establishes the consistent
practices for conducting and documenting ESSA studies at ADNOC Group.
All ESSA studies for ADNOC Group shall be undertaken in accordance with the requirements of this
Standard.
The purpose of the ESSA is to identify the emergency systems of the facility and to assess their
survivability during major accident event. That is, to assess the ability of the emergency systems to carry
out their safety function under conditions when that function is required.
3. SCOPE
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to the ADNOC Group and its
Contractors.
ADNOC Group and its Contractors shall ensure that all requirements listed herein are fully understood,
implemented, complied with and monitored at all times.
This standard is applicable for all ADNOC Group activities, assets and operations where MAH has the
potential to impact the emergency systems.

7.1. ESSA LIFECYCLE
The ESSA is an integral part of the risk management process to ensure that the risk is managed and
demonstrated to ALARP.
Typically, an ESSA is performed during the Front End Engineering Design (FEED) Stage of the project
and is later updated during the EPC (Detailed Engineering) Stage based on updated safety studies,
documents and newly-established/updated information/data. ESSA study later forms part of the HSEIA
dossier.
For existing facilities, verify the requirements stated in this standard for conducting ESSA and update the
HSEIA Dossier accordingly as per ADNOC HSEIA Standard, HSE-RM-ST02 [Ref. 3].
As part of 5-year COMAH/HSEIA update, ESSA shall be reviewed to determine if significant changes to
emergency systems were observed or carried out. ESSA study shall be updated based on these changes
to ensure assessment reflect the updated changes to emergency systems.
Where no significant changes are identified over five years and outcome of previous ESSA report is still
applicable and technically robust, no update of ESSA is required. In such case, GC CHSE shall develop
technical note as part of HSEIA process justifying the same with relevant supporting documents (such as
Hazard Identification (HAZID), risk register review, risk assessments, MOC, etc.) and shall submit to
ADNOC as part of HSEIA process for Regulatory Approval.

7.2. ESSA METHODOLOGY
7.2.1. OVERVIEW
ESSA is an assessment of survivability for emergency systems based on credible major accident
scenarios. The survivability of each selected emergency systems is checked against three factors:
 Vulnerability – whether an emergency system can be impaired/damaged by the major accident and
if so, whether it can survive long enough during the major accident and perform its intended function
successfully
 Fail-safe – when impaired, whether an emergency system will perform its intended function or not
 Redundancy – whether there are auxiliary systems available to replace the main system if it is
impaired
If an emergency system is found not to be vulnerable to a major accident or if it is vulnerable but fails
safe or has redundancy, it is deemed to be able to survive and capable of achieving its safety function.
Otherwise, recommendations/action plans shall be proposed in order to improve its survivability.

7.2.5. SURVIVABILITY ASSESSMENT
Upon identification of relevant MAH and emergency systems/HSECES, these systems shall be assessed
for their survivability. The assessment will look into whether the identified system:
1. is vulnerable to MAH; or
2. is fail-safe; or
3. has redundancies in the context of the performing intended function as a whole considering effects
of the MAHs.
Risk assessment studies such as Fire and Explosion Risk Assessment (FERA), Dropped Objects Study
(DOS), Quantitative Risk Assessment (QRA), Escape, Evacuation and Rescue Assessment (EERA), etc.

shall be used to provide specific inputs to support the assessment. These steps are further detailed in
the following sections.
(i) Vulnerability
A HSECES is considered vulnerable if there is the possibility of a MAH impairing the HSECES’s
ability to carry out its safety function under conditions when that function is required. The location
of the HSECES is critical as it influences the survivability of the system. For example, in the Piper
Alpha incident, the control room and radio room were both not within a safe area, hence they were
impaired when an explosion occurred. Therefore, determining the location of each HSECES is a
key part in assessing its vulnerability.
Furthermore, some systems are designed to perform and survive major accident scenarios while
other systems are not required to do so. For example, Fire and Gas (F&G) detectors do not need
to survive the entire duration of a fire. Once it has already sent a signal to the F&G panel and the
alarm has sounded, its survivability is no longer important. However, for an emergency power
system, the system should remain functioning for the entire major accident duration or until
personnel have been safely evacuated from the installation. Recommendations shall be raised for
any identified shortfalls.
If systems are found to be susceptible to impairment by a major accident event, it is then required
to determine the duration for which the system is required to function. Input from safety studies
conducted can be used for this purpose.
System that is designed to withstand the ADNOC criteria for Design Accidental Load (DAL) as
defined in ADNOC HSE Risk Management System Standard, HSE-RM-ST01 [Ref. 4] or found to
be able to survive long enough to perform its intended function is not considered as vulnerable. If
any system does not meet this requirement, then it will need to either be fail-safe or have
redundancies (or both) to be considered adequate.
(ii) Fail-safe
A fail-safe system is a system that performs its required function automatically upon failure of a
system component. For example, instrument air supply line to the ESD valve fails, then
automatically the ESD valves shuts or opens, performing its intended fail-safe function.
Systems (and their sub-components) that are found to be vulnerable shall be reviewed for its failure
mode (whether fail-safe or not). Systems that are found to be fail-safe are considered to be
adequate as they will carry out their intended function irrespective of major accident event impact.
If any emergency system does not meet this requirement, then it will need to either be invulnerable
to major accident events or have redundancies to be considered adequate.

(iii) Redundancy
Redundancy is the duplication of HSECES or its critical components with the intention of increasing
its survivability, in the form of a backup. The location of the redundancy should not be close to its
main system to avoid simultaneous impairment. For example, life rafts are provided at different
location of the facility to avoid simultaneous impairment.
The location of the redundancy is highly important, as having redundancies but placed closely
together (i.e. likely to be impaired simultaneously during a major accident event) defeats the
purpose of having the redundancy in the first place.
Systems that have redundancies and the back-up/duplicate systems are located such that they
cannot be impaired simultaneously are deemed to be adequate. If any system does not meet this
requirement, then it will need to either be invulnerable to major accident event or be fail-safe (or
both) to be considered adequate.
While assessing for modification, brownfield projects, extension projects, etc. the integrated approach
shall be taken and emergency system shall be considered as whole and not as standalone elements.
Irrespective of system acceptability in terms of meeting the expectation of vulnerability or fail safe, all the
three criteria shall be assessed for each system.
While carrying out survivability assessment, the following information shall be captured as minimum for
identified emergency system.
(i) Emergency system description
(ii) Main system components
(iii) System & subsystem/components vulnerability assessment against each identified MAH
(iv) System Fail-safe assessment against each identified MAH
(v) System redundancy assessment against each identified MAH
(vi) Assessment Summary

7.2.6. ESSA WORKSHOP
ESSA workshop should be conducted once the initial assessment is carried out. Based on the
assessment the recommendations to be refined/made specific or any additional recommendations can
be brainstormed and captured. It is recommended to follow ALARP principles (elimination, prevention,
mitigation, procedural, administrative, etc.), while discussing the additional recommendations. The ESSA
workshop should be attended by a multi-discipline team and as a minimum should be participated by
team conducting the study, project representatives, operation representatives and HSE team
members/SMEs.
7.2.7. ACTION PLAN
If the emergency system is assessed to be vulnerable for a particular major accident event, is not fail-
safe and does not have any redundancies, then remedial action plans will have to be developed in order
to ensure the survivability of the HSECES. The improvement may be in any one or more of the three
criteria assessed, as necessary to ensure survivability of the system as whole.

8. COMPLIANCE ASSURANCE
ADNOC will conduct compliance audit of this Standard at approximately three-year intervals. These
Audits will be in addition to GC’s internal audits and where required, ADNOC will take steps to minimize
overlap and duplication between ADNOC Corporate and Group Companies internal audits.
The main audit deliverable is a formal and structured report for the attention of GC Management and the
respective ADNOC Directorate.
