1.

INTRODUCTION

The  use  of  air  transport  is  essential  to  Abu  Dhabi  National  Oil  Company  (ADNOC)  and  Group
Company operations. Each Group Company shall be sufficiently equipped and staffed to manage the
hazards and risks associated with air transport operations where aircraft take off from facilities and
locations owned and operated by ADNOC Group Companies.

ADNOC  Health,  Safety  and  Environment  (HSE)  policy,  commits  to  ensure  that  a  safe  working
environment  is  maintained.  This  includes  all  areas  and  all  persons  involved  with  air  transport
operations  are  fully  aware  of  the  hazards  involved  and  have  the  required  competence,  training,
procedures, policies, equipment, instructions and awareness to undertake their duties safely and in
compliance with appropriate rules and regulations.

2.

PURPOSE

This  standard  provides  those  ADNOC  Group  Companies  involved  in  air  transport  operations
(personnel and materials), and those authorized as a Primary Accountable Organization (PAO) by the
General Civil Aviation Authority (GCAA), with the standard requirements on managing safety of all air
transportation operations.  These standards will demonstrate how ADNOC group of companies comply
with GCAA regulations and publications.

3.

SCOPE

This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates &
Functions at HQ, GCs and Affiliates) and its Contractors.

ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood,
implemented and thoroughly monitored.

It covers all ADNOC personnel and facilities involved in air transport operations. It includes:

(i)

(ii)

Provision, operation and management of Aerodromes on ADNOC Operated islands;

Provision,  operation  and  management  of  Helipads/helidecks  and  heliports  on  ADNOC
Operated islands and offshore facilities;

(iii)  Oversight  of  Helipads  on  ADNOC  contracted  marine  facilities/vessels  operating  within

ADNOC controlled areas; and

(iv)  Operation and oversight of aircraft: fixed wing (aeroplanes), rotary wing (helicopters), others

(UAS/drones).

Provision,  operation  and  maintenance  of  support  systems  such  as  aircraft  refuelling  systems,
navigation systems, communication systems, emergency management systems etc.

This standard does not apply to the following:

(i)

Aerodromes  in  Abu  Dhabi  or  the  UAE  that  are  government  or  commercially  owned  and
operated  by  other  authorities  in  compliance  with  the  GCAA  approved  procedures  and
regulations;

(ii)

Critical Infrastructure and Coastal Protection Authority (CICPA) controlled areas; and

(iii)  UAE Military Aviation facilities and equipment.

REQUIREMENTS

7.1.

OVERVIEW

Air transport operations support oil and gas industry. They provide services such as passenger/cargo
transportation  to  offshore,  islands  and  onshore  locations/facilities/installations  and  emergency
response activities. Air transport operations are strictly regulated and carried out to highest standards
of safety performance.

ADNOC Group  Companies involved in air transport operations  and any  authorized as a  PAO shall
establish a Safety Management System (SMS) acceptable to and in accordance with the latest version
of the GCAA CAR Part X.

Risks from air transport operations shall be assessed and control measures implemented to ensure a
level that is As Low as Reasonably Practicable (ALARP).

7.2.

PRIMARY ACCOUNTABLE ORGANIZATION

ADNOC  Corporate shall assign ADNOC Group Company as  PAO for management of air transport
operations. The PAO shall have ultimate accountability for all aspects of air transport operations and
their safety across ADNOC Group Companies.

The PAO shall develop an authority structure for accountability at the following levels:

(i)

(ii)

Oversight level i.e. PAO level accountability;

Asset owner level i.e. facility level accountability; and

(iii)  Operation level i.e. aerodrome/helicopter landing area level accountability.

The PAO shall require asset owners of sites with aerodrome facilities to provide the following support
services before seeking approval of aerodromes for operations:

(i)

(ii)

Generic HSE services to the PAO;.

Vehicle supply and maintenance;

(iii)  Aerodrome/helicopter landing area maintenance; and

(iv)  Rescue and Firefighting Service (RFFS).

The PAO shall approve the helicopter landing area by initial and annual inspection and ensure that
helicopter landing area complies with requirements of the GCAA.

All  risk  assessments  related  to  air  transport  operations  shall  be  submitted  to  the  PAO  nominated
person for safety for inclusion on the unified aviation safety risk register.

The PAO shall develop and implement an Air Transport Interface Management Manual that includes
details on formal interface arrangements among all stakeholders and relevant authorities for ensuing
that safety standards are maintained across ADNOC Group air transport operations.

The interface arrangements for the following shall be included in the manual:

(i)

(ii)

Leadership & Commitment

Policy & Strategic Objectives

(iii)  Organisation, Resources & Competence

(iv)  Risk Evaluation & Management

7.8.10.  HELICOPTER EXTERNAL WINCH MOVE/UNDER SLUNG OPERATIONS

Helicopter  external  winch  move  or  under  slung  operations  are  considered  as  one  of  the  most
hazardous operations conducted offshore, including on natural and man-made islands. This section
sets  out  the  minimum  requirements  that  shall  be  complied  across  ADNOC  Group  Companies,  to
ensure helicopter under slung operations are conducted safely and effectively.

ADNOC  Group  Companies,  for  special  operational  requirements  and  circumstances,  may  require
transporting loads by helicopters while suspended externally by means of cables and hooks i.e. Under
Slung Load (USLs). During the flight operations, USLs are free and can twist, orientate into positions
of high drag, spin about the vertical axis or take on a swinging motion. These effects generally become
more pronounced with an increase in the helicopter speed. Therefore, those operations require special
planning, notification and precautions in order to protect both the helicopter crew/third parties involved
in  such  operations  and  the  load  itself  from  falling  and  getting  damaged  and/or  lost.  This  includes
various safety aspects of external load operations, Pilots and persons competency and securing and
detaching  of  loads. Where  practicable,  the  helicopter  shall  always  land  rather  than  winch  because
safety is enhanced when the time spent hovering is reduced. All helicopter external winch operations
shall be conducted in compliance with the GCAA CAAP 71 and other applicable GCAA regulations
and publications referred there in.

7.12.  DESIGN OF AIR TRANSPORT FACILITIES

7.12.1.  AERODROMES

Aerodrome is a defined area on land or water (including any buildings, installations and equipment)
intended to be used either wholly or in part for the arrival, departure and surface movement of aircraft.

The design and operation  of aerodromes requiring aerodrome certificates in ADNOC facilities shall
comply with the requirements of the GCAA CAR Part IX.

7.12.2.

TAXIWAYS

Taxiways shall be provided to permit the safe and expeditious surface movement of aircraft. Sufficient
entrance and exit taxiways for a runway shall be provided to expedite the movement of aircraft to and
from the runway and provision of rapid exit taxiways considered when traffic volumes are high.

The design and operation of taxiways in ADNOC facilities shall comply with the requirements of the
GCAA CAR Part IX.

7.12.3.  APRONS

Aprons shall be provided where necessary to permit the on- and off-loading of passengers, cargo or
mail as well as the servicing of aircraft without interfering with the aerodrome traffic.

The  design  and  operation  of  aprons  in  ADNOC  facilities  shall  comply  with  the  requirements  of  the
GCAA CAR Part IX.

7.12.4.  RUNWAYS

Runway is a defined rectangular area on a land aerodrome prepared for the landing and take-off of an
aircraft.

7.14.

ENVIRONMENT MANAGEMENT OF AIR TRANSPORT OPERATIONS

Air transport and aerodrome operations have potential for causing adverse impacts to the environment.
This includes but not limited to the impact to the air quality, use of energy resources, generation of
waste,  impact  on  soil  and  ground  water  and  noise  pollution.  ADNOC  and  Group  Companies  shall

has subscribed to.

for  managing  fuel  leaks.  In  addition,  relevant  ADNOC  integrity  standards  shall  be  followed  for
managing the installation and integrity of fuel tanks and supply systems.

communication  [Ref.  24]  shall  be  followed  for  the  requirements  for  communicating  the  hazards  to
personnel involved in air transport operations along with latest version of the GCAA CAR Part IX [Ref.

7.19.

SIMULTANEOUS OPERATIONS (SIMOPS)

Simultaneous Operations (SIMOPS) are combined operations that occur when two or more potentially
conflicting activities or operations are being conducted at the same location and at  the same time.
SIMOPS  involve  hazards  and  risks  in  addition  to  those  of  individual  operations  and  require  careful
planning coordination and conduct.

ADNOC Group Companies shall take into account the possibility  of SIMOPS  when conducting risk
assessments for any operation which involves air transport operations in line with ADNOC standard
on Work Management Systems.

7.20.  MANAGEMENT OF CHANGE

The management of change is a formal process that identifies external and internal changes that may
affect established processes and operations. It is an integral part of the risk and safety management
process to ensure that there is no adverse effect on safety of air transport operations and the safety
of aircraft are created by any changes. A change can introduce new hazards that may impact on the
effectiveness of any existing risk control and mitigation measures, procedures and processes.

Aerodrome  certificate  obligations  require  the  Group  Company  with  air  transport  operation  at  an
aerodrome, to have in place, a policy, procedure and process to assess any changes to the aerodrome
facilities, services and personnel. The GCAA requires any infrastructure changes to be approved under
the requirements of CAAP 59, prior to any changes being implemented. In addition, ADNOC standard
on  Management  of  Technical  Changes  [Ref.  29]  shall  also  be  referred  for  appropriately  managing
changes related to air transport operations.

7.21.  COMPLIANCE ASSURANCE OF THE GCAA REGULATIONS

ADNOC Group Company authorized as PAO shall ensure that a process is established for assurance
of compliance with the GCAA regulations.

ADNOC Group Company certified aerodromes and AFIS shall be audited by the GCAA on an annual
basis. This may involve Abu Dhabi based auditing of safety oversight and may involve a site visit to
one, or more, helideck operations.
