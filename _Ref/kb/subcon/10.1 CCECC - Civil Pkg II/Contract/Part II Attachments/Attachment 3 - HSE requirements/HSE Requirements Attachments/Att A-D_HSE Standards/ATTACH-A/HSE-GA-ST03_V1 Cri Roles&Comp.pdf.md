1. INTRODUCTION
ADNOC commitment towards 100% HSE has raised expectations related to competence of employees
performing HSE Critical Activities & Tasks. Lack of competence has been identified as a significant
contributor to major accident and incidents within the international oil & gas industry.
This Standard defines the process for developing a Competence Assurance System (CAS) and sets
requirements which will enable Group Companies (GC’s) to fulfil their obligations set out in this standard.
Obligations by group companies are:
(i) Immediate implementation of competence assessment of HSE critical roles identified within
this document. Minimum requirement is assessment against practitioner level competence
contained within the HSE Critical Role competence matrix.
(ii) All HSE Critical Activities shall have defined competency levels recorded which are
periodically reviewed and improved, as appropriate.
(iii) All personnel performing HSE-Critical Activities shall be appropriately experienced/qualified
and trained to ensure they are competent to undertake their work and implement associated
risk control measures.
(iv) An HSE Competency Assurance Process shall be in place for all personnel who perform
HSE Critical Activities (HSECA). Competency levels shall be reassessed at intervals that
are appropriate to their criticality. Shortfalls shall be documented and addressed in a timely
manner.
(v) Quality Performance Standards shall be identified, which will provide assurance that HSE
Critical Activities are performed by Competent Persons applying procedures that are the
best available and fit for purpose.
(vi) There shall be a comprehensive and structured training system to develop Competence for
all staff involved in managing HSE Critical Activities.
(vii) Recruitment, deployment and succession plans shall be in place to enable the correct level
of subject matter expertise, communication & supervisory capabilities and enabling
behaviours to meet business needs and strategic objectives.
2. PURPOSE
The purpose of this Standard is to provide ADNOC and its Group Companies with mandatory minimum
requirements, benchmarked against international standards, to establish a world-class Competence
Assurance System (CAS) that ensures employees and contractors engaged in HSE Critical Activities are
sufficiently trained and competent to undertake the tasks to which they have been assigned.
The competence assurance system shall ensure, in a systematic manner that only competent personnel
perform HSE critical activities and associated tasks.
3. SCOPE
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates &
Functions at HQ, Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors. ADNOC Group and Contractors
shall ensure that all requirements listed herein are fully understood, implemented, complied with, and
monitored at all times.

7. REQUIREMENTS
7.1. OVERVIEW OF COMPETENCE AND TRAINING
7.1.1. COMPETENCE
The ability to perform a particular job in compliance with industry accepted performance standards. This
encompasses the technical requirements and skill to perform the job as well as having knowledge and
understanding to enable the job to be carried out successfully under different and changing conditions,
and to handle emergency situations which may possibly occur.” [Ref. 5]
Demonstrating required level of Competence for performing HSE Critical Tasks shall involve application
of technical training, demonstration of behaviour and specific skill, particular to the HSE Critical Tasks
being performed.
7.1.2. TRAINING
Training enables people to acquire the necessary skills and knowledge to ensure they are capable to
undertake a particular activity or task.
Training may include formal classroom training, on the job training or specific activity training with
extensive testing and assessment on completion.
Training shall be focused on the HSE elements of the HSE Critical Task being performed and shall include,
as applicable, formal classroom instruction, on the job training and coaching. The key to effective training
is to understand the specific job requirements and individual abilities.

Different types of training shall be identified for employee’s performing HSE Critical Tasks, i.e. worker,

7.2. COMPETENCE ASSURANCE SYSTEM (CAS) DEVELOPMENT
Group companies shall establish a Competence Assurance System that meets the needs of their
organisation and reflect risks associated with their operations, including normal, abnormal and emergency
situations. The greater the risk their operations present the more detailed and robust the competence
assurance system shall be.
Group company CAS does not have to be standalone, it can be incorporated into existing GC systems,
however an integrated system must clearly identify HSE critical competence matrix.
This Standard sets the minimum requirements that shall be met when developing and implementing a fit
for purpose Competence Assurance System.
7.2.1. COMPETENCE ASSURANCE SYSTEM DESIGN
Group Company CAS shall specify how the assessment process is managed, how HSE Critical Activities
and Tasks are identified, how HSE Critical Roles and associated Competence matrix are developed, and
how the competence assurance process is monitored and implemented.
Minimum requirements of a Competence Assurance System are:
(i) Identification of HSE critical Activities, tasks and roles;
(ii) Identification and application of competence matrix containing appropriate performance
standards at two levels, practitioner and practitioner plus;
(iii) Assessment and quality assurance processes sufficient to ensure assessments are fair,
reliable and consistent;
(iv) Training and assessment to ensure maintenance of identified competence;
(v) Competence is a significant factor in the recruitment, deployment, succession planning,
promotion, transfer and confirmation in position of HSE critical role holders.
The Competence Assurance System shall include all ADNOC employees, as well as contractor and
subcontractors.

Tasks are a sub-set of an activity, and thus each identified HSE Critical Activity requires a number

Tasks are steps in a process, work to be completed; e.g. inspections, document review, and

Evaluation of tasks shall consider the impact of the task not being performed by that person /
position (i.e. position is vacant) on:
(i) Legal compliance;
(ii) HSE Management system implementation;
(iii) Site safety in the immediate or reasonably foreseeable future; and
(iv) The level of skill or group company knowledge/experience required to successfully complete
the task.

(i) Competence Requirements at Practitioner Level

sufficient breadth of experience and understanding to work in unfamiliar situations as well

(e) Assessment Methodology
Group companies shall develop an assessment methodology that identifies appropriate evidence
to enable critical role performers to demonstrate their competence following a holistic assessment
process.
Evidence can be derived from different sources, direct observation, questioning, questionnaires,
professional discussion, work products, drills, exercises, demonstrations, simulations and
documentary evidence.
Simulation and demonstration shall be kept to a minimum and reserved for difficult to observe
situation such as emergency response.
Evidence shall include minimum two types and shall represent more than one task subset, i.e.
electrical technician shall be able to perform two types of isolation.
Questioning to determine underpinning knowledge is common practice, questions shall relate to
role performance standards, require a sufficient level of answer to meet the criteria and be
unambiguous. Multi-choice questions shall be avoided wherever possible.
Good use of action verbs is recommended to guide the candidate to the required detail for the
answer. Refer to command words of the National Examination Board Occupational Safety & Health
(NEBOSH) for guidance; e.g. Identify, Outline, Describe and Explain, as shown in Appendix 6.
7.3. COMPETENCE ASSESSMENT
Each HSE Critical Role shall be assessed against associated competence matrix by trained and
competent assessors.
Assessment shall provide candidates with the best opportunity to demonstrate their capabilities.
Assessment shall only be carried out by assessors that:
(i) Meet ADNOC minimum requirements;
(ii) Have appropriate level of industry expertise, (5 years in Oil and Gas Industry);
(iii) Hold appropriate assessor qualification, Minimum ADNOC Youth Development assessor;
(iv) Have Sufficient experience in the role they will be assessing; (minimum 3 years).
Assessment shall be planned, open, fair, consistent and follow ADNOC & International Standards of
assessment practices.
Assessments conducted by “trainee” assessors shall be checked, authenticated and countersigned by a
competent assessor.
Assessment evidence shall meet the “rules of evidence” criteria; i.e. shall be Valid, Authentic, Currents,
Reliable and Sufficient

assessment decision.

and integrity of group company assets the following steps shall be followed:
(i) Record the assessment decision, detailing identified gaps and their impact;
(ii) Notify line management and internal quality assurance;
(iii) Line management review evidence and determine next actions.

7.4. INTERNAL QUALITY ASSURANCE
Quality of the assessment process is one of the most important aspects of any competence assurance
system. Subsequently Internal Quality Assurance (IQA) shall be robust, consistent, open, fair and planned.
Internal Quality Assurance personnel, sometime referred to as Internal Verifiers, shall over time, ensure
all assessors, all roles and all elements of each role are quality sampled.
Appropriately qualified Internal Quality Assurance personnel shall be appointed; minimum requirement
ADNOC YDP (Youth Development Program).
IQA shall only be carried out by Internal Verifiers that:
(i) Meet ADNOC minimum requirements;
(ii) Have appropriate level of industry expertise, (minimum 7 years in Oil and Gas Industry);
(iii) Hold appropriate Internal Verifier qualification;
(iv) Have Sufficient experience in the role they will be verifying; (minimum 5 years).
Internal Quality assurance shall identify those high risk aspects to the assessment process and provide
sufficient sampling to ensure assessment practices do not deteriorate.
Risks to the assessment practice include:
(i) Assessor experience and qualifications;
(ii) Assessor Workload;
(iii) Complexity of Competence Matrix;
(iv) Distance from the assessment HQ/IQA .
Internal Quality Assurance shall be recorded, outcomes communicated including feedback and any
corrective actions.
Regular communication and standardization of assessment practices shall be conducted.

7.7.1. TRAINING NEEDS ANALYSIS
“Training Needs Analysis” (TNA) is a systematic process which identifies all of the competency skills
required for a particular job / role and then highlights shortfalls in the skills of the employee assigned to
that role.
Training need shall be evaluated through a thorough evaluation of HSE risks associated with a particular
job / activity, conducted by an experienced and competent person and input to TNA shall come from:
(i) Reviewing records of job-specific HSE incidents to identify causes of loss of control;
(ii) Evaluation of working methods and practices associated with a particular HSE Critical Role
/ HSE Critical Activities;
(iii) Reviewing Lessons Learnt from previous incidents, HSE risk assessments and updated
HSEIA’s;
(iv) Assessing via discussion with personnel coupled with on-site observations, to verify if all
necessary HSE controls are in place and being properly used;
(v) The role of line managers and supervisors in ensuring HSE performance criteria are met.
TNA shall be addressed at three levels:
(a) Organizational Level
Every employee within ADNOC and its Group Companies shall receive adequate training based
on their level in the organization, to ensure they are aware of:
(i) Relevant laws and regulations that govern their work activities and that shall be adhered to;
(ii) ADNOC Standards that govern the management HSE Critical and HSE Critical Integrity
Activities related to their work activities and that shall be adhered to;
(iii) HSE Policy, HSEMS and the parts of it that are relevant to their work activities.
The level of details provided during training sessions shall depend upon the level of seniority of the
staff attending and on whether they are new recruits or experienced employees.
(b) Task / Activity Level
Training for this level depends on whether the job is managerial, supervisory or worker.
For manager, TNA shall focus on HSE Criticality of core competencies (not HSE only) to include:
(i) Leadership and Communication;
(ii) Adequate knowledge of HSE Critical Activities and related tasks relevant to their job profile;
(iii) Management of HSE CA, HSECIA and HSECES related activities/tasks;
(iv) Training, instruction, coaching and problem solving;
(v) Understanding of risks related to core activities within the areas/facilities/operations for
which they are responsible;
(vi) Knowledge of relevant legislation and regulations, including ADNOC HSE Standards;
(vii) Knowledge of planning processes, performance reviews, and auditing arrangements related
to HSE Critical Activities.

It is important to clearly identify the objectives of training at the outset, so that assessment of the

(ii) Providing management skills and understanding to recognise the degree of risk and put in
place the controls to reduce the risks to as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP);
(iii) Identifying hazards associated with the HSE Critical Activity and control measures required;
(iv) Enhancing the skill of individuals to enable them to perform their job/role with minimum risk.
