CHEMICAL TREATMENT AND CORROSION MONITORING SPECIFICATION
Introduction
This specification describes the chemical treatment and corrosion monitoring requirements for COMPANY facility
during projects stage.
The chemical treatments included are corrosion inhibitor, biocide and oxygen scavenger. Chemicals excluded from
this document are process chemicals including demulsifiers, H2S scavengers, scale inhibitors, polyelectrolyte
flocculants, antifoamers and flotation agents. Also excluded are drag reducing agents, hydrate inhibitors for
produced gas pipelines and wax inhibitors for oil systems.
The requirements for corrosion monitoring include intrusive, non-intrusive, on-line and off-line techniques. This
specification does not include inspection activities (manual UT wall thickness measurements, radiography, In-Line,
Inspection (ILI) and specialist inspection techniques), techniques for monitoring coatings and cathodic protection
systems and plant operations directly or indirectly related to corrosion control, e.g., dewatering pigging.
Rotating and control equipment (e, g. pumps, compressors, fans, valves etc.) are not included in this specification.
Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to define the main chemical treatment and corrosion monitoring requirements
to manage corrosion in upstream and downstream processes. The requirements are intended to provide
information at the project design stage.

SECTION B (PART 1) – TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS
(CHEMICAL TREATMENT)
CHEMICAL TREATMENT
Chemical treatment, as defined in this Specification, is used to modify corrosion processes and thereby lower the
threat of equipment failure by reducing and/or limiting the corrosion rate to an acceptable level using the optimum
concentration of chemical over the service life.
The provision of chemical treatment systems shall be considered during the project stage.
Corrosion Inhibition
Corrosion inhibitors used in oilfield and downstream applications are surface active chemicals that adsorb onto the
surface of carbon steels to form protective films that inhibit the diffusion of corrodents to the surface. The
concentration of inhibitor in the fluids must be maintained above a minimum value to maintain a target inhibited
corrosion rate to ensure the corrosion allowance is not completely consumed during service life, whilst also being
cost effective.
Most fluids in carbon steel piping and pipelines will require treatment with corrosion inhibitor to control general and
localised corrosion. The use of corrosion inhibitors is required in downhole tubing, subsea and surface flowlines,
risers, export pipelines, water injection pipelines, topsides and refinery piping.
Application in Downhole Facilities
Intermittent batch treatments shall be used downhole to apply corrosion inhibitors to protect tubing where
continuous injection would be impractical. The mode of chemical injection (e.g., batch injection, continuous
injection, injection via annulus or Macaroni string) shall be dictated by the availability of existing injection facilities.
The corrosion inhibitor displaces the production fluids allowing the corrosion inhibitor to soak the tubing. After
several hours, the corrosion inhibitor is pushed from the tubing to film on the remaining tubing and surface
equipment when the well is brought back on-line.
Corrosion inhibitor squeeze applications may be used to protect tubing and topside equipment. The chemical is
pumped into the formation through the tubing string. The chemical absorbs onto the rock during soaking and when
the well is brought online it gradually disperses into the water during production, protecting the wellbore and tubing
string.
Application in Surface Facilities
Crude Oil
Continuous corrosion inhibition is the preferred method of application for wet hydrocarbon systems, it may be used
for downhole (if practical) and for topsides including wellheads, separation system, flowlines and pipelines. In
processing facilities, corrosion inhibitor is normally carried in the water phase and following separation further
continuous injection of corrosion inhibitor may be required in the gas and oil phases.
For these facilities, continuous injection of corrosion inhibitor is the most cost-effective method of application. The
rate of corrosion inhibitor injection must be optimised to be cost-effective and to minimise separation problems and
water quality issues due to emulsification caused by high doses of corrosion inhibitor.
The corrosion inhibitor injection point shall be upstream of the location at which the chemical is required and
injected into flowing conditions to ensure adequate mixing with the process fluids for maximum effectiveness. The

Process Gas
Continuous injection of gas phase inhibitors may be required for wet gas/condensate. Injection of inhibitor into dry

Atomizer nozzles shall be used for injection of corrosion inhibitor into gas streams. The nozzle shall be designed

Injection Water
Produced water used for injection will contain CO2 and H2S and be corrosive. It shall be treated with continuous
injection of corrosion inhibitor. Aquifer water or seawater used for injection will contain oxygen and shall be treated
with oxygen scavenger in addition to mechanical deaeration.
Application in Refinery Facilities/Gas Treatment
Continuous injection of corrosion inhibitors shall be used to:
Treat crude unit overhead corrosion
Control corrosion in CO2 removal systems
Treat utility services such as fresh cooling water or steam/boiler piping.
Corrosion inhibitor shall be compatible with the downstream fluids, catalysts, metallic and non-metallic materials
in the treated system. This also applies to corrosion inhibitors used in utility services such as fresh cooling water
or steam/boiler piping.
Corrosion Inhibitor Testing and Selection
Corrosion inhibitor chemicals shall be qualified and selected based on independent laboratory testing. Individual
laboratory testing programme may vary due to the wide range of oilfield and utility applications for corrosion
inhibitors.
Corrosion inhibitor laboratory testing is part of CONTRACTOR scope to prepare detailed scope of work and
minimum technical acceptance criteria subject for COMPANY approval. It should be noted that many of the
laboratory tests do not have specific pass or fail criteria defined in standards and acceptability is based on overall
results of screening and performance tests. The final corrosion inhibitor selection shall be made by COMPANY
based on the results of field trials and economic considerations.
CONTRACTOR shall arrange for SUPPLIER to provide samples of candidate chemicals for laboratory testing.
For brown field projects, COMPANY may use existing corrosion inhibitor products injected in existing facilities
provided operating parameters are similar with respect to fluid type, pressure, temperature, CO2, H2S, chloride
content levels and water chemistry, and hence pre-qualifying new corrosion inhibitor candidates through 3rd party
specialist laboratory is not required.

Physical Properties
The pH, flash point, viscosity and colour and appearance at ambient temperature and pressure shall be reported
for the as-received corrosion inhibitor.
Specific Gravity
The specific gravity at the required test temperature of each corrosion inhibitor shall be reported. To be conducted
in accordance with ASTM D 891.
Flashing of Corrosion Inhibitor Solvent at Injection Point
The corrosion inhibitor viscosity shall be reported. If any solids form after exposure the corrosion inhibitor shall be
disqualified. To be conducted in accordance with ASTM G 170.
Spectroscopic Analysis using Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR)
FTIR shall be used to obtain a fingerprint of each as-received corrosion inhibitor and the corrosion inhibitor after
thermal exposure, refer to 5.1.6.7.
Screening Tests
Test specimen preparation and evaluation to be conducted in accordance with ASTM G 1, where appropriate.
Solubility
The corrosion inhibitor shall be disqualified if deposits form or the liquid phase separates during the test period.
Tests to be conducted in accordance with ASTM G 170.
Oil and Water Partitioning (Kettle / Bubble Test)
The corrosion rate and time required to reach maximum inhibition shall be reported. Tests to be conducted in
accordance with ASTM G 170.
Analysis using a colorimetric method for determination of the quantity of corrosion inhibitor as amine shall be
performed based on ASTM D 2327: Double Extraction Method.
Chemical Compatibility
Based on oil and water partitioning tests described in ASTM G 170 to assess corrosion inhibitor compatibility in
combination with other dosed chemicals e.g., biocide. Test specimen preparation and evaluation to be conducted
in accordance with ASTM G 1. The impact on corrosion inhibitor performance shall be reported.
Compatibility with Materials
The compatibility of the corrosion inhibitor with 316 stainless steel transfer lines, elastomeric seals, flexible
umbilical, production tree and flowline materials. To be conducted in accordance with ASTM G 170. Corrosion
inhibitor shall be disqualified if attack on materials is excessive.
Emulsification Tendency
The time required for full separation and appearance of the two phases shall be reported. If precipitates or a third
liquid phase develops during the test the corrosion inhibitor shall be disqualified. To be conducted in accordance
with ASTM G 170.

If corrosion inhibitor fails to achieve the target inhibited corrosion rate or fails to fully mitigate pitting corrosion or it
is demonstrated that it promotes preferential weld corrosion (PWC), the corrosion inhibitor shall be disqualified.
Rotating Cage (RC) Tests
RC shall be used to evaluate corrosion inhibitor performance under controlled shear stress in simulated field
conditions in accordance with ASTM G 170 and ASTM G111.
If corrosion inhibitor fails to achieve the target inhibited corrosion rate or fails to fully mitigate pitting corrosion the
corrosion inhibitor shall be disqualified.
Jet Impingement (JI)
Jet impingement (JI) shall be used to evaluate corrosion inhibitor performance at high shear stress under simulated
field conditions in accordance with ASTM G 170 and ASTM G111. It is noted that quantification of shear stress in
JI is not as well established as other test methods.
If corrosion inhibitor fails to achieve the target inhibited corrosion rate the corrosion inhibitor shall be disqualified.
Flow Loop Tests
Flow loop testing shall be used to evaluate corrosion inhibitor performance in controlled shear stress under
simulated field conditions in accordance with ASTM G 170 and ASTM G111.
Flow loop testing is mandated when evaluation of corrosion inhibitor film persistency is required.
If corrosion inhibitor fails to achieve the target inhibited corrosion rate and demonstrates poor film persistency or
fails to fully mitigate pitting corrosion or it is demonstrated that it promotes preferential weld corrosion (PWC), the
corrosion inhibitor shall be disqualified.

Biocide

Biocides are usually applied as batch treatments to be in contact in the system for a limited period of time.
Applicable Systems
Bacteria are active in water injection systems, oil production systems and cause souring of sweet reservoirs. In
aerated waters, iron oxidizing bacteria may form large adherent deposits that block pipelines.

Dead leg sections of piping or pipelines, with limited or no flow, are vulnerable to MIC and under deposit corrosion

the pipe. The design of piping layout shall eliminate or avoid dead legs as far as reasonably practicable. COMPANY
review and approval of dead legs is required. Dead legs shall be avoided by one or more of the following:
Avoid additional extension of pipe running on pipe ways, racks or trenches for supporting purposes.
Arrange piping configurations to avoid stagnancy e.g., manifolds.
If a dead leg is unavoidable, upgrade the Materials of Construction to CRA in accordance with
AGES-GL-07-001.
Unavoidable dead legs shall be managed by COMPANY Dead Leg Management system/specification.
Oxygen Scavenger
Oxygen scavengers are chemicals that react with oxygen to reduce residual dissolved oxygen in aqueous systems.
Oxygen corrosion is prevented by strict exclusion of oxygen entry into production systems. For water injection
systems, injecting sufficient amounts of oxygen scavenger, such as ammonium bisulfite, following mechanical
deaeration, reduces oxygen levels to acceptable levels.
Applicable Systems
Aquifer water and seawater used in injection systems will be oxygenated and shall require mechanical deaeration
followed by treatment with oxygen scavenger.

CORROSION MONITORING
Corrosion monitoring comprises direct and indirect techniques that include intrusive, non-intrusive, online and
offline systems. Corrosion monitoring data is used to enhance knowledge of corrosion degradation or effectiveness
of inhibition and process treatment, therefore allowing action to be taken to prevent potential failures.
Direct techniques measure a parameter changed directly by corrosion and may be intrusive or non-intrusive.
Intrusive techniques e.g., weight-loss coupons, must penetrate through the pipe or vessel wall to gain access to
the internal process. Non-intrusive techniques monitor from the outside of the pipe or vessel without having to
enter the equipment e.g., ultrasonic sensors.
Indirect techniques measure a parameter that affects, or is affected by, corrosion. Indirect techniques may be
online or offline. Online measurements are made whilst the monitoring device remains in the system, and measure
parameters changed directly by corrosion, e.g., hydrogen patch probe. Examples of offline methods include
samples or specimens that are taken away for analysis.
The design and installation of corrosion monitoring systems shall be considered during the project stage.
Corrosion monitoring points shall be situated to enable reliable assessment of the corrosion mitigation measures
and to monitor the effectiveness of the chemical treatment programme. The location, orientation and number of
corrosion monitoring access fittings shall be in accordance with COMPANY requirements and all fittings shall be
installed by certificated technicians.
All materials shall be specified in line with the requirements of ISO 15156 / NACE MR-0175, AGES-GL-07-001
and AGES-SP-07-003.
Primary Corrosion Monitoring Systems
All proposed corrosion coupons and high sensitivity Electrical Resistance (ER) probes shall be approved by the
COMPANY before installation. All corrosion coupons and ER probe elements shall be PMI tested and the
identification punch shall be verified before installation. Installation shall be done in the presence of COMPANY
representative.
Weight Loss Corrosion Coupon Probes
Corrosion Coupons (CC) Probes are used to determine the average fluid corrosivity by measurement of weight
loss. Coupons can provide details relating to the susceptibility to pitting, bimetallic corrosion, stress corrosion
cracking (if a stress coupon is used), weldment corrosion (if a welded coupon is used), scaling and erosion may
also be determined. Weight loss coupons shall be supplemented, as necessary, with ER probes or LPR probes.
For pipelines, the metallurgy of the strip/disc shall be identical to the pipeline metallurgy. The corrosion coupon
type shall be identified by the SUPPLIER. Strips shall be 3 inch long x 1/16 inch thick x ½ inch wide with 2 mounting
holes. The exposed surface area shall be 2.99 in². Circular disc coupons shall be of 1.25 inch dia. x 1/8 inch
thickness with one mounting hole of ID 0.312 inch with an exposed surface area of 2.4 in².
For cross country pipelines, the corrosion coupons shall be flush and retrievable type ASTM A1018 material grade
circular disc coupons of 1.25 inch dia. x 1/8 inch thickness with one mounting hole of ID 0.312 inch with an exposed
surface area of 2.4 in².

ER Probes - Traditional
ER probes suitable for measuring low corrosion rates using the electrical resistance (ER) direct intrusive
measurement technique shall be used in accordance with ASTM G 96.
All traditional ER probes are similar and can be interrogated by any traditional ER instrumentation. The most
common type of ER instrumentation are portable (intrinsically safe) units which are connected to the probe and a
“probe reading” taken. Units give a digital readout (and can also include a facility to record values, together with
probe ID and date/time of reading for later download to a computer to aid data collection analysis. Portable
instrumentation is available that can interrogate a number of different probe types, for example, ER, Linear
Polarisation Resistance and Galvanic Probes.
Fixed monitoring instrumentation is also available where readings are taken at regular intervals (typically once
every 1 to 6 hours). Data is either stored on a data card for retrieval (typically monthly / three monthly) and then
analysed post-event, or data can be transferred to a plant control system for more immediate analysis (either via
hard-wired or wireless communication systems).
The ER Probes shall be high resolution, high sensitivity, high pressure retrievable and shall have a flush mounted
body in 316 stainless steel with a hollow plug and designed for installation on standard 1.5 inch and 2 inch access
fittings with provision for online removal and replacement.

ER Probes – New Generation (High Resolution probes)
The sensitivity of traditional ER probes and meters is such that they are not able to provide rapid responses to
changes in corrosion conditions. However, new generation ‘high resolution’ ER probes are now available which
can provide very rapid and almost continuous corrosion rates.
However, whilst traditional ER probes and instrumentation are completely interchangeable (such that any
manufacturers probes can be used with any manufacturers instrumentation), this is not the case with the new
generation systems. New generation instrumentation can only be used in conjunction with new generation probes
and only those from the same manufacturer.
Any use of new generation ER systems is subject to review and approval by COMPANY.
Linear Polarisation Resistance (LPR) Probes
LPR probes shall be used in low resistivity liquids to measure general corrosion rates in real time.
LPR probes are typically a two or three electrode configuration with either flush or projecting electrodes, allowing
information on changing corrosion conditions and the effect of process changes to be identified during successive
measurements. Dosage rates from chemical injection systems can be optimised by correlating the data with the
change in dosage.
Errors may arise in sour service if conductive iron sulfide films form on the probe surface, causing electrical shorting
between electrodes.
To be conducted in accordance with NACE TR 3T199 and ASTM G96.
Galvanic Probes
Galvanic probes shall be used in conductive aqueous conditions, i.e., seawater injection, produced water or
separated water, to provide information on the risk of galvanic corrosion between different metals or alloys in a
system and/or as a secondary indicator of the presence of oxygen.
This technique is predominantly used in water phase systems and in water flood systems for enhanced oil recovery
to detect corrosion. Galvanic probes are normally located downstream of the deaerator tower, after the injection
of oxygen scavenger, to monitor the oxygen content of the system.

Hydrogen Probes
Hydrogen patch probes shall be attached to the exterior of a sour service pipes or vessel to measure the hydrogen
diffusing through a pipe or vessel wall which could lead to hydrogen cracking and potential rapid failure
(AGES-SP-07-003).
Patch probes do not give an output that is directly related to corrosion rate but are used to identify changes in
corrosion conditions, effectiveness of inhibitors and the risk of embrittlement of the metallurgical structure that may
lead to cracking or blistering.
Dry hydrogen probes shall be attached to the pipe or vessel enclosing a small airtight volume. A vacuum is applied
to the enclosed volume and the rate of hydrogen penetration is measured by monitoring the change in pressure
on a vacuum gauge.
Wet hydrogen probes create a small volume between a palladium foil and the pipe wall. The volume is filled with
an oxidizing electrolyte. Applying a constant voltage between palladium foil and the pipe the atomic hydrogen
migrating through the pipe wall is oxidised and the hydrogen penetration current measured.
Further information on use of hydrogen probes is given in NACE TR 1C184.

Selection of Corrosion Monitoring Methods
The corrosion monitoring method to be applied should be selected to provide useful, accurate and reliable
information taking into account environmental conditions, actual and predicted corrosion mechanisms, technical
and economic feasibility of application (including data acquisition costs) and HSE considerations.
More than one corrosion monitoring method shall be used in any one system in order to have greater confidence
and confirmation of the data collected, as errors within corrosion monitoring systems are a common occurrence.
Generally, any corrosion monitoring scheme should include, as a minimum, three primary methods, normally
comprising:
Corrosion coupons
Another method (ER, LPR, Galvanic Probe or Fixed UT)
Fluid sampling and analysis
Selection of item 2, above, will be dependent on the particular process/asset type and expected corrosion
rate/morphology. Consideration shall also be given to incorporating secondary monitoring systems (bacterial
monitoring (bio-probes), hydrogen monitoring) deployed in addition to the primary methods, where feasible.

Corrosion Monitoring Locations
The selection of suitable corrosion monitoring locations is very important for effective monitoring and there are no
fixed rules. Guidelines and factors that shall be accounted for in the selection of monitoring locations are detailed
below and in Section 8.1.
Corrosion monitoring locations shall be selected to be representative of the corrosivity of the system. The following
areas shall be prioritised:
Areas considered to be generally representative of the system.
Locations known or anticipated to be susceptible to high corrosion rates resulting in severe consequences
from failure.
Pipework sections which can be easily isolated in the event of access fitting problems.
Experience from similar operations shall be considered in identifying the most suitable monitoring locations.
Inspection and maintenance reports will provide information on areas vulnerable to corrosion in each system.
Once the corrosion monitoring locations have been selected for the system, they shall be marked up on control
copies of the technical drawings. Factors to consider in the determination of suitable corrosion monitoring locations
and orientations include:
The Corrosion Risk (Criticality) Assessment to determine locations which have a high consequence of
failure.
The location of corrosive attack must be determined where corrosion rates have been predicted.
The mode/type of attack is dependent on the material and the corrosion environment. Understanding of
the likely corrosion mechanisms is necessary to determine the most suitable corrosion monitoring system.
The corrosivity and the location of attack are affected by the flow regime and the flow rates. The water
content will determine the optimum corrosion monitoring location. For example, in crude lines with low
water cuts (typically < 2%), a separate water phase is only expected under upset conditions and
consideration should be given to use of water traps. In liquid lines with high water cuts, corrosion
monitoring should be placed directly into the pipeline or side streams should be used.

HSE Considerations
For Ultra High sour facilities, the design and installation of retrievable, fixed intrusive corrosion monitoring methods
such as Corrosion Coupons (CC) Probes, Electrical Resistance (ER) Probes and Linear Polarization Resistance
(LPR) Probes are not permitted. Non-Intrusive methods such as Real-Time corrosion monitoring using Ultrasonic
Wireless Sensors shall be used for corrosion monitoring.
For other facilities, the design and installation of retrievable, fixed intrusive corrosion monitoring methods such as
Corrosion Coupons (CC) Probes, Electrical Resistance (ER) Probes and Linear Polarization Resistance (LPR)
Probes are not recommended, and shall be used only in agreement with COMPANY.
For non-sour conditions, a considerable amount of hydrocarbon products may be released during the removal of
corrosion coupons. These products are highly flammable and shall not be drained to ground or water. Provision
shall be made to ensure safe drainage of these hydrocarbon products for disposal.
Additionally, other necessary safety precautions shall be taken such as the use of correct protective personnel
equipment (PPE) and compliance with the Task Risk Assessment (TRA) as per the prevailing COMPANY safety
guidelines while carrying out site activities during periodic corrosion coupon assessments.

ACCESS FITTINGS FOR CHEMICAL INJECTION AND CORROSION MONITORING
General Requirements
Access fittings shall be fitted to allow chemical injection and corrosion monitoring elements, i.e., coupons
and probes, to be installed and retrieved from pressurized operational pipelines, piping and vessels. An
illustration of the types of corrosion access fittings, which may be used on COMPANY facilities, is given
in Figure 8.1. 2 inch system flare welded or flanged access fitting is recommended.
Access fittings shall be installed in the pipeline or pipe work at areas of worst-case corrosion, i.e., the
coupon is exposed to the corrosive water phase or where the fluid characteristics or operational
conditions change and where there is sufficient space to use and retrieve the corrosion monitoring
coupons and probes safely and reliably. The minimum distance between two access fittings should be
200 mm from outer surface to outer surface of each access fitting and the other.
Where corrosion control is by use of chemical treatment, corrosion monitoring shall be used to confirm
performance and effectiveness of the chemical injection programmes. The corrosion monitoring Access
Fittings should be located downstream of the injection points. The access fittings should be situated
sufficiently downstream the injection Access Fittings by a minimum of 5 pipeline diameters to ensure
adequate dispersion of the corrosion inhibitor before the treated fluid reaches the monitoring location.
Upstream access fittings could be installed whenever required and pending availability of space. For
transition pipelines, additional corrosion monitoring Access Fittings downstream of the pipeline (arrival)
should be installed.
For flowlines and piping two access fittings shall be installed. This will allow both weight loss coupons
and an alternative monitoring technique to be used and, if one fitting is damaged or unusable, will ensure
that monitoring is possible.
For water pipelines three access fittings shall be installed. This provides an additional access point for
the installation of bio probes.
Access Fitting shall be of the flare-weld or Flanged attachment types, Standard NPS 2 system which
meet / NACE MR0175/ISO 15156 and related pressure of 418.7 bar (6000 psi) and temperature of -26
up to 204°C (-15 up to 400°F). Lower pressure Access Fittings are not accepted unless otherwise
specified in writing. (For more detail see Table 8.1 for details of access fitting component).
Experience in COMPANY systems indicates that worst-case corrosion in pipelines in hydrocarbon
service is usually at the bottom of the line where water and debris are accumulated. All new corrosion
monitoring access fitting shall be installed at the 6 o’clock position in pipelines and network piping. For
cross country pipelines it shall be located on the pipeline at receiver end.
Pipeline details like material, thickness, operating and design conditions, location and quantity of
corrosion probes, corrosion coupons and access fittings to be installed shall be decided based on the
process design basis and engineering documents.

Access Fittings for Chemical Injection
Chemical injection points are used to inject corrosion inhibitors, biocides, oxygen scavenger, scale
inhibitor, anti-foam and demulsifiers into pipelines, piping and vessels. In this Specification, the principal
use described is corrosion inhibitor injection. These Access Fittings are usually installed at areas of high
turbulence to ensure that the inhibitor will be adequately mixed into the fluid stream.
Chemical injection points should be installed on the top of the line wherever possible.
The chemical injection access fittings differ from corrosion monitoring fittings in having tees attached with
a welded flange matching the tee piping class rating. This type of fitting is illustrated in Figure 8.1. The
fittings may be attached using flanges or by welding. 2 inch system Flare welded fittings are the only type
recommended to be used.
The tee pipe to the access fitting must be of minimum NPS 1 and fabricated in steel of a schedule
adequate for the design pressure of the system and the external mechanical and thermal loads, which
may be imposed.
The tee should be as short as is practicable, but the length of the tee should not be less than 83 mm to
permit Access Fittings of the isolation valve. Longer tees are acceptable but may require support to
prevent mechanical damage.
Only access fittings with welded tees are approved. Threaded tee fittings are not accepted.
It is critically important that there is adequate space for the installation of the retriever service/isolation
valve. This valve is installed temporarily when an internal fitting, e.g., the injection quill, is removed from
the operating pipeline. Often the bolts or studs and nuts used to fix the permanent isolating valve interfere
with installation of the retriever valve and prevent retrieval of the injection device in the access fitting.
The length of the tee and studs and nuts should be such that there is a clear 3.25-inch (83 mm) between
the body of the access fitting and the upper bolts/studs/nuts. The area of concern is shown in Figure 8.3.
The internal injection Access Fittings should be for tee-type fittings and of removable type using a
retriever and isolation valve assembly. Tee-less injection fittings are available but are not accepted for
service.
A large proportion of COMPANY hydrocarbon production and operations are sour, and all Access Fittings
should be assumed to be required for sour service and all parts, which will be exposed to process fluids,
and welding and other operations associated should conform to the requirements of NACE MR0175/ISO
15156 and all COMPANY requirements including AGES-SP-07-007 and AGES-SP-07-003.
The tee pipe will terminate at flange rating and type (RF/RTJ) to the line pressure and fabricated in the
same pipe class and material as the tee pipe.
A corrosion resistant alloy isolation valve and a non-return valve will be fitted to the flange to isolate the
injection line from the system. In sour service and high pressure systems (above 1,500 psig) double
isolation valves are required in line with AGES-PH-08-001 Process Isolation Philosophy.
