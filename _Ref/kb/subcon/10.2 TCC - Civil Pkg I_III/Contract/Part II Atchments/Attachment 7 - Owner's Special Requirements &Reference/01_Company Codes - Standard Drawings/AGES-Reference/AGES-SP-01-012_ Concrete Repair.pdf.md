GENERAL
INTRODUCTION
This specification describes the minimum technical requirements for design and construction aspects of
repair and rehabilitation of concrete in ADNOC facilities.
PURPOSE
The purpose of this specification is to provide the minimum requirements for repair of concrete structures,
selection and application of materials and methods for repair and rehabilitation of concrete structures.
This is generally applicable for rehabilitation of existing concrete structures; however, relevant sections
can be applied to new concrete structures subject to COMPANY approval.
This specification does not cover structural integrity assessment and life extension study of concrete
structures and buildings.

SECTION B – TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS
CONCRETE REPAIR PROCESS
Determination of Cause of Damage
a. The first step in concrete repair is to assess the current condition of concrete structure.
CONTRACTOR shall review the available design and construction documents, maintenance record,
any previous repair work accomplished, visual examination findings, destructive (core drilling) and
non-destructive testing and laboratory analysis results of concrete samples.
b. Defects in concrete structures can result from inadequate design, specification, supervision,
execution, and materials, including:
i. Inadequate structural design
ii. Inadequate mix design, insufficient compaction, insufficient mixing
iii. Insufficient cover
iv. Insufficient or defective waterproofing
v. Contamination, poor or reactive aggregates
vi. Inadequate curing
c. Other defects may become apparent during service, including the effects of:
i. Reinforcement corrosion
ii. Service climate, atmospheric pollution, chloride, carbon dioxide, aggressive chemicals
iii. Structure overloading
iv. Impact damage, expansion forces from fires
v. Erosion, aggressive groundwater, seismic action
d. An assessment shall be made of defects in concrete structure, their causes, and of the ability of the
concrete structure to perform its function.
e. The process of assessment of the structure shall include but not to be limited to the following:
i. The visible condition of the existing concrete structures
ii. Testing to determine the condition of the concrete and reinforcement
iii. The original design check
iv. The environment, including exposure to contamination
v. The history of the concrete structure, including environmental exposure
vi. The conditions of use, (e.g., loading or other actions)
vii. Requirement for future use.
f. The nature and cause of defects, including combinations of causes, shall be identified and recorded
(The common cause of defects in concrete and corrosion of reinforcement are summarized as below)

Assessment of Damage
a. Prior to commencing repair works, all previous information on the structure shall be collected and
reviewed by CONTRACTOR. When defects are observed, additional testing and assessment shall be
carried out to establish the cause and extent of the defects and to predict future performance for the
desired function.
b. The condition of the concrete and reinforcement shall be established and documented and the data
shall be recorded.
c. A typical assessment would include the following in-situ testing by an approved agency.
i. Concrete Core test: for evaluation of compressive strength, petrographic analysis
ii. Rebound Hammer test: quality and uniformity check of concrete
iii. Half-cell Potential Test: corrosion measurement
iv. Dust Sampling: checking presence of chloride and sulphate content
v. Concrete Cover meter: to check the actual concrete cover available for main rebar
vi. Depth of carbonation: to assess the penetration of carbon
vii. Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity test: to assess the condition in the interior/body of concrete
The type of test and number of tests shall be decided based on type and damage extent specific to
existing concrete elements
d. Generally, corrosion of embedded reinforcement ultimately results in cracking and spalling of the
concrete cover. However, it shall be kept in mind that active corrosion may occur for a considerable
time before cracks appear and also that, under certain conditions, the corrosion may not be expansive
and therefore may not result in cracking. This being the case, electrochemical testing shall be
considered because it is able to detect active corroding reinforcement even though there are no
outward visible signs. Such hidden damage also needs to be considered in the structure management
strategy by the CONTRACTOR.

e. CONTRACTOR shall assess the existing condition of concrete structure and predict the future
performance preferably through consideration of previous tests made at suitable intervals and
information on the history of the concrete structure.
f. An assessment is normally carried out separately before the start of the repair works. Assessment
carried out some time ago for the proposed repair works may not represent the current condition. In
such cases the assessment needs to be updated before the repair works are executed. In all cases,
it is essential to assess the full extent and causes of the defects.
g. A condition assessment may be undertaken in more than one stage. For example, a preliminary stage
may be required to provide immediate advice on the safety of the concrete structure and any risk to
third parties, with a more detailed assessment undertaken immediately before the works are designed.
h. The assessment of defects, the prognosis for their further development and the structural assessment
shall be recorded by CONTRACTOR.
Evaluation of Extent of Damage
a. Based on cause of damage and supporting data, CONTRACTOR shall determine the mechanism or
mechanisms that caused the problems. Before proceeding with remedial effort, make sure that the
problem designated for repair have been properly evaluated as to the cause, effect and degree of
influence of those problems have on the present and long term serviceability and integrity of the
structure.
b. Crack mapping shall also be carried out at this stage. The condition of the element/member varies
from ‘Undamaged’ (requiring only surface improvement/coating only), to ‘Damaged’ (Requiring skin
repair/ coating or reconstruction, dependent on crack width), to ‘Severely damaged’ (requiring
complete reconstruction).
c. Consideration of the options and their consequences will generally include examination of different
aspects, for example initial cost, maintenance costs and the possible need to introduce restrictions on
the use of the structure. Each option is likely to have a different level of future deterioration risk.
d. When choosing options for protection and repair systems, an important consideration is design life of
the individual products, as they may not last for the design life of the concrete structure. Factors such
as access to the works and the renewal and reparability of protection and repair systems are important
consideration.
e. Only after evaluation is complete, CONTRACTOR shall develop a suitable remedial action plan, select
material, and prepare drawings and method statement. Repair work shall be carried out only after
completion of inspection by COMPANY and approval of CONTRACTOR’S remedial plan.
Repair Method
a. CONTRACTOR shall be responsible for design of the concrete repair and shall select most economical
method subjected to the acceptance of COMPANY.
b. The selection of appropriate repair material and methodology shall be based upon the following
considerations:
i. Advantages and disadvantages of making permanent versus temporary repairs worked out in
detail, in terms of cost incurred throughout the service life of structure. Select the material and
methods that will match the intended life of the repair.
ii. Structural safety before, during, and after the repair (Safety shall not be endangered by the repair
work)
iii. Requirement for temporary supports, shoring, and strengthening shall be determined. Temporary
supports shall be designed to withstand the maximum load capacity of the element/member to
be repaired/replaced.

Preparation of Drawings and Specifications

provisions for temporary structural support be included by CONTRACTOR.
c. Effective repair specification shall be clear and concise. Scope of work shall include material,
application considerations, and the performance testing standards with reference to specific
requirements and related supporting documents.

CONCRETE REMOVAL
a. Repair or rehabilitation involves removal of deteriorated, damaged, or defective concrete.
CONTRACTOR shall select concrete removal techniques that are effective, safe, economical, and
that minimize damage to the concrete left in place. The removal technique chosen may have significant
effect on the length of time that a structure must be out of service.
b. All areas of unsound concrete to be repaired will be marked for COMPANY inspection once the
CONTRACTOR has cleaned the existing surface as per section 11 of this specification. The contractor
shall saw cut the outer perimeter of the repair area 25 mm deep or as directed by the COMPANY.
Feathered edges will not be acceptable. The CONTRACTOR shall take care to ensure that the existing
reinforcing steel are not damaged during saw cutting. Any damage caused by the CONTRACTOR to
the portion of the structure not intended for repair shall be repaired by the CONTRACTOR, at the
CONTRACTOR’S expenses, to the satisfaction of the COMPANY.

c. The CONTRACTOR shall remove all areas of unsound concrete by chipping or other approved
methods. Only chipping hammers of the 15 Kg class or less shall be used, and operated at an angle
of 45 degrees or less from the horizontal. The CONTRACTOR shall take extreme caution and take
care not to damage any existing reinforcement steel intended to remain in place.
d. Breaking of concrete shall be carried out in a phased manner to suit the degree of temporary support
possible, the extent of repair required, the size of the element/member and the phasing shall be
subjected to COMPANY approval. No two adjacent foundations designated for reconstruction shall be
worked on at the same time.
Cutting Methods
Cutting methods include diamond wire/saw cutting, Impacting method and high pressure water jets.
a. Diamond wire cutting:
This system can be used to cut a structure of any size as long as the wire can be wrapped around the
unsound concrete. This method provides an efficient method for cutting up and dismantling large to
small concrete structures.
b. High pressure water jets:
High pressure water jets producing pressure of 69 to 310 MPa and above can be used as a primary
removal method. Applicable for making cut-outs through slabs, decks and other thin concrete
members.
c. Diamond Saw:
Diamond saw or cable saw can be used to cutting depth up to 1.3 m and makes precision cuts,
applicable for making cut-outs through slabs, decks and other thin concrete members.
d. Impacting Methods:
i. This method can be used in areas of limited work space and applicable for limited volume of
concrete removal. The hand held breakers are available in various sizes with different levels of
energy and efficiency.
ii. Small hand-held breakers can be used in partial removal of unsound concrete or concrete around
reinforcing steel. The large breakers can be used for complete removal of large volume of
concrete.
SURFACE PREPARATION
a. Prior to any concrete repairs, the CONTRACTOR shall remove all dusts, dirt, water and debris from
the surface of the concrete in a manner that will not result in the material being deposited into the
channel or onto the underlying surface below.
b. The entire repair area shall then be jet washed with potable water, then oil free air blasted until dry.
The prepared area shall be designated as the final extent of repair.
c. The CONTRACTOR shall supply and erect appropriate protection barriers/shrouding or other
approved means as required working at higher elevations so as to completely contain all loose or
flying debris from the surface removal preparations. The means of containment shall be subjected to
the approval of COMPANY. No debris shall fall into the drain channels or onto any underlying
roadways or ground surfaces.
REINFORCEMENT REPAIR
a. The following basic preparation and repair procedure shall be used by CONTRACTOR.

i. After the cause of the damage has been determined, it is necessary to expose the steel, evaluate
the condition, and prepare the reinforcement for the repair techniques.
ii. Extreme care should be exercised to ensure that further damage to the reinforcing steel is not
occurred by the process of removing the concrete.
iii. Impact breakers can heavily damage reinforcement steel if the breakers are used without regard
to the location of reinforcement. CONTRACTOR shall use Pachometer/Covermeter (to determine
the location and depth of the reinforcement in the concrete) and a copy of the structural drawings
shall be used to determine the location of reinforcement.
iv. Care shall be taken not to vibrate the reinforcement or otherwise cause damage to its bond to
concrete adjacent to the repair area.
v. Small chipping hammer shall be used to remove the concrete in the vicinity of the reinforcement.
vi. If the reinforcing bars are only partially exposed after all unsound concrete is removed, it may not
be necessary to remove additional concrete to expose the full circumference of the reinforcement.
vii. If reinforcing steel is exposed and found to have rust due to corrosion and not well bonded to the
surrounding concrete, then it is recommended that concrete removal shall continue to create clear
space behind the reinforcing steel of 12 mm plus the diameter of the maximum size aggregate of
the repair material.
viii. Reinforcing steel shall be cleaned and carefully inspected to determine whether the reinforcement
needs to be replaced.
ix. If the reinforcement has been damaged by corrosion, it shall either be cleaned and protected or
replaced.
x. Corroded rebars having less than 20% section loss shall be thoroughly cleaned by grit blasting or
wire mesh to SA 2.5 and washed by water.

REPAIR TYPES
Protection and repair are based on chemical, electrochemical or physical principles that can be used to
prevent or stabilize the deterioration of concrete or electrochemical corrosion on the steel surface, or to
strengthen the concrete structure. Based on Depth of repair types are broadly classified as:
Skin Repair
Depending on the depth of repair, skin repairs are broadly classified as partial depth type A and B and
full depth repair as described below.
All skin repairs shall be performed using either “Form and Pour” or “Form and Pump” technique as
appropriate unless otherwise agreed with COMPANY.
11.1.1 Partial Depth Repair Type A (Deep Skin Repair)
a. Type “A” repair is defined as removal of unsound concrete to a depth a minimum of 25 mm beyond
the existing outer mat of reinforcing steel. The repairs shall be carried out in a staggered manner
(Maximum, 2 adjacent sides of a member) and simultaneous repair of any pair of adjacent members
shall be avoided. In any case, the exposed/repaired section shall not exceed 1.5 m in length unless
otherwise indicated on the drawings. Temporary supports shall be provided whenever required or
where applicable to ensure integrity of the facility.
b. Following works are involved as a minimum:
i. Removal of all unsound coating/concrete (Refer section 7).
ii. Surface preparation as per section 8.
iii. Reinforcement repair as per section 9 (as required).
iv. If indicated in drawing embed the engineered discrete zinc anode to protect the reinforcing steel
from corrosion
v. Application of bonding agent to substrate using approved materials on the prepared surface as
per MANUFACTURER’s recommendation.
vi. Adequate formwork shall be provided in accordance with ACI repair application procedure.
vii. Repair of damaged surface.
viii. Curing for minimum 10 days or as per MANUFACTURER’s recommendation.
ix. Coating or protection of above and below ground concrete surfaces as per AGES-SP-01-001.
11.1.2 Partial Depth Repair Type B (Skin Repair)
a. Type “B” repair is defined as removal of unsound concrete to a depth not greater than the top of the
existing reinforcement steel. This include surface cracks, small honey combing where, unsound
surface, broken edges to a depth not greater than 50 mm.
b. Following works are involved as a minimum:
i. Removal of all unsound coating/concrete from the areas as determined by the COMPANY until
a sound concrete substance is exposed (Refer section 7).
ii. Surface preparation as per section 8.
iii. Application of corrosion inhibitor (in case of high chloride content in the concrete substrate) to the
substrate as per MANUFACTURER recommendations
iv. Application of bonding agent to substrate using approved materials on the prepared surface as
per MANUFACTURER’s recommendation.

from approximately 0.15mm upward can be successfully injected using either adhesive "surface"
packers (wide cracks >300 microns) or drilled and fixed "expansion" packers as required. The
adhesive for surface packers and sealing the cracks prior to injection is an epoxy bedding
compound and adhesive or equally approved.
ii. Damp or Wet / Dynamic or Static Cracks – cracks that require sealing to prevent water ingress
can be sealed with either an Acrylic or Polyurethane based resin system depending entirely upon
the degree of moisture / water ingress that is being experienced. In such cases each individual
application has to be closely examined to determine the best method of injection and the most
suitable product for the job at hand.

and shall be allowed to cure for a minimum of 10 days or as per MANUFACTURER’s
recommendations. Concrete coating and its time of application shall comply with AGES-SP-01-001
c. Material for coating shall be based on following performance characteristics as listed below.
i. Water permeability – resistance to water absorption is a critical factor in protective system. Water
permeability is a measurement of the quantity of water that can pass through a surface treatment
over a period of time.
ii. Resistance to reinforcement corrosion – A primary reason to provide surface treatment is to
increase the resistance to reinforcement corrosion by providing a less corrosive environment.
The water and vapour permeability characteristics are indirect measures of the resistance of
concrete to reinforcement corrosion.
iii. Crack bridging – Moisture penetration through cracks may defeat the purpose of surface
treatment by allowing localized reinforcement corrosion. Penetrants and surface sealers do not
have significant crack bridging capabilities, cracks wider than 0.25 – 0.375 mm shall be routed
and sealed prior to application of the membrane.
iv. Skid resistance – Penetration do not affect the skid resistance of the concrete surface. Because
penetrants are located at or beneath the surface, they are subjected to wear to the same extent
as the concrete surface. Surface sealers and high build coatings may make the surface less skid
resistant, whereas membrane system and overlays have the potential to make the surface more
skid resistant.
v. Protection system for underground concrete shall match with the existing and seamlessly
integrated with the adjacent areas.
