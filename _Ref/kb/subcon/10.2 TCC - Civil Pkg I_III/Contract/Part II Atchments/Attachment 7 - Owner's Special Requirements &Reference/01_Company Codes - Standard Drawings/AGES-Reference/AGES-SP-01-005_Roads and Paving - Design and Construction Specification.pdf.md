GENERAL
Introduction
This specification covers the minimum requirements of the work and responsibilities for plant roads and
area paving. It includes the requirements for subgrade preparation, base courses, bituminous concrete
paving, cement concrete paving, interlocking paving blocks, geotextiles, membrane liners, erosion
protection, road markings, traffic signs and road barriers.
This specification does not include the requirements for road cutting and embankment earthworks and
drainage.
Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to provide the minimum requirements for standards, materials and
execution to accomplish the design and construction of plant roads and area paving. Roads and paving
requiring a local authority approval shall be designed and constructed in accordance with the local
authority’s guidelines, standards and procedures.

ROAD AND PAVING TYPES
Functional Requirements
8.1.1 Functional requirements for roads and paving areas are:
a. All roads and tracks shall provide stable and durable access, free from surface water, to all locations
of the site for those transport means for which they are designed.
b. Provision of reliable and easy access of personnel and equipment for construction, operation and
maintenance.
c. Provision of a foundation base for light elements such as ladders, stairs, instrument panels, etc.,
where required on area paving within facilities.
d. Protection of the soil and the groundwater against a spillage of chemicals including hydrocarbons.
e. Prevention of erosion of the soil.
f. Routing of spillages (water, chemicals, oil) to sumps and drains.
g. Stabilization of soil mainly in areas next to equipment.
h. Sand control.
8.1.2 Surfacing of unpaved areas shall satisfy the following:
a. Prevent soil erosion by wind and water.
b. Able to support people on foot and light vehicles (i.e. 4-wheel drive type).
c. Keep the subject area clear of undesirable vegetation.
Access and Plant Roads
a. Access and Plant roads are generally surfaced with bituminous concrete or reinforced concrete or
interlocking concrete blocks depending on type and shall be as specified in the Project Specific
documents. Where there might be spillage or dripping of hydrocarbons or chemical spills that could
cause damage to the bituminous concrete surface or infiltrate the road base, the roads shall be
paved with cement concrete.

for bituminous concrete roads. Road shoulders for cement concrete road paving shall be of the
same concrete as the roadway or, where hydrocarbon or chemical spillages will not occur,
interlocking concrete blocks. Interlocking concrete block roadway paving may be either extended to
form road shoulders or formed using gatch material. Shoulders shall be capable of supporting slow
moving or parked vehicles for which the road is designed.
c. Plant roads passing through facilities with area paving shall be classified as for equivalent access
roads except that the shoulders may be omitted if the adjacent paving acts as a substitute for road
shoulders.

Interface with Site Preparation
a. Fill to form road embankments or to raise general site elevations in areas to be paved shall be

in accordance with AGES-SP-01-006, Site Preparation, Earthworks and Bund Construction

placement of embankment or general site fill during site preparation there is no requirement for
subgrade preparation.
b. If, subsequent to completion of site preparation earthworks, material is required to raise pavement
levels in order to achieve the minimum measurement from pavement surface to groundwater level
as indicated in Section 9.6.1, it shall be Structural Fill or General Fill to suit the paving duty.
Structural or General Fill shall be placed in accordance with AGES-SP-01-006, Site Preparation,
Earthworks and Bund Construction Specification.
c. Culverts, piping, conduits and other features under subgrade shall be installed prior to
commencement of pavement construction works, unless otherwise approved by COMPANY.
Subgrade Preparation
a. Road embankment or site fill surfaces that have been exposed to the effects of weathering or
degradation leading to softening, loosening or contamination by deleterious substances and
materials shall be improved. Natural soils forming the subgrade on which roads and paving are to
be constructed without the addition of fill shall also be improved.
b. Preparation and improvement shall comprise;
i. The removal of all deleterious material to the full depth of improvement. This shall include, but
not be limited to, topsoil, vegetation, roots and other organic matter, large stones, rocks and
demolition materials such as timber, concrete and brick rubble.
ii. Excavation, mixing, moisture conditioning to between ± 2 % of optimum moisture content,
relaying and compaction to achieve a minimum of 95 % maximum dry density. Minimum depth
of improvement shall vary according to subgrade material as follows;
1. Natural soil 250 mm
2. Structural Fill 150 mm
3. General Fill 300 mm
c. Preparation and improvement shall be carried out over the full area of paving. For roads this shall
include the shoulders.
Subgrade Construction
a. Confirmation of subgrade properties and control of subgrade preparation for construction shall be
in accordance with Section 31.2.1.
b. Where material is added to raise the subgrade level following compaction, scarification of the
subgrade surface shall be done in order to allow the existing and added material to mix evenly
before recompacting. Where material is removed to reduce the subgrade level the resulting surface
shall be proof rolled to recompact any remaining loose material.

13.3.3 Construction of Bituminous Surface Course
a. Bituminous Concrete Production
i. Bituminous concrete production shall be in accordance with AASHTO M156.
ii. Rates of feed of aggregates shall be regulated so that moisture content and temperature of
aggregates will be within specified tolerances. Aggregates, mineral filler, and bitumen shall be
conveyed into the mixer in proportionate quantities required to meet the JMF for either binder
course or wearing course. Mixing time shall be as required to obtain a uniform coating of the
aggregate with the bituminous material.
iii. The bituminous plant shall be of such capacity to produce the quantities of bituminous mixtures
required for the project within the scheduled period. Hauling equipment, paving machines,
rollers, miscellaneous equipment, and tools shall be provided in sufficient number and capacity
and in proper working condition to place the bituminous paving mixtures at a rate equal to the
plant output.
iv. The mixing plant shall be an automatic or semiautomatic controlled, commercially manufactured
unit designed and operated to produce a mixture within the tolerances specified for the binder
course or wearing course JMF.
v. COMPANY shall have access at all times to all parts of the paving plant for checking adequacy
of equipment in use; inspecting operation of plant; verifying weights, proportions, character of
materials; and temperatures maintained in preparation of mixtures.
b. Aggregate Storage
i. The aggregates shall be stored on a clean and horizontal area. Aggregates shall be spread in
horizontal layers ≤ 1 m thickness.
ii. Mineral aggregates shall be delivered to the site of the bituminous mixing plant and stockpiled
in such manner as to preclude fracturing of aggregate particles, segregation, contamination, or
intermingling of different materials in the stockpiles or cold-feed hoppers. Mineral filler shall be
delivered, stored, and introduced into the mixing plant in a manner to preclude exposure to
moisture or other detrimental conditions.
c. Mixing
i. Bituminous concrete shall be mixed in a plant approved by COMPANY. The plant shall be
equipped with separated batching hoppers.
ii. Mixer loading shall be by mechanical batcher via a conveyor. The mineral filler shall not be
placed directly in the mixer.

31.2.1 Field Compaction Trial
a. Large scale field compaction trials shall be conducted prior to commencing road and paving
construction to optimise the requirements for subgrade preparation and sub-base and base material
placement, in accordance with AGES-SP-01-006, Site Preparation, Earthworks and Bund
Construction Specification.
b. Subgrade, sub-base and base course material control shall be based on achieving 95 % maximum
dry density at optimum moisture content.
c. At the start of the field compaction trial samples shall be taken of the subgrade, sub-base and base
material for laboratory compaction testing to ASTM D1557 to establish the target maximum dry
densities and optimum moisture contents.
d. The trial is to enable the selection of compaction equipment, the thickness of fill layers and the
number of passes to achieve a minimum of 95 % of the target maximum dry density and confirm
the pavement layer stiffness characteristics used in the pavement design. For sub-base and base
courses, the method established shall ensure that the full design layer thickness is achieved by sub
layers that are between 100 mm and 200 mm compacted thickness.
e. In-situ density measurements shall be carried out by the sand cone method to ASTM D1556.
f. In situ plate loads tests to BS 1377-9 shall be carried out during the trial to confirm the subgrade,
sub-base and base design modulus of reaction or resilient modulus used in bituminous concrete or
cement concrete pavement design. Minimum 760 mm diameter plates should be used in testing
subgrade. Sub-base and base course trial thicknesses shall be sufficient to ensure plate tests loads
are confined to the sub-base or base course material and not influenced by underlying soils (and
sub-base in the case of testing the base layer). For interlocking concrete block paving the subgrade
design CBR value shall be confirmed by in situ CBR testing to BS1377-9.
g. If the design values for modulus of reaction, resilient modulus or CBR value, as appropriate, of any
of the subgrade, sub-base or base material cannot be replicated in compaction field trials the road
and paving designs shall be modified to suit the proven values for COMPANY approval.
h. The use of other properties co-relatable to elastic modulus, such as static or dynamic cone testing,
may be used subject to demonstration of the co-relation and COMPANY approval.
