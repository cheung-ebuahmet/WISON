GENERAL
Introduction
1.1.1 This specification covers the minimum requirements associated with the following activities:
a. Site levels and tolerances.
b. Design including seismic aspects.
c. Site preparation.
d. Dewatering.
e. Earthworks materials.
f. Earthworks excavation, filling and compaction.
g. Subgrade preparation for roads, paving, foundations, and for pipe and cable bedding.
h. Excavation and backfill requirements for underground installations.
i. Final grading and landscaping.
j. Control of earthworks.
k. Scour protection, coastal defence and shoreline protection works.
l. Erosion protection for slopes and embankments.
m. Stability of slopes.
n. Ground improvement.
o. Road embankments – specific requirements.
p. Tanks foundations and tank bunds - specific requirements.
q. Geosynthetics.
r. Particular geotechnical conditions.
1.1.2 The specification does not cover the following activities:
a. Determining the required layout, spacing and sizing of tank bunds. This is covered in AGES-GL-03-
001 ‘Facility Layout & Separation Distances Guidelines’.
b. Land reclamation works.
c. Permanent site drainage. These requirements are covered in AGES-SP-01-007 ‘Drainage Systems
Design and Construction Specification’.
d. Permanent roads, paving and gravel surface areas. These requirements are covered in AGES-SP-
01-005 ‘Roads and Paving Design and Construction Specification’.
e. This Specification does not cover climatic conditions outside of the UAE and Arabian Gulf region.
f. Geotechnical investigation requirements. These are covered by the Specification for Onshore
Geotechnical Investigation.

foundations, tank bund construction and all associated works (onsite & offsite).
It also includes the requirements for all underground installations (onsite & offsite) such as structural
foundations, drainage structures, underground pipes, duct banks, conduits, and cables (including
electrical, instrument, communication, etc.).

DEWATERING
De-watering Requirements
a. Excavations shall be kept free from water at all times.
b. Surface water shall be prevented from flowing into excavations by installing ditches, trenches,
protective swales, temporary channels or other approved measures as described in Section 9.4.
c. Excavations for foundations and other underground installations shall not be used as temporary
drainage ditches.
d. Where bulk or local excavation will extend below the ground water table, the water table shall be
lowered to at least 0.5m below the deepest excavation level. Lowering of the water table shall be
carried out from a height of not less than 0.5m above the water table so that at no time shall there
be a positive hydraulic gradient into the excavation. This is required to prevent groundwater
seepage through the base or sides of the excavation which may lead to loosening of subgrade soils,
loss of fines, development of boils, development of quick conditions, slope instability and slumping,
external soil erosion and internal piping erosion.
e. The ground water table shall be continuously maintained (24 hours a day, 7 days a week) at a depth
of at least 0.5m below the excavation bottom until all foundations and utilities are constructed and
the excavation backfilled.
f. The design of the dewatering system shall allow for seasonal variations in ground water levels and
water flows, and for sudden changes in levels and flows that occur during storm conditions. Any
damage caused by inadequacy or failure of the dewatering system shall be rectified by
CONTRACTOR at no additional cost to COMPANY.
g. A contingency plan shall be developed that addresses the failure of any critical component of the
dewatering system.

existing structures and services, nor disturb or alter the condition of the in-situ soils.

approval. The design shall indicate clearly:

De-watering of Trenches by Over-excavation
a. Where appropriate, pipe and cable trenches may be de-watered by over-excavation as described
below, subject to COMPANY approval. This method may not be suitable where the trench is
excavated within unstable soils:
i. The trench shall be over-excavated to 150 mm deeper than the specified depth.
ii. Well-graded gravel or crushed stone, wrapped entirely within an approved non-woven
geotextile material, shall be placed in the bottom of the trench, 300 mm below and directly
underneath the pipe or cable, in lieu of the specified bedding material.
iii. The well-graded gravel or crushed stone shall meet the requirements of ASTM C33 gradation
67 (19.0 – 4.75 mm). The average wear of the aggregates shall not exceed 40 %. The test for

Fill Placement and Compaction
a. It shall be CONTRACTOR’s responsibility to place, spread, moisture condition, and compact the fill
in strict accordance with this Specification.
b. Superficial loose and unsuitable materials shall be removed from the area to be graded to receive
fill.
c. Where significant depths of loose material extend below ground water level, the method of treatment
of such material, if any, shall be proposed by CONTRACTOR for COMPANY approval.
d. The exposed surface of existing natural soils shall be scarified to 150 mm depth, moisture
conditioned to ± 2 % of optimum and compacted to a density of not less than 95 % of the modified
Proctor maximum dry density (ASTM D1557).
e. Where excavation is within bedrock, bottom surfaces shall be levelled smooth and even. All loose
material and dust shall be removed from the excavation. Rough areas or pockets shall be filled with
mass concrete to bring the surface to a smooth and even level.
f. Filling shall be performed using only suitable fill material as defined in Section 11.3 of the
Specification.
g. Each layer shall be spread evenly in horizontal layers, moisture conditioned as required, and
thoroughly mixed during spreading to obtain uniformity of material and moisture in each layer.
Placing, spreading, controlling the water content and compacting the permanent fill to the required
dry densities shall be in strict accordance with this Specification. Moisture control is critical in hot
climates and the fill should not be allowed to dry out between placement and compaction.
h. The water used for moisture conditioning shall comply with Section 14.2 of this specification.
i. After placing, mixing and spreading each layer, it shall be thoroughly compacted to the specified
dry density (see Section 14.3) using suitable mechanical equipment. Compaction equipment shall
be employed as used in the field compaction trials and shall compact the fill material with the same
number of passes as established in the field compaction trials. Where the width of fill to be
compacted is wider than the width of the roller, the fill shall be compacted in parallel paths, with an
overlap of adjacent trips of not less than 450 mm.
j. Earth moving plant shall not be used as compaction equipment. Compaction by water jetting or
flooding shall not be permitted.
k. Compaction shall be continuous over the entire area and the equipment shall make sufficient
passes to obtain the desired density uniformly. Compaction of a layer shall be completed prior to
placement of the following layer. The layer of fill shall be fully compacted prior to stopping work at
the end of shift.

not be left in the ground.
f. Backfill shall not be placed against substructures or foundations for at least seven days after placing

to membranes shall be repaired prior to start of backfilling.
h. Backfill shall be placed symmetrically to prevent eccentric loading upon or against structures.

substructure. Within these areas CONTRACTOR shall be responsible for selection of appropriate
light compaction plant, combined with thinner compacted layer thicknesses, that shall not damage
nor cause movement of the substructure or foundation.

face, and the space above the chamber roof, shall be backfilled with selected excavated material

STABILITY OF SLOPES
a. The overall stability of slopes shall be determined considering all potential contributory factors which
may include the following (list not exhaustive):
i. Geological profile, including all soil and rock units within and below the slope.
ii. Engineering properties of all soil and rock materials within and below the slope.
iii. Groundwater and surface water conditions, including seasonal variations in groundwater and
surface water elevations.
iv. Groundwater flow and seepage.
v. Slope drainage.
vi. Existing shear zones in soils or rocks.
vii. Long-term and short-term stability of slopes.
viii. Slope stability during seismic shaking.
ix. All potential failure modes (e.g. slip circular failure mode in soil; wedge, planar or toppling failure
modes for cuttings in rock; global and local slope failures; bench failures in rock; etc).
x. Temporary or permanent surcharge at crest of slope.
xi. Temporary or permanent excavations at toe of slope.
xii. Long-term slope erosion leading to progressive deterioration of slope face or undercutting of
slope toe.
xiii. Risk of rockfall.
xiv. Consequences in the event of slope failure
b. Slopes shall achieve the minimum stability requirements required by the project.
c. Where appropriate, measures shall be taken to stabilise the slope in order to achieve the required
minimum stability requirements. This might include changes to the slope geometry, materials or
drainage, or use of retaining systems such as mechanically stabilised earth or retaining walls.
d. CONTRACTOR shall prepare full details of proposed slope stabilisation works, including slope
stability calculations, and submit for COMPANY approval.

ground improvement. After completion of the CPT testing, CONTRACTOR shall submit the CPT

validate the effectiveness of ground improvement.

are lowest. Each Acceptance Area shall be tested with at least one Zone Load Test. If there is
significant variation in the results of the CPT profiles, the number of Zone Load Tests shall be
increased accordingly.
f. In the area of the Zone Load Test, four CPT tests shall be carried out prior to the Zone Load Test
being performed (one in each corner of the test zone).
g. Zone Load Tests shall load a rigid square or rectangular base of minimum 3m by 3m plan dimension
in five equal increments up to 125 % of the design allowable bearing pressure. Each increment shall
be applied for a minimum of 2 hours with the final increment being left for a minimum of 48 hours.
Settlements shall be recorded at each corner of the rigid base. Upon completion of the test the rigid
base shall be unloaded and the recovery recorded.

Tank Foundation Functions and Requirements
23.2.1 Tank Foundation
a. The following functions of a tank foundations shall be met:
i. To spread and transfer the load from the tank and its contents via the tank foundation base and
shoulder to the subgrade so that the resulting settlements, both total and differential, remain
within the allowable limits.
ii. To raise the tank bottom above ground water, capillary water, surface water and the standing
fluid level resulting from minor spillages.
iii. To provide a smooth surface with sufficient bearing capacity and stability for tank construction
and operation.
iv. To channel rainwater away from the tank shell and tank bottom quickly.
v. To provide a raised elevation to the tank base to enhance the NPSH (net positive suction head)
of pumps.
23.2.2 Shoulder of Tank Foundation
a. The following requirements for the shoulder of tank foundations shall be met:
i. To provide sufficient lateral support to the tank foundation during construction, operation and
maintenance activities.
ii. To resist edge settlement (cutting) beneath the tank shell.
iii. To resist wash-out of the tank foundation as a result of tank bottom leakages or possible ingress
of rainwater.
iv. To resist erosion by wind and/or water.

Tank Foundation Finishes
a. To minimize future corrosion problems and maximize the effect of corrosion prevention systems
such as cathodic protection, the material in contact with the tank bottom shall be fine and uniform.
Gravel or large particles shall be avoided.
b. Clean washed sand 75mm to 100mm deep shall be used as the top surface finishing layer because
it can be readily shaped to the bottom contour of the tank to provide maximum contact area, and
will protect the tank bottom from coming into contact with large particles and debris.
c. Large foreign objects or point contact by gravel or rocks shall be avoided since they could lead to
development of corrosion cells that cause pitting and premature tank bottom failure.
d. During construction the movement of equipment and materials across the grade will mar the graded
surface. These irregularities shall be corrected before bottom plates are placed for welding.
e. Adequate provisions, such as making size gradients in sublayers progressively smaller from bottom
to top, shall be made to prevent the fine materials from leaching down into the larger material, thus
negating the effect of using the fine material as a final layer. This is particularly important for the top
of a crushed rock ringwall.
f. The final levels of the placed and compacted surface finishes under the tank shall comply with the
requirements of the tank design specification (BS EN 14015 or API 650).
g. The surface finish to the shoulder shall have a gradient slope of 1V:10H from the underside of the
annular plate of the tank in accordance with standard drawing ‘Typical Tank Foundations, Bund
Wall and Internal Bund Finish’ to avoid ingress of water under the tank after some tank settlement
has occurred.

a. The settlement of tank foundations (both total and differential settlement) shall not exceed the

Special Tank Foundations
23.10.1 Concrete Foundation Ring Under the Tank Shell
a. A concrete foundation ring under a tank shell shall be used only where it is required to provide a
downward force to compensate for wind and the uplift caused by internal pressure (e.g. for butt-
welded high-pressurised dome-roof tanks).

that may produce unacceptably large differential settlements over very short distances and
associated cracking of the base plates through which product leakage could occur.
c. If the calculated settlements (total or differential) exceed more stringent of the limits specified in
AGES-SP-01-003 ‘Structural Design Basis-Onshore Specification’ or VENDOR stipulated values,
concrete raft or piled foundations shall be used.
d. If concrete foundation rings are used the top of the ring shall be sealed to prevent water ingress
below the tank base, as shown on standard drawing ‘Typical Tank Foundations, Bund Wall and
Internal Bund Finish’.
e. The number of anchor bolts shall be determined by carrying out a stability calculation.

shall be suitably sealed and free to expand and contract. Sufficient free space shall be provided

sealing material between the pipe and sleeve shall be malleable to remain functional in the event
of movements. Where flammable materials are stored within the bund, penetration sealing shall be
fire resistant with metal protection plates to the inside (tank side) face.

GUIDELINES ON FIELD COMPACTION TRIALS
A1.1. A test area 20 m long by 15 m wide should be prepared on the actual site of construction from which
the topsoil has been removed. The fill material to be used should then be spread over this area in three
strips 5 m wide, the depth of the loose material in the strips being varied over the range of thicknesses.
Normally, the range of values investigated would be in the range 150 mm to 450 mm. No adjustment
should be made to the moisture content of the fill material that, apart from minor fluctuations, should be
in its natural moisture condition.
A1.2. The test area should then be compacted with the plant it has been decided to use and the mean bulk
density, moisture content and dry density of the full depth of each strip should be determined after 2, 4
and 8 passes for each types of roller (except for sheepsfoot rollers, for which the density and moisture
content determinations should be made after 4, 8 and 16 passes). The densities of the soil should be
determined by the core-cutter, sand replacement, gamma radiation or Nuclear Density Gauge (NDG),
whichever is most suitable, and the mean of five determinations should be obtained for each soil
condition. NDG shall be combined with more robust physical testing (sand replacement, core cutter or
water replacement tests) to ensure the NDG is accurate and reliable. Samples of the fill material should
be taken and the modified Proctor maximum dry density (ASTM D1557) and optimum moisture content
determined.
A1.3. This procedure should be repeated at two other moisture contents, the values suggested being the
optimum moisture content given by ASTM D 1557 and an intermediate moisture content between this
value and the natural moisture content of the soil. If the natural moisture content of the soil is similar to
the optimum moisture content, it is suggested that the additional tests be conducted at moisture contents
2 % on either side of this value. These tests should be conducted with fresh fill material laid on an
adjacent area from which the topsoil has been removed.
A1.4. The dry density and moisture content test results should be compared against the maximum dry density
that is achieved at the optimum moisture content, to determine the percentage of compaction achieved
in the construction trial. This degree of compaction should be compared with the requirements of this
Specification to ensure compliance.
A1.5. Soil resistance to penetration can be measured to verify the compaction trials, using Cone Penetration
Test (CPT). CPT measurements should have associated water pressure measurements.
A1.6. The test procedure, which has been described above in detail, can usually be considerably shortened
by noting the trend of the early results, and by the experience of the engineers carrying out the tests.
These trials take about a week to complete and can be started as soon as the necessary plant arrives
on the site, while preliminary clearing operations are in progress. The trials can thus be completed
before the main construction starts.
A1.7. From the results of the trials and knowledge of the costs of the various procedures, it is possible to
determine the most economical procedure to be adopted to obtain the specified degree of compaction,
and the subsequent control work can then be finalised.

A2.5. Settlement Behaviour During Testing
A2.5.1 General
a. The measured daily settlement rate during the equilibration and monitoring period between the
various stages and during the period after the tank is filled (i.e. after completion of Stage 4) is
expected to diminish with time (i.e. the settlement rate on the second day is expected to be less
than that of the first day).
b. In the event of a static or increased settlement rate during the above-mentioned equilibration and
monitoring periods there is a danger of instability. In such a case the following actions shall be taken:
i. COMPANY shall be informed and the tank partly emptied to achieve a stable and safe condition.
ii. A geotechnical engineer and tank specialist should be consulted.
A2.5.2 Floating Roof Tanks
a. Floating roofs are equipped with rim seals that can compensate the out-of-roundness of the tank
shell. Out-of-roundness of the tank shell may occur due to:
i. Limits in accuracy of construction. A tank shell could easily show an out-of-roundness up to
approximately 50 mm (depending on diameter) due to practical limits of accuracy during
construction.
ii. Out-of-roundness caused by uneven soil settlement. In one direction the shell will increase in
diameter, in the other direction it will decrease in diameter.
b. When the ultimate outward movement of the seal is reached, a gap opens between the seal plates
and the shell. At other locations the ultimate inward movement of the seal may be reached, which
may result in the roof becoming stuck so that the roof seal is damaged.
A2.6. Evaluation of Tank Settlement Characteristics
A2.6.1 Compliance with the above recommended procedures and this Specification will theoretically ensure
that some of the maximum overall tank settlement will occur during the hydrostatic testing period.
