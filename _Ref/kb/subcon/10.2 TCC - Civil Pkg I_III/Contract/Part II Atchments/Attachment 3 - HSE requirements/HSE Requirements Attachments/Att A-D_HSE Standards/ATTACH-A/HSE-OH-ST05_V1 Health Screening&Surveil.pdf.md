INTRODUCTION
This Occupational Health standard provides requirement of conducting risk based health screening and
surveillance. Linking of health status risk identified as an outcome of health screening and/ surveillance
to the identified Similar Exposure Groups (SEGs) as an outcome of Occupational Health Risk
Assessment (OHRA) (Qualitative and/ Quantitative) risk rating among employees and contractors are
also addressed.
This Occupational Health standard provides best practice to ADNOC Group and Contractors to enable
them to implement reliable and effective risk based health screening and surveillance program so as to
ensure healthy workforce across ADNOC Group.
PURPOSE
This Standard specifies ‘mandatory requirements of Occupational Health Screening and Surveillance to
be conducted by ADNOC Group Companies and their contractors to ensure that the occupational health
risks [risks arising of individual health status and hazards (physical, chemical, biological, ergonomic &
psychosocial)] are adequately managed at a level which is As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP).
This Standard provides information to HSE Department within ADNOC Group Companies and its
Contractors, to enable informed decisions to be made regarding quantitative assessment, as well as the
extent of occupational exposure to hazardous agents.
SCOPE
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates &
Functions at HQ, Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors.
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood,
implemented and thoroughly monitored.

HEALTH SURVEILLANCE
Health Surveillance is defined as an assessment of an employee's health using medical or biological
procedures (biological monitoring and biological effect monitoring) to identify any significant
abnormalities attributed to exposure to hazardous agents, at as early stage as possible. Health
Surveillance can only be done when:
(i) The available procedures used shall be as far as possible of acceptably high sensitivity,
specificity and predictive value in detecting abnormalities related to the nature and degree
of exposure;
(ii) The criteria for interpreting the data should be known and the procedures should be safe,
easy to perform, preferably non-invasive and acceptable to the employees;
(iii) Surveillance shall only be undertaken:
1. if the possible detected changes are reversible or measures are available to prevent
their further development; and
2. if it is reasonably likely that the adverse effect will occur given the prevailing work
conditions.
Health surveillance is important for [Ref 17]:
(i) Detecting ill-health effects at an early stage, so employers can introduce better controls to
prevent them getting worse;
(ii) Providing data to help employers evaluate health risks;
(iii) Enabling employees to raise concerns about how work affects their health;
(iv) Highlighting lapses in workplace control measures, therefore providing invaluable feedback
to the risk assessment; and
(v) Providing an opportunity to reinforce training and education of employees (e.g. on the impact
of health effects and the use of protective equipment).
Risk based periodic health surveillance examinations are hazard specific and shall include at an interval
history, a physical examination, and / or clinical and laboratory screening test from accredited
laboratories, medical questionnaires based on workplace requirements and demands. Risk based
periodic health surveillance examinations shall be carried out for employees to ensure that they remain
fit for their job and that their health has not been adversely affected by their work.
HSP shall be established to include the requirements for hazards specific health screening and
surveillance (refer to Appendix 3) apart from workplace hazard exposure history and relevant body
system examinations [refer to health questionnaire forms (but not limited to) in Appendix 5 Hazard
Specific Health Questionnaire] for relevant employees as applicable.
Health surveillance examination shall be conducted with appropriate frequency based on the following:
1. as per Table 7.2.1 below which links to the outcome (risk rating categories) of the OHRA -Job
Title Specific SEG based;
2. six monthly as minimum as per UAE Law for specific occupational exposures [Ref 13]; and
3. as per recommendation from ADNOC GMSD/ ADNOC Group Contractor Clinics following fitness
medical examinations (includes assessing employee’s personal health risk factors which make
the individual susceptible/vulnerable to aggravate the health condition while working for the SEG
specific job title) as per ADNOC Group Medical Fitness Guidelines - AHQ-HCA-GSA-GID-003-
R00-19 dated 15 Jul 2019 [Ref. 7].

Biological monitoring is the measurement and assessment of chemicals or their metabolites (substances
the body converts the chemical into) in exposed workers. These measurements are made on samples
of breath, urine or blood, or any combination of these. Biological monitoring measurements reflect the
total uptake of a chemical by an individual by all routes (inhalation, ingestion, through the skin or by a
combination of these routes).
Biological effect monitoring is the measurement and assessment of early biological effects caused by
absorption of chemicals. It normally involves measuring biochemical responses (for example, measuring
plasma and erythrocyte cholinesterase activity in workers exposed to organophosphorus pesticides; or
measuring increases in urinary protein following exposure to cadmium). These responses may have
potential health implications for the individual, and may arise from causes other than occupational
exposure. Consequently, biological effect monitoring should always be carried out with the close
involvement of an occupational health physician.

APPENDIX 2 ADNOC LIST OF OCCUPATIONAL ILLNESSES
Occupational diseases or illnesses are the health conditions (mostly chronic) that occur as a result of work or are associated with an occupational [i.e. work related]
activity. Other types of occupational hazards that are “traumatic” or “acute” (e.g. falls, slips, trips, cuts, burns, or animal bites & stings) cause injury but are not strictly
categorised as “occupational diseases”, even though they occur at the workplace. In the main, these acute conditions are known as “occupational injuries”.
An “occupational disease” or “illness” is identified when it is shown by medical diagnosis and tests that it is more prevalent in a given population of workers than in the
general population, or indeed in other worker populations.
