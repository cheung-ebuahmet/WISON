1 PROJECT DESCRIPTION
1.1 Introduction
The Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 2 (RSHT-2) is owned by ADNOC and operated by
ADNOC Gas (AG) and is located approximately 240 km west-southwest of Abu Dhabi City in
the Ruwais Industrial Area. RSHT-2 facility is designed to handle bulk granulated sulphur
coming from Shah and Habshan plant via railcars and transported via new federal railway
(Union Railway) to Ruwais Terminal (SHT-2).
Based on ADNOC recent directives, the Sulphur Handling Scenario at Ruwais SHT-2 (Ruwais
Sulphur Granulation Plant-RSGP) will be developed further due to consideration of receiving
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur from Hail & Ghasha to the new plant, via pipeline. This new plant
will be located to the East of the BOROUGE terminal-3 & to the west of RSHT-2 as depicted
in Fig.1.1 below.
Figure 1: New RSGP Site Location near Existing RSHT-2 Facility
1.2 Project Objective and Scope
The primary objective of the RSGP Development project at RSHT-2 is to accommodate
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur received via pipeline from the Hail & Ghasha OPP. This involves
constructing new infrastructure for liquid sulphur receiving storage and transfer, sulphur
forming (Granulation), Granulated sulphur transfer and storage, stacking and reclaiming and
export to existing ship loaders. The project also includes implementing dust control systems,
sulphur remelting unit, utility systems and integration with existing facilities. The developed
facility aims to ensure efficient and environmentally sound sulphur processing, support
ADNOC's sulphur production, and maintain operational safety and reliability.

8.1 Waste Management Strategy
The Project works will be executed in stages: early works, peak construction activities, finishing
works, and commissioning phase. The type and volume of waste generated will vary
depending on the stage of work, number of personnel, and equipment in operation.
Early works: Waste generation will be relatively low, primarily from civil works, site preparation,
and establishment of temporary facilities. Wastes will consist largely of non-hazardous
materials.
Peak construction period: The largest volume of waste will be generated during this stage,
predominantly non-hazardous waste such as timber, excavated materials, ferrous scrap,
municipal-type waste (e.g., food waste, office and camp waste). Waste volumes will directly
correlate with workforce size.
Finishing and closing works: Waste will mainly consist of materials from painting, insulation,
and demolition/decommissioning of temporary facilities.
Pre-Commissioning & Commissioning phase: Generation of hazardous waste (e.g.,
flushing oils, chemicals, catalysts) is expected during system testing and commissioning.
A Waste Management Planning Worksheet (Annexure-1) shall be used to forecast, monitor,
and control waste streams at each stage.
A Waste Manifest Template shall be maintained to track waste handling, transportation, and
final disposal.
With due consideration of these developments of the Project, the overall waste management
strategy is based on the principles that waste generation shall be minimized wherever possible
and that waste materials shall be managed as close to the source as practicable.

8.6 Waste Storage
The following conditions shall apply to the storage and handling of waste containers at the
Project site:
 Hazardous waste shall only be stored in designated hazardous waste storage areas;
Hazardous waste shall only be stored at dedicated on-site facilities provided by approved
waste transport provider.
 All waste containers shall be clearly labelled or color-coded to identify the waste type.
 Labels must, at a minimum, indicate the nature of the hazard (e.g., corrosive, flammable,
toxic); the required PPE for handling; and the emergency actions to be taken in case of
leaks or spills.
 All hazardous waste containers must display the relevant hazard label in accordance with
regulatory standards.
 An appropriate MSDS shall be maintained by the Contractor HSE Department for all
hazardous wastes stored on site.
 Each waste type shall be stored in a manner that minimizes risks to health, safety, and
the environment.
 CONTRACTOR must comply with applicable local and international standards and be
suitable for the chemical and physical properties of the waste.
 Adequate containers shall be provided by CONTRACTOR and its Subcontractors to safely
store their own waste.
 CONTRACTOR shall ensure that all selected container and storage types are in
conformance with applicable local standards and regulatory requirements.
 CONTRACTOR tor shall maximize the use of returnable containers for hazardous
materials to reduce single-use waste.
 Hazardous material containers shall be suitable for decontamination and reuse (e.g., rinse
and refill), thereby minimizing packaging waste.
 CONTRACTOR shall decontaminate hazardous materials containers by full drainage and
triple rinsing, ensuring all rinse residues are properly contained and treated.
 Containers and storage materials shall be selected based on their potential for local
recycling or reuse. Ferrous and non-ferrous metals are expected to provide the greatest
recycling potential, followed by labeled plastics (PET, HDPE) and glass.
 Use of multi-material containers shall be minimized to facilitate recycling.
 Waste containers shall be designed and maintained to prevent access by vermin or
scavenging animals.

The transportation of waste shall be managed in a safe, compliant, and environmentally
responsible manner. The following requirements apply:
CONTRACTOR shall transport all non-hazardous waste to the location designated by the

movements, and the use of designated vehicles or sealed containers.
CONTRACTOR shall engage only COMPANY-approved and regulator-approved
Environmental Service Providers (ESPs) who are licensed by TADWEER to operate in the

Form (Waste Manifest, template attached as ATTACHMENT 03). Transportation agents must
have demonstrable experience and knowledge of the waste materials being transported.
CONTRACTOR shall submit details of such agents to the COMPANY prior to assigning work.
SUBCONTRACTORS shall ensure that all waste transportation is carried out safely and
responsibly. As a minimum, SUBCONTRACTORS must:
 Prohibit the overloading of vehicles.
 Provide adequate containment for waste during transport, including covers for open trucks.
 Ensure drivers are trained and fully aware of the hazards associated with the waste being
transported.
 Provide drivers with appropriate PPE and, where necessary, a spill containment kit, which
must include absorbent pads, rolls, or granules; two heavy-duty plastic sacks;
impermeable gloves; and shovel.
 Ensure vehicles transporting hazardous waste are clearly marked with the relevant hazard
classification (e.g., flammable, corrosive, toxic) and carry a copy of the relevant SDS.
 Provide facilities to maintain the cleanliness of vehicles leaving the site, thereby
minimizing the risk of waste deposition on public roads.
