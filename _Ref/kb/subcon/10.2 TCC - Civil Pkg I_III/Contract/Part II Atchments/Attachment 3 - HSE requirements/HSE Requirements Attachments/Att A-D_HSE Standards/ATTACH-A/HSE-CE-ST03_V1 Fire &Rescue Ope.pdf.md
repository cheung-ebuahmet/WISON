1 INTRODUCTION
ADNOC Group is committed to having a world-class emergency response provision, in line with its
Corporate HSE Policy.
The requirements defined in this Standard will enable Group Companies and SPC Affiliates achieve the
corporate objective if strict, disciplined and sustained compliance is ensured and alignment with the
prescribed generic framework for fire and rescue operations is adopted and customized across the
organization.
Stipulated requirements based on international standards and industry best practices shall be complied
with to ensure an optimal level of emergency preparedness is in place at each operating site, tailored to
handle the specific incident scenarios, based on facility/plant risk profile.
ADNOC philosophy for fire and rescue operations shall follow the below process.
IDENTIFY FIRE & RESCUE HAZARDS
DEVELOP FIRE & RESCUE SCENARIOS
EVALUATE ESCALATION CONSEQUENCES
SELECT SCENARIOS FOR FU RTHER ANALYSIS
PREPARE PROVISIONAL RESPONSE PHILOSOPHY
COMPARE DETAILED RESPONSE RESOURCE OPTIONS
SELECT COST EFFECTIVE OPTIONS
FINALIZE RESPONSE FIRE & RESCUE PHILOSOPHY
PROVIDE RESPONSE RESOURCES
DEFINE PRE-PLAN TO US E RESOURCES
TEST RESOUR CES
MAINTAIN RESO URCES
REVIEW/UPDATE FIRE & RESCU E RESPONSE PHILOSOPHY

2 PURPOSE
This Standard is aimed at ensuring set out mandated requirements, which if complied with should ensure
that Fire and Rescue operations will be, at all times, safely, effectively and efficiently implemented by
competent and suitably equipped Tactical Response Teams (TRT).
It shall apply as a minimum but not limited to the following facilities/phase
Onshore
Offshore, (including natural and artificial islands)
Project Phases (i.e. Conceptual Stage, FEED Stage, Detailed Engineering etc.)
Emergency scenarios necessitating the initiation of site Tactical Response Teams have
been identified and listed in the facility hazard and effect risk register.
It does not apply to floating offshore facilities such as Floating Production Storage & Offloading [FPSO],
Floating Storage & Offloading [FSO] or work barges/marine vessels of any type.
The Fire and Rescue emergency scenarios shall not be limited to the first level of the tiered response
model thus including scenarios with potential severity classified as Major, Catastrophic or Disastrous as
per ADNOC Corporate Risk Matrix [Ref. 20].
3 SCOPE
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates &
Functions at HQ, Group Companies and SPC Affiliates) and its Contractors.
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood,
implemented and thoroughly monitored. Although NFPA Codes and other Standards are referenced
throughout this document, ADNOC recognizes the adoption and implementation of other internationally
accepted standards and best practices aligned to Group Company Fire Service requirements. ADNOC
accepts the use of such referenced standards/practices where applicable to reflect the needs of the
concerned Group Companies, (in line with the provision of evidence to support the implementation and
intent of said standards / practices and where a higher standard is demonstrated by the implementation
of any deviation, e.g. OPITO, British Fire Service, etc.).
4 LAWS AND REGULATIONS
Relevant legislations applicable to this Standard includes but not limited to:
UAE Federal Law No. 8 (1980), Federal law No (23) of 2006, pertaining to Civil Defence.
Federal Law Ministerial law No .24 (2012), pertaining fines & violations.
Group Companies shall ensure that their activities comply with all relevant Federal and Abu Dhabi laws
and regulations at all times, including any that maybe introduced after the publication of this Standard.

Speed & Weight of Attack The speed and weight of emergency response is dictated by the nature
of the risk; potential life loss, environmental impacts, incident escalation
and impact on ADNOC’s reputation for identified emergency events.
These are based on risk assessments of credible scenarios and lead to
site based requirements for the number of emergency response
resources required to effectively deal with the incident (Weight) and the
response time to be met to mitigate the impacts of these emergency
events (Speed)

at an incident and at regular intervals thereafter.

hazard area and thereby, exposed to greater risk.
Defensive Mode: Crews are outside of the designated hazard area.

7.1 OVERVIEW
ADNOC Emergency and Crisis Management Standard [Ref. 21], stipulates having in place a level of
emergency preparedness with capabilities and resources sufficient to respond to all worst case credible
scenarios identified and listed in site risk registers.
The core values of ADNOC fire and rescue tactical response teams shall be to protect people, assets,
the environment and company reputation, through optimal preparedness and readiness to respond safely,
and effectively.
The effectiveness of fire & rescue operations shall rely not only on the core ability of the TRT with respect
to tactical and operational aspects but also and very importantly on the availability and appropriateness
of the resources that are needed to mitigate the negative consequences of each identified incident
scenario.
Such level of emergency preparedness across the whole organization necessitates a common framework
that will ensure consistency of approach, interoperability between sites and fit for purpose of the
arrangements needed to deliver, at all times, a best in class Fire and Rescue emergency intervention.
All GC’s and SPC Affiliates shall align with the following common framework for fire & rescue operations
while making sure site specific needs assessment in terms of organizational structures are carried out in

skills and experience.

paramedics as well as other trained and certified personnel.
Auxiliary Fire and Rescue Personnel
Direct support to and form part of TRT’s, auxiliary fire and rescue personnel are an essential and
integral part of the front-line support on the emergency incident ground and form support to Fire &
rescue emergency response arrangements.

7.2.4 TOTAL RESPONSE TIME
In alignment with NFPA 1710 Standard (Standard for the Organization and Deployment of Fire
Suppression Operations, Emergency Medical Operations, and Special Operations to the Public by Career
Fire Departments), which defines Total Response Time to be from the moment the emergency notification
is acknowledged to the moment the first resource arrives at the scene; the time objective for all ADNOC
Group ERT’s / OERT’s / Fire & Rescue Teams shall be targeted to be 480 seconds. This targeted time
is an average benchmarked response time that is subject to site/risk specific assessment which may
decrease or increase the total response time based on risk to life and potential for incident escalation.
Aiming to achieve the above time objective, each site/location “turnout time” shall be determined using a
holistic approach that takes into account the identified specific scenarios.
A holistic approach shall be followed, as illustrated in below Figure 7.2.1 which covers:
Call handling time – defined as the minutes and seconds from the time of call to the time that a
responding station is alerted. The operator may stay on the line after the station is alerted to gather
additional information or provide advice and reassurance as necessary. This extra time is not
included in the call handling time calculations. (This shall not exceed 30 seconds – 90% KPi target).
Crew turnout time – defined as the minutes and second from the time the station is alerted to the
time the first vehicle departs.
Drive time – defined as the minutes and seconds from the first vehicle to leave a station to the first
vehicle to arrive at the scene of the incident. Note that these may not necessarily be the same
vehicle.

The fire and rescue tasks performed in the upstream and downstream sectors within ADNOC Group have

These operations are dynamic in nature and require in many cases calculated risk taking and structured
command to prevent the realization of potentially serious outcomes to both casualties and rescuers.
Dynamic Risk Assessment (DRA) concept shall be introduced and TRT members educated on related

the immediate hazards;

7.3.3 WORST CASE CREDIBLE SCENARIOS (WCS)
A worst-case credible scenario is a concept in risk management wherein the planner, in planning for
potential disasters, considers the most severe possible outcome that can reasonably be projected to
occur in a given situation.
GC’s and SPC Affiliates shall follow the process as stated within ADNOC Emergency Response Plan
Standard [Ref. 26] to identify and documenting their site/location specific hazards and associated
scenarios, prioritized for potential severity for the risk level that are falling within the boundary of tactical
response by fire and rescue teams.
GC’s and Affiliates shall identify and describe the most hazardous emergency scenario with the most
severe consequence and highest potential for escalation, for each typical emergency.
The typical worst case scenario shall include the initiating event, its causes (sequence of
failures/deviations/errors), its anticipated development, escalation and the full range of consequences.
Pre-Incident Plans (PIP) shall be established and maintained for each worst case scenario identified.
Worst Case Credible scenarios shall include only the ones classified as notable, major and serious,
against ADNOC Corporate Risk Matrix, which is provided in ADNOC HSE Risk Management System
Standard [Ref. 25].
Hazard identification methodology shall comply with HAZID/ENVID/OHID Standard [Ref. 27].
7.3.4 PRE-INCIDENT PLANNING (PIP)
A Pre-Incident Plan is a document developed by gathering relevant information and data that is used by
emergency response personnel to effectively manage identified emergencies for the protection of people,
the environment, assets, and the ADNOC reputation.
The primary purpose of a pre-incident plan is to assist Emergency Response personnel to effectively
manage and prepare for identified potential emergency events. Pre-planning is key to enhancing the
safety of firefighters and other likely to be affected by emergency response operations. Effective Pre-
Incident Plans guide Fire and Rescue Services to secure suitable and sufficient resources to meet all
identified credible events that they may be called upon to deal with. .
Pre-planning shall be underpinned by risk familiarisation, testing and exercising by Fire &
Rescue/Emergency Response staff.
Each identified scenario shall require the development of a corresponding Pre-Incident Plan (PIP).
Scenarios are not limited to the Worst Case Credible Events and as good practice PIP’s shall be
developed for ALL Major Accident Hazards (MAH) identified in COMAH studies as a minimum and
consider risk identified in site specific Risk Register to ensure that Fire & Rescue teams are correctly
resourced and prepared for potential incidents they may be called upon to respond.
PIP’s covering fire and rescue scenarios shall be developed following a methodology that is aligned with
the criteria defined in NFPA 1620 Standard for Pre-Incident Planning.
ADNOC HSE and Fire & Rescue SME’s shall prescribe the methodology to be used and data to be
collected/utilized by GC’s and SPC Affiliates when developing their site specific PIP’s, taking into account
the location constraints and limitations, as applicable.

7.3.5 STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES (SOP’S)
The use or well defined, tactically proven SOP’s has been demonstrated to play a fundamental role in
enhancing the effectiveness of on the scene fire and rescue operations. The purpose of SOPs is wide
ranging they help to identify the tactical goals of the TRT, set the expectations of response personnel,
provide administrative and operational benchmarks. They define how emergency intervention operations
are to be conducted, establish job performance requirements. They ensure consistency between differing
TRT / emergency response teams.
Although the dynamics that cause situations on the ground to evolve rapidly and require TRT leaders to
adjust or change their tactical plans, it is vital that fire and rescue team have as much information as
possible, in the form of SOP’s and other associated documents, before they reach the location .
SOP’s thus play a central role in TRT’s preparedness by ensuring necessary details are covered for all
site specific emergency scenarios that have been identified.
All GC’s shall ensure that all their fire and rescue SOP’s include the risk assessed tactics and related
tasks applicable to the location where intervention will take place, as well as the required equipment and
tools that have to be used.
SOP’s and other associated documents shall form the basis upon which TRT’s training takes place to
save crucial time during the initial minutes on the fire and rescue emergency scene.

7.3.6 TACTICAL AIDE GUIDE
Upon arrival at an incident scene the first task of the TRT /ERT/OERT Leader shall be to gather all
available information relating to the incident. To assist with this process information shall be obtained at
the pre-planning stage and available on Tactical Aide Guides (TAG). The TAG will identify such
information as but not limited to; Life Risk, Hazards, Firefighting Strategies and Initial Actions.
This is in addition to the information received from the caller or detection system received on route or
passed on by persons already in attendance e.g. Field Operators, Technicians etc.
TRT / ERT / OERT Leader shall apply professional judgment in conjunction with the TAG and / or other
PIP(s) to decide the most appropriate course of action, weighing the consequences of proceeding with a
task against the benefits likely to be gained. It is important to “think before you act than act before you
think”. The consequences of a wrong decision at this stage may be irreversible.
Note: The decision making process shall be in line with the principles of DRA (Refer Section 7.3.1).

7.4.3 DUAL AGENT, (HYDROCHEM, CHEMCORE, ETC.)
Dual Agents are solutions of water mixed with potassium acetate, potassium carbonate, potassium citrate
or combinations thereof, having superior cooling capabilities, which keep temperatures below the auto
ignition point, to achieve complete extinguishment. Twin-agent technology combines the fast flame
knockdown of a dry chemical with the fire securing capabilities of aqueous film-forming foam. The twin-
agent attack provides the most effective extinguishing and securing capability known for many flammable
liquid fires which are of benefit to the Oil and Gas, Petroleum, Petrochemical, marine and aviation.
The specification, selection, care, and maintenance of each type shall comply with relevant international
standards and manufacturers recommendations, as determined by ADNOC Fire & Rescue SME’s.
7.4.4 WATER
Water is used as a firefighting media to extinguish fires by cooling, using its ability to absorb massive
amounts of heat, while converting from the liquid phase to the vapour phase. Without heat the fuel
combustion cannot be sustained.
Water also provides smothering effect as its vapour dilute the oxygen in the immediate atmosphere, thus
removing the key elements required for combustion.

7.5.2 DETERMINATION OF STAFFING ESTABLISHMENT LEVELS
The minimum crew level shall be determined by the ridership factor (i.e. considering surplus crew in case
of any absence). The ridership factor shall be used by all Group Companies to calculate how many
firefighters the Group Company should employ at each and every fire station based on standard crewing
levels needed to maintain 24/7 emergency cover. e.g. for a standard crew of 5 to be maintained 24 hours
a day; the Group Company would need to multiply 5 by a ridership factor which is calculated by taking
into account the predictable unavailability of firefighters over an average year. Losses from operational
crewing incurred from sickness, attending training courses, leave, National Service, and other authorized
absences are all used in the equation to calculate the ridership factor. Typically, an average Ridership
factor would be 1.45 to account for these identified absences, however each Group Company should
calculate absenteeism levels within their organization to produce their own specific Ridership factor.
Determination of staff establishment levels to maintain the identified minimum crew levels: Using the
example of a minimum crew level of 5 and applying the ridership factor of 1.45 will define an organisational
staffing level of 7:
Example:
Minimum Crew level = 5 (As defined through pre –incident planning requirements)
Shift Pattern = 12 hour rotation
Shift Rotation = 4 on – 4 Off
Ridership Factor = 1.45 (example)
Staff number per shift required = 5 x 1.45 = 7
Staff number per station required = 7 x 4 = 28*
*Operational Response Firefighters excluding station administration, training and station management
personnel.
Note – Ridership factors should be reviewed and adjusted if required annually to ensure that the
multiplying factor is still applicable and provides the numbers of staff required.

7.7.1 FIRE STATION
This Standard sets out requirements for ADNOC Group and SPC Affiliates’ fire station buildings, which
shall be located in unclassified/safe areas, away from any potential exposure to risk and as close as
reasonably practicable to the fire control area where additional personnel are available.
ADNOC fire stations shall be classified into only one category, which takes into consideration all the risk
factors that relate to:
Size of the site/area covered;
Combined fire and emergency factors;
Fire protection system installed within the site/area;
Availability of employees trained in firefighting operations;
Availability of other emergency responders; and
Additional services provided by the Fire & Rescue Team i.e. maintenance, inspection and
testing of equipment.
Fire stations shall be available at all operational areas with specific incident scenarios identifying fire
appliances as a mandatory supplement to the active and passive fire protection arrangements.
This includes but not limited to refineries, petrochemical complexes, chemical plants, gas treatment plants,
loading terminals etc. with final decision residing with the Group HSE Emergency Response and Crisis
Management Team.
Each fire station shall have the number of fire truck parking bays that is determined based on needs
assessment by the Group Company Fire & rescue SME’s and assured by an ADNOC Fire & Rescue
SME.
