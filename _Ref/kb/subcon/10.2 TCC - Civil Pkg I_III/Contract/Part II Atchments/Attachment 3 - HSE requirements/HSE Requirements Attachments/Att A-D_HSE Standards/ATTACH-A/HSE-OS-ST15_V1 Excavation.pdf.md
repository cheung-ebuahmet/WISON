1. INTRODUCTION
Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC), as part of its Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) policy,
commits to ensure that a safe working environment is maintained in all areas and that all personnel who
undertake Excavation activity, are fully aware of the hazards involved and have the competence, training
and awareness to undertake such activities.
Excavation work generally means a man-made cut, cavity, trench, or depression in the earth's surface
formed by earth removal irrespective of depth. It includes breaking into ground (including drilling), cutting
into floors, walls, or ceilings and penetrating areas with presence of underground cables or pipes.
Personnel undertaking the activity shall manage risks associated with all kind of excavations at the
workplace with a depth of 1.2 meters and below.
2. PURPOSE
The purpose of this Standard is to provide an overall understanding of activities related to excavations
and its application within the ADNOC Group. This Standard establishes a consistent guideline to the
personnel required for performing excavations in accordance with safe working practices within the
ADNOC Group.
All works related to excavations carried out for ADNOC Group shall be in accordance with the
requirements of this Standard.
3. SCOPE
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates &
Functions at HQ, Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors.
ADNOC Group and its Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood,
implemented and thoroughly monitored
4. LAWS AND REGULATIONS

7. REQUIREMENTS
7.1. OVERVIEW
Excavation includes:
(i) All processes and activities that move or disturb or break ground;
(ii) Any work that involves driving an object into the ground;
(iii) Any other work, which may:
1. Strike, damage, undermine of underground services; and
2. Expose or damage of protective covers, warning tapes or earthing conductors.
Key excavation activities include:
(i) All digging, including ditches, shafts, wells, and trenching or trench filling;
(ii) Grading;
(iii) Tunnelling, boring and /or drilling; and
(iv) Post driving, including earth electrodes, driving of stars pickets and driving of fence posts.
(a) Excavation Plan/Method Statement
It is important to approach each new job with care and preparation irrespective of the number of
past experience with regard to trenching, shoring, and backfilling. The majority of on-the-job
incidents result from inadequate initial planning which leads to slowing down the activity, increase
in the cost of the project, and makes a cave-in or other excavation failure more likely.
All excavations shall be planned and executed only with an excavation certificate in line with the
requirements as per the ADNOC Permit to Work Standard. [Ref. 5]. Wherever a specific excavation
plan and method statement is required the same shall be in line with excavation certificate.
For all excavations works, that requires a method statement, the Job Performer shall consult and
gain written approval of the method statement from the Authorized Civil Personnel. A typical
checklist for Excavation Plan / Method Statement is provided in Appendix 1.
(b) Excavation Prerequisites
All available service location drawings shall be identified and thoroughly examined. Available plans
and location drawings shall not be considered as totally accurate but serve only as an indication of
the likelihood of the presence of services, their location and depth. The total working area shall be
checked on underground service plans and where required shall be scanned using an ADNOC
Group approved Cable Avoidance Tool (CAT) to ascertain the presence of electrical cables and
underground services. Before excavation work is carried out, it shall ensure a competent person
assesses the work requirements for the site.

7.2. HAZARDS ASSOCIATED WITH EXCAVATION
Common hazards associated with excavation works include the following:
(i) Contact with underground services;
(ii) Contact with overhead electrical lines;
(iii) Collapse of excavation’s sides;
(iv) Materials, including spoil, falling onto people working in the excavation;
(v) People and objects falling into the excavation;
(vi) People being struck by mobile excavating equipment;
(vii) The instability of any adjoining structure caused by the excavation;
(viii) Presence of or possible inrush of water or other liquid;
(ix) Undermining adjacent properties/ structures;
(x) Specific hazards associated with confined spaces;
(xi) Asphyxiation from toxic gases;
(xii) Exposure to hazardous substances/contaminated ground; and
(xiii) Water Ingress.
7.3. RISK MANAGEMENT PROCESS
7.3.1. HAZARD IDENTIFICATION
The first step in managing risks associated with the activity is to identify all hazards that could potentially
cause harm to people. These shall be identified by:
(i) Conducting a walk through assessment of the workplace;
(ii) Observing the work and talking to workers about how the activity is carried out;
(iii) Inspecting plant and equipment used during the activity; and
(iv) Reviewing incident reports.

7.3.2. RISK ASSESSMENT
ADNOC Group shall ensure risk assessment for all the work related to excavation which helps to:
(i) Identify which workers are at risk;
(ii) Determine what sources and processes are causing that risk;
(iii) Identify if and what kind of control measures should be implemented; and
(iv) Check the effectiveness of existing control measures.
When assessing the risks associated with excavation, as a minimum the following shall be considered:
(i) Local site conditions, including access, ground slope, adjacent buildings and structures,
water courses (including underground) and trees;
(ii) Depth of the excavation;
(iii) Soil properties, including variable soil types, stability, shear strength, cohesion, presence of
ground water and the effect of exposure to the elements;
(iv) Proximity of live services to the excavation;
(v) The number of people involved;
(vi) The need to enter/work in the excavation;
(vii) The possibility of unauthorised access to the work area;
(viii) Local weather conditions; and
(ix) The length of time the excavation or trench will be open.

7.3.3. HIERARCHY OF CONTROLS
The hierarchy of controls is a systematic approach to managing safety in the workplace by providing a
structure to select the most effective control measures to eliminate or reduce the risk of certain hazards
that have been identified as being caused by the activity. While undertaking a risk assessment for the
activity, the following hierarchy of controls shall be adopted. (Refer to Figure 7.3.1):
Figure 7.3.1: Hierarchy of Controls
(a) Elimination
This includes removing the hazard from the workplace. This is the most effective control measure
and shall always be considered before any other control measure. For example, redesign the job
or substitute a substance so that the hazard is removed or eliminated. If eliminating the hazard is
not reasonably practicable, then substitution, isolation, engineering and procedural control
measures, or a combination of these control measures to minimize the risk shall be considered.
(b) Substitution
Minimize the risk by substituting or replacing a hazard or hazardous work practice with a safer one,
for example using an excavator with rock breaker verses manual method.
(c) Isolate/Separate:
Minimize the risk by isolating or separating the hazard or hazardous work practice from personnel,
for example using concrete barriers to separate pedestrians.
(d) Engineer
This includes engineering the control measures to minimize risk, for example for example benching,
battering or shoring the sides of the excavation to reduce the risk of ground collapse
(e) Organization
This includes organization of personnel trainings and improving competencies for the assigned
task and ensuring effective communication among various parties involved in the assigned task.

(f) Procedures
This includes identification and implementation of documented procedures designed to conduct
the activity in safe manner. For example, reducing the time workers are exposed to hazards i.e. by

(g) Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

7.4. CONTROL MEASURES
7.4.1. GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS
Following are general considerations before the start of excavation work:
(i) Excavation operations shall not commence until everyone involved are aware of the hazards
and what to do in the event of an emergency;
(ii) The location and nature of aboveground and/or underground services;
(iii) Predicted nature of the ground to be excavated;
(iv) Expected presence or absence of water in the ground;
(v) Potential for water to run into the excavation from the surface;
(vi) Stability of underground structures;
(vii) Stages at which timbering or sheet piling shall be placed in the excavation as work proceeds;
(viii) Presence of loose ‘pockets’ in what is otherwise firm, stable ground;
(ix) Nature of any adjacent works and the potential impacts;
(x) Nearness of any buildings or roads and the weight of traffic;
(xi) Whether any other important work is to be carried out adjacent to the proposed excavation;
(xii) Whether any materials are likely to be stacked nearby;
(xiii) Method of excavation and stages of the excavation which are to be dug by machine or by
hand tool;
(xiv) Amount of room which will be needed for raising and lowering material in the course of work;
(xv) Whether the sides of the proposed excavation can be splayed to allow more freedom and
make wedging easier, the period that the excavation will be open, the maximum allowable
trench length and the weather that may be expected;
(xvi) Whether the timbering or sheet piling can be removed safely as the backfilling proceeds;
(xvii) Access to and egress from the excavation;
(xviii) Emergency evacuation from the excavation;
(xix) Requirements for a gas testing programme;
(xx) Sources of soil vibrations (highway traffic, machinery etc.); and
(xxi) Arrangements for temporary site shelters at long-duration excavations in open areas.

7.4.2. EXCAVATION EQUIPMENT REQUIREMENTS
(i) Employees working near operating excavation equipment shall not be permitted to work in
locations that place them in danger of being struck by the moving equipment;
(ii) Excavation equipment shall be selected on the basis of suitability for the planned operations
and the expected site conditions;
(iii) Equipment inspections shall be done on a regular basis and all hired Equipment should be
included in the process;
(iv) All excavating equipment’s shall be inspected and approved by the respective ADNOC
Group;
(v) Only operators who have an authorized driving license and have been confirmed competent
shall be allowed to operate earthmoving equipment; and
(vi) Excavation equipment shall always be guided by banks man/flag man who shall be aware
of the movement and operation of heavy equipment.
7.4.3. CONTROLLING BLIND SPOTS NEAR EXCAVATION EQUIPMENT
A wide range of powered mobile plant including earthmoving machinery may be used for excavation work.
Operators of powered mobile plant can often have severely restricted visibility of ground workers or
nearby pedestrians. Powered mobile plant operating near ground personnel or other powered mobile
plant shall be equipped with warning devices (for example reversing alarm and a revolving light).
An effective system of communication based on two way acknowledgement between mobile plant
operators and Banksman shall be established before work commences. The system should stop ground
workers from approaching mobile plant until the operator has agreed to their request to approach.
Similarly, the system should stop operators from moving plant closer than a set distance from ground
workers until the operator has been advised by ground workers that they are aware of the proposed
movement.

7.4.4. CABLE AVOIDANCE TOOL
Where mechanical digging is to be used, the use of a CAT to identify possible buried services shall be
considered. The use of mechanical diggers or graders or any other mechanical means for the removal of
topsoil or grass and vegetation, prior to digging pilot trenches is strictly forbidden. Use of the CAT shall
be restricted to trained persons only.
The location of any encountered services shall be clearly marked with a red pen and the type of service
(pipe or cable) identified. Scanning shall not be limited to the trench width, but shall include any additional
areas used for drain points, battering, benching etc. Where the excavation is to be greater than 1 metre
width, scanning shall be carried out at both sides of the proposed excavation and at every 5 metres across
the trench.

7.4.6. PERSONNEL CROSSING POINTS
Crossing excavations shall only be permitted at predetermined points. Any gangway, bridge or crossing
point provided shall be suitable for the intended task and its construction and materials used shall be
approved by a competent engineer. Gangways shall be:
(i) Fitted with guard-rails and toe-boards; and
(ii) At least 0.46 m wide for personnel access and 0.6 m wide for personnel and materials
access.
The spacing or location of crossing points should be such that personnel are encouraged to use them,
rather than to attempt other means of crossing the excavation.
7.4.7. GAS TESTING
Excavations can under different circumstances be subject to toxic, asphyxiating, explosive or dusty
atmospheres, which may accumulate at the bottom of an excavation. Excavations deeper than 1.2 meters
in depth; with a width to depth ratio of less 2.0 or presence of hazardous atmosphere where there is a
risk of dangerous dust or fumes or gases being present, the atmosphere shall be sampled prior to entry
into the excavations and then at intervals thereafter as specified by the gas testing programme forming
part of the Method Statement/PTW.
Gas tests carried out and the test equipment shall be:
(i) Suitable for the atmosphere to be tested;

(ii) Of an approved type; and
(iii) Properly calibrated, maintained and supplied with a valid calibration certificate.
As a general rule, tests shall be carried out to identify the following but not limited to:
(i) Oxygen content;
(ii) Airborne concentration of flammable contaminants; and
(iii) Airborne concentration of potentially harmful contaminants (for example, hydrogen sulphide

Monitoring shall be repeated, and the results recorded at regular intervals whilst personnel are working
in the excavation, and in any event the maximum ‘interval’ between tests shall not be exceeded. The Area
Authority (AA) shall decide the interval period between tests.
Gas Testing shall be carried out where:
(i) The potential exists for an oxygen deficient atmosphere or an unexpected release of
flammable or toxic gases. Where oxygen enrichment is possible, e.g. when oxy/propane
burning equipment is used, the atmosphere shall be continuously monitored. If oxygen
deficiency or enrichment occurs while work is in progress, the excavation shall be evacuated,
and re-entry permitted only after further tests produce satisfactory results.
(ii) The excavation takes place in ground known or suspected to be contaminated with oil or
other potentially flammable materials to such a degree that a flammable atmosphere may
occur. Work shall be suspended if a Lower Explosive Limit (LEL) is exceeded.
(iii) Chalk or limestone deposits when in contact with acid groundwater can release carbon
dioxide.
(iv) Depending on the nature of the excavation and the location of supporting equipment, there
is a potential for a build-up of toxic gases such as carbon monoxide (emitted from internal
combustion engines) or hydrogen sulphide (produced as a by-product of bacterial decay or
leakage from pipes). Work shall be suspended if the Short Term Exposure Limit (STEL) for
the toxic gas is reached.
Where a hazardous atmosphere is found, the work shall be suspended, and shall proceed only after
necessary additional precautions.
7.4.8. LIGHTING AND WARNING SIGNS
It shall be ensured that:
(i) Lighting system is sufficient to the extent that the hazards are visible which enables to
conduct tasks safely and evacuate the area in the event of an emergency;
(ii) Excavations in public areas and near permanent public access ways shall be illuminated of
the edges;
(iii) When work is in progress during the hours of darkness or in shafts and tunnels, sufficient
lighting shall be provided to allow safe working, access and egress but should not create
glare or other distractions for passers-by;
(iv) Battery operated headlamps (to avoid trailing cables) are considered for individual use; and
(v) Adequate lighting shall be made available wherever required. And as a minimum the
illumination should not be less than 200 lux.

7.4.9. HAZARDOUS SUBSTANCE
Digging may uncover buried substances that have the potential to be hazardous to health. The history of
the site should be examined to try to identify if hazardous substances have been buried there during its

There is always the presence of vermin to consider and this can increase the risk of several diseases.
7.4.10. BARRIERS
All excavations, irrespective of the depth, shall be suitably guarded to restrict access by personnel not
associated with the excavation activities. Barriers and stop blocks are to be erected at a minimum of 1
metres distance from the excavation. However, depending on the type of soil, excavation method and
supports and factors such as the presence of water in the excavation, this distance may need to be
increased. Barriers and stop blocks will help prevent:
(i) Personnel or vehicular traffic from damaging the excavation; and
(ii) Accidental access to the excavation of personnel and equipment.
In addition, the edges of all excavations where personnel may fall more than 1.8 metres (as mentioned
in ADNOC Working at Height Standard [Ref. 9]) shall be protected by rigid barriers or excavations
securely covered. Below 1.8 meters a physical demarcation of the excavation edge is required.
In some cases, the shoring method used can provide this barrier by ensuring the top of the shoring
extends sufficiently above the edge of the excavation. Guardrails shall meet the same standards as those
provided for working platforms. [Ref. 7]
During darkness, the edges of an excavation shall be marked with hazard warning lights, especially where
they are close to public thoroughfares.
Where excavation work is carried out on the roads outside the ADNOC jurisdiction, Traffic Police approval
is necessary and appropriate barricades and warning notices shall be erected, as per the requirement of
ADNOC Group.
Non-rigid barriers may be used in areas where access for persons and transport is not required. The
materials used should give an obvious warning by being immediately apparent and brightly coloured.
Wherever barriers are set back from the edge of the excavation, all access to the excavation shall be
confined to proper paths and roads with no materials stacked in the space between the barriers and the
edge of the excavation.

7.5. EXCAVATION METHODS
The nature of the excavation work being undertaken will affect the selection of an excavation method and
a safe system of work. Careful consideration shall be given to health and safety issues when planning
the work where the excavation involves anything other than shallow trenching and small quantities of
material.
7.5.1. TRENCHING
Personnel who proposes to excavate a trench at least 1.2m deep, shall minimize the risk to any person
arising from the collapse of the trench by ensuring that all sides of the trench are adequately supported
by one or more of the following:
(i) Shoring by shielding or other comparable means (for example boxing);
(ii) Benching; and
(iii) Battering.
A combination of these control measures may be the most effective depending on the work environment
and characteristics of the excavated material. Where a person enters a trench and there is a risk of
engulfment, these control measures shall be implemented regardless of the depth of the trench.

7.6.1. PROTECTION AGAINST FALLING AND DISLODGING MATERIALS
(a) Temporary Spoil or Material Heaps
In order to provide a safe footing at the edge, and to prevent spoil falling into an excavation, a clear
space at least 1 meter (3.28 feet) wide shall be maintained on all sides from the excavation to the
barriers.
(b) Undermining other Structures

Check that excavations do not undermine scaffold footings, buried services or the foundations of
nearby buildings or walls. Decide if extra support for the structure is needed before you start.
Surveys of the foundations and the advice of a structural engineer may be required.
(c) Effect of Excavating Equipment
Excavating equipment shall not be parked close to the sides of excavations. The extra loadings
can make the sides of excavations more likely to collapse.
Protection shall be provided by placing and keeping such materials or equipment at least 1 meter

or other heavy objects on a level above and near an excavation, the sides of the excavation shall
be sheet-piled, shored, and braced as necessary to resist the extra pressure due to such loads.
When mobile equipment is utilized or allowed adjacent to excavations, substantial stop logs, or
barricades shall be installed to prevent the equipment falling into the excavation.

7.6.5. PILOT TRENCHING
Prior to use of mechanical digging, pilot trenches shall be dug in order to locate any underground services.
Note that pilot trenching may not be required if there is clear indication that the presence of underground
services could be extremely unlikely e.g. in virgin desert locations.
Pilot trenches shall always be dug using hand tools only in maximum depth steps of 1.2 meters (4 feet),
relative to the adjacent ground level. Pilot trenches dug to a depth exceeding 1.2 meters are considered
to be confined spaces and the rules around confined space entry apply in line with ADNOC Confined
Space Standard and ADNOC PTW Standard. [Ref. 6 & Ref. 5]
A CAT shall be used in conjunction with up to date drawings of underground services and pilot trenching
in order to locate underground services. If any are detected, their whole length within the proposed
excavation shall be exposed by hand digging.
7.7. DEWATERING
Ingress of water may occur through rainfall, flood, or when excavation continues below the natural
groundwater level. Consideration shall be given to the likelihood of water entering the excavation and the
measures to be implemented in order to control water entering the excavation and water levels between
it. When an excavation is liable to water ingress, the stability of the walls can be undermined; this will
influence the choice of shoring.
7.7.1. RAIN & SURFACE WATER
Wherever surface ditches, streams, etc. are incapable of draining off excess water, trenches shall be
constructed to carry the water away from the excavation. Trenches shall be designed to cope with the
maximum rainfall expected whilst the excavation is in progress. If unexpectedly high rainfall occurs during
excavation, water should be extracted from the excavation and pumped to sumps for settlement from
where it can be pumped out for disposal.
7.7.2. GROUND WATER
Where the presence of ground water may create difficult working conditions or endanger the stability of
the sides and/or floor of the excavation, the excavation should be surrounded by interlocking steel
sheeting, an in-situ concrete diaphragm or a grout curtain to prevent the water from entering the
excavation. As an alternative, a ground de-watering programme may be used to keep excavations clear
of water whilst work proceeds or to reduce the loads, which bear on the earthworks support materials.
The ground de-watering programme shall be either by drainage, or by installing well-points around the
site and pumping out the water to lower the ground water table to below that of the excavation. Before a
ground de-watering programme is instigated, expert advice may need to be taken to establish:
(i) The feasibility of such a programme in the ground conditions pertaining to the excavation;
(ii) That, as a result of de-watering, no fine materials will be drawn from under adjacent
structures with consequent risk of settlement.

7.8. INSPECTION & SUPERVISION
7.8.1. INSPECTION OF EXCAVATION
All excavation work requires careful watching, especially when they are first opened and sides are
unsupported, even when support work has been installed, constant vigilance is essential.
The following specifies the frequency and conditions requiring inspections:
(i) At the start of each shift before work begins;
(ii) After any event likely to have affected the strength or stability of the excavation;
(iii) After any accidental fall of rock, earth or other material.
(iv) After every rainstorm;
(v) After other events that may increase hazards, e.g. windstorm.
(vi) When fissures, tension cracks, sloughing, undercutting, water seepage, bulging at
(vii) When there is a change in the size, location, or placement of the spoil pile; and
(viii) When there is any indication of change or movement in adjacent structures.
It shall be ensured that:
(i) Work be immediately stopped if the inspection shows the excavation to be unsafe; and
(ii) Examination of the excavation is carried out by a competent person after substantial collapse
or damage. A typical inspection checklist provided as an example in Appendix 4.
7.8.2. WORK SUPERVISION
Employees working in an excavation shall at all times be supervised. The person supervising shall remain
outside the excavation at all times and shall be responsible for ensuring that he is capable of recognising
and assessing any potential dangers as they arise which includes unexpected ground conditions that
may require a change in construction technique, or unusual smells which may indicate the presence of
noxious or dangerous gases.
The relevant department personnel from Telecom, Instruments, Electrical, Flowlines/Pipelines and
Cathodic protection should monitor the excavation site regularly while the excavation is in progress, to
ensure that the respective underground facilities are not damaged.
7.9. DISPOSAL OF SPOIL & BACKFILL
7.9.1. DISPOSAL OF SPOIL
Non-shored excavations - spoil may be disposed of along the sides of the excavation, if the distance to
the edge of the nearest portion of the spoil heap is not less than the final excavation depth.
Shored excavations - spoil may be disposed of along the sides of the excavation, if the distance to the
edge of the nearest portion of the spoil heap is greater than 1 metre.
7.9.2. DISPOSAL OF BACKFILL
Where backfill using wheeled or tracked vehicles is to take place or the excavation is in an area where it
is foreseeable that vehicles may attempt to approach close to the excavation, stop boards or stop blocks
should be fitted at a distance of 1 metre from edge of the excavation.
