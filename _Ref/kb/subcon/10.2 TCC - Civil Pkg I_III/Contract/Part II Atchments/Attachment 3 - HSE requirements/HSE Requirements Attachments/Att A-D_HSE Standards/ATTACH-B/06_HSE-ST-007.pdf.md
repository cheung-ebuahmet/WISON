1. INTRODUCTION
GASCO’s HSE Management Manual (sub-element 6.4) outlines requirements to have and maintain
Standard for reporting, classifying and investigating hazardous situations, near misses and incidents,
which must conform to ADNOC Code of Practice (COPs). This standard is developed to meet these
expectations and ensure effective management of HSE risk associated with actual or potential events
that have or could have resulted in accidents. This update addresses the improvement
suggestions/comments received since last revision and ensures continual improvement in GASCO’s
way of incident management.
This standard defines processes for effective incident management through accurate reporting,
classification, investigation and analysis and follow up, to ensure valuable information and lessons are
documented and provided to improve GASCO operations and prevent reoccurrence of similar events.
The objective of this Standard is to provide guidelines for:
 Proactive reporting of hazardous situations by employees and contractors, with appropriate
mitigation or corrective actions put in place to prevent the situation from resulting in accidents.
 Effective and timely reporting of near misses and incidents with actual damage for proper
management, investigation and analysis, to identify root causes and recommendations to prevent
reoccurrence of similar events.
 Ensuring remedial actions from recommendations are effectively implemented and lessons
learned from incidents are communicated across GASCO and other ADNOC Group companies.
2. TYPES OF INCIDENTS
GASCO considers incidents to include potential or actual events associated with activities performed
within the work environment that could result, or have resulted in damage. Within the scope of this
standard, three types of incidents are considered and defined as follows:
2.1 Damaging Incidents
A Damaging Incident is an unplanned event or chain of events which has resulted in damage with
impact to people, environment, assets and/or company reputation. This includes unplanned,
uncontrolled and sudden Process releases (even of small quantities).
2.2 Near Miss
A Near Miss is an unplanned event or chain of events, which under slightly different circumstances
could have resulted in a damage or impact on people, environment, assets and/or company
reputation but was avoided or reduced.
Also included are events or conditions that could have resulted in Process releases.
2.3 Unsafe Acts or Conditions
Unsafe Acts are actions performed by a person in the work environment that can result in an accident
or injury.
Unsafe Conditions are situations in the work environment which can result in an incident or injury.

3. CLASSIFICATION OF INCIDENTS
All types of Incidents will be classified in line with the ADNOC HSE Performance reporting
requirements described below. Detailed classification and sub-classifications are described with
illustrations in Appendix C.
3.1 Process Release Event
Process release event are incidents which are unplanned or uncontrolled Loss of Primary Containment
(LOPC) of any material including non-toxic and non-flammable materials (e.g. steam, hot condensate,
nitrogen, compressed CO2 or compressed air) from a process, or an undesired event or condition that
under slightly different circumstances could have resulted in a loss of containment of a material.
3.2 Occupational Safety Incident
These are generally related to incidents which have resulted in the following:
- Personnel injury or fatality including third-parties and personnel from other ADNOC group
companies.
- Incidents associated with transportation activities,
- property damage,
- Equipment damage not involving unplanned or uncontrolled Loss of Primary Containment (LOPC).
- Fires
3.3 Occupational Illness
Any work related abnormal condition or disorder of a person, other than an injury, which is mainly
caused by exposure to environmental factors associated with the employment. It includes acute and
chronic illness or diseases that may be caused by inhalation, absorption, ingestion or direct contact.
3.4 Environmental Incident
Events resulting in an unplanned or uncontrolled release of a product or chemical with negative impact
to the environment – water, air, soil, animals, plants, ecology and social life will be classified as
Environmental Incidents.
For an event to be classified as Environmental Incident it should have one of the below listed
consequences ;
- Direct spill to land (not from process)
- Loss of Secondary Containment
- Discharges to water bodies
- Emissions to air beyond statutory limits
Note: It is possible that some incidents can be classified as several of the above.

5. INCIDENT SEVERITY AND POTENTIAL RISK RANKING
All incidents shall be evaluated to determine the potential risk ranking using the GASCO Qualitative
Risk Assessment matrix attached in Appendix B. The potential risk ranking determined drives the level
of ownership and investigation of the incident. However, for damaging incidents, the actual severity
must also be evaluated.
5.1 Actual Severity
The actual severity of an incident is determined by evaluating the resulting damage and assigning a
numeric value (1 to 5) using the consequence categories described in the GASCO Qualitative Risk
Assessment matrix. Where an incident results in more than one consequence categories, the overall
severity ranking will be determined by the consequence category with the highest level of severity.
5.2 Potential Risk Ranking
All Near Misses and Damaging Incidents shall be analysed to determine potential risk ranking (low,
medium or high) by evaluating the probability and potential consequences under slightly different
circumstances using the GASCO Qualitative Risk Assessment matrix. Near Misses and Damaging
Incidents analysed with high potential risk will require formal investigation.
Unsafe Acts and Conditions can be optionally categorized using the GASCO RAM to determine the
potential risk ranking.
5.3 Serious Incidents and High Potential Incidents
A Serious Incident is “a generic category which includes all incidents with actual or potential
consequences of catastrophic, severe and critical nature”. By this definition, incidents with severity of
3 and above as per the GASCO Qualitative Risk Assessment matrix will be categorised as Serious
Incidents.
Near Misses and Damaging Incidents assessed to have a risk ranking of RAM Red, Yellow 5A, Yellow
4A and Yellow 4B using the GASCO Qualitative Risk Assessment matrix are categorized as High
Potential Incidents (HiPo). These incidents, under slightly different circumstances, could have resulted
in more severe consequences.
All Serious and High Potential Incidents will require formal investigations to identify root causes and
improvement opportunities to prevent reoccurrence.
Sites shall report these incidents to Corporate HSE division within 24 hours. These incidents shall then
be Risk Ranked (initial) within 24 hours of reporting of incident. Corporate HSE division shall than
report to ADNOC and Shareholders using Serious Incident Notification Form attached in Appendix H.

6. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
The following roles and responsibilities are identified for effective implementation of the incident
management process:
6.1 Incident Reporter
A reporter is someone who observes an incident and makes an initial report. All GASCO employees
and contractors are responsible for:
 Identifying and reporting unsafe acts and conditions, near miss or incidents to their supervisor or
line manager and in the online incident reporting system.
 Cooperating with investigation team if required during the investigation process
 Completing assigned actions from an incident investigation recommendations as required.
6.2 Site Incident Focal Point
A site incident focal point is a designated person within a site or department who reviews incident
reports made on the online reporting system. The site incident focal point is responsible for:
 Reviewing all initial incident reports to ensure accurate information has been provided.
 Liaising with appropriate persons/stakeholders to determine and agree on classification based on
the incident outcomes.
 Entering relevant data specific to an incident classification for review by the relevant Consequence
Specialist and for HSE performance reporting.
 Enter Initial Risk Ranking of Incident based on the available information.
 Enters the incident investigation team members nominated by the incident owner
 Identification of immediate causes for incidents not requiring detail investigation
 Development of Remedial Action Plan for incidents and follow up till completion.

The site incident focal point plays a critical role in the review and evaluation of an incident report and
must be competent in understanding the definitions of incident types, classification, severity,
potential risk ranking and the entire GASCO Incident management process.
6.3 Consequence Specialist
Incidents with actual damage may often result in one or more consequences and will be classified
either as Process Release, Occupational Safety, and/or Occupational Illness and/or Environmental
Incidents.
A Consequence Specialist is someone within a Site / Department assigned to provide guidance on
actual consequence of an incident and review relevant data associated with the incident classification
for HSE performance reporting. Consequence specialist will liaise with the Incident Focal Point to
determine the incident type, actual severity, potential risk ranking and level of incident ownership and
investigation required. They will also be required to participate in investigations of incidents
associated with their respective specialist areas.
In line with the different incident classifications, the following consequence specialist have been
identified:
 Process Safety Specialist – responsible for reviewing relevant data entered for all process releases
and process / plant equipment damages.
 Occupational Safety Specialist – responsible for reviewing relevant data entered for occupational
safety related incidents such as occupational injuries/fatalities, damages to non-process
equipment including transportation and incidents with impact on company reputation.
 Occupational Illness Specialist – responsible for reviewing relevant data entered for occupational
illnesses including non-accidental deaths.
 Environmental Specialist – responsible for reviewing relevant data entered for environmental
related incidents including product/chemical spills to land, water or air.
6.4 Responsible Person
A Responsible Person is someone within a Site or Department with direct oversight or responsibility
for supervising a work location, equipment, activity/operation or person(s) involved in an incident.
The Department associated with such incidents becomes the Responsible Department. Responsible
Persons would usually be the first line supervisor or direct reporting line manager and must be notified
whenever an incident occurs.
Responsible Persons are to:
 Encourage open, honest and timely reporting of unsafe acts and conditions, near miss and
incidents that occur within their areas of work supervision.
 Ensure timely up-line notification of incidents to senior management where necessary.
6.5 Incident Owner
An Incident Owner is someone within a Site or Department who is accountable for managing a specific
work location, equipment, activity/operation or person(s) involved in an incident. Ownership of an
incident resides with the Site or Department with an oversight of the specific work location,
activity/operation, equipment or person(s) involved in the incident.

 prepare summary of the incident for external communication (HSE Alert / LFI)

 a Department Head or Manager level individual (for medium/high potential risk) outside or within

7. INCIDENT MANAGEMENT PROCESS DESCRIPTION
7.1 Process Overview
The framework of the incident management process covers incident reporting, classification,
investigation and analysis, and follow-up.
When an incident is observed, initial response must be provided to secure the incident location and
prevent further escalation of the situation. This response must be in line with the emergency response
plan and safety procedures of the facility or location where the incident occurred.
Once the situation has been brought under control, the incident must be timely reported and classified
appropriately depending on the resulting consequences or severity. The potential risk will also be
evaluated in order to determine the level of ownership and investigation required. An Investigation
team will be nominated to carry out investigation and identify the causes with recommendations to
address the causes.
As part of follow-up process, recommendations from the incident investigation will be developed into
actions for implementation. Lessons learned from the incident will be communicated across GASCO
and other ADNOC group companies. Periodic analysis of root causes, common failures, nature of
operation, contact analysis, etc. shall be conducted.
A full incident management process workflow is provided in Appendix A.
7.2 Initial Response
All GASCO employees and contractors must be vigilant while working to proactively identify unsafe
acts/conditions, near miss and incidents, notify their immediate supervisor or line manager
accordingly. Where possible, appropriate actions should be taken to intervene or prevent the situation
from escalation including stopping associated work activity.
For incidents resulting in actual damage or impact, on-site emergency response services and site
management must be notified immediately and emergency response procedures implemented in line
with the facility’s emergency response plans and safety procedures. Once the incident situation has
been put under control, the incident location must be secured to preserve evidences that will be
required during investigation. An on-site team will be set up to gather relevant facts and evidences
that maybe useful for up-line reporting and formal investigation. Where photographs are required,
appropriate approval shall be obtained from applicable authorities.
7.3 Initial Report
All incidents shall be reported in the online incident reporting system within 24 hours or as soon as
the situation has been brought under control. For incidents occurring at GASCO managed or
supervised project sites, where there are no access to the reporting system, paper forms (sample in

7.4 Investigation
Incident investigation for HiPo incidents shall begin within 48 hours or as soon as the incident has
been put under control and site secured, and should be concluded within 30 days except for cases
where detailed analysis and testing of evidences such as failed parts/equipment will be required by
third-party agencies.
Formal investigation process will be initiated by the Incident Owner with a terms of reference (TOR),
nominating the investigation team leader and members using the criteria described in the
responsibilities section of this standard. Sample format of TOR is attached in Annexure L.
A formal investigation should involve the following activities:
7.4.1 Investigation Planning
The investigation team shall develop a plan on how to conduct the investigation in line with the TOR
provided. The plan will include:
 Identifying key deliverables and resources required (including appropriate personal protective
equipment - PPE)
 Establishing timelines and assigning responsibilities within the team to ensure timely completion
of the investigation
 Requesting access to the incident site, relevant documents and personnel to be interviewed in
order to gather evidence and information.
7.4.2 Gathering of Evidence
Evidence collected must be factual, gathered systematically and labelled (if possible) for easy
identification. The evidence can be gathered and classified under the following heads:
 Technology: Review technical related information such as equipment, maintenance, buildings,
structures, vessels, pipework, transport vehicles, operations, processes, operating instructions,
materials, chemicals, power, energy, design and layout.
 Organisation: Review work organisation including management and supervision, safety culture
and morale, methods, procedures, safety controls and systems, legal requirements and codes of
practice, contractor activities, resources, finance, work planning, procurement and purchasing,
quality.
 People: Review personnel related information including injuries, illnesses, activities and tasks,
knowledge and decision making, attitude and behaviour, skills and training, health and fitness,
emotional state, communications, personal protection equipment, individual testimonies, fatigue
due to overtime.
 Similar Events: Review previous or similar incidents that might have occurred in the past.
 Environment: Review working and environmental conditions prior and during the incident
including weather, natural effects, pollution, contamination, general conditions, housekeeping,
location, flora and fauna, biological and radiological factors, error inducing conditions.

 Time and Sequence: Review the timing and sequence of events prior, during and after the
incident, including sketches to illustrate how the incident occurred.
7.4.3 Analysis and Causes
The evidence and facts gathered will be reviewed to identify the causes using incident investigation
methodologies. GASCO approved investigation tools are Tripod Beta (Investigator-3) and Root Cause
Analysis (RCA refer GASCO RCA Procedure OEX-PR-005).
Investigation team shall identify the tool to be used. At least one of the team members should be
proficient in the use of selected tool.
Tripod Beta should be used for all High Potential and Serious incidents.
In identifying the causes, the significant findings should be evaluated as failed barriers from the Tripod
tree logic, from which the Immediate Causes and Root Causes will be analysed. Immediate Causes
are sub-standard acts, conditions, omissions or technical failures that caused the barrier to fail. Root
Causes represent an organisational deficiency or anomaly that can be attributed to the immediate
cause of an incident.
Each Finding will be risk ranked using the GASCO Incident Investigation and Finding Risk Ranking (in
Appendix B) and assigned specific recommendations. These recommendations must address root
causes. In addition, each root cause will be mapped to appropriate management systems and
processes and also linked to failed GASCO HSEMS expectations. A comprehensive list of immediate
causes, root causes and management systems is provided in Appendix E.
Incidents with breach of GASCO Life Saving Rules must be identified and reported to the Life Saving
Rules Committee for further investigation in line with Life Saving Rules Standard (HSE-ST-072).
Investigation process shall identify the Life Saving Rules not followed and assign Immediate Cause and
Root Cause to it for developing recommendations.

recommendations for implementation. Investigation reports for incidents with High Risk Ranking will
require quality review by HSE Division before approval by the Incident Owner.

I. Process Release Events
An unplanned or uncontrolled Loss of Primary Containment (LOPC) of any material including non-
toxic and non-flammable materials (e.g. steam, hot condensate, nitrogen, compressed CO or
2
compressed air) from a process, or an undesired event or condition that under slightly different
circumstances could have resulted in a loss of containment of a material.
A Process includes assets and equipment used for production, distribution, storage utilities or
plant facilities. E.g. reactors, vessels, piping, furnaces, boilers, pumps, compressors, exchangers,
cooling towers, refrigeration systems, storage tanks, ancillary support areas, onsite remediation
facilities, distribution piping under control of the company as defined in the API-754.
Process Release Incidents are classified into Tiers (1, 2, and 3) depending on the consequences as
defined by the API-754.
Tier-1:
A LOPC resulting in one or more of the following consequences:
- an employee, contractor or sub-contractor lost time injury and/or fatality
- a hospital admission and/or fatality of a third-party
- an officially declared community evacuation or community shelter-in-place
- a fire or explosion resulting in greater than or equal to $25,000 of direct cost to the company
- a pressure relief device discharge to atmosphere whether directly or via a downstream
destructive device that results in one or more of the following four consequences: liquid
carryover, discharge to a potential unsafe location, an on-site shelter-in-place, public
protective measures (e.g. road closure).
- release of material greater than the threshold quantities in a one-hour period as described in
the Appendix D Table 1
Tier-2:
A LOPC resulting in one or more of the following consequences and not reported as Tier-1:
- an employee, contractor or sub-contractor recordable injury
- a fire or explosion resulting in greater than or equal to $2,500 of direct cost to the company
- a pressure relief device discharge to atmosphere whether directly or via a downstream
destructive device that results in one or more of the following four consequences: liquid
carryover, discharge to a potential unsafe location, an on-site shelter-in-place, public
protective measures (e.g. road closure).
- release of material greater than the threshold quantities in a one-hour period as described in
the Appendix D Table 2

opportunity to identify and correct weaknesses within the barrier system. They are intended for

II. Occupational Safety Incidents
This covers incidents resulting in injuries or death of a GASCO employee, contractor or sub-
contractor. Sub-classification of Occupational Safety Incidents include work-related injuries,
transportation incidents, fire incident, minor property damages and incidents resulting in third-
party injuries, fatalities or property damage.
Work-related Injuries
 Fatality: A death resulting from and injury or illness, regardless of the time intervening
between the injury/illness and death.
 Permanent Total Disability (PTD): Any work-related injury or illness which permanently
incapacitates an employee from doing any work and results in termination of employment.
 Permanent Partial Disability (PPD): Any work related injury/illness which results in the
complete loss or permanent loss of use of any parts of the body or any permanent impairment
of function or parts of body, regardless of any pre-existing disability of the injured member of
impaired body function.
 Lost Time Injury (LTI): Any absence from work related Fatalities, Permanent Total Disabilities,
Permanent Partial Disabilities and Lost Workday Cases. LTI is the sum of Fatalities, Permanent
Total Disabilities, Permanent Partial Disabilities and Lost Workday Cases.
 Lost Workday Case (LWC): Any work related injury or illness which renders the injured person
temporarily unable to perform any regular job or restricted work within 24 hours of the
moment that the in occupational injury was received or illness determined. The criteria “24
hours” include rest days, weekends, scheduled holidays, public holidays or subsequent days
after ceasing employment.
Note: A Lost Workday Case is not the same as a Lost Time Injury (LTI) as it does not include
fatalities. Also, a single accident can give rise to several lost workday cases, depending on the
number of people injured as a result of that incident.
 Restricted Workday Case (RWC): Any work-related injury or illness other than a fatality or lost
workday case which results in a person being unfit for full performance of the regular job
within 24 hours after the moment on which the occupational injury was received or illness
determined.

Appendix E: GUIDELINES AND ILLUSTRATIONS FOR DETERMINING WORK-RELATED INJURIES /
ILLNESS
All injuries and illnesses must be evaluated to determine work-relationship. Consequently, injuries or
illnesses are considered work-related if events or exposures in the work environment caused or
contributed to the resulting condition or significantly aggravated pre-existing injuries or illnesses.
In order to determine work relationship of an incident resulting in injury or illness, one or more of the
following circumstances must be established:
a. Nature of Activity Performed:
 Is the work activity directly managed or supervised by GASCO?
 Is the work activity related or exclusively to GASCO?
 Is the employee or contractor voluntarily participating in sports or leisure activities including
those organized by the company?
Illustration:
An employee or contractor sustains injury while performing work at a project construction site.
The incident will be reportable but will be considered as Work-related ONLY if the construction
activity is exclusive to GASCO, managed and supervised by GASCO.
An employee or contractor sustains injury while using gym or recreational facilities at a GASCO
plant, or while participating in a sporting or recreational event organised by GASCO. Such incidents
are reportable but will not be considered as Work-related injuries even if the incident occurred in
a GASCO controlled or managed premises.
b. Location and Time of Incident:
 Is the worksite/location exclusive for GASCO activity?
 Is the worksite/location controlled or supervised by GASCO?
 Is the employee or contractor present at site as part of work conditions?
 Is the work activity performed during approved working hours?
Illustration:
An employee or contractor sustains injury while working in a GASCO office or plant, or while using
a GASCO owned or lease equipment or vehicle. Such incidents are reportable but will not be
considered as Work-related if the employee or contractor was not working during his normal
working hours, shift or work schedule. In essence, an injury sustained in the work environment
after company approved working hours, days or work schedule will be reportable but not Work-
related.
c. Travelling and Commuting between Work Locations:
 Is the employee or contractor travelling for work related activities/functions?
 Is the employee or contractor travelling in company owned vehicle or bus?
 Is the employee or contractor travelling outside approved journey route
Illustration:
Motor vehicle accidents with injuries sustained during travelling from home to work are
considered reportable but not Work-related even if the trip was with a company vehicle. This is

because time of travelling from home to work is not considered as company time. Actual company

An employee or contractor is involved in a motor vehicle accident travelling to or from GASCO

 The trip was for official purpose duly approved or authorised by a line manager.
