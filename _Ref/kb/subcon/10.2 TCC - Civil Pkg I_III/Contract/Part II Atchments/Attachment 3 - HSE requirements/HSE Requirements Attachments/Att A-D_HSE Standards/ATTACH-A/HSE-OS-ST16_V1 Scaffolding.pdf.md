1. INTRODUCTION
Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC), as part of its Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) policy,
commits to ensure that a safe working environment is maintained in all areas and that all persons who
are involved in scaffolding erection, inspection, operation, alterations, dismantling and training are fully
aware of the hazards involved and have the competence, training and awareness to undertake such
activities.
Throughout this standard, the following words, when used in the context of actions by ADNOC Group
companies and others (contractors), have specific meanings as follows:
(i) “shall” indicates a requirement;
(ii) “should” indicates a recommendation; and
(iii) “may” indicates a permission.
2. PURPOSE
To provide standard requirements, guidelines, instructions, rules and precautions for scaffolding erection,
inspection, operation, alterations, dismantling and training requirements.
3. SCOPE
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group and its Contractors.
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood,
implemented and thoroughly monitored.
4. LAWS AND REGULATIONS
ADNOC and Group Companies shall ensure that their activities comply with all relevant Federal and Abu
Dhabi laws and regulations at all times, including any that maybe introduced after the publication of this
Standard. The following UAE laws and regulations shall be applicable to this standard:
(i) Federal Law no. 8 of 1980, Chapter 5 - Safety Protection and the Health Article 91 states
that ‘Every employer must provide adequate means of protection for the employee from the
hazards of injuries and vocational diseases’.
(ii) Regulation of Labor Ministerial Decree no. 32 of 1982 on Protection of Employees Against
Occupational Hazards.

7. REQUIREMENTS
7.1. SCAFFOLDING
A scaffolding is a temporary structure erected to support access or working platforms. Scaffolding are
commonly used in construction/maintenance work so that workers have a safe and stable work platform
when work cannot be done at ground level or on a finished floor.
7.2. SCAFFOLDING STRUCTURAL COMPONENTS AND DIMENSIONS
7.2.1. BASIC COMPONENTS
Basic scaffolding components include three (03) general components:
(i) Standard;
(ii) Ledger; and
(iii) Transom.
(a) Standard
A standard, also known as upright, is a long pipe or tube that connects the mass of the scaffolding
directly to the ground and runs the length of the scaffolding. The base of each standard is
connected to a base plate, which helps distribute the weight of each standard to the ground.
(b) Ledger
A ledger is a long pipe or tube that runs horizontally between each standard and adds further
support and weight distribution.
(c) Transom
A transom is placed on top of ledgers at a right angle, runs horizontally and comes in several
different forms. There are two (02) basic types of transom:
(i) Main Transoms: These transoms provide support for standards by holding them in position
as well as supporting boards; and
(ii) Intermediate Transoms: These transoms are placed alongside main transoms to lend
additional board support.

7.2.2. OTHER SUPPORTING COMPONENTS
There are several other supportive components that serve to reinforce the fundamental scaffolding. These
components include:
(a) Braces
Braces, such as cross braces and facade braces, and additional couplers, can be used in varying
combinations to support the structure in several ways.
(i) Cross braces run diagonally between ledgers and securely attach to standards to increase
overall rigidity of a structure. However, they can also secure themselves to ledgers, in which
case they are simply called ledger braces;
(ii) Facade braces help prevent a structure from swaying, and are attached on the face of the
scaffold, running the length of the face of the structure and securely attaching at every level;
(iii) Couplers help connect structural elements, and come in several variants:
1. A right-angle coupler, used to join tubes right angle to each other, to connect a ledger
or transom to a standard;
2. A putlog or single-coupler can be used to connect the ledger to the transom; and
3. For any other angle of connection between scaffolding piping, a swivel coupler can be
used.
(b) Node
A theoretical intersection point where two or more members are connected together, although they
are not actually in the same plane. It is usually the intersection between a standard and ledger.
(c) Guardrail
Guardrail is a member in scaffolding structure that prevents the fall of a person from a platform or
access way.
(d) Base Jack
Base jack is used to hold the standard, adjust the height of the scaffolding and increase scaffolding
stability.
(e) Base Plate & Sole Board
A metal plate with a spigot for distributing the load from a standard or raker or other load bearing
tube. Sole boards are boards placed under the base plates to support the scaffold on softer ground.
(f) Putlog
A horizontal tube with a flattered end, to rest in or on part of the brickwork or structure.
(g) Reveal Pin
A fitting used for tightening a reveal tube between two opposite surfaces.
(h) Reveal Tube
A tube fixed between two opposite surfaces by means of a threaded (expanding) fitting or by
placing it across an opening in a structure to form an anchor to which the scaffolding may be tied.

(i) Toe board
A tube spanning across ledgers to form the support for boards or units forming the working platform,
or to connect the outer standards to the inner standards.
(j) Brick Guard
Brick guard is a metal or other fender filling the gap between the guard-rail and toe board, and
sometimes incorporates one or both of these components. These can be hung from the guard rails
and should be prevented from outward movement.
(k) Ties
Ties provide a secure link between scaffolding and adjoining building or structure. Depending on
the kind of scaffolding, the tie patterns and types of ties used can vary. For example:
(i) Through ties can be used to secure a scaffolding to a building by entering a building’s open
window;
(ii) Box ties help secure a scaffolding by attaching to a building’s external features, such as
strong pillars;
(iii) Lip ties (standard duty) consists of an L-shaped arrangement of tubes and couplers to hook
the scaffold behind parts of the building;
(iv) Reveal ties (light duty), in case where it is impracticable to open windows for tube and
coupling ties, the attachment of scaffold may be made by reveal ties which rely on friction;
and
(v) Masonry anchors, an anchorage can be provided by casting in a threaded socket into a part
of the structure itself.
(l) Ladders
The most common means of access to a scaffolding is by ladder.

7.3.1. INDEPENDENT SCAFFOLDING
This type of scaffolding is usually used to provide a working platform to a vertical structure. It uses two
sets of parallel standards; one close to the structure and the other set at the width of the work platform.
It is erected so that it is independent from the structure and does not rely on it for its primary stability.
However, it is usual to tie the scaffolding to the structure in order to prevent the scaffolding falling towards
or away from the structure. This is referred to as an independent tied scaffold.

7.3.3. SUSPENDED SCAFFOLD
Suspended (also known as slung) scaffoldings are working platforms or cradles suspended by ropes,
cables or other non-rigid means, from an overhead structure.
Figure 7.3.3: A Typical Suspended Scaffolding (Illustration Purpose only) – Image Courtesy
OSHA
The most common type of suspended scaffolding is a two-point adjustable suspended scaffold. This is
hung by parallel cables or ropes and connected to each end of the platform or cradle. There are usually
controls to lower and raise the platform or cradle operated by personnel on the platform.
Similarly, a single-point adjustable scaffolding is a platform or cradle suspended by a single cable or rope
from an overhead structure. It will also have a means to raise or lower the platform by personnel on the
platform. This is sometimes known as a boatswain's or bosun’s chair.

7.3.4. MOBILE TOWER SCAFFOLD
Tower scaffolding with castors is sometimes used to provide mobile access. They are widely used as
they are convenient for work which involves frequent access to height over a short period of time in a
number of locations that are spaced apart.
Figure 7.3.6: A Typical Mobile Tower Scaffolding (Illustration Purpose only) – Image Courtesy
OSHA
Mobile scaffoldings are mostly used with buildings for repair and maintenance activities and are easy to
move from one place to another. Since work can be carried out in a safer and more spacious environment,
workers generally find mobile scaffolding more productive, making this type of scaffolding a very cost-
effective option.
Mobile scaffoldings shall not exceed a height to base width (including outrigger supports, if used) ratio of
four to one (4:1). If that ratio is more than 4:1, those scaffoldings shall be restrained from tipping by guying,
tying, bracing, or equivalent means [Ref. 2].
The height to base width ratio of the scaffolding during movement is 2:1 or less [Ref. 3].

7.6. CONTROL MEASURES
7.6.1. WORK AT HEIGHT
(i) Before any work is carried out, the risks from working at height shall be assessed. All
personnel who are required to work at height must be trained to identify risks and take the
necessary precautions.
7.6.2. FALLING OBJECTS
(i) As far as practicable barricades shall be provided by keeping a clear distance from
scaffolding structure;
(ii) Mechanise shifting of material and tools such as by using rope, pulley and bag; and
(iii) No material shall be kept inside the scaffolding pipe opening as it can fall during scaffolding
dismantling;
7.6.3. CLEARANCE FROM ELECTRICAL/PROCESS LINES
(i) Any part of operational or pressurized process line shall not be used as a support for
scaffolding;
(ii) Clearance shall be defined in planning stage and maintained during erection and use of
scaffolding for overhead electrical lines and nearby process lines.
7.6.4. PPE
(i) Personnel shall wear standard PPE at all times at work area;
(ii) Safety helmets with chinstraps shall be provided while working on scaffolding including
erection and dismantling;
(iii) Personnel shall wear full body harness during erection & dismantling of scaffolding and on
scaffolding after erection if there is a risk of falling from height and falling objects on a case-
by-case basis; and
(iv) Personnel shall wear Personal Floatation Device (PFD) while working, erection and
dismantling over water.
7.6.5. MANUAL HANDLING
(i) Safe manual handling practices shall be followed by all personnel when required to manually
handle scaffolding material and equipment [Ref. 5].

7.9.3. MATERIAL STANDARDS
(a) Steel Tubes and Fittings
(i) All steel tubing, couplers and fittings for scaffolding shall conform to the relevant version of
BS EN 1139 standard or acceptable equivalent;
(ii) Tubes shall be straight, free from cracks, splits, surface flaws and other defects. The ends
of the tubes shall be cut clean and square;
(iii) All couplers and fittings shall be properly oiled and maintained; and
(iv) Nuts shall have a free running fit on their bolts. Bolts with worn or damaged threads shall be
replaced.
(b) Aluminium Tubing
(i) All Aluminium tubing, couplers and fittings for scaffolding shall conform to the relevant
version of BS EN 1139 standard or acceptable equivalent;
(ii) Aluminium tube shall not be mixed with steel tube or steel fittings due to different loading
capacities and the fact that steel fittings could crush aluminium tube;
(iii) Aluminium tube shall not be straightened; the straight parts of the tube may be cut out and
re-used, but the remainder shall be disposed of; and
(iv) Aluminium tube shall not be heated by welding or flame cutting etc.

hydrocarbons are likely to be present as they are vulnerable in case of fire. Mixing scaffolding

7.9.4. LADDERS
(i) When scaffolding platforms are more than 600mm above or below a point of access, ladders
or similar access mechanisms shall be used. Cross braces shall not be used as a means of
access [Ref. 2];
(ii) Ladders used for access to another level shall be tied and extend at least 1 m above the
landing point to provide a secure handhold, unless adequate hand-holds are provided;
(iii) The vertical distance between landings shall not exceed 6m;
(iv) Ladder angle shall be 4:1 [Ref. 6];
(v) Care shall be taken to ensure that there is adequate space between the rungs to give a firm
foothold, and that there is no possibility of the foot being trapped between a rung and a
ledger, transom or brace tube;
(vi) The landing area shall be kept clear of obstructions, stacked materials etc. and have guard
rails and toe boards;
(vii) The access opening shall be as small as practically possible; and
(viii) Any scaffold over 8m high shall have the access ladder turned inside the framing, and
alternate sides of landing.

7.11.2. SCAFFOLDING VISUAL INSPECTION
Scaffolding shall be inspected by a competent person to ensure that it has been erected in compliance
with the design requirements. This shall identify any defects and corrective actions taken, even when
those actions are taken promptly as this assists with the identification of any recurring problems. The
inspection checklist shall be attached to the inspection report.
Scaffolding inspection shall ensure the following as a minimum:
(i) Scaffolding has been built in accordance to the approved design;
(ii) Scaffolding structure is appropriate;
(iii) Supporting structure is appropriate;
(iv) Working platforms are secured and protected;
(v) Access and egress is appropriate; and
(vi) Scaffolding shall enable the work to be performed appropriately and safely.
Scaffolding inspection frequency may vary depending on weather and site conditions, the type and size
of the scaffolding and the risks associated with scaffolding collapse. As a minimum, scaffolding
inspections shall be carried out:
(i) By a certified scaffolding inspector;
(ii) Based on applicable standard/internationally recognized scaffolding standards;
(iii) Based on original equipment manufacturer guidelines;
(iv) As material is delivered to the site;
(v) During erection - to avoid any deviation from the original plan and design standard in order
to take corrective action in a timely manner;
(vi) After completion of scaffold erection for the purpose of certification;
(vii) Before it is taken into use;
(viii) After any modifications, exposure to adverse weather or periods without use;
(ix) At least every seven (07) days; and
(x) After any event that could affect the stability of the scaffolding (for example, following strong
winds or storms).
More frequent inspections where required or identified shall be conducted as deemed necessary by the
competent person.
Where defects are found, those shall be rectified and the root causes of serious or recurring defects shall
be identified and corrective and preventive action taken to prevent a recurrence.

7.11.3. SCAFFOLDING HANDOVER
Once the scaffolding has been erected and inspected, it shall be handed over to the party who requested

7.11.4. ALTERATIONS TO EXISTING SCAFFOLDING
If an alteration is required, it shall only be carried out by a competent person. Depending on the extent of
the modification, the design calculation may need to be repeated to ensure that the new design is still
adequate. The altered scaffolding shall be re-inspected prior to use.
7.12. SHARED SCAFFOLDING
In some instances, scaffolding may be used simultaneously or consecutively for different jobs or by
different groups or personnel at the same time. For each single group or party, it is important to be aware
of any other parties who may be using the same scaffold, the nature of the work and to coordinate all
activities including access.
When there are two or more parties using the scaffolding simultaneously or the scaffolding is to be used
for more than one purpose, the following requirements shall be considered before the scaffolding is
designed:
(i) Which party or group will the scaffolding be handed over to?
(ii) The duration of use by each party;
(iii) Ensure that the width of the working platform and access/egress routes are sufficient to cope
with all workers;
(iv) Ensure that the scaffolding is designed to the higher class considering that all parties could
be using the scaffolding simultaneously.
(v) Ensure that the scaffolding is designed to fulfil the requirements of all parties, so that
alterations are minimized.
7.13. SCAFFOLDING DISMANTLING
The dismantling of scaffolding shall be planned so that the scaffolding remains stable, workers are

and ties and by restricting the imposed loads due to stacked scaffolding components;
(iv) The procedure of dismantling shall be clear, in order, planned appropriately and dismantling
proceed generally from the top in horizontal sections;

SCAFFOLDING DESIGN INFORMATION
The requirements are provided for scaffoldings constructed from prefabricated components or from tubes and
couplers, such as façade scaffolding. Other scaffoldings shall comply with their relevant international standards.
General
Every area for access and working shall be so arranged as to provide a convenient working place, and to:
(i) protect people from the risk of falling;
(ii) provide safe storage of materials and equipment; and
(iii) protect those below from falling objects.
Attention shall be paid to ergonomic considerations. The area shall be fully decked and shall be provided with
appropriate side protection when ready for use.
Connections between separate parts shall be effective and easy to monitor. They shall be easy to assemble and
secure against accidental disconnection.
Width Classes
The width is the full width of the working area including up to 30 mm of the toe board. Width shall be selected as
per seven width classes provided in BS EN 12811-1.
The clear distance between standards shall be at least 600 mm; the clear width of stairways shall not be less
than 500 mm.
Headroom
The minimum clear headroom between working areas shall be 1.90 m.
Working Areas
(i) It shall be possible to secure platform units against dangerous displacement e.g. unintended dislodging or
uplifting by wind forces;
(ii) Platform units should have a slip-resistant surface. A timber surface normally meets the requirements for
slip-resistance. The risk of tripping from any method used to secure the platform unit or from overlapping
should be minimized;
(iii) Each end of a platform, unless cleated or otherwise restrained by hooks or equivalent means, shall extend
over the centreline of its support at least 150mm;
(iv) The gaps between platform units shall be as small as possible but not exceeding 25 mm; and
(v) Working areas shall be as level as practicable. If the slope exceeds 1 in 5, securely attached full width
footholds shall be provided. Except that, where necessary, there may be gaps not exceeding a width of
100 mm in the centre of the footholds to facilitate the use of wheelbarrows.
Principal Guardrail
The principal guardrail shall be fixed so that its top surface is 1 m or more above the adjacent level of the working
area everywhere (absolute minimum height 950 mm).

Toe board
A toe board shall be fixed so that its top edge is at least 150 mm above the adjacent level of the working area.
Holes and slots in a toe board shall, except for handling holes be no larger than 25 mm in one direction.
Side Protection
Working and access areas shall be safeguarded by a side protection consisting of at least a principal guardrail,
intermediate side protection and a toe board. The toe board may be dispensed with on stairways. Side protection
shall be secured against unintended removal.
Base Plates and Base Jacks
The strength and rigidity of the base plates and base jacks shall be sufficient to ensure that it can transmit the
maximum design load from the working scaffold to the foundations. The area of the end plate shall be a minimum
of 150 cm2. The minimum width shall be 120 mm.
Base plates made of steel shall conform to EN 74.
Base jacks shall be provided with a centrally positioned adjusting spindle of such dimensions that, in the unloaded
condition, the greatest inclination of the axis of the shaft from the axis of the standard does not exceed 2.5 %.
The minimum overlap length at any position of adjustment shall be 25 % of the total length of the shaft, or 150
mm whichever is greater. The thickness of the endplate shall be at least 6 mm. Shaped endplates shall have at
least the same rigidity.
Access and Egress
Safe access to and egress from scaffolding during the erection, use and dismantling shall be provided by
considering the following:
(i) temporary stair towers or portable ladder access systems installed at the start of erection, progressed with
the scaffolding and used by the scaffolder whenever reasonably practicable;
(ii) permanently installed platforms or ramps;
(iii) build-in access for mobile towers/system scaffolding;
(iv) mechanical personnel hoists used in conjunction with permanent stairs or temporary stair towers which
are to be used in an emergency or in the event of a power failure; and
(v) using the existing stairs of a building, provided such access is safe.
