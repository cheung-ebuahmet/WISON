1. PURPOSE
The determination and identification of HSE critical equipment & systems (HSECES) is a very significant activity to aid asset
integrity. The prioritization of maintaining HSECES functionality is foremost in company’s management of assets.
The purpose of this document is to provide detailed guidance to appropriate technical staff to assign correct HSE Critical
Equipment & Systems (HSECES) group to the operational asset tags/locations. This guideline is intended to increase the accuracy
of HSECES & categorization of equipment tags in MAXIMO, (where HSECES tags/locations are normally are recorded) in a
consistent manner across all GASCO sites.
The guide is aimed at establishing common principles, guidance and assumptions to result in consistency of identification across
all GASCO assets. Accurate HSECES identification & categorization is a pre‐requisite for effective implementation of HSECES
performance standard.
2. OBJECTIVES
The specific objectives of this procedure are:
1. Accurate & consistent identification of HSECES tags/locations for MAXIMO operational asset tags/locations for all
HSECES categories in line with asset COMAH reports and GASCO HSECES Management manual;
2. To assign the correct HSECES category based on their MAH management role; and
3. To assign (if appropriate) a second HSECES category to the HSECES tags/locations.
3. DEFINITIONS
HSECES: Health Safety Environment Critical Equipment System
Major Accident (or MAH): Major Accident means an ‘uncontrolled occurrence’, in the operation of a site or pipeline which leads
to severe or catastrophic consequences to people, assets, the environment and/or company reputation [for further details and
examples, refer ADNOC CoP V6‐01].
Performance Standard: A statement which can be expressed in qualitative or quantitative terms, of the performance required of
a system, item of equipment or computer programme, and which is used as the basis for the assurance and verification of
HSECES integrity throughout the life cycle of the installation, including project execution.
CMMS: Computerised Maintenance Management System (MAXIMO)
4. INTER‐RELATIONSHIPS & CUSTOMERS
This Procedure is part of GASCO Integrity Assurance (IA) Division corporate procedures. The site technical authority are
responsible to identify HSECES tags/locations. MAXIMO engineers (or Major project contractors) who responsible to incorporate
HSECES tag/location status in MAXIMO are the intended users of this procedure.

5. PROCESS RESPONSIBILITY
It is the responsibility of designated site/project GASCO staff to ensure that all HSECES operational tags are correctly identified

6. WORK PROCEDURE (INCL. PROCESS MAP)
The following key considerations and assumptions have been made in this HSECES Identification procedure:
 The justification to categorize tags as HSECES will depend on their significant Process Safety management role in
relation to GASCO Major Accident Hazards (MAHs). These are identified in asset COMAH reports.;
 It is expected that all operational locations/tags for all sites are included in MAXIMO;
 Locations/tags will be added and HSECES status assigned (using the HSECES identification & Categorization
procedure) as required due to plant modifications and projects (Technical /Major Projects); and
 If required, IA Technical Integrity Specialist shall resolve any conflicts that may arise during the HSECES decision
making process in discussion with the appropriate Technical Authority (TA).
Approach:
The HSECES identification should follow a systematic approach and should work HSECES Group by Group in line with the flow
chart included in Appendix 2 by following the 6 (Six) step process described below.
The HSECES identification process explained in this procedure is to enable a review & validation of the accuracy of existing
locations/tags in MAXIMO. In case locations/ tags are not available in MAXIMO for any of the HSECES categories; these shall be
discussed and agreed with the respective section heads and included in MAXIMO.
The six step process is explained below to provide the required coverage, accuracy and consistency while assigning HSECES
status to operational asset locations/tags. The HSECES identification project pre‐requisites include accurate asset register and
availability of a project team (using internal resources or using external consultants). Sites should designate focal point for the
smooth progress of HSECES Implementation project. Please note that unless HSECES Categorization is complete, the HSECES
performance standards Implementation should not begin.
The cross‐functional flow diagram (Appendix 3) illustrates the roles & responsibilities to carry out the HSECES Categorization
process.

Step 1: Identify asset‐specific HSECES groups from COMAH
Refer asset HSECES Register in the latest asset COMAH report and list all applicable HSECES Groups for the site/plant.
HSECES Asset‐ Applicability Table is included as Appendix 4 for reference.
Step 2: Download asset register from MAXIMO & allocate operational locations to appropriate site Asset
Groups/engineering disciplines
Download all operating asset locations/tags from MAXIMO and include in the Asset HSECES Record (template included
in Appendix 1). The locations/tags that belong to the ‘Asset Group’/engineering discipline groups (ELEC/INST, etc.)
should be allocated to the respective site section engineers for carrying out activities described in Step 3 & Step 4.
Step 3: Does the operating location or tag have a MAH management role?
The ‘Typical structure of HSECES Groups’ (Appendix 6) illustrates the hazard management using barriers/HSECES based
on their defined roles (Prevent/Control/Mitigate/Recovery). The groups HSECES category are set out in the COMAH
reports for each site, based upon identified hazards and accident scenarios. The categorization of specific equipment to
its HSECES role (Prevent/Control/Mitigate/Recovery) needs to be derived from consideration of specific credible hazard
release scenarios. These scenarios are often demonstrated in the COMAH in the form of bow tie diagrams/analysis.
If the detail contained within the COMAH is insufficient to identify specific equipment with a MAH role, then there
needs to be a careful assessment of what are the specific credible MAH scenarios, and then an assessment of HSECES
Groups, to equipment where it plays a specific MAH role. To perform this task there needs to be awareness of the
requirements to perform such a process hazard assessment.
If the location/tag has a MAH management role (or has a high SIL rating of 1 or above or high (safety impact) criticality
in RBI or RCM), then it will be a HSECES categorized or else, it will be identified as a non‐HSECES tag. If the
equipment/system has been ranked high criticality in RBI /SIL 1 (excluding SIL 1a) or higher in IPF assessments /high
criticality in RCM assessment, then the location/tag will be categorized as HSECES.
The HSECES identification guidelines provided in Appendix 4 may be referred to aid the decision. This is a critical step in
the HSECES Categorization process.
The application examples provided below will help the user to visualize credible MAH scenarios to decide if a
location/tag is HSECES or not.
If the location/tag has a MAH management role (or has a high SIL rating of 1 9excluding 1a) or above or high criticality
in RBI or RCM), then it will be a categorized as HSECES or else, it will be identified as a non‐HSECES tag.

The HSECES identification guidelines provided in Appendix 5 may be referred to take a decision. This is a critical step in
the HSECES Categorization process.
A few examples are provided below to illustrate the process explained in this Step:
Example 1:
A smoke detector installed in a residential camp/a room does not have a major hazard management role since no
major hazards are identified outside of the process area / camp area/rooms. In this case, the location/tag should be
assigned non‐HSECES status.
Example 2:
A structure supporting hydrocarbon piping should be typically identified as a HSECES in STRUCT group since failure of
this structure can lead to loss of containment of hydrocarbon (MAH).
Example 3:
A Strainer located within hydrocarbon piping should typically not be identified as HSECES since its failure will not
typically lead to loss of containment of hydrocarbon (MAH) because the strainer is located within the piping.
Example 4:
Pressure Relief Valve on gas separator would be a HSECES, though a Pressure Relief Valve on air tool compressor would
not be an HSECES tag.
Appendix 5 contains more specific guidance to carry out this Step accurately.
Step 4a: Populate HSECES Record by assigning HSECES status to locations
The relevant plant section engineers for various asset groups shall review and assign the appropriate HSECES status
(Note 1) and include justifications for all operating asset locations/tags using Step 3 in accordance with the HSECES
Groups identified in Asset COMAH HSECES Register. The details of identified HSECES locations/tags shall be populated
in HSECES Record (template provided in Appendix 1) along with the justifications. The appropriate HSECES categories
should be included in the HSECES record (and later in MAXIMO).
The recording the justification of HSECES is very important for any future reviews of data. The reason for categorizing as
HSECES should be indicated.
Note 1: In MAXIMO, there are two fields (Category 1 & Category 2) that can be assigned for each of the HSECES
locations/tags based on their HSECES function. For instance, a flammable gas detector located in hazardous area will
have 2 HSECES categories; e.g. Category 1 will be FGD and Category 2 will be CEE. A few examples are provided below
for guidance.

Step 4b: Validate HSECES List
Once the assessed HSECES status is assigned for all operating locations/tags (as per Step 3) by section engineers, site
section heads shall validate the HSECES location/tag list to ensure correctness and consistency. In addition, each section
engineer/consultant has to ensure that no major safety critical equipment classes are missing in MAXIMO. In case some
missing equipment classes are found then new locations have to be created in MAXIMO in line with the site control
procedure.
Step 5: Incorporate Validated HSECES details in MAXIMO
Site MAXIMO support shall revise the MAXIMO records in line with validated HSECES status.
Step 6: Verify HSECES status in MAXIMO
HSECES Project lead/Site HSECES Focal point/Site MAXIMO team shall verify the HSECES records of operational asset
locations/tags in MAXIMO against the validated HSECES list.
Common issues that might be encountered in HSECES Categorization & potential solutions:
It is possible that the site engineers encounter some of the following issues during the HSECES identification process
and the solutions for these common issues are provided below for guidance:
 While reviewing & assigning HSECES status to MAXIMO locations/tags, section engineers may find the
location/tag as non HSECES and might assign NA (Not Applicable) status for both HSECES categories based on
his understanding/responsibility. Once all the locations/tags are assigned by all discipline engineers, the NA
status assigned for all locations/tags shall be reviewed in a workshop (all designated discipline engineers who
reviewed & assigned HSECES status to participate) before validation step and correct HSECES status. Once they
are agreed in workshop, corrections shall be made in MAXIMO. The HSECES Record should be corrected
accordingly.
 Although it is ideal that one HSECES category gets assigned to one site section (ELEC/INST, etc.), however, this
may not be always be the case. Multiple site sections might be responsible (review, PM alignment,
performance assurance, etc.) for the HSECES categories. For the sake of clarity and guidance for users, the
responsible Asset Group/ site discipline are included in HSECES Applicability Table in Appendix 4.
