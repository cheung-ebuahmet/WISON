1. INTRODUCTION
Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC), as part of its Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) policy,
commits to ensure that a safe working environment is maintained in all areas and that all personnel who
undertake cutting, welding and allied processes are fully aware of the hazards involved and have the
competence, training and awareness to undertake such activity.
Cutting process involves separating or severing a piece of metal through intense heat generated to melt
the metal. It includes the separation of metal materials by the application of heat, plasma arc or a high
pressure water jet.
Welding is a process of joining two or more pieces of metal together to form a single piece. Molten metal
is generated through an intense heat source, such as oxygen and fuel gas or an electrical arc.
The allied processes include but are not limited to arc welding, oxy-fuel welding, open-flame shouldering,
brazing, thermal spraying, oxygen cutting, arc cutting and gouging.
By products resulting from the processes include fumes and other noxious gases which may pose a
serious health hazard. Moreover, safety hazards may exist such as the potential for fire and/or explosion,
injuries from arc radiation, open flame, electrical shock and material handling.
2. PURPOSE
The purpose of this Standard is to provide an overall understanding of cutting, welding and allied
processes, and its application within the ADNOC Group. This Standard establishes requirements for
activities requiring the use of cutting, welding and allied processes within the ADNOC Group.
All works related to cutting, welding and allied processes carried out for ADNOC Group shall be in
accordance with the requirements of this Standard.
3. SCOPE
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates &
Functions in ADNOC Head Quarter, Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors.
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood,
implemented and thoroughly monitored.

7.2. HAZARDS ASSOCIATED WITH CUTTING, WELDING AND ALLIED PROCESSES
The hazards associated to cutting, welding and allied processes include but not limited to the following:
7.2.1. FIRE AND EXPLOSION HAZARDS
Fire and explosion hazards are mainly caused with the gas supply system, high temperature of the flame
used or the hot slag produced in the process. These hazards include:
(i) Fire and explosion resulting from the release of flammable fuel gases or oxygen into the
atmosphere from damaged gas hose or piping, or from leaks at joints, hose connections or
fittings of the gas supply system, or as a result of valves of the gas supply line or blowpipe
being left open inadvertently;
(ii) Fire and explosion in the gas supply system caused by:
1. Flashback from the blowpipe due to fuel gas supply lines not completely purged before
lighting up, or back-feeding of fuel gas into the oxygen line or vice versa;
2. Decomposition or detonation of acetylene in the absence of oxygen or air due to
flashback at the blowpipe or overheating of gas cylinder; or
3. High-pressure oxygen gas (without fuel gas) which promotes combustion of materials
such as oil, grease, organic compounds, aluminium and its alloys, and elastomers
used in valve seats and seals.
(iii) Explosions from over-pressurization of the gas supply system;
(iv) Fire arising from the flammable or combustible residue in the workpiece; and
(v) Fire from the ignition of flammable or combustible materials in the vicinity by the flame of the
blowpipe, hot surfaces of the workpiece or hot slag from the process.
7.2.2. ELECTRICAL HAZARDS
The use of electrical equipment involves a risk of electric shock or electrocution which may result in
serious burns or death. Electric shock or electrocution can occur through direct contact with the electrode,
live parts, the work piece, or through contact with a device such as an unearthed cable or tool. ADNOC
Electrical Safety Standard [Ref. 24] shall be referred for working near or with electrical equipment.
7.2.3. RADIATION HAZARDS
They can produce following types of radiation:
(i) Intense visible (VIS) light.
(ii) Ultraviolet (UV) radiation.
(iii) Infrared (IR) radiation.
The radiation may have the following effects:
(i) Tans, burns or irritation to the skin;
(ii) Long-term exposure may cause skin cancer; and
(iii) Injures the cornea and mucous membrane.

7.2.4. CHEMICAL HAZARDS
The following chemical hazards are envisaged during the activity:
(i) Nitric oxide formed from oxygen and nitrogen in the air, which is toxic, has a delayed action,

agent;

is completely dry, is toxic and has a delayed action;

ADNOC Chemical Hazards Standard [Ref. 19] shall be referred for assessing chemical hazards.
7.2.5. HEALTH HAZARDS
Health hazards of gas welding and flame cutting are mainly due to the radiation and toxic fumes or gases
emitted during the process. The resultant health problems include:
(i) Eye injuries, such as
1. Eye discomfort and burns from the intense light and heat emitted from the activity;
2. Heat cataract caused by radiation from molten metal, leading to inability to see things
clearly; or
3. Corneal damage and conjunctivitis from foreign particles e.g. slag and cutting sparks.
(ii) Skin irritation and reddening due to over exposure to radiation;
(iii) Illness like metal fumes fever due to inhalation of fumes or gases formed during the process;
(iv) Increased risk of lung cancer, possibly cancer of the larynx (voice box) and urinary tract;
(v) Personal injuries arising from
1. Handling gas cylinders or large workpieces; and
2. Scattering of hot particles of metals during the activity.
7.2.6. OTHER HAZARDS
(a) Heat
The sparks generated through the intense heat of the activity can cause burns. Eye injuries can
result from contact with hot spattered slag, metal chips and sparks. In addition, excessive exposure
to heat can result in heat stress or heat stroke. Personnel shall be aware of the symptoms, such
as fatigue, dizziness, loss of appetite, nausea, abdominal pain, and irritability. ADNOC Physical
Hazards Standard [Ref. 18] shall be referred for hazards associated due to exposure to heat.

7.3. RISK MANAGEMENT PROCESS
7.3.1. HAZARD IDENTIFICATION
The first step in managing risks associated with the activity is to identify all hazards [Refer to Section 7.2]
that could potentially cause harm to people. These shall be identified by:
(i) Conducting a walk through assessment of the workplace;
(ii) Observing the work and talking to workers about how the activity is carried out;
(iii) Inspecting plant and equipment used during the activity;
(iv) Reading product labels, Safety Data Sheets (SDS) and manufacturer’s instruction manuals; and
(v) Reviewing incident reports.
7.3.2. RISK ASSESSMENT
ADNOC Group shall ensure risk assessment for all the work related to cutting, welding and allied
processes. The factors to be considered in risk assessment include, but not limited to the following:
(i) The fuel gas and oxidant gas required and the gas supply system;
(ii) The working environment, such as
1. Ventilation of the workplace;
2. Working in specific environments, such as confined space, pressurized or oxygen-
rich environment;

ADNOC HSE Risk Management System [Ref. 30] for the activity, the following hierarchy of controls shall
be adopted (Refer to Figure 7.3.1):

(a) Elimination
This includes removing the hazard from the workplace. This is the most effective control measure
and shall always be considered before any other control measure. For example, redesign the job
or substitute a substance so that the hazard is removed or eliminated. If eliminating the hazard is
not reasonably practicable, then substitution, isolation, engineering and procedural control
measures, or a combination of these control measures to minimize the risk shall be considered.
(b) Substitution
Replace a hazardous process or material with one that is less hazardous; for example, using
submerged arc welding instead of flux-cored wire welding shall reduce the risk of exposure to

7.4.1. VENTILATION
(a) Key Role of Ventilation
Ventilation removes heat from the environment and reduces exposure to fumes and other
atmospheric contaminants in the work area. The major functions of ventilation in the workplace
include:
(i) Removing gas from leakage (e.g. hoses), if any, so as to prevent accumulation of
flammable/explosive mixtures in the atmosphere;
(ii) Removing heat as well as hazardous fumes and gases such as metal and metal oxide fumes,
oxides of nitrogen, fluorides arising from the cutting or welding activity; and
(iii) Supplying fresh air to meet the respiratory needs of persons inside the workplace.
(b) Types of Ventilation
For workplace involving cutting, welding and allied processes, the key major types of ventilation
other than natural ventilation that shall be considered are:
(i) General Dilution Ventilation: The ventilation where fresh air is introduced in workplace by
mechanical means and is mixed thoroughly with the contaminants in the air by removing the
contaminants out of the workplace.
(ii) Mechanical Dilution Ventilation: Mechanical dilution ventilation uses wall fans, roof exhaust
fans, or other mechanical means to prevent airborne contaminants from entering a person’s
breathing zone.
(iii) Local Exhaust Ventilation (LEV): The ventilation where air contaminants are captured and
removed by forced air current through hood and duct near the point of emission and
discharged to the atmosphere outside the workplace.
(c) General Requirements
(i) Adequate ventilation shall be provided for all cutting, welding and allied processes such that
personnel exposures to hazardous concentrations of airborne contaminants below UAE
regulatory limits (refer to ADNOC OHRM Standard [Ref. 17] for guidance on applicable
Occupational Exposure Limits (OEL));
(ii) Mechanical ventilation shall be supplied when there is less than 10,000 cubic feet or 283
cubic meters of space per welder [Ref. 8];
(iii) Mechanical ventilation shall be supplied when the ceiling or overhead height is less than 16
feet [Ref. 8];
(iv) Contaminated air exhausted into the open air from ventilation shall be sufficiently separated
from air intakes such that it will not be recirculated into the space;
(v) Respiratory protection as defined in Section 7.4.5 shall be used when ventilation is
inadequate or impracticable.
(d) Ventilation Considerations
(i) Ventilation may be natural, mechanical, or provided by a positive pressure hood;
(ii) Natural ventilation is acceptable where necessary precautions have been taken to ensure
that the fume plume is kept away from the breathing zone and where air sampling has
determined that concentrations of contaminants are below the regulatory allowable limits;
(iii) Mechanical ventilation shall include local exhaust, local forced air, and general air
mechanical movement. Local exhaust is the preferred method of ventilation;

7.4.2. FIRE PREVENTION
(a) Areas Containing Combustibles and Flammable Materials
(i) No cutting or welding activities shall be authorized unless the atmosphere is non-
flammable and combustibles or flammable materials are moved away or protected from
fire hazards;
(ii) Where practicable, the work shall be removed to the fabrication bay. Where it is not
practicable to move the work, all moveable combustibles and flammable materials with a
radius of 10.7 meters [Ref. 8] shall be relocated to a safe location. Where the work and
the combustibles or flammable materials are not moveable, safeguards shall be employed to
protect the combustibles or flammable materials from fire hazards such as hot sparks,
molten spatter, electric arcs, or open flames;
(iii) Where combustible floors are present, they shall be clean and protected by wetting with
water or covering with damp sand, sheet metal, or equivalent non-combustible or non-
flammable material. Wood floors laid directly on concrete shall not require wetting; and
(iv) All cracks or openings in floors or walls shall be sealed or covered with non-combustible
materials. Open doors and windows into adjacent areas shall be closed.
(b) Welding or Cutting of Containers
(i) Empty containers may have residual materials in cracks or crevices which may release
hazardous vapors or fumes when heated by welding or cutting activity. By products of
internal corrosion may form dangerous and explosive hydrogen mixtures inside a container,
even containers which normally hold water shall be considered hazardous unless declared
safe; and
(ii) Containers shall be rendered safe by cleaning and purging with nitrogen or by other
acceptable methods.

Welding habitats are designed to provide a safe and controlled work area where naked flame or
hot work activities such as welding, grinding, cutting, can be carried out in classified hazardous
areas without the need for a plant/production shutdown. The use of habitats shall be considered
for Welding/Cutting activities in Hazardous classified areas.

7.4.5. PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT
The general requirements of ADNOC Personal Protective Equipment Standard [Ref. 26], are applicable.

(a) Eye and Face Protection
The arc emits a high intensity ultraviolet light which could damage the eye if viewed without
protection. Therefore welding arcs shall never be viewed with naked eye at a distance closer than
50.0 ft. (15.2 m).
A welding helmet shall be used to protect eyes and face from harmful light and burns from weld
spatter. Welding helmets are constructed from a pressed fibre insulating material (shell material)
with an adjustable padded headband. It fits over the head and can be swung upward when not in
use. This encourages the user to refrain from removing it.
The helmet shall be dull black in colour to minimize reflection from the intense light, The chief
advantages of the helmet is that it is comfortable and does not need to be held, leaving both hands
free to weld.
The protective welding helmet is equipped with lens (filter) holders. For added protection against
the intensity of arc radiation, the filters are usually coloured green, blue, or brown. Be sure that the
filters are the correct shade for arc welding (see Appendix 1) and are tempered to avoid breaking
if hit by flying weld spatter. The coloured lens is protected from molten metal spatter by using a
cover glass or plate over the lens. This shall be replaced it if it becomes damaged or spotted by
weld spatter.

7.5.1. OXY-FUEL GAS WELDING AND CUTTING SAFETY
(a) Oxygen and Combustibles
(i) Keep Oxygen from Combustibles:
1. Oxygen cylinders, cylinder valves, couplings, regulators, hoses, and apparatus shall
be kept free from oil, grease, and other flammable or explosive substances.
2. Oxygen cylinders or apparatus shall not be handled with oily hands or gloves.
(ii) Prohibited Uses for Oxygen:
1. Oxygen shall not be used as a substitute for compressed air. Oxygen shall not be
used in pneumatic tools, in oil preheating burners, to start internal combustion engines,
to blow out pipelines, to dust clothing or work, or to create pressure for ventilation or
similar applications.
2. Jets of oxygen shall not be permitted to strike an oily surface, greasy clothing, or enter
fuel oil or other storage tanks.
(iii) Oxygen Equipment:
1. Oxygen cylinders, equipment, pipelines, or apparatus shall not be used
interchangeably with any other gas.
(b) Attachments for Gas Mixing:
(i) No device or attachment facilitating or permitting mixtures of air or oxygen with flammable
gases prior to consumption, except at a burner or in a torch, shall be allowed unless
approved for the purpose.
(c) Backfires and Flashbacks
(i) Backfires: A backfire is when the flame burns back into the torch, often with a sharp bang.
This may happen when the torch is held too close to the workpiece, or if the nozzle is partly
blocked. The flame may go out or it may reignite at the nozzle. Sometimes the flame burns
back into the torch, and burning continues inside it. Backfires do not usually cause injury or
damage, but they can indicate a fault in the equipment. In case a backfire occurs appropriate
response shall be taken as mentioned in Section 7.6.3 (b).
(ii) Flashbacks: Flashbacks are commonly caused by a reverse flow of oxygen into the fuel gas
hose (or fuel into the oxygen hose), producing an explosive mixture within the hose. The
flame can then burn back through the torch, into the hose and may even reach the regulator
and the cylinder. Flashbacks can result in damage or destruction of equipment, and could
even cause the cylinder to explode. The following precautions will help to prevent flashbacks:

1. Use the correct lighting-up procedure. Purge the hoses before lighting the torch to
remove any potentially explosive gas mixtures. Use a spark igniter and light the gas
quickly after turning it on;
2. Make sure the blowpipe is fitted with spring-loaded non-return valves;
3. Use the correct gas pressures and nozzle size for the job;
4. Maintain the equipment in good condition.
These measures will reduce the risk of a flashback but will not completely eliminate it. Non-return
valves will not stop a flashback once it has occurred. However the flashback arrestor provides a
certain measure of protection against the hazards of flashback.
(d) Flashback Arrestor
There are two types of arrestors:
(i) A torch-mounted arrestor provides the first line of defence for the operator and his equipment
against damage resulting from flashback, and flame entering the attached hoses. It shall be
used in conjunction with a regulator-mounted arrestor.
(ii) A regulator-mounted arrestor is installed at the outlet of the gas regulator valve on the
cylinder. It provides primary protection for the entire gas supply system against flashback
and is the only arrestor that protects against burnback.
All Flashback arrestors shall be marked with the date of manufacture shown as month and year.
(e) Torches
(i) Connections shall be checked for leaks after assembly and before lighting the torch;
(ii) Before lighting the torch for the first time each day, hoses shall be purged individually;
(iii) A friction lighter, stationary pilot flame, or other suitable source of ignition shall be used.
Matches, cigarette lighters, or welding arcs, shall not be used for lighting torches;
(iv) In confined spaces, the torch valves shall be closed and in addition, the fuel gas and oxygen
supply to the torch shall be positively shut off at a point outside the confined area whenever
the torch is not to be used, such as during lunch or overnight. Unattended torches and hoses
shall be removed from the confined space;
(v) Ensure torch inlet threads and nozzle seats are in good condition with no oil or grease
contamination on either inlet;
(vi) Close all valves on the torch before opening the regulator valve;
(vii) Never hang a lighted torch over a cylinder or regulator;
(viii) Torches shall be dead man’s type.
(f) Hose and Hose Connections
(i) Hose for oxy-fuel gas service shall comply with the Rubber Manufacturers Association IP-7
Specification for Rubber Welding Hose;
(ii) Hoses for oxy-fuel gas service shall be color coded - Red for Acetylene and Blue for Oxygen;
(iii) When parallel lengths of oxygen and fuel gas hose are taped together for convenience and
to prevent tangling, not more than 4 inches (100 millimeters) in each 12 inches (300
millimeters) shall be covered by tape;
(iv) Hose showing leaks, burns, worn places, or other defects rendering it unfit for service shall
be replaced.

(g) Pressure-Reducing Regulators
(i) Pressure reducing regulators shall be used only for the gas and pressures for which they
are labelled;
(ii) Union nuts and connections on regulators shall be inspected before use to detect faulty
seats which may cause leakage when the regulators are attached to cylinder valves or hoses.
Damaged nuts or connections shall be replaced;
(iii) When an approved device such as a hose check valve or flash-back arrestor is used in an

accordance with the manufacturers’ instructions;
(iv) Gauges used for Oxygen service shall be marked “USE NO OIL”;
(v) Regulators shall be drained of oxygen before they are attached to a cylinder or manifold, or
before the cylinder valve is opened. Oxygen cylinder or manifold valves shall always be
opened slowly.
(h) Cylinder Storage, Handling and Use
The storage, handling and use of cylinders shall be in line with ADNOC Compressed Gas Cylinders
Standard [Ref. 22]. As a minimum, the following precautions shall be considered:
(i) The storage temperature of the cylinder contents shall not be allowed to exceed 125°F
(52°C);
(ii) Cylinders shall be stored where they will not be exposed to physical damage, tampering, or
subject to temperatures which would raise the contents above the limits;
(iii) Cylinders shall be stored away from elevators, stairs, or gangways in assigned places where
cylinders will not be knocked over or damaged by passing or falling objects;
(iv) Cylinders shall be secured in storage to prevent falling;
(v) Acetylene shall not be utilized at a pressure in excess of 15 psig (103 kPa) or 30 psia (206
kPa);
(vi) Cylinder valve protection caps should be kept with the cylinders so they can be reassembled
when the regulator is removed;
(vii) When a high-pressure (non-liquefied) gas cylinder is in use, the valve shall be opened fully
in order to prevent leakage around the valve stem;
(viii) Cylinders shall be kept far enough away from actual welding or cutting operations so that
sparks, hot slag, or flame will not reach them, otherwise fire resistant shields shall be
provided;
(ix) Cylinders shall not be placed where they might become part of an electrical circuit;
(x) Cylinders should not be so grounded, or located, where they can become part of an electric
circuit. Arc damaged cylinders may leak or explode.
(i) Cylinder Manifold
(i) All manifolds and parts shall be used only for the gases for which they are approved;
(ii) Fuel gas and Oxygen manifold capacity limits and locations shall be in accordance with
NFPA 51;

7.5.2. ELECTRON BEAM WELDING AND CUTTING SAFETY
Apart from the generic hazards mentioned earlier, the following potential associated with electron beam
welding shall be guarded against:
(a) Electric Shock
(i) All doors and access panels on electron beam welding equipment shall be properly secured
and interlocked to prevent accidental or unauthorized access;
(ii) All high-voltage conductors shall be fully enclosed by grounded, conductive barriers that are
also interlocked; and
(iii) A grounding probe shall be used before servicing the electron beam gun and high-voltage
power supplies.

(b) Gas and Fumes
(i) Positive exhaust ventilation and filtering from the medium and non-vacuum Electron Beam

(i) The leaded glass used in the viewing ports shall provide sufficient optical viewing protection

7.5.3. BRAZING AND SOLDERING SAFETY
Apart from the generic hazards mentioned earlier, the following potential hazards associated with brazing
and soldering activity shall be guarded against:
(a) Flammable and Corrosive Substances.
(i) Isolate containers of alcohol-based or alcohol-containing flux solutions from open flames
and heat sources during assembly as well as when in storage;
(ii) As fluxes are corrosive substances, they shall be stored, transported, and disposed of in
accordance with regulatory practices for acidic materials;
(iii) The SDS for flammable and corrosive substances shall be consulted before these
substances are used. Brazing furnace atmospheres shall be purged using safe procedures
before the introduction of flammable gases.
(b) Burns
(i) All assemblies, tools, and surfaces that are at high temperatures shall be labelled with the
appropriate sign;
(ii) When dip brazing, assemblies and fixtures to be immersed in the molten salt bath shall be
completely dry;
(iii) Appropriate precautions when handling aluminium to prevent serious burns from very hot
assemblies shall be taken;
(iv) Appropriate precautions when using hydrogen as a fuel gas to prevent burns to the
personnel shall be taken.
(c) Fluxes and Fillers Metals
(i) Casual contact between the hands, face, nose, or mouth shall be avoided when handling
filler metals, solder, fluxes, or base materials;

7.6. EMERGENCY PREPAREDNESS
Emergency preparedness is vital in order to timely and effectively manage emergency situations. It shall:
(i) Identify the effect and impact of all possible emergency situations arising from the activities;
(ii) Establish emergency procedures;
(iii) Provide and maintain emergency equipment and other resources; and
(iv) Ensure that the personnel exposed to the activities familiarize themselves with all the
possible hazards.
7.6.2. EMERGENCY RESPONSE PROCEDURES AND EQUIPMENT
(i) Emergency Response Procedures shall be established for the ADNOC Group employees
and its contractors in the event of a situation presenting serious and imminent danger;
(ii) The procedure shall include guidance for stopping the work and move to a safe location;
(iii) Appropriate emergency equipment shall be provided for handling emergency situations.
Such equipment shall include but not limited to the following:
1. Fire alarm;
2. Fire-fighting equipment, such as fire hose, fire extinguishers, buckets of dry sand; and
3. First aid facilities, such as first aid kits.
(iv) Personnel shall be aware of the location and its use of all the emergency equipment.
The emergency response shall be in accordance to ADNOC Emergency Response and Crisis
Management Standard [Ref.14].
7.6.3. POTENTIAL EMERGENCY SITUATIONS AND RESPONSES
The sections below provide potential emergency situations associated with the activities and their
response guidelines:
(a) Gas Leakage
(i) Isolate the gas supplies by closing the valves of the gas cylinders or gas supply systems;
(ii) Inform all the personnel in the workplace;
(iii) Isolate all ignition sources, such as distinguishing flames; and
(iv) Evacuate the workplace and call the Emergency Response Team.
(b) Backfire/Burnback
(i) Close the oxygen valve of the blow pipe followed by the fuel valve;
(ii) Complete the shutting down procedure, which are as follows:
1. Open both valves at the blowpipe to vent the pressure in the equipment;
2. Close the outlets of the adjustable pressure regulators for both oxygen and fuel gas;
3. Close both valves at the blowpipe.
(iii) Investigate the cause and rectify the fault;
(iv) Re-light the blowpipe only after it is completely cooled down.

(c) Flashback or Fire in the Gas Hose
If there is a flashback into the gas hose and equipment, or a hose fire or explosion, or a fire at the
pressure regulator connections:
(i) Isolate the fuel gas and oxygen supplies by closing the cylinder valves only when this can
be done safely;
(ii) May attempt to control the fire by fire-fighting equipment only when there is no undue risk of
personal injury;
(iii) Activate the fire alarm and call the Emergency Response Team.
(d) Fire Involving Acetylene Cylinder
Any fire involving acetylene cylinder always poses a risk of cylinder explosion, and shall be dealt
with by the Emergency Response Team. However, under safe circumstances, the following initial
measures may be suitably adopted:
(i) Cool the cylinder by spraying with water only if it is safe to do so;
(ii) Close the cylinder valve to control the fire only if it is safe to do so;
(iii) Evacuate the building by activating the fire alarm or by any other means.
Note: Never attempt to remove an acetylene cylinder that has been involved in a fire, or move one
which has been affected by heat from a nearby fire even if it appears to have been cooled down.
Moving the cylinder may lead to explosion.
The Fire and Rescue response shall be in accordance to ADNOC Fire and Rescue Operations Standard
[Ref.15].
7.7. TRAINING AND COMPETENCY
A training program shall be provided to ensure that all persons involved in the activity acquire the
understanding, knowledge and skill necessary for the safe performance of all duties. Training shall include
but not limited to the following:
(i) The identification and assessment of risks associated with cutting, welding and allied
processes safety at the workplace;
(ii) Use of appropriate PPE when exposed to hazards related to the activities;
(iii) First aid treatment and Emergency Response Plan;
(iv) Follow rotational job plans and necessary breaks to minimize exposure to the activities;
(v) Welding activities shall only be carried out by welders qualified in accordance with
internationally accepted standards (e.g. AWS, ASME and API standards); and
(vi) Fire watch.
Periodic training shall be provided to personnel associated with cutting, welding and allied processes.
