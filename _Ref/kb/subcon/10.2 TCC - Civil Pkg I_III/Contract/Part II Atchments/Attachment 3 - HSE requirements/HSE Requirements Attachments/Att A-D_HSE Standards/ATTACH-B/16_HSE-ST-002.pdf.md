1. INTRODUCTION
GASCO (COMPANY) Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) Documentation is an integral part of the
COMPANY Health Safety and Environmental Management System (HSE-MS) and Process Safety
Management Standard.
1.1 Purpose
The purpose of this document is to provide a framework for conducting Project HSE Reviews
(PHSER) in line with the ADNOC HSE Codes of Practice (Best Practice note for the preparation
of Project HSE Plans, COPV1-13) and GASCO Process Safety Management Standard.
1.2 Applicability
The use of this document is a requirement for all those who may be involved in the FEED and
Engineering, Procurement, Construction, Commissioning and De-commissioning phases of
Projects. It is not applicable to Projects for which ‘de-minimus’ can be demonstrated (de-
minimus applies where there are no Major Accident Hazards, no Planned Significant
Environmental Impacts and no High Occupational Health Risks).
2. OBJECTIVES
 The objective of a PHSER is to provide assurance to the COMPANY that HSE hazards have been
identified and assessed and that appropriate controls have been, or will be, implemented to
reduce the identified HSE risks to a level that is As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP).
 The objective of a PHSER team is to conduct, within an agreed timetable, a qualitative audit at a
level of enquiry, which generally avoids getting involved in unnecessary detail. The team must
review the information as presented and avoid the temptation to indulge in 'opinion engineering'.
The responsibility for the design, construction, Commissioning and De-commissioning of facilities
rests at all times with the Project organization, including the Contractors involved. The
responsibility for subsequent operation rests with the COMPANY.
 The PHSER process is a high level multi-discipline HSE review, it is not a detailed business or QA
audit, HAZOP or an engineering design review. The success of a PHSER in identifying and resolving
any problematic issues, will come from a well experienced and dedicated team effort receiving
full management support.
3. SCOPE OF APPLICATION
This Standard describes the framework for managing PHSER reviews of all COMPANY Projects to
fulfill the ADNOC CoP requirements.
The Project HSE Review shall be conducted in five stages in GASCO Projects:
 PHSER Stage II - FEED or Basic Engineering
 PHSER Stage III – Detail Engineering Stage
 PHSER Stage IV - Construction
 PHSER Stage V – Pre-Commissioning / Commissioning
 PHSER Stage VII - Abandonment ( De-commissioning/Demolition/ Disposal)
Stage I (Conceptual Phase) and Stage VI (Operation Phase) PHSER will not be implemented in GASCO
Projects. As part of continual improvement to the PHSER, Stage III (Detailed Engineering) shall be
conducted in GASCO Projects in combination with Stage IV (Construction). Stage II (FEED or Basic
Engineering) and V (Pre-commissioning/ Commissioning) which are currently implemented in GASCO
Projects will be continued. Stage I and VI will be implemented in later stage after COMPANY
evaluation of implementation of PHSER Stage II, III & IV ( Combined) , V and VII as required by ADNOC
CoP.

4. PHSER MANAGEMENT
4.1 PHSER Review Initiation
The responsibility for planning and initiating the various stages of the PHSER process and the
completion of action items resides with COMPANY Senior Project Manager.
PHSER review for every stage of project shall be conducted as defined in section 5 (Timing of
PHSER) and shall ensure sufficient time to implement the findings and recommendations
arising out of PHSER review.
The COMPANY Senior Project Manager is responsible for the selection of the PHSER team for
the various stages of the PHSER process in consultation with HSE- Projects and Technical
Safety Group. He shall ensure that the Chairman of the team is both competent and
independent of the Project.
Project team shall be accountable for providing sufficient lead time for the PHSER Chairman
to prepare and for supporting availability of adequate resources necessary for an effective
PHSER.
The COMPANY Senior Project Manager is responsible for communicating the results of the
PHSER to SVP- Projects and providing regular updates of Action Item status.
4.2 Terms of Reference (TOR)
PHSERs shall be performed against an agreed Terms of Reference that defines scope of work,
study objectives, time scale, and team members. Typical contents of terms of reference are
shown in Appendix G.
4.3 Team Members
4.3.1 Chairman:
PHSER Chairman shall be a competent person appointed by Senior Project Manager and
approved by HSE, familiar with design aspects, construction, commissioning & de-
commissioning activities, QA/QC procedures and the PHSER process. He should be familiar
with international design standard, ADNOC code and legal requirements. It may be
appropriate to appoint an independent Third Party Consultant for this role if GASCO internal
resources from HSE or others are not available. .
The role of the Chairman is particularly demanding and requires sound common sense and
clarity of purpose. The Chairman's main responsibility is to achieve the objectives defined in
Terms of Reference. This requires being involved in finalizing the review scope and initial
preparation of the Terms of Reference, being involved in selecting PHSER team members,
leading and motivating the team and meeting the agreed TOR within the timeframe.
Chairman must ensure timely production of the draft and scheduling of the final PHSER
report.
At the initial meeting, the Chairman must brief the team thoroughly on the Terms of
Reference, the review scope, the objectives, and methodology. The Chairman should control
the meeting at the outset making it particularly clear that the PHSER is concerned solely with

acceptable levels of occupational health, safety, environmental, fire protection and energy

PHSER Chairman shall be accountable for delivery of a quality PHSER review and achieving the
objectives outlined in the PHSER terms of reference.
The Chairman shall have minimum 10 years of Oil and Gas industry experience. 3 years of

4.3.2 PHSER Team
PHSER team shall be selected in accordance with requirements of each review stage and
nature of the project. PHSER team members, if possible, shall be independent of the project
to provide an objective and comprehensive HSE review. Representatives of the Project being
reviewed are not part of the PHSER review team, but their participation and sharing of
information during the review are critical for an effective review. The team should be as small,
skilled and experienced as far as possible but shall be consistent with possession of full
understanding of the Project development & its technology. If practical, some continuity of
the review team from one stage to the next is desirable.
Review members typically include project management, an operations representative, and
HSE Representative(s).Team members should be selected with the required expertise to
support the terms of reference. The required review team is generally different for each stage
review. For example, the detailed engineering PHSER requires construction safety expertise;
whereas the pre start-up PHSER requires commissioning, start-up, and operations expertise.
End user representatives should include Department Head level for all PHSER stages.
Project representatives should provide the PHSER team with logistics, information gathering,
and scheduling of review sessions and interviews. PHSER team should not develop technical
solutions to any problems that it identified. PHSER Chairman may consult with appropriate
specialists outside of the core PHSER team if the experience of individuals on the review team
is not sufficient to address an issue and specialist advice is needed.
Team composition for each stage of PHSER are defined under respective PHSER sections in
Appendix A to F
4.4 Information Requirements
Project team should make information available for PHSER review. If key information is not
available, PHSER Chairman and team shall assess the importance of the information to the
integrity of the PHSER and decide to issue a recommendation to develop the information
The documentation requirement for each stages of PHSER are detailed under the respective
PHSER sections in this document. The documents listed are not exhaustive in nature, the
Chairman shall review the same and add to the list those documents as required for the PHSER
review.
4.5 PHSER Findings
PHSER team shall prioritize PHSER recommendations based on the targeted timing to
complete the action item. PHSER findings may also be prioritized on the relative severity of
the finding
The ‘findings and recommendations’ shall be brief, factual informative statements on
observations, with a clear recommendation stating optional or a required course of action.
Technical references should be included wherever possible to enable the recipient identify
the source of the concern.
The PHSER findings shall be risk ranked as low, medium and high according to ADNOC Risk
Assessment Matrix (RAM). The PHSER Chairman shall risk rank the PHSER findings as low,

The report findings and responses on any issue should generally avoid being judgmental,

4.6 PHSER Stage Reports
Upon completion of each project HSE review, a PHSER close-out meeting with COMPANY
Senior Project Manager shall be conducted to convey and agree upon the findings and
recommendations resulted from the PHSER review. During the close-out meeting the PHSER
chairman shall present the findings to the COMPANY Senior Project Manager.
The PHSER Chairman shall then issue a draft report with findings and recommendations to
COMPANY Corporate HSE, Senior Project Manager, and PHSER Team for review. The team
shall provide comments to the draft report. The report shall be updated as per the comments
provided and issued as final. The final report shall be addressed COMPANY HSE - Vice
President. The report shall be reviewed by HSE and PHSER Team for completeness. After the
acceptance of PHSER report by HSE - Vice President, the report shall be updated as per the
comments provided and issued as final. The final report shall be sent to COMPANY Senior
Project Manager for PHSER recommendation implementation. The project team and key
stakeholders may be copied as required. The PHSER report may be sent to an agreed
distribution list.
Report should be prepared as a resource for the project team and the next stage review team.
PHSER report shall document scope of the review, project systems, conclusions of review,
findings and recommendations of the review and the list of documents examined by the
PHSER team. Report should summarize the status of the project at the time of the review.
Refer to Appendix H.
Stage reports shall be produced in time to allow recommendations to be addressed before
the next review stage is initiated.
Project team shall develop a project response to each accepted PHSER recommendation in
the HSE Action Tracking Register maintained for the Project.
4.7 PHSER Close-Out Report
Project PHSER close-out report shall be prepared for each stage of the PHSER review. The
response provided in Project PHSER close-out report shall be reviewed by HSE, Projects and
Technical Safety staff with support from stake holders within the COMPANY. Focus shall be
made to recommendation listed as high priority actions. All actions related to HSE Critical
Equipment and System (HSECES) shall by default be high priority actions. High priority actions
shall be resolved before a project can proceed to the next stage and shall be deemed
necessary for approval of PHSER close-out Report.
Project shall not move to next stage prior to the approval of PHSER Close-out Report. Actions
shall be reviewed in the subsequent stage PHSERs to confirm appropriateness of the
resolution.
Subsequent PHSER teams should review previous PHSER reports to ensure continuity of the
review process and be able to ascertain relevant developments since the last review and
confirm that agreed upon actions have been adequately closed out.

5. TIMING OF PHSER
PHSER Stage II - FEED or Basic Engineering shall be carried during final stage of the FEED
PHSER Stage III - Detailed Engineering & PHSER Stage IV - Construction (Combined) shall be
scheduled near the 40% Construction after the completion of Detail Engineering.
PHSER Stage V - Pre-Commissioning / Commissioning shall be started when construction is nearing
completion and the facilities are approaching the pre-commissioning / commissioning phase – but
prior to the introduction of hydrocarbons into facilities.
PHSER Stage VII - Abandonment (De-commissioning/Demolition/ Disposal) shall be conducted after
ADNOC HSEIA Phase 4 requirements are completed.
6. REPORTING
A written Report is provided to COMPANY at each stage of the PHSER Process. Project Responses to
the findings of the review team are to be incorporated in the Report. A remedial Action Plan shall
be specified in the Report recommendations. It is recommended to upload the PHSER actions in
QUARTZ system.
For typical Report Format refer Appendix H.

9.1 APPENDIX A: PHSER Stage II - FEED or Basic Engineering
As the project FEED stage nears completion, a PHSER-II must be scheduled which will be conducted
by the COMPANY, with the assistance and participation of the design Consultant / Contractors to
clarify any design issues.
HSE representatives and integrity representatives should be part of the review team.
It is important that an appropriate amount of time is allocated for this stage PHSER because the review
is long and detailed. It is also important that the design team have Engineering hours left to amend or
correct any issues prior to FEED close out. The timing of this review is therefore critical and should be
scheduled carefully in the HSE Plan.
a. Objective - FEED or Basic Engineering
The stage II FEED PHSER is considered one of the most important, being the final opportunity to
incorporate requirements before the EPC tender and award. It is important that a detailed review is
conducted, ensuring that all HSE philosophies are incorporated and interpreted correctly within the
specifications.
The objectives of FEED stage PHSER are
 To ensure that HSE related aspects of engineering designs eliminate or mitigate Major Accident
Hazard (MAH); meet or exceed HSE and regulatory requirements; and meet HSE commitments in
HSEIAs and contractual agreements; and that satisfactory project codes and standards and design
philosophies have been established.
 To confirm that the applicable ADNOC CoP’s, specifically Best Available Technique (BAT), Best
Practices (BP) and Pollution Prevention and Controls have been studied and applied within the
early development of the design.
 To provide assurance that adequate identification of the HSE and energy issues associated with
the materials, substances, (feeds, products, - intermediate streams and discharges), processes
and the location has been carried out. This also includes high, intermediate and low pressure
flares.
 To confirm that the P&ID, project technical specification and project scope consider adequately
the impact of these health, safety and environmental hazards and their influence on detailed
design, procurement, fabrication, construction, testing, commissioning, operations, maintenance
and abandonment.
 To confirm that the proposed development complies with ADNOC Codes of Practice and
COMPANY HSE Standards and Procedures.
 To provide assurance that adequate identification of the HSE hazards associated with the Design
and the location of the new facilities has been carried out.
 To confirm that the HSE risks associated with these Hazards have been assessed and that
appropriate mitigating measures and controls have been, or will be, implemented.
 To confirm that the HSE and Asset Integrity requirements for all aspects of the facility are, or will
be, adequately defined in the Project documentation (EPC Scope).

The risk matrix is divided into three risk categories as defined below:
High Risk Unacceptably high
This level of risk exposes the Company to intolerable losses to People,
Assets, Environment or Reputation. The hazard should be eliminated or its risk
reduced to tolerable levels immediately.
Immediate Action Must be taken to lower the Risk.
Medium Risk Acceptable but must be managed by ALARP
The hazard(s) must be managed to reduce the frequency and/or the severity of
the hazardous events to ALARP.
Risk Reduction measures must be planned and documented
Low Risk Acceptable without requiring further action
Correction[s] may be applied as resources allow
Detailed breakdown of consequences for Four Risk Management Categories
People
Severity Description
5 Multiple fatalities - from an accident or occupational illness (e.g. poisoning, or cancer).
4 Single fatality or permanent total disability - from an accident or occupational
illness (poisoning, cancer, etc.).
3 Major injury or health effects (including Permanent Disability) – Affecting work
performance in the longer term, e.g. prolonged absence from work. Irreversible health
damage without loss of life, e.g. noise induced hearing loss, chronic back injuries.
2 Minor injury or health effects –Affecting work performance, e.g. restriction to
activities (restricted work case –RWC) or need to take a time off work to recover (lost
time incident – LTI). Limited, reversible health effects, e.g. skin irritation, food poisoning.
1 Slight injury or health effects (incl. first aid case –FAC and medical treatment case –
MTC). Not affecting work performance or causing disability
Assets
Severity Description
5 Extensive damage – Substantial or total loss of operation (costs in
excess of US$10,000,000).*
4 Major – Partial operation loss (2 weeks shutdown, costs up to US$10,000,000).*
3 Local damage – Partial shutdown (can be restarted but costs up to US$500,000).*
2 Minor damage – Brief disruption (costs less than US$100,000).*
1 Slight damage - No disruption to operation (costs less than US$10,000)*.

These losses in US$ are meant as guidance only. Individual ADNOC Group Company may use
more appropriate figures provided they are within the above quoted limits.
Environment
Severity Description
5 Massive effect – Persistent severe environmental damage or severe nuisance extending
over a large area. In terms of commercial or recreational use or nature conservation,
a major economic loss for the company. Constant, high exceedance of statutory or
prescribed limits
4 Major effect – Severe environmental damage. The company is required to take
extensive measures to restore polluted or damaged environment to its original
state. Extended exceedance of statutory or prescribed limits
3 Localized effect – Limited loss of discharges of known toxicity. Repeated exceedance
of statutory or prescribed limit. Affecting neighborhood. Spontaneous recovery of
limited damage within one year.
2 Minor effect – Contamination. Damage sufficiently large to attack the environment.
Single exceedance of statutory or prescribed criterion. Single complaint. No
permanent effect on the environment.
1 Slight effect – Local environmental damage. Within the fence and within systems.
Negligible financial consequences
Reputation
Severity Description
5 International impact – International public attention. Extensive adverse
attention in international media. National/international policies with potentially severe
impact on access to new areas, grants of licenses and/or tax legislation.
4 National impact – National public concern. Extensive adverse attention in the national
media. Regional/national policies with potentially restrictive measures and/or impact
on grant of licenses. Mobilization of action group.
3 Considerable impact – Regional public concern. Extensive adverse attention in local
media. Slight national media and/or local/regional political attention. Adverse
stance of local government and/or action groups.
2 Limited impact – Some local public concern. Some local media and/or political attention
with potentially adverse aspects for company operations.
1 Slight impact – Public awareness may exist, but there is no public concern
