1 PROJECT DESCRIPTION
1.1 Introduction
The Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 2 (RSHT-2) is owned by ADNOC and operated by
ADNOC Gas (AG) and is located approximately 240 km west-southwest of Abu Dhabi City in
the Ruwais Industrial Area. RSHT-2 facility is designed to handle bulk granulated sulphur
coming from Shah and Habshan plant via railcars and transported via new federal railway
(Union Railway) to Ruwais Terminal (SHT-2).
Based on ADNOC recent directives, the Sulphur Handling Scenario at Ruwais SHT-2 (Ruwais
Sulphur Granulation Plant-RSGP) will be developed further due to consideration of receiving
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur from Hail & Ghasha to the new plant, via pipeline. This new plant
will be located to the East of the BOROUGE terminal-3 & to the west of RSHT-2 as depicted
in Fig.1.1 below.
Figure 1: New SGP Site Location near Existing RSHT-2 Facility
1.2 Project Objective and Scope
The primary objective of the RSGP Development project at RSHT-2 is to accommodate
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur received via pipeline from the Hail & Ghasha OPP. This involves
constructing new infrastructure for liquid sulphur receiving storage and transfer, sulphur
forming (Granulation), Granulated sulphur transfer and storage, stacking and reclaiming and
export to existing ship loaders. The project also includes implementing dust control systems,
sulphur remelting unit, utility systems and integration with existing facilities. The developed

1.3 Purpose
This Emergency Response Plan (ERP) outlines the framework for preparedness, response,
and recovery in the event of emergencies arising from various types of incidents associated
with the Sulphur Granulation Plant Project. The plan ensures the safety of personnel,
protection of assets, preservation of the environment, continuity of business operations, and
compliance with applicable regulatory requirements.
This ERP applies to all Greenfield sites, work areas, and locations where the CONTRACTOR
and its Subcontractors are engaged in activities within the defined Project scope. In cases
where work is performed within an operational plant or controlled facility, all emergency
response actions and evacuation procedures shall fully comply with the established
emergency protocols and requirements of the plant or facility owner.
The plan is supported by the Company’s Facility Incident Management Team (IMT) and
Tactical Response Team (TRT), with clearly defined procedures and responsibilities to
minimize risks of injury, property damage, environmental impact, business disruption, and
regulatory non-compliance. It also ensures prompt recovery and resumption of safe operations
following any incident.

6.8 Emergency Classification
In accordance with ADNOC Crisis Management & Emergency Response Standard (HSE-CE-
ST01), the CONTRACTOR has classified emergencies into the following three tiers based on
the nature, severity, and potential consequences of the incident:
 TIRE 1 (Minor)
Defined as a minor incident such as a small, controllable fire, limited chemical or liquid
spillage, first aid cases, or medical situations with minimal impact.

the First Aid Station (manned by approved Nurse) and CONTRACTOR Emergency
Response Team (ERT). No external support or assistance is typically required for Tier 1
emergencies.
 TIRE 2 (Moderate)
Defined as a moderate emergency situation where the incident may exceed the immediate
capacity of the CONTRACTOR’s internal resources and where assistance from other
parties within the area, such as the COMPANY and other contractors or stakeholders on
site, is necessary to manage the situation effectively.
 TIRE 3 (Major / Catastrophic)
Defined as a severe event of potentially catastrophic proportions, involving Fatalities,
Serious or widespread environmental damage, or Major accidents affecting multiple
personnel or critical infrastructure.
This type of emergency necessitates the activation of COMPANY’s Emergency Response
Organization, involvement of regulatory authorities, and mobilization of both internal
CONTRACTOR resources and external specialized support services (such as Civil Defence,
CICPA, medical evacuation services, and environmental agencies).

7 EMERGENCY SCENARIOS & RESPONSE
7.1 Fire
In the event of a fire occurring within the construction site, the following emergency response
protocol shall be followed:
 Upon discovering a fire, the observer shall immediately notify the area Supervisor and HSE
Officer, who will promptly initiate the manual siren/alarm system at site.
 In the case of a small, manageable fire (e.g., the size of a small bucket), and if the observer
is trained and it is safe to do so, an attempt may be made to extinguish the fire using
available portable fire extinguishers.
 In the case of a large or rapidly spreading fire, the observer shall evacuate the immediate
area and the area Supervisor will promptly inform both the Incident Commander (IC) and
On-Scene Commander (OC).
 The Incident Commander will mobilize the Fire Fighting Team to the incident location, direct
containment and firefighting actions using available resources, notify the Project Manager
and the COMPANY Representative, and summon Civil Defence and any external
emergency services if further support is required.
 Upon hearing the alarm, all personnel shall immediately cease work and evacuate their
work areas, proceeding quickly and calmly to their designated Assembly Points.
 All evacuees must remain calm at all times, evacuate in an orderly manner by using the
nearest safe evacuation route, and stay at the designated Assembly Point and await further
instructions.
 The area Supervisor, supported by the HSE Team, shall conduct a headcount at the
Assembly Point and report the results, including any missing persons, to the Incident
Commander / On-Scene Commander.
 Once the fire has been successfully contained, the Incident Commander will assess the
affected area to confirm it is safe for personnel, notify the On-Scene Commander (OC) and
give the official “All Clear” signal to authorize the return of personnel to work areas.
 No personnel shall leave the Assembly Point or re-enter the affected area until the official
All Clear has been given by the Incident Commander.

7.3 Chemical Spillage
In the event of a chemical spillage or loss of containment (including oil, diesel, gasoline,
chemicals, lubricants, etc.), the following emergency response protocol shall be implemented.
 The Area Supervisor shall take immediate control of the situation and report the incident
regarding the location, nature, and estimated quantity of the spill.
 The Incident Commander shall inform the Project Manager and COMPANY HSE
Representative, and mobilize environment supervisor to the scene to assess and
coordinate response actions.
 The Area Supervisor shall organize and supervise clean-up activities utilizing the trained
Spill Response Team.
 The Clean-up Team must stay upwind of the spill to avoid inhalation of hazardous fumes
and wear appropriate chemical-resistant personal protective equipment (PPE) during clean-
up operations.
 Gasoline and its vapors are highly flammable. All sources of heat, sparks, open flames,
friction, and static electricity must be removed from the area.
 If the on-site Spill Response Team is unable to fully manage the spill containment and
clean-up, the CONTRACTOR shall engage qualified external emergency response
agencies.
 Consult the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for the specific hazards associated with the spilled
substance, including potential reactivity with other materials present in the area.
 Minor spills within contained areas should be neutralized (if appropriate). Free product that
cannot be recovered or reused must be treated as hazardous waste if the product
characteristics require.
 Spills of acids, caustics, or other dangerous chemicals outside contained areas must be
immediately diked or dammed using absorbent material, clay, or soil to prevent the liquid
from reaching storm water systems or natural water bodies.
 Clean-up of large volumes of free liquid shall only be performed by trained and authorized
personnel.
 All spill clean-up waste materials must be placed in approved hazardous waste containers,
properly labeled, and disposed of in accordance with the project’s Waste Management Plan.

7.6 Snake Bite
In the event of a snake bite incident on site, the following emergency response procedures
shall be implemented:
 Do not attempt to suck out the venom from the wound under any circumstances.
 The bite area shall be gently cleaned with clean water to reduce the risk of secondary
infection.
 The affected limb shall be immobilized using a firm supporting bandage or splint to restrict
movement.
 Do not apply a tourniquet or make any incisions above the wound.
 The affected limb should be kept lower than the level of the heart where possible, or in a
neutral position, to slow the spread of venom.
 A firm but not constrictive pressure bandage should be applied to the affected area if trained
personnel are available to do so.
 The Medical Team shall be immediately mobilized to the scene to assess and stabilize the
casualty.
 The casualty shall be promptly transported to the nearest hospital for evaluation and
administration of anti-venom as necessary.
 Continuous monitoring of the casualty’s vital signs shall be maintained during transportation.

7.7 Scorpion Siting
In the event of a scorpion sting incident on site, the following emergency response procedures
shall be implemented:
 Clean the affected area with clean water to reduce the risk of infection.
 Immobilize the affected limb to limit the spread of venom.
 Apply a firm but not constrictive supporting bandage to the affected area, keeping the limb
elevated to reduce the rate at which venom circulates toward the heart.
 If available and practical, apply cold therapy (such as immersing the sting site in cold or
iced water) to reduce pain and slow venom absorption.
 Do not apply antiseptic lotions, antibiotic creams, or any tourniquet.
 Tourniquets are not recommended, as they can block blood supply and cause a sudden
release of venom upon removal.
 In most cases, pain management using appropriate medication will suffice, as recovery is
typically rapid.
 The Medical Team shall be immediately mobilized to assess and stabilize the casualty.
 The casualty shall be transported without delay to the nearest hospital for further
assessment and treatment as necessary.

7.12 Epidemics / Pandemic
An epidemic or pandemic refers to the rapid spread of an infectious disease to a significant
number of individuals within a specific population, typically within a short timeframe. Such
diseases are transmissible through various routes including direct physical contact, exposure
to bodily fluids, contaminated surfaces, or airborne transmission.
In the event of a suspected or confirmed case of a communicable disease on site, the following
protocol shall be implemented:
 The first priority is to immediately isolate the affected individual, restricting their movement
and separating them from the general workforce to prevent further transmission.
 The patient shall cease all physical activities and remain in isolation.
 The Male Nurse shall assess the individual’s condition. If it is determined that the case
cannot be managed within the First Aid Station facilities, the patient will be referred to the
nearest designated hospital for specialized medical care.
 The patient shall be transported via ambulance to the hospital in accordance with
established MEDEVAC procedures.
 A Male Nurse shall monitor the patient while in the on-site Isolation Room until
transportation is arranged.

7.16 Fall from Height / Suspension
In the event of a person falling from height and being suspended by a fall arrest system on
site, the following actions shall be taken:
The incident observer or any personnel in the vicinity shall immediately notify the Supervisor.
The Supervisor shall inform the Incident Commander (IC), providing initial information
regarding the incident and secure the incident area and stop all traffic and equipment
movement in the immediate vicinity.
The Incident Commander (IC) shall notify the Project Manager and COMPANY Representative,
and mobilize the Medical Team and Rescue Team to the incident location without delay.
Speed is critical in this situation to prevent suspension trauma. The following immediate rescue
procedures shall be undertaken:
 The nearest Mobile Elevated Work Platform (MEWP) shall be deployed and positioned at
the incident location.
 A trained Rescue Team member will approach the casualty from below by MEWP.
 The MEWP basket shall be maneuvered into a position below the suspended person.
 The rescuer shall attach a new fall protection lanyard to the casualty’s harness and secure
it to a certified anchor point inside the MEWP basket.
 The casualty shall then be safely lowered to the ground in the MEWP basket.
 Once the secondary lanyard is secured, the primary fall arrest lanyard shall be carefully
detached.

7.17 Transportation of Radiation Source
The transportation and handling of radioactive sources shall be performed exclusively by a
specialist subcontractor duly licensed and authorized for such activities. The following
protocols shall be adhered to:
 Transport shields for radioactive sources shall have a radiation level not exceeding

for safe transportation.
 All packages containing radioactive sources shall be Prepared, marked, and labelled in
accordance with applicable national regulations.
 Road transportation shall be carried out using vehicles specifically licensed and registered
by the Traffic Police of the United Arab Emirates for the purpose of transporting radioactive
materials.
 All warning signs and notices shall be removable and only displayed when the vehicle is
actively transporting radioactive material
 The transport shield containing any radioactive source shall be Contained within the

 Provided the above requirements have been satisfied, and unless otherwise necessary, the

all external locks remain intact and secured.
