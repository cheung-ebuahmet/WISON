1. INTRODUCTION
The oil spill Standard is a document, or suite of documents, that provides guidance on how to respond to
an oil spill of any tier as applies to the risks of an organization. The frequency and magnitude of oil spill
incidents in the GCC area and the world in general has emphasized the need for enhancements in oil
spill planning and preparedness for the oil and gas industry.
2. PURPOSE
The purpose of this Standard is to specify ADNOC requirements for the development of oil spill
contingency plans and oil spill response that fulfils the expectations of UAE laws and regulations and is
in line with industry best practice.
3. SCOPE
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates &
Functions at HQ, Group Companies and SPC Affiliates) and its Contractors. This includes exploration,
production, transportation, refining, storage and distribution activities.
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood,
implemented and thoroughly monitored.
4. LAWS AND REGULATIONS
UAE Legislations applicable to this Standard includes but not limited to:
(i) Federal Law No. 8 (1980) UAE Labour Law and its Amendments Chapter V: Safety,
Protection, and the Health and Social Care of the Employees; Article (91).
(ii) UAE Ministerial Order No. (32), 1982. Specifying Preventive Methods and Measures for
Protecting Workers against Work Hazards.
(iii) UAE Ministerial Order No. (37/2), 1982. Establishing the level of medical attention the
employer is obliged to provide to his workers.
(iv) Federal Law No. 24 of 1999 for the Protection and Development of the Environment.
(v) Petroleum Ports Law No. 12 of 1973 and the amending Laws No. 1, 3, 34 and 45 issued at
Abu Dhabi on 27th February 1980, 28th April 1981, 5th May 1985, and 4th November 2003
respectively.
Group Companies shall ensure that their activities comply with all relevant Federal and Abu Dhabi laws
and regulations at all times, including any that may be introduced after the publication of this Standard.
In addition, Group Companies shall also comply with all international treaties and conventions that have
been ratified by the UAE such as the Kuwait Regional Convention for Cooperation on the Protection of
the Marine Environment from Pollution and the International Convention on Oil Pollution Preparedness,
Response and Co-operation (OPRC).

7.2. OIL SPILL RESPONSE RESPONSIBILITY & PLANNING SYSTEMS
7.2.1. OIL SPILL CONTINGENCY PLAN
All ADNOC Group Companies shall ensure that they update their Oil Spill Contingency Plans to meet the
requirements of this Standard. The Oil Spill Contingency Plan shall provide specific guidance to personnel
who are involved in an oil spill response. It shall provide the Oil Spill Response Team with tactical and
strategic response guidance, procedures and information required during and upon completion of an oil
spill response operation. This shall be aligned with the typical pattern of oil spill response and sequence
of activities that will occur over the duration of a response.

7.2.2. MULTI-TIER OIL SPILL RESPONSE PHILOSOPHY
A multi-tier oil spill response philosophy shall be adopted for the Oil Spill Contingency Plan. The tier
definitions shall be based upon where the resources required to respond to the spill are sourced from. An
oil spill risk assessment shall be conducted to determine suitable response techniques and capability of
each tier level response. The capability for each tier shall be commensurate with the assessed risk. The
3-tier response planning structure shall be as follows:
Table 7.2.1: Response Tiers and Description
Tier Description
Tier 1 Spills are the most mild, causing localized damage usually near the company's own
facilities. In most cases, this type of spill occurs as a result of the company's own activities.
Tier 2 Spills are assumed to extend outside the remit of the Tier 1 response area and possibly be
larger in size, where additional resources are needed from a variety of potential sources
and a broader range of stakeholders may be involved in the response.
Tier 3 Spills are classified as those that, due to their scale and likelihood to cause major
consequences, call for substantial further resources from a range of national and
international sources.
It is entirely feasible that contrasting tier definitions could be established for different operations in the
same area as well as for the same type of operation in different areas, due to factors such as logistical
constraints or geographical factors.

7.2.3. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER EMERGENCY RESPONSE PLANS
The relationship to other internal and external emergency response plans that may be utilized or
accessed during an oil spill response shall be described in the Oil Spill Contingency Plan. It shall describe
relationships to other related plans, the operations and geographical area covered by the plan, and any
requirements the plan is intended to satisfy (such as this Standard).
In particular, the Oil Spill Contingency Plan shall describe the relationship to the following:
(a) Group Company Emergency Response Plans
The Group Company Emergency Response Plans shall link to the National Response Framework
& Oil Spill Contingency Plan.
(b) Area Contingency Plans
The Oil Spill Contingency Plan shall describe how the plan is consistent with applicable Area
Contingency Plans. All Area Contingency Plans applicable to the scope of the Oil Spill Contingency
Plan shall be listed, with links or references to their location.
(c) Bridging Documents
The Oil Spill Contingency Plan shall also cover operations that involve third-party drilling rigs or
other vessels/facilities. Therefore, there shall be a brief description on the use of bridging
documents between the third-party and the Company and how they shall be utilized to pre-define
coordination of response activities, such as notification and initial oil spill response actions.
When contracts are being drawn up with drilling rig contractors, the responsibility for preparing the Oil
Spill Contingency Plan and for responding in the event of an oil spill shall be agreed in the terms of the
drilling contract. When no contract is in place, the drilling rig contractor shall have appropriate Tier 2
arrangements in place. This applies to Abu Dhabi and Fujairah waters.
7.3. SPILL ASSESSMENT
(a) Spill Trajectories/Modelling
A stochastic pre-spill trajectory modelling shall be conducted for selected facilities or groups of
facilities that could threaten shoreline or offshore sensitive areas within a reasonable amount of
time. Facility selection for pre-spill trajectory modelling shall be based on:
(i) Proximity to shore or offshore sensitive areas;
(ii) Sensitivity of potentially impacted areas;
(iii) Potential spill volume;
(iv) Oil properties (persistence);
(v) Prevailing wind and current directions and speeds; and
(vi) Containment and recovery resource deployment time frames.
For Group Companies with multiple facilities, representative trajectory models can be run for
groups of facilities in the same general area, taking into consideration similarities in oil types and
volumes and their relative geographic locations. The resulting trajectory information showing the
projected path and speed of the spill should be included in the Spill Assessment section of the Oil
Spill Response Plan.

Company-owned or contracted oil spill response equipment shall be maintained and inspected, according

readiness system documentation shall be provided.
7.4.5. OIL SPILL RESPONSE SPECIALITY EXPERTISE
A roster of specialized oil spill response experts who are readily available shall be provided. Subject
matter experts and consultants that may be utilized include, but are not limited to:
(i) Scientific support (including hazard assessment and spill/air trajectory modelling);
(ii) Shoreline clean up assessment technique (SCAT) specialists; and
(iii) Wildlife protection organisations.

7.6. TRAINING & COMPETENCE
7.6.1. OVERVIEW
The Training and Exercise Program is a critical component of ADNOC’s oil spill preparedness. Training
sessions and exercises shall be conducted regularly to improve and evaluate ADNOC’s capability to
execute one or more portions of its response plans. They are used to improve both individual skills and
the overall emergency management system.
Effective oil spill preparedness requires personnel who understand and can perform, a variety of
emergency response and incident management functions. The purpose of oil spill training is to ensure
that these personnel is identified and given appropriate opportunities to learn and maintain relevant
knowledge and skills. For the large majority of personnel, their role in oil spill preparedness will be an
additional duty to their normal or daily job. Some of the skills applicable to a person’s normal job may be
directly relevant to their allocated role during an emergency, for example:
Managerial or supervisory capabilities;

General logistical, procurement or administrative knowledge; and

Specialist knowledge on Safety, Legal or Government and Public Affairs.

However, in all cases the unique and varied challenges posed by an oil spill response will require an
understanding that extends beyond the normal experiences of a person’s job. All personnel will therefore
require some level of Oil Spill Training to enable them to perform Safely and Effectively in the case of an
emergency.

The following definitions are provided to clarify some of the key terminology used within the context of
this section:
Training: The process of imparting specific skills and understanding to undertake defined tasks.
Knowledge: To comprehend the theoretical or practical details of the subject.
Understanding: The ability to perceive or predict, or make judgement on the outcome of actions; it goes
deeper than knowledge and recognizes a greater scope for problem solving and decision making.
Skill: The practised and expert ability to carry out an action.
Competence: The ability to perform a particular job in compliance with performance standards. This
encompasses the technical requirements and skill to perform the job as well as having the relevant
knowledge and understanding to enable the job to be carried out successfully under different and

Function.
7.6.2. TRAINING PROGRAM

shall be designed and instructed to achieve the set requirements for the members of the Tactical

7.6.3. EXERCISE PROGRAM
In addition to the Training Program, an Exercise Program shall also be in place. The Exercise Program
helps to ensure that the Training Program has been effective and that personnel are well prepared to
respond to an incident. It also helps to test the plans and the procedures that are in place and provides
an opportunity to correct any shortfalls before an incident occurs. Large scale exercises bring together
responders in various roles and from various agencies, including regulatory agencies, support agencies,
contractors and subject matter experts. This allows those most likely to be involved in an incident a
chance to work together in a constructive environment and to practice their roles and responsibilities.
Following each exercise, a debriefing session shall be conducted and opportunities to improve shall be
identified, noted and the action to incorporate changes to plans recorded in the incident reporting & safety
management database with an identified individual, clearly articulated actions and a completion time. A
process for identifying and addressing recommendations arising from the exercise shall be in place. A
comprehensive exercise program is one of the most effective ways of:
Assessing response plans and procedures;

Determining the readiness of responders;

Clarifying roles and responsibilities;

Promoting awareness of potential incidents; and

Preparing for real spill events.

A robust exercise and drill program provides the response and emergency management teams with the
opportunity to practice skills that will be required in an emergency, to work together closely and develop
working relationships, and to make complex decisions under what can be often stressful circumstances.
The exercises are designed to bring together the key personnel likely to be involved in a real incident,
developing working relationships and encouraging teamwork. The incident response expertise shall be
able to provide exercise and drill development, facilitation, observation and involvement to help test and
improve ADNOC’s preparedness and response capability.

(a) Notification exercise:
Notification exercises test the procedures to alert and call out the response teams and are
conducted through telephone and other means of communication, as stipulated in the response
plan. They can be used to test communications systems, check availability of personnel, evaluate
travel options and the speed at which travel arrangements can be made, and assess the ability to
transmit information quickly and accurately. Such an exercise will typically last one to two hours
and may be held at any time, day or night, announced or unannounced.
(b) Table-top exercises:
Table-top exercises normally consist of interactive discussions of a simulated scenario among
members of a response team but do not involve the mobilization of personnel or equipment. They
are usually conducted in a conference room or series of rooms connected by telephone lines, and
focus on the roles and actions of the individuals, the interactions between the various parties and
the development of information and response strategies.
A simple and early form of table-top exercise would be a response team going through the
contingency plan, page by page, testing each other’s activities in response to an imaginary situation.
A more complex table-top exercise might involve several groups, including outside parties, playing
their roles. A table-top exercise might typically last two to eight hours and should be announced
well ahead of time to ensure availability of personnel.
(c) Equipment deployment exercise:
Equipment deployment exercises involve the deployment of oil spill response equipment at
particular locations in response to an oil spill scenario and in accordance with strategies laid down
in the plan for a particular spill scenario. These exercises test the capability of a local team to
respond to any tiered spill, provide the experience of local conditions and of spill scenarios and
enhance individual skills and teamwork. It is important that other parties who would normally be
part of such a response, such as providers of boats, barges, and trucks, be involved so that their
availabilities and capabilities can be assessed; other organizations might also be invited to observe.
Such an exercise would typically last four to eight hours and should be repeated frequently until
teams are acquainted with the equipment. In some instances, an equipment deployment exercise
might be run in conjunction with a table-top exercise or incident management exercise. This can
enhance the reality of the exercises but can be more complicated to oversee.
(d) Incident management exercises:
Incident management exercises are often more complex in that they simulate several different
aspects of an oil spill incident and involve third parties. Such an exercise may be of limited scope,
for example, using own personnel to role-play the main external parties, or of full scope, when
outside agencies and organizations are invited to provide personnel to play their own roles within
the exercise. Whilst internal exercises are beneficial in the early stages of team development, it is
only by exercising with the actual people who would be involved in a real emergency that a
response team can be properly tested and trained.

7.7. OIL SPILL RESPONSE STRATEGIES AND TACTICS
7.7.1. SPILL SCENARIOS
Spill scenarios shall identify the following information:
(i) Asset or facility name;
(ii) Type of oil;
(iii) Potential causal factors;
(iv) Source of the oil discharge;
(v) Time to detect and shut down;
(vi) Source control procedures and timeframes;
(vii) Potential spill volumes (instantaneous volume, or daily rate);
(viii) Sensitive areas and/or shorelines that could be threatened; and
(ix) Potential spill movement and time to impact sensitive areas/shorelines.
7.7.2. RESPONSE STRATEGY
The response strategy is the combination of different oil spill response techniques that are used in the
event of a spill, at defined tier levels and indicative timeframes. The strategy shall be based on the spill
scenario(s) and the Spill Impact Mitigation Assessment (SIMA).
7.7.3. TACTICAL SUPPORT TACTICS
(a) General Overview
When developing a response strategy (or strategies), the associated tactical support activities shall
also be identified and included in each strategy. The most common tactical support activities
include spill surveillance, and simultaneous operations (SIMOPS).
(b) Spill Surveillance
During daylight hours, initial spill surveillance activities shall be provided by any available offshore
support vessel(s) unless otherwise engaged with safety support at the asset. Vessel based
surveillance activities shall remain in place until aerial surveillance is established. Communications
for vessel and aerial support shall be coordinated and documented.
Guidelines on aerial surveillance and how it can be used to track spill movements and coordinate
containment, recovery, dispersant and other response operations shall be provided. Internal or
external sources of surveillance/tracking equipment and/or services shall be identified.
At times, there may be the need to establish aerial and marine exclusion zones for reasons that
may include the following:
(i) to grant full geographical access control to the Oil Spill Response Team;
(ii) maximising response operations using platforms in the area; and

(iii) to prevent secondary contamination of vessels and areas as vessels transit through the

A SIMOPS Plan shall be developed for any oil spill incident. The potential elements of the SIMOPS

(iii) specific role and responsibilities for SIMOPS;

A SIMOPS situation status display shall be used to display equipment, vessel and aircraft locations,
and track the status of all concurrent operations. This information shall be updated (real time or
periodically) as well as accessed, viewed and/or communicated to external parties (e.g., technical
specialists).
7.7.4. ON-WATER SUPPORT TACTICS
(a) Offshore Containment and Recovery
All containment and recovery operations shall be conducted during daylight hours only. Mechanical
recovery equipment shall not be deployed in wind speeds exceeding 15m/s (30kts), wave heights
exceeding 2m (7ft), or in visibility less than 1 nautical mile. Air monitoring shall also be conducted
to ensure the safety of responders.

7.7.5. SHORELINE / SENSITIVE AREA PROTECTION
Spill trajectory analyses shall also be conducted for facilities located within a reasonable proximity of
known onshore or offshore sensitive areas to identify which areas are most at risk and the approximate
time to impact from associated discharge releases. Stakeholders for these sensitive areas shall be
identified and referenced in the Oil Spill Contingency Plan. Where modelling indicates shoreline impact
in less than 12 hours, a detection system shall be installed on site/ field. Companies with multiple facilities
covered by the same Oil Spill Contingency Plan can provide the above information for groups of facilities
in the same geographic region instead of individually.
For situations where sensitive areas are not likely to be impacted in the first 24 hours following a spill, the
applicable Area Contingency Plans can be referenced for locations of sensitive areas and their specific
protection strategies and tactical plans. Information on applicable sensitive area/shoreline protection
techniques shall be provided. The information shall include descriptions, general logistical requirements,
limitations, depictions of typical deployment configurations and a technique selection guide.
(a) Shoreline/Sensitive Area Identification
The Oil Spill Contingency Plan shall provide brief descriptions of the types of local environmental,
socio-economic and cultural sensitivities that are known to be present in the potential impact area.
This shall be based on spill trajectory analyses and the sensitivity mapping. A list and map of the
sensitive areas that could be impacted in the first 24 hrs shall be provided. References shall be
made to Area Contingency Plans or other documents for information on protection of sensitive
areas that could be impacted beyond the 24-hour time frame.

Information on what permits, approvals or trustee involvement may be required to conduct wildlife hazing

General responsibilities/regulatory requirements and company policies for wildlife surveys, wildlife

7.8.2. SURVEYS
Tier 2 providers shall carry out aerial surveys to identify the presence of higher concentrations of birds

areas shall be referenced here.

Hazing methods, authorizations required, and sources of equipment and personnel shall be described in

Plan.

Company policies and regulatory requirements for oiled wildlife rescue and rehabilitation shall also be
described and referred to in this section of the Oil Spill Contingency Plan. Contractors and/or agencies
that can provide related services, shall be referenced within the section and available resources shall be
identified.

7.9. WASTE MANAGEMENT
7.9.1. WASTE MANAGEMENT PLAN
Oil spills to the marine environment can generate significant amounts of oily waste that need to be
collected and disposed of properly, in accordance with all applicable laws and regulations. A waste
management plan shall be in place as part of the Oil Spill Contingency Plan. The waste management
plan shall have the following objectives:
(i) identify all potential sources and understand the character of all wastes associated with oil spill
response activities;
(ii) align the waste management plan with UAE regulatory requirements and ADNOC Group Practices;
(iii) provide a framework for incident-specific spill plans to manage waste, as far as practicable, under
the waste management hierarchy and treat waste close to its source of generation;
(iv) identify potential waste management facilities, including third-party facilities, that are approved to
receive wastes; and
(v) assign responsibilities and define the required resources for implementing the waste management
plan, and describe the verification and monitoring measures that are required.
The waste management plan shall provide the framework for contingency planning and response
preparation for waste management activities associated with the clean-up operations for a potential oil
spill. It shall contain details as to how recovered oil and other wastes will be segregated, classified, stored,
and shipped for treatment and/or disposal after recovery. This can be in the form of an MOU or an
agreement/contract with waste management providers/organizations, ensuring that it is fully licensed
under UAE regulations.
Further details required in the waste management plan include:
(i) types of waste expected to be generated;
(ii) analytical requirements for characterizing the waste;
(iii) waste segregation procedures;
(iv) storage and handling procedures;
(v) equipment requirements;
(vi) requirements for temporary storage sites;
(vii) waste documentation and tracking;
(viii) transportation requirements;
(ix) temporary handling sites; and
(x) the location of approved facilities for final treatment /disposal of various solid and liquid waste
streams.
7.9.2. INTERIM STORAGE AND SEGREGATION
General strategies and available resources for interim storage of liquid and solid wastes both offshore
and onshore shall be provided. Procedures for segregating wastes to minimize quantities requiring
special disposal shall be included as well.

7.10. DECONTAMINATION & DEMOBILIZATION
7.10.1. DECONTAMINATION
(a) Overview
The overall objective of decontamination is to minimize worker exposure and limit secondary
contamination through the spread of oil to uncontaminated areas.
In the event of a spill, a decontamination plan shall be developed. The decontamination plan shall
describe the general strategies for decontaminating personnel and small equipment at localized
decontamination facilities during on-going response operations.
The decontamination process shall be coordinated with Site Safety Plan requirements.
A separate plan may be required to describe larger-scale decontamination facilities and procedures
for major equipment (vessels, barges, storage tanks, skimmers, boom, etc.) at designated
locations, often performed as part of demobilization.
The proposed plan shall be reviewed and approved by the Group Company Environment
Department/Division.
(b) Personnel
Specific areas that are designated “dirty” and “clean” shall be established to organize personnel
who are in the process of suiting up or decontaminating. A sequential decontamination process
shall be established for all personnel to follow.
(c) Equipment
The decontamination of equipment shall take place in designated areas only. A dry cleaning of
equipment may take place depending on the level of oiling and potential re-use of equipment.
Equipment such as skimmers and hoses that are not being used and are awaiting decontamination,
shall be stored in such a way that any oil dripping from the equipment is captured. This can be
achieved by storing such equipment in dedicated containers designed for this purpose, which are
stored on the deck of the vessel.
Upon completion of the clean-up effort, all equipment shall be cleaned thoroughly so that the
equipment is ready for subsequent use.

7.10.2. DEMOBILIZATION
(a) Overview
A spill-specific demobilization plan shall be developed, appropriate with the nature and extent of
the spill, incorporating guidance from regulatory authorities and the Oil Spill Response Team. The
demobilization and repatriation of resources shall be conducted in accordance with the
demobilization plan.
As the response transitions from emergency response phase to a planned recovery effort, the
demobilization of incident resources shall be conducted in an efficient and safe manner and shall
not interfere with ongoing incident operations.
The Incident Commander shall approve the demobilization of critical resources identified by
command staff prior to demobilization from the incident. Those resources shall be identified in the
daily operational period planning cycle.
(b) Equipment Check-out Procedures
The check-out procedures for equipment upon demobilization include:
(i) final decontamination inspection;
(ii) turning-in small equipment and unused supplies;
(iii) equipment inspection;
(iv) final repairs;
(v) returning equipment to rightful owners;
(vi) damage settlement process; and
(vii) final storage of purchased equipment and supplies.
(c) Personnel Check-out Procedures
The check-out procedures for personnel upon demobilization include:
(i) require debriefing to ensure critical information is recorded;
(ii) ensure turn-in of documentation and equipment;
(iii) ensure downloading of spill response information from personal computers;
(iv) describe any policy concerning the passing of spill related information to unauthorized
sources; and
(v) describe individual personnel check-out forms and process.

The following procedure and actions provide the initial guidance required to respond to an incident involving
ADNOC Group Company and external client Oil Spill & HNS Response
The action plan is activated by the Duty Manager (DM) or the VP OS & HNS Response, following initial notification
of an oil spill incident.
24 Hour Emergency Hotline Numbers
 +971 50 8118024
 +971 56 6879955
 +971 50 6227261
EMAIL: OSR.HNS.LS@adnoc.ae
These numbers are manned on 24-hour basis. On placing a call, the caller should make it clear that they are
reporting an Oil Spill incident or HNS incident (or potential incident) and wish to make contact with the Duty
Manager. They will be asked to provide the following information:
Name of caller and position

Name of mobilizing GC

Location of caller

Telephone contact numbers (both mobile and landline if appropriate)

Facsimile number (if available)

Email address

Brief details of incident

The Duty Manager will attempt to establish contact with the caller within 10 minutes. Once contact has been
made; further details will be collected to agree on the mobilization and response strategy.
