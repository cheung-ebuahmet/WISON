1. INTRODUCTION
Analysis of GASCO incidents from 2008 to 2013 identified personal safety, control of work
and road safety as areas with a high number of reports. The analysis also identified root
causes associated with behaviour and non‐compliance with existing GASCO’s policies,
standards and procedures.
Based on these findings, and international best practices in our industry, ten (10) GASCO
Life Saving Rules have been developed with the aim of raising awareness of those work
activities which will prevent injuries to personnel. GASCO Life Saving Rules are consistent
with The GASCO Way Attributes of ‘Always Putting Safety First’ and ‘Doing The Right
Thing’ to ensure safety of employees and contractors. Deliberate violations of GASCO Life
Saving Rules will be considered as a breach of the Employees Code of Conduct and
disciplinary measures may be taken following investigation.
1.2 PURPOSE
This document serves to provide guidance to Supervisors and Life Saving Rule Champions on
the application of GASCO Life Saving Rules so as to:
 Provide clarity and understanding;
 Explain roles and responsibilities,
 Define consequence management,
 Monitor and audit the effectiveness of the process.
It follows the same format as the Life Saving Rule booklets (which are available in English,
Arabic and Urdu) but has some additional supporting information, especially for the role of
Supervisors.
1.3 SCOPE OF APPLICATION
GASCO Life Saving Rules apply to all GASCO Plants, Pipelines, Projects Divisions and Head
Office; covering all work‐related activities performed by employees and contractors both
within and outside GASCO premises where the company’s management controls are in place
(directly or indirectly) to ensure safe and efficient operations.

2. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
2.1 Employees and Contractors
GASCO Employees and Contractors must:
 Comply with GASCO Life Saving Rules;
 Proactively intervene and stop non‐compliance with the GASCO Life Saving Rules;
 Report cases of deliberate violation/refusal to comply with GASCO Life Saving Rules
to Supervisors.
2.2 Supervisors
Supervisors are responsible for:
 Ensuring GASCO Life Saving Rules are understood and followed by their employees
and contractors;
 Reporting cases of deliberate violation/non‐compliance to line management for
investigation.
2.3 Site HSE Department Heads
Site HSE Department Heads are responsible for:
 Providing support to the line;
 Providing advice on investigation processes;
 Collecting data on the implementation and functioning of the Life Saving Rules.
2.4 Site Life Saving Rules Committee
The Site Life Saving Rules Committee shall be nominated by the Site Vice President and
include Contractor Representatives.
The Committee is responsible for:
 Assisting HR when conducting investigations of Life Saving Rules violations and
preparing an investigation report to the respective site SVP for review;
 Providing advice to the VP on the effectiveness of the GASCO Life Saving Rules.

3. THE GASCO LIFE SAVING RULES
The GASCO Life Saving Rules outlined in this document support existing Management
Systems, HSE Policies, Standards or Procedures.
Each rule is provided with a visual icon with corresponding guidance.
Rule #1. Protect yourself against a fall when working at height
Whenever work has to be carried out at height (1.8 meters or higher) it
is necessary to provide the worker with a safe means of access to the
work. This normally achieved by existing platforms with handrails and
toe boards or by certified scaffolding. This is called a Protective
Environment. If it is not possible to provide this Protective Environment,
for example during construction of scaffolding, the persons working at
height must wear Fall Protection Equipment.
When working at height outside the Protective Environment, you must:
 Have authorization;
 Have a full understanding how your fall protection equipment operates and perform
a pre-use inspection of the equipment to identify any problems;
 Use the fall protection equipment and ensure 100% tie-off.
Supervisors and Persons in Charge of work being done outside the Protective Environment
must:
 Verify all the necessary precautions including the provision of fall protective
equipment, and authorise the work. You must check that the person to whom the
equipment is issued is fully competent in its use and if necessary ask the person to
demonstrate their knowledge and ability. Make sure the person knows the location
of the tie off points and the correct method of their use. Periodically during the work
check that the equipment is being used correctly.
Refer to GASCO Working at Height Standard HSE-ST-017.

Rule #2. Conduct atmospheric gas testing when required
Atmospheric gas testing is necessary when there is reason to believe
that flammable gas, toxic gas or an oxygen deficient / oxygen
enriched atmosphere may be present at the work place. This
requirement will be specified on the Permit to Work. It is to provide
a warning of the presence of flammable gas, toxic gas or an oxygen
deficient / oxygen enriched atmosphere.
It is a key requirement for work in a Confined Space or undertaking
Hot Work in, or introducing Potential Sources of Ignition to
Hazardous Areas.
When performing work with potential ignition source, or entering a confined space you
must:
 Confirm with the Person in Charge of work that atmospheric gas testing has been
performed and it is safe to start work;
 Stop work and withdraw from the risk area if toxic or flammable gases are detected.
The Permit to Work Issuing Authority is responsible for:
 Deciding which gasses to test for in the risk area. Consider all possibilities using your
knowledge and experience of the area. It is your responsibility to identify when gas
testing is required and then arrange for this testing to take place;
 Deciding where to test for the gas. Testing may have to be done away from the
precise work place for example, to cater for activities nearby where gas may be
released;
 Deciding the number of tests including continuous testing if necessary. Continuous
testing will be required when confined spaces are involved, or when surfaces are
being disturbed such as sludge removal leading to release of gas due to the work
itself progressing;
 Explaining why the gas testing has to be done. Explaining the rationale to those doing
the work is helpful because it increases their awareness of potential danger and they
can intervene on their own initiative.
Authorised Gas Testers must:
 Be authorised by Site Line Management to undertake testing’
 Conduct the gas testing as defined in the Permit to Work and provide advice on the
interpretation of the results.
Refer to GASCO Confined Space Entry Standard HSE-ST-018.

Rule #3. Do NOT stand or walk under a suspended load
Crane operations, lifting and rigging activities involve the transfer of
a suspended load, which has a potential of falling. Walking,
standing or performing work directly under a suspended load is
unsafe and can result in accidents with severe consequences if the
load falls.
All Personnel must:
 Not cross a barricade controlling an area where lifting operations are being
performed unless authorised by the Person in Charge of the Lift;
 Never be under a suspended load;
 Always follow the instructions of the Person in Charge of the Lift.
The Person In Charge of the Lift must:
 Mark and barricade the area where the lift is being performed. Particular care must
be taken to alert people who may enter the lift area by mistake, for example,
walking between equipment or exiting stairways;
 Ensure that nobody works / walks under the suspended load. It is sometimes a
natural instinct for persons to move around while a lift is in progress, for example, if
they see something needing attention. Be ready for well-meaning but unplanned
actions by people and immediately intervene. Plan the lift to avoid anyone having to
go underneath the suspended load.
Refer to GASCO Lifting and Hoisting Procedure TT-PR-007.

Rule #5. Obtain authorization before entering a Confined Space
A confined space is a fully or partially enclosed area that:
• Is not designed for continuous human occupancy
• Has limited or restricted means of access or egress
• Has a risk of injury or health effects from hazardous substances or
conditions that may be present.
Confined spaces include (but are not limited to) vessels, tanks, pits,
pipes, etc. Authorisation MUST be obtained before entering a
confined space.
If you are entering a confined space you must:
 Obtain authorisation from the Supervisor or Person in Charge of the Work that it is
safe to do so;
 Confirm with the Standby Man that you can enter the confined space;
 Follow the precautions and requirements outlined in the Permit to Work and
associated Confined Space Entry Certificate.
The Supervisor or Person in Charge of the work must confirm that:
 Necessary precautions and requirements of the Confined Space Entry Permit are in
place. This means personally checking the requirements;
 The Confined Space Entry Standby Man is competent and present on site at all
times. Check his competency by questioning, using ‘what if?’ situation examples;
 It is safe to start work.
Refer to GASCO Confined Space Entry Standard HSE-ST-018.

Rule #6. Verify hazardous energy isolation before working on equipment
Adequate control of hazardous energy for work activities involving
process, mechanical and electrical isolations provides protection
and prevents human exposure. Hazardous energy isolation
methods include, but are not limited to, isolation valves, double
block and bleed valves, rated-spades, spectacle blinds, electrical
breakers and switch gears, etc.
When you work on equipment requiring energy isolation you must:
 Confirm with the Supervisor or Person in Charge of work that it is safe to start work.
The Permit to Work Issuing Authority is responsible for:
 Identifying the isolations required. These must be precisely identified and recorded
on the Permit;
 Confirming that the isolations are in place. This may be done by delegation, for
example, an authorised electrician can sign the permit to confirm electrical isolation
is in place;
 Confirming that there is no residual risk from the hazards, for example trapped
pressure or liquid. This is a particular skill requiring knowledge of the actual
equipment being worked on. Gas may be trapped between valves, it cannot always
be confirmed that drain lines used are not blocked, for example. In certain cases
motive power such as wind can cause unsecured fan blades to rotate. Or there may
be flammable material that can’t be seen behind vessel baffles or linings;
 Defining the precautions if residual risk may exist. It is particularly important that if
a residual risk is, or may be present, that it is discussed with those doing the work.
Refer to GASCO Permit to Work Standard HSE-ST- 009, chapter No. 4.

Rule #7. Work ONLY with a valid Permit to Work
A Permit to Work is a control document which authorises a person
to perform work. It outlines the necessary precautions to be
followed for safe execution of the task. It is important that you
obtain a valid Permit to Work and follow the precautions outlined in
it before performing assigned tasks.
If you are going to do work on the Site you must:
 Confirm that a Permit to Work has been issued which includes the specific task you
will be involved with;
 Confirm with your Supervisor or the Person in Charge of work that it is safe to start
work;
 Only do work specified on the Permit;
 Comply with the time period for which the Permit to Work is valid;
 Stop work if conditions change.
The Supervisor or Person in Charge of the Work must:
 Ensure their staff will not start work until a Permit to Work has been issued. This
should be done at the earliest possible opportunity. People cannot be allowed to
start work even though they know that the supervisor has gone for the permit. It
must be issued and seen by the work party before the commencement of work;
 Understand the scope of the Permit to Work and explain and enforce the
precautions and requirements to those doing the work. It is useful to do this at the
tool box Talk. Listen to any concerns and invite challenges. People must not be
forced to do work where they believe the precautions taken or guidance given is
inadequate.
Refer to GASCO Permit to Work Standard HSE-ST-009.

Rule #8. Obtain authorization before overriding Safety Critical Equipment.
Safety critical equipment are provided to ensure the safety of
people and the safeguarding of process systems.
Examples of safety critical equipment include fire and gas alarm
systems, process trip systems, isolation devices / emergency
shutdown valves, relief valves, critical alarms, shaft guards, crane
computers etc. These safeguards may be taken out of service
(override or disabled) for maintenance purposes provided the
appropriate level of authorisation has been obtained. This level of
authorisation must be defined in the Site Procedure.
Persons working on Safety Critical Equipment must:
 Obtain authorisation from the Supervisor or Person in Charge before overriding or
disabling the safety critical safeguard.
Person providing the Authorisation must:
 Ensure risk associated with overriding safety critical equipment is assessed, and
documented, and that risk control measures are put in place. This means that you
must know what the Safety Critical Equipment available is. Read the relevant
COMAH Report. Risk control measures mean that you must know and document
what may happen, the precautions you have put in place and what actions you will
take if the equipment is called upon to function but can’t because it is overridden;
 Maintain a Register of Overrides and discuss them during shift handover meetings.
Do this formally by documenting it, so there is no doubt that the oncoming shift
knows the override is in place. Just as important the oncoming shift must satisfy
themselves that the precautions are adequate.
Refer to Code of Practice on Identification & Integrity Assurance of HSE Critical Equipment &
Systems ADNOC-COPV6-01.

Rule #9. Wear a Seat Belt
A seat belt reduces the risk of injury in the event of a motor vehicle
accident. Wearing of seat belts applies to all GASCO and Contractor
owned or hired vehicles including cars, buses, mini buses, trucks,
cranes and forklift trucks when on company business.
It is mandatory that both drivers and passengers comply with this
rule.
Drivers and Passengers must:
 Always use a 3-point seatbelt except for buses, trucks, cranes and forklift trucks
where only lap belts may be available;
 Check that your seatbelt operates properly and keep it correctly fastened as per its
design at all times;
 Check that everyone in the vehicle is wearing a seatbelt and intervene when you find
someone who is not.
In this rule the driver is the individual to whom the rule applies but he is also, in effect, the
supervisor as he has responsibilities for car and passengers.
Refer to GASCO Road Safety Standard HSE-ST-006.

Rule #10. Do Not Exceed the Speed Limit
Speed increases the risk of losing control of your vehicle while
driving. It is mandatory that all GASCO and contractor drivers
observe speed limits.
Drivers must:
 Drive within the speed limit for the route / road and vehicle type being driven as
required by the UAE traffic laws and regulations.
Passengers must:
 Intervene and caution a driver exceeding the speed limit.
The speed limit referred to above is the sign-posted speed limit, not the camera speed
limit.
Refer to GASCO Road Safety Standard HSE-ST-006.

4. LIFE SAVING RULE INTERVENTION PROCESS
GASCO Life Saving Rules intervention process provides opportunity primarily for proactive
intervention and correction of unsafe behaviours by Peers and Non-Peers.
4.1 Life Saving Rule Breach observed by Peer
Peers are people who work together on a day-to-day basis and have no line supervisory
responsibilities to each other, examples are:
 Process operator to another process operator
 Process operator to a maintenance technician and contract workers (scaffolders,
painters, welders)
 Maintenance supervisor to a process supervisor
 Contractor worker to another contractor worker (welder to a scaffolder, crane
operator to a welder).
The peer intervention process mandates all GASCO employees and contractors to intervene
and stop all unsafe acts and behaviours, including those outlined in the Life Saving Rules,
which have potential to result in accidents. This should be done with courtesy, without
endangering the person performing the unsafe act or others. It is expected that all persons
approached and advised of their unsafe behaviour will take corrective action and comply
immediately.
There is no action if the person complied during the intervention. It is however required to
be reported for lessons sharing and recording purposes.
Where there is a deliberate refusal to comply by the violator, such cases should be reported
to the GASCO Supervisor for investigation.
4.2 Life Saving Rule Breach observed by Non-Peer
Anyone in a hierarchical or supervisory /oversight relationship (boss to subordinate or vice
versa), examples are:
 Line supervisor to a worker on site
 Cross line supervisors to workers e.g. (maintenance supervisor to a process
operator, supervisor of a crew of welders to a scaffolder in another crew)
 Manager on site visit to anyone on site
 HSE coordinator/officer/lead to any one on site
 Staff engineer on site-visit to anyone on site
 Permit Issuer to workers working under the permit
 Employee to their supervisor

5. INVESTIGATION AND CONSEQUENCE MANAGEMENT PROCESS
5.1 Life Saving Rule Investigation Process
The investigation process as outlined in the GASCO Incident Management Standard (IMS)
and Personnel Policy Manual should identify facts related to the incident, discuss the
offense with the concerned employee or contractor in a reasonable manner giving
him opportunity to explain or give reasons for his actions or behaviour. Following the
investigations, the Site Life Saving Rules Committee will prepare a formal investigation
report for review with section/department heads, respective site/division VP and SVP and
HC.
In general the investigation would identify any existing pre‐conditions that promote similar
unsafe behaviours or possible violation of the Life Saving Rules and should establish how
any other person faced with similar circumstance would act, such instances should be
identified and reported to the Line Manager for taken immediate corrective action.
Examples of pre‐conditions are:
 Inadequate work planning (SIMOPS, lifting extended over other activities)
 Inadequate job placement, appropriate personnel not identified, not available or
not provided (Confined Space Entry standby man)
 Inadequate or no training provided
 Poor design (no anchoring point), unavailability of safety or protection devices
(no seat belt)
 Unclear or contradictory writing instruction or procedures
 Tools & equipment inadequate availability / inadequate adjustment, repair or
maintenance
 Incorrect item / device received etc.

5.2 Life Saving Rule Consequence Management
5.2.1 Life Saving Rule Consequence Management for GASCO
Refusal to comply with GASCO Life Saving Rules will be considered a safety violation and
misconduct. The procedures as stated in the GASCO Employee Relations Disciplinary Code
will be adhered to, as explained in GASCO Personnel Policy Manual GPPM,
recommendations for disciplinary actions will be either Written Warnings (First, Second
and Third/Final), Dismissal after Warning and Dismissal without Warning (dependant on
the frequency of the Violation and the severity outcome). Details of its application are
provided in the Disciplinary Procedure of the GASCO Personnel Policy Manual (GPPM).
Table (1) outlines the Offences and Disciplinary Action Code (Ref. HCP1-MAN-001-9/4
Table 1), also Table (2) outlines warning durations and responsibilities for any disciplinary
action process (Ref. HCP1-MAN-001-9/4 Table 2).
Refer to GASCO Personnel Policy Manual
5.2.2 Life Saving Rule Consequence Management for Contractors
Contractor should meet all HSE obligations and risk reduction measures. GASCO Life Saving
Rules are part of HSE essential measures to reduce incidents and injuries.
Life Saving Rules Consequence Management for Contractors and Sub-Contractors shall be
imbedded in all new contractual terms and conditions.
Contractors and/or Sub-Contractor disciplinary action shall be:
- First time breach of a Life Saving Rule:
 Contractor or Sub-contractor Staff removed from site.
 If a Supervisor sets the conditions for rule breaking, then supervisor removed from
site/contract and contractor shall increase supervision manpower.
- Repeated breaches of Life Saving Rules:
 Removal of Contractor or Sub-contractor Line Manager(s) from site or contract.
 Significant breaches will be regarded as a breach of contract, and may affect the
extension or bidding for future contracts.
Again, all Life Saving Rule breaches will be investigated as per the GASCO Life Saving Rule
Human Failure Investigation Process.

6. PERFORMANCE MONITORING
To evaluate the effectiveness of the Life Saving Rules process the following matrixes
and key performance indicators are to be captured monthly by the Site HSE
Department for stewardship by the Head Office HSE Division:
Proactive Statistics:
 Employees and contractors formally trained/briefed on GASCO Life Saving Rules.
 Proactive Life Saving Rules intervention reports, identified violation promoting/pre‐
conditions, corrective actions and assigned responsibilities.
 Open/closed corrective actions. Overdue actions greater than 30, 60, 90 and 120 days.
Reactive Statistics:
 Deliberate Life Saving Rules violation reports, concluded investigations and
disciplinary actions.
