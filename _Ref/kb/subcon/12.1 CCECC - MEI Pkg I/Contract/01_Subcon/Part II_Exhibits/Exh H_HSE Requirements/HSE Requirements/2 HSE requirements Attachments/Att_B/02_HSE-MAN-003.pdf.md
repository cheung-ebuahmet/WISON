1. INTRODUCTION
The ADNOC Health Safety and Environment (HSE) Code of Practice (COP) requires that GASCO implements a
systematic approach to HSE which is consistent with the ADNOC HSE Management System. Therefore the
GASCO HSE Management Manual (HSEMM) serves as the GASCO Management System (HSEMS) and describes
expectations in line with the ADNOC COP and provides an overview of how these expectations are met.
This Manual supports, and is fully aligned with, the GASCO HSE Policy. The hierarchy of documents within
GASCO is structured as follow:
Figure 1
HSE management in this context refers to:
 Occupational Health and Hygiene Management
 Personal Safety Management
 Process Safety Management
 Environmental Management
The HSEMM demonstrates to ADNOC and other shareholders how GASCO meets the health, safety and
environmental expectations as set by the ADNOC Code Of Practice (COP) and provides a detailed system by
which GASCO can measure and audit its compliance as a means to further improve.

ELEMENT-2: POLICY AND STRATEGIC OBJECTIVES
This element addresses GASCO intentions, action principles and aspirations with respect to health, safety and
environment with the aim of improving HSE performance.
Policies and objectives provide clear statements on what GASCO intends to achieve. These are set at the
corporate level and apply to the entire organization. Additional policies and objectives can be established by
assets, businesses or other levels of the organization manage specific operating activities and local
environments.
• “Policies” provide high-level commitments on general principles, limitations and statements of intent
about how the Company will operate.
• “Objectives” support the policies with more detailed requirements and longer term aims which will
reflect the scope of the Company’s HSEMM in relation to specific types of risks, threats or impacts.
Basic Requirements
GASCO must have a written HSE policy, embodying, as a minimum, the ADNOC Group Policy contents.
HSE objectives must be challenging, understood by all and consistently incorporated in policies. In
setting objectives, management must consider the overall risk levels of their Companies’ activities, and
must identify those critical operations and installations requiring a fully documented demonstration that
risks have been reduced to as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP).

ELEMENT-3: ORGANISATION, RESOURCES AND COMPETENCE
This element addresses the organisation of people, resources and competence required for sound HSE
performance. The HSEMM must be consistently implemented across GASCO, covering all assets, activities,
locations and the entire workforce. Consistency comes from being well organised with the right resources and
competence. In other words, striving to have the right equipment and the right people, with the right skills in
the right place at the right time, to deliver every task successfully, achieve GASCO’s objectives and meet
stakeholder requirements.
Basic Requirements
Necessary organisation and resources to comply with the HSEMM must be provided. This Element
ensures that GASCO has clarity on who does what, that everything needed is in place, and that the
necessary skills and experience are applied by:
• Providing a structured organisation to deliver the planned objectives effectively and efficiently;
• Establishing clear reporting relationships within the organisational structure, with unambiguous lines
of communication and control;
• Clearly defining individual job scope, authority, responsibilities and accountabilities;
• Ensure that the organization has availability and accessibility of human and financial resources to
provide the equipment, materials, services and skills needed for its activities; and
 Confirming that managers and the workforce have the competency to work safely and effectively

ELEMENT-4: RISK EVALUATION AND MANAGEMENT
This element addresses the identification, evaluation and management of HSE hazards and risks, and the
development of controls to manage (i.e. eliminate or minimize) these. A wide variety of HSE risks exist within
the oil and gas industry with the potential for unintended consequences which include process safety hazards,
safety hazards, occupational health exposures, environmental and social impacts
Basic Requirements
The risk management process for all HSE-critical operations and installations shall include:
 An inventory of major hazards and vulnerabilities of the environment, health and safety of
personnel, the public and customers from all the activities, materials, products and services;
 An assessment of all risks in terms of likelihood and potential consequences, followed by a
prioritization of risks that are considered to be significant;
 Establish controls or barriers to mitigate significant risks to ALARP and recovery procedures in
case of control failure.
Health, Safety and Environmental Impact Assessment (including, where practicable, a consideration of
social impacts) must be conducted prior to all new activities, facility developments and/or significant
modifications to existing ones. Soil and groundwater contamination and other forms of environmental
degradation must be assessed and, where required, control or remediation must be in-hand. Health risk
assessment must address physical, chemical, biological, ergonomic and psycho-social hazards associated
with the work environment.
An HSE assessment must form an integral part of any proposal for acquisition, divestment, abandonment
or merger of business entities.
The Inventories of hazards and risks must be made available to the workforce, customers and others
stakeholders. Product stewardship must be applied at all stages of product life cycle relevant to GASCO
activities.
There are four sequential steps in the risk management process:
 Identify
 Evaluate
 Manage
 Review

APPENDIX - C : GLOSSARY
ALARP As Low as Reasonably Practicable: is the point at which the cost and effort (time and
trouble) of further risk reduction is grossly disproportionate to the risk reduction
achieved.
Asset An asset is an engineered piece of equipment (i.e. it excludes reservoirs, people, etc.).
An asset can be categorized in various ways e.g. in order of increasing detail business
unit, offshore platform or onshore plant, process train/unit, equipment type (e.g.
pipelines, structures) or equipment tag numbers (Definition taken from ADNOC CoP V6-
01).
Asset Integrity An asset has integrity if it operates as designed for its assigned life (or greater) with all
its risks as low as reasonable practicable, or as nominated (Definition taken from
ADNOC CoP V6-01).
Asset Integrity means the ability of the asset to perform its required function effectively
and efficiently whilst safeguarding life and environment. (Definition taken from the UK
HS Executive).
COMAH Control of Major Accident Hazards
DGS Design General Specifications
FEED Front End and Engineering Design of a project. It will define the Basic Design and
Engineering Package together with a 10% accurate cost estimate of the defined project.
After approval of the FEED, the Engineering, Procurement and Construction (EPC) phase
will start covering the implementation of the project.
Fitness to Means: an employee is currently in a physical and psychological condition in which
Work (FTW) he/she can carry out Specific Work, without significant risk to either him/herself, the
business and / or third parties. FTW is a category of occupational health controls, like
elimination, substitution, engineering, procedures and personal protective equipment.
Hazard Anything that has the potential to do harm
Hazardous A situation with the potential to cause harm, including ill health and injury, damage to
Situation property, products or the environment, production losses or increased liabilities.
A Hazardous Situation is also called: a Hazard
HAZID Hazard Identification
HEMP Hazard & Effects Management Process, the ‘backbone’ of the COMAH report
HSE Critical An activity necessary to provide or maintain Barriers and Recovery Preparedness

are met.
