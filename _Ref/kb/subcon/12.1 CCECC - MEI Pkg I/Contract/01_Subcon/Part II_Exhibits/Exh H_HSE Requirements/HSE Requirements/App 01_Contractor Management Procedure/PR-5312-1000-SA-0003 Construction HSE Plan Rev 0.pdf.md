1 PROJECT DESCRIPTION
1.1 Introduction
The Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 2 (RSHT-2) is owned by ADNOC and operated by
ADNOC Gas (AG) and is located approximately 240 km west-southwest of Abu Dhabi City in
the Ruwais Industrial Area. RSHT-2 facility is designed to handle bulk granulated sulphur
coming from Shah and Habshan plant via railcars and transported via new federal railway
(Union Railway) to Ruwais Terminal (SHT-2).
Based on ADNOC recent directives, the Sulphur Handling Scenario at Ruwais SHT-2 (Ruwais
Sulphur Granulation Plant-RSGP) will be developed further due to consideration of receiving
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur from Hail & Ghasha to the new plant, via pipeline. This new plant
will be located to the East of the BOROUGE terminal-3 & to the west of RSHT-2 as depicted
in Fig.1.1 below.
Figure 1: New RSGP Site Location near Existing RSHT-2 Facility
1.2 Project Objective and Scope
The primary objective of the RSGP Development project at RSHT-2 is to accommodate
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur received via pipeline from the Hail & Ghasha OPP. This involves
constructing new infrastructure for liquid sulphur receiving storage and transfer, sulphur
forming (Granulation), Granulated sulphur transfer and storage, stacking and reclaiming and
export to existing ship loaders. The project also includes implementing dust control systems,
sulphur remelting unit, utility systems and integration with existing facilities. The developed
facility aims to ensure efficient and environmentally sound sulphur processing, support
ADNOC's sulphur production, and maintain operational safety and reliability.

1.3 Purpose
The purpose of this Construction HSE Plan is to define, document, and schedule all Health,
Safety, and Environmental (HSE) related activities required during the EXECUTE Phase of the
Project. This document outlines the HSE management framework and details the specific
activities, responsibilities, and deliverables necessary to effectively manage HSE risks
throughout the construction phase.
This document is considered a living document and will be reviewed and updated as necessary,
particularly prior to the commencement of construction works or upon changes in project scope,
site conditions or COMPANY requirements.
The Construction HSE Plan has been prepared in accordance with ADNOC HSE-GA-ST05
“Project HSE Plan”, relevant COMPANY guidelines, and applicable statutory regulations. It
outlines the essential HSE activities, control measures, and monitoring requirements
necessary to protect personnel, property, the environment, and ensure compliance with
COMPANY’s HSE Management System during the construction phase.

Vibration exposure occurs when workers come into contact with vibrating tools, equipment, or
machinery. The body parts directly in contact with these sources absorb the vibration energy,
potentially causing long-term health effects.
When operating hand & power tools, such as grinders, breakers, jackhammers, and drills,
vibration is typically transferred to the hands and arms. This type of exposure is known as
Hand-Arm Vibration (HAV).
Health Hazards
The most recognized condition associated with HAV is Vibration-Induced White Finger (VWF),
also known as Raynaud’s phenomenon, which affects blood circulation in the fingers.
Prolonged and repeated exposure to hand-arm vibration can also lead to Hand-Arm Vibration
Syndrome (HAVS). HAVS is a serious and progressive disorder that affects:
 Circulatory system (numbness, blanching of fingers)
 Musculoskeletal system (joint stiffness, reduced grip strength)
 Nervous system (tingling, loss of sensation)
These symptoms develop gradually over months or years, and may become irreversible if early
intervention is not provided. Whole-body vibration (WBV), typically resulting from prolonged
operation of heavy vehicles or machinery, may cause lower back pain, spinal disorders, and
digestive problems.

18.11 Heat Stress
This section outlines the requirements and procedures for preventing and managing heat-
related illnesses on site. It establishes a comprehensive Heat Stress Management Program
that includes risk assessment, environmental monitoring, work-rest cycles, hydration practices,
acclimatization strategies, emergency response, and personnel training.
The objective is to ensure that all CONTRACTOR and Subcontractor personnel working in
high-temperature environments are protected from heat stress hazards through proactive
planning, effective controls, and continuous supervision in alignment with ADNOC HSE
requirements and industry best practices.
Definitions
Heat Stress: The overall heat load on the body from environmental factors, physical activity,
and clothing.
Heat Strain: The body’s physiological response to heat stress.
Acclimatization: Gradual adaptation of the body to work in hot environments.
WBGT (Wet Bulb Globe Temperature): A composite temperature used to estimate the effect
of temperature, humidity, wind speed, and solar radiation on humans.
Heat Index: A measurement combining air temperature and relative humidity to reflect
perceived temperature.

Wire Rope
The main components of a wire rope are the core, strands, wires, and center wire. For example,
a designation such as 6x7 indicates that the rope is made up of 6 strands, with each strand
containing 7 individual wires, arranged around a central core.
The term rope lay refers to the direction and pattern in which the wires and strands are twisted
within the rope. This rotation can be either right-hand (clockwise) or left-hand (anticlockwise).
A rope lay length is defined as the distance along the rope in which a single strand makes one
complete revolution around the rope’s axis.
Wire ropes shall be selected by a competent person to ensure their suitability for the intended
application. The factor of safety must be considered in accordance with the manufacturer’s
recommendations. Wire ropes shall be discarded immediately if they are worn, damaged, or
fail to meet the manufacturer’s specified criteria for safe use.

23.3 Evacuation Instructions
Evacuation procedures, including the locations of Assembly Points and emergency contact
numbers, shall be prominently displayed at all worksites and on the inside of all temporary
facility doors.
A site layout plan indicating Assembly Points and designated evacuation routes shall be
developed, maintained, and updated for each phase of the project. This layout plan shall be
posted on notice boards and regularly communicated to the workforce.
The Toolbox Talk program will be utilized to reinforce evacuation requirements and
responsibilities and to inform personnel of any updates or changes to these instructions.
In the event of an emergency evacuation, the following instructions shall be followed:
 All equipment shall be shut down safely if conditions permit. All electrical appliances such
as lights, air conditioning systems, and computers must be turned OFF.
 Doors must be closed by the last person leaving a room or building.
 Personnel must remain calm at all times and evacuate in an orderly manner. Running is
strictly prohibited to prevent the risk of stampedes or accidents.
 All personnel must proceed directly to their designated Assembly Point.
 Evacuees must remain in their workgroup and report immediately to their assigned
supervisor.
 The supervisor shall conduct a head count of all personnel under their supervision and
report the results to the On-Scene Commander at the Assembly Point.
 The On-Scene Commander will report to the Incident Commander and wait for further
instruction determined by the Incident Commander.
 All evacuees must stay at the Assembly Point until the official “All Clear” signal is given by
the Incident Commander/ On-Scene Commander.
 No one is permitted to leave the Assembly Point without the permission of their supervisor
or the Incident Commander/ On-Scene Commander. Unauthorized absence may endanger
search and rescue teams and this may put the search party at risk and take resources away
from controlling the emergency.
Evacuation instructions will be periodically reviewed and updated to reflect changes in site
conditions, work scope, or new hazards. Updates will be communicated via Toolbox Talks,
safety briefings, and posted notices.

search progress.

be summoned to the location for prompt medical assistance.
