1. INTRODUCTION
The Project HSE Plan Standard is one of the ADNOC HSE Standards that underpins the ADNOC HSE
Management System. Similar with the other Standards, this Standard shall assist ADNOC Group in
ensuring compliance where applicable, with the corporate ADNOC HSE Policy.
To demonstrate compliance with ADNOC HSE Policy and achieve the associated strategic corporate
HSE objectives, it is imperative to:
Design the right facilities;
◼
Effectively manage all the HSE aspects related to engineering in a methodical/structured manner;
◼
Eliminate all HSE high risks or minimize them to ALARP, as applicable.
◼
Achieving the above necessitates an excellent project delivery, supported by a best in class “Project
Specific HSE Plan”.
The mandatory requirements prescribed in this Standard shall be strictly and consistently complied with
throughout the project lifecycle; in alignment with:
ADNOC HSEIA Standard [Ref. 1];
◼
ADNOC Project HSE Review (PHSER) Standard [Ref. 2]; and
◼
ADNOC Value Assurance Process (VAP) Manual [Ref. 3].
◼
The Project HSE Plan shall be considered as a live document that specifies for each project stage all
relevant HSE deliverables such as:
HSE studies;
◼
HSE reviews;
◼
HSEIAs;
◼
Details of any submissions for approval; and
◼
Any other verification.
◼
Each Project HSE Plan is specific to the relevant project and is developed and owned by the Project
Management Team. It is also used central repository of the HSE deliverables.
2. PURPOSE
The purpose of the Standard is to provide guidance to the project team and design engineers for
preparation and updating of Project HSE Plans and to ensure the following:
(i) ADNOC HSE mandatory requirements are captured and updated in a structured and uniform
manner, throughout the life cycle of all projects;
(i) Timely preparation and submission of projects’ HSEIA dossiers and HSEIA reports for
“Regulatory Authority” approval by ADNOC Group HSE function;
(ii) Project specific HSE Philosophy is developed in compliance with ADNOC HSE Design
Philosophy Standard [Ref. 4]; and
(iii) Compliance, as applicable, with the requirements of relevant ADNOC HSE Standards; and
applicable ADNOC Engineering Standards.

7. REQUIREMENTS
7.1. OVERVIEW
The Project HSE Plan is a document developed at the screening and concept stage and then updated
before being formally issued prior to the commencement of each succeeding stage of project
development, when a closer definition of HSE requirements has been established.
The HSE Plan shall be further updated capturing necessary input from the “Project HSE Philosophy”
document as detailed in this Standard, thus ensuring all HSE requirements for any new project; from the
conceptual Prior to Operations stage to (Operations start-up) and decommissioning / abandonment stage,
at the end of facilities lifecycles.
A list of required HSE deliverables for each stage is provided in table form under Section 7.3.5 (List of
HSE Actions & Deliverables at Each Stage) of this Standard.
7.2. HSEIA REQUIREMENTS
As defined in ADNOC HSE Governance Standard [Ref. 6], ADNOC is a self-regulator in HSE matters for
all ADNOC Group Companies and affiliated Companies; acting under the direction of the Supreme
Petroleum Council and in accordance with Law No.1, 1988.
The ADNOC Health Safety and Environmental Impact Assessment (HSEIA) Standard [Ref. 1], is the
primary mechanism through which ADNOC exercises its regulatory duties through approval of HSEIA
Reports by the ADNOC Group HSE Function.
HSEIA Dossiers and HSEIA reports are, technically approved by Group Companies’ Corporate HSE
before submission to ADNOC Group HSE Function for ‘Regulatory Approval’.

The ADNOC HSEIA Standard [Ref. 1] specifies the mandatory requirements for HSEIA, which shall be
developed, updated and submitted as required, for all projects; during the full lifecycle of each project.
The HSEIA Screening Study is the main tool to determine the HSE studies which needs to be completed
for associated project HSEIA phase.
The HSEIA is the main HSE administrative tool used to assess all HSE risks from a project and being a
living document, it considers the full lifecycle.
The outline purpose of project HSEIA is principally to ensure that HSE integrity is maintained at the
various phases, which are:
(i) FEED (Front End Engineering Design) Stage - describes 'how to design';
(ii) Engineering, Procurement and Construction (EPC) Stage - describes 'how to build'; and
(iii) Operations Stage - describes 'how to operate and maintain'.
ADNOC uses the HSEIA process as the primary mechanism for exercising its regulatory duties to approve
HSEIAs for the following project stages:
(i) FEED (Front End Engineering Design)
(ii) Detailed Engineering (EPC)
(iii) Operations
The main components of the HSEIA during the design phase are principally the following:
(i) An Environmental Impact Assessment [EIA], which is the product of the Environmental
Impact Assessment Study supported by baseline data.
(ii) A COMAH Report, which addresses all Major Hazards identified in the HAZID, ENVID and
OHID Register.
(iii) An Occupational Health Risk Assessment [OHRA] which addresses identification of health
hazards and evaluation of risk to health, taking account of existing control measures.
(iv) A HAZID/ENVID/OHID Register which documents and categorises all HSE risks and the
measures to be taken to reduce these to ALARP.
When a project does not follow the ‘normal’ lifecycle, for example drilling or seismic survey projects, a
combined HSEIA is sufficient to fulfil ADNOC requirements (see Ref. 1). This combined HSEIA
condenses the requirements of all the first three phases of a ‘normal ‘project into a single phase, where
all hazards induced by the activity are identified, assessed and managed. As an example, for operations
involving drilling rigs, the rig HSE Case shall be used in combination with the site specific QRA, EIA to
produce the required HSEIA.
Full details of the latest HSEIA requirements for each phase are given in the ADNOC HSEIA Standard
[Ref. 1].
All Project Managers shall ensure that any changes to the requirements contained in the latest version
of this Standard and related Standards, are duly be considered when preparing HSE Plans.

7.3. CONTENTS OF A TYPICAL PROJECT HSE PLAN
7.3.1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
A brief introductory description of the project shall be detailed within this section to include a description
of the project scope, objectives, the location (field or facility involved), the production aims, the duration
and lifespan, the stakeholders involved, as well as description of the main process plant and equipment.
7.3.2. SCOPE
The full scope of the Project HSE Plan shall be outlined within this section. This shall include a brief
description of the applicable ADNOC HSEIA phases, engineering studies, specialist HSE studies and all
Group Company and Contractor HSE activities required throughout the project.
7.3.3. OBJECTIVES
The objectives of the Project HSE Plan shall be briefly stated within this section. The main purpose of the
Project HSE Plan is to define and schedule all HSE related activities required throughout the project
lifecycle, in order to comply with all relevant ADNOC HSE Standards and self-regulatory requirements.
The objectives shall therefore include a definition of:
(i) All Authority submittals for approval, and their timing;
(ii) All applicable UAE laws and regulations;
(iii) All applicable ADNOC Standards and guidelines; and
(iv) All applicable international standards and guidelines.
The objectives within the Project HSE Plan shall also ensure:
(i) That all design team discipline engineers work in unison with one another, thereby achieving
a 'risk based' design, compliant with all mandatory requirements;
(ii) That the design is risk assessed and meets the ADNOC 'ALARP' criteria; and
(iii) That the field or facility HSEIA, HAZID/ENVID/OHID Register and Emergency Response
Plans are updated prior to operational handover, or abandonment.
7.3.4. ORGANIZATION AND RESPONSIBILITIES
An overview of the HSE organisation, roles and responsibilities for all the project stages shall be defined
in this section. It shall be based upon the requirements of the project and interface with ADNOC and other
Group Companies, the focal points and alternate contact persons representing each of the involved
parties shall be identified and indicated with names and contact details.
The organization chart shall include both ADNOC and Contractor personnel. Depending on the stage of
the project, there may not be a specific HSE personnel assigned. However, ownership of HSE shall be
the Project Manager’s responsibility and the Project Manager shall be responsible for acquiring required
input from Group Company Corporate HSE or ADNOC Group HSE Function, depending on the nature of
the project.

(i) Hazard Identification Studies
The purpose of the initial conceptual Hazard Identification is to establish a preliminary
register of the hazards anticipated and risks involved. The initial Hazard Identification studies
are performed qualitatively utilizing the ADNOC Corporate Risk Matrix criteria to identify all
Health, Safety and Environmental hazards encountered throughout the duration of the
project’s design, construction and operational phases.
The commonly used techniques for this Hazard Identification are HAZID, ENVID, OHID, and
‘what if’. If preliminary P&IDs are available, a HAZOP study could be conducted at Concept
Stage of the project. It is important to have representation in the Hazard Identification team
from all relevant site personnel and HSE personnel. Each workshop shall produce its own
hazards register. Further information on these studies may be referred to in the ADNOC
HAZID/ENVID/OHID Standard [Ref. 8] and the ADNOC HSE Risk Management Standard
[Ref. 7].
(ii) HSE Design Philosophy Document
This document details all Codes, Standards & Practices design requirement which shall be
applicable for the Project. It also defines ADNOC’s Standards and Group Company’s
corporate Philosophies that shall be adopted into the design.
The document developed which combines all adopted philosophies may then be referred to
as the “Project HSE Design Philosophy”. It is important that this document is as accurate as
possible prior to commencement of the FEED, so it provides specific guidance on HSE
design requirements and shall comply with the requirements of the ADNOC HSE Design
Philosophy Standard. [Ref. 4]
(iii) Inherently Safer Design (ISD) Study
Inherently Safer Design (ISD) describes a broad, philosophical approach to process safety
in which the focus lies upon the designing of processes and products in such a way as to
eliminate hazards completely or sufficiently minimize their magnitude so as to eliminate the
hazard and negate the need for elaborate safety systems incorporated in the design.
Further information on the ISD Study may be referred to in the ADNOC Inherently Safer
Design Standard [Ref. 10].

Within the Concept Design Stage, limited information is available to conduct a full QRA.

(vi) HSEIA Screening Study
At the Concept stage of all projects, irrespective of their cost or value, a HSEIA screening
study shall be carried out to determine the content of the HSEIA Report and/or relevant HSE
studies to be carried out as part of the HSEIA.
The study shall be based on a thorough review of all the relevant information relating to the
project, the anticipated HSE risks and the sensitivity of the surrounding environment.
This HSEIA Screening Report shall include following:
1. Coarse HAZID, ENVID, Qualitative OHRA Review; and
2. HSEIA Screening including HSE Studies to be carried out in project life cycle.
The conclusions of the HSEIA Screening Study shall define the scope of COMAH, EIA and
OHRA including all relevant HSE studies. HSEIA screening shall also highlight if any non-
ADNOC Regulatory Approval may be required during various project lifecycle such as EAD
approval, Civil Defence, etc.
(vii) Update of Initial Project HSE Plan
Following the HSEIA Screening Study and communication with ADNOC GC CHSE, the
Project HSE Plan shall therefore be updated to reflect the requirements for the HSEIA report
and the specific HSE studies and reviews necessary during the FEED stage of the project.

PHSER 1 shall be scheduled and conducted at 80% of the Concept Stage to review the
status of HSE aspects and requirements related to the Concept Stage.
Further reference on this can be found in the ADNOC PHSER Standard [Ref. 2].
