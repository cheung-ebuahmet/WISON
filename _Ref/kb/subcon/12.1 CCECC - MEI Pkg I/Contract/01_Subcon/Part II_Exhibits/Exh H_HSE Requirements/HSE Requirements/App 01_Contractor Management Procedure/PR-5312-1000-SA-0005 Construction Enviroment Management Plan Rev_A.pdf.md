1 PROJECT DESCRIPTION
1.1 Introduction
The Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 2 (RSHT-2) is owned by ADNOC and operated by
ADNOC Gas (AG) and is located approximately 240 km west-southwest of Abu Dhabi City in
the Ruwais Industrial Area. RSHT-2 facility is designed to handle bulk granulated sulphur
coming from Shah and Habshan plant via railcars and transported via new federal railway
(Union Railway) to Ruwais Terminal (SHT-2).
Based on ADNOC recent directives, the Sulphur Handling Scenario at Ruwais SHT-2 (Ruwais
Sulphur Granulation Plant-RSGP) will be developed further due to consideration of receiving
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur from Hail & Ghasha to the new plant, via pipeline. This new plant
will be located to the East of the BOROUGE terminal-3 & to the west of RSHT-2 as depicted
in Fig.1.1 below.
Figure 1: New RSGP Site Location near Existing RSHT-2 Facility
1.2 Project Objective and Scope
The primary objective of the RSGP Development project at RSHT-2 is to accommodate
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur received via pipeline from the Hail & Ghasha OPP. This involves
constructing new infrastructure for liquid sulphur receiving storage and transfer, sulphur
forming (Granulation), Granulated sulphur transfer and storage, stacking and reclaiming and
export to existing ship loaders. The project also includes implementing dust control systems,
sulphur remelting unit, utility systems and integration with existing facilities. The developed

4.2 Roles & Responsibilities
4.2.1 Project Manager
Project Manager (PM) is responsible for ensuring that all contractual obligations of the
PROJECT are fulfilled. Project Manager shall take all reasonable measures to prevent any
non-compliance and shall promptly correct any deviations from the environmental
requirements.
PM will be held responsible for the following:
 Conduct construction activities with minimal environmental impact on the surroundings;
 Prevent contamination of land, water, and air;
 Ensure waste is managed and disposed of according to the CONTRACTOR’s and
COMPANY’s waste management plans;
 Support all CONTRACTOR personnel and subcontractors in environmental protection;
 Attend meetings addressing environmental matters related to the COMPANY;
 Communicate environmental information and promote a positive environmental culture;
 Ensure compliance with environmental legislation and regulations of Abu Dhabi.
Project Manager will be supported by the construction manager, HSE Manager, and all other
project personnel.
4.2.2 Construction Manager
Construction Manager plays a critical role in the planning and implementation of the
Construction Environment Management Plan (CEMP). Construction Manager shall:
 Ensure an effective system communicates the CEMP to all site personnel, including
subcontractors;
 Ensure all plant, equipment, and materials are maintained for use on site and comply with
COMPANY standards;
 Ensure key personnel and line supervisors take ownership of environmental responsibilities
and the workforce complies with the CEMP;
 Report successful implementation of the CEMP and any environmental incidents to the
Project Manager.

6.3.2 Vibration Control Plan
Work-related vibration is categorized into Hand-Arm Vibration (HAV) and Whole-Body
Vibration (WBV).
 Hand-Arm Vibration (HAV): Generated by handheld vibrating tools such as pneumatic
breakers, concrete/stone cutters, angle grinders, drills, impact wrenches, and jackhammers.
Prolonged exposure can damage blood vessels and nerves in the hands and fingers.
 Whole-Body Vibration (WBV): Transmitted through machinery or vehicle seats during the
operation of bulldozers, excavators, dump trucks, cranes, forklifts, and other earth-moving
equipment. WBV can cause lower back pain, spinal disorders, fatigue, dizziness, and loss
of balance.
The level of health risk depends on the average daily exposure. Contractors shall comply with
ADNOC requirements by controlling vibration exposure to remain below the Action Values and
Limit Values specified in HSE-OH-ST08, Table 7.3.3.
Effective vibration control relies on applying a combination of measures within the Hierarchy
of Controls framework:
1) Elimination & Substitution
 Select low-vibration tools and equipment.
 Avoid prolonged use of high-vibration machinery where possible

incident and cleaned up immediately to prevent further spread into the environment.

8.4 Records and Reports
8.4.1 Daily Environmental Observations
All site personnel are responsible for taking action when deviations from established
environmental practices are observed. CONTRACTOR’s environmental supervisor will
prepare daily observation reports, specifically assessing compliance with project
environmental requirements.
Each daily observation report will document:
 The nature of the observation;
 The location of the observed item or activity;
 The organization responsible for monitoring environmental compliance;
 Whether appropriate corrective actions have been taken in the event of violations;
 The status of any corrective measures.
All observations will be recorded in an environmental observation register, which
CONTRACTOR will maintain, ensuring regular follow-up and closure of corrective actions. If
corrective actions are overdue or remain unresolved for extended periods, they will be
escalated to the CONTRACTOR HSE Manager and relevant line management for resolution.
CONTRACTOR will perform trend analyses on observation data to identify areas for
improvement or potential system weaknesses. The observation register and trend analyses
will be shared with COMPANY regularly and upon request.
If an observation meets the threshold of an incident, the project’s Incident Investigation and
Reporting procedure will be followed. Daily observations should capture both positive and
negative findings.
