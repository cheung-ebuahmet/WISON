1 PROJECT DESCRIPTION
1.1 Introduction
The Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 2 (RSHT-2) is owned by ADNOC and operated by
ADNOC Gas (AG) and is located approximately 240 km west-southwest of Abu Dhabi City in
the Ruwais Industrial Area. RSHT-2 facility is designed to handle bulk granulated sulphur
coming from Shah and Habshan plant via railcars and transported via new federal railway
(Union Railway) to Ruwais Terminal (SHT-2).
Based on ADNOC recent directives, the Sulphur Handling Scenario at Ruwais SHT-2 (Ruwais
Sulphur Granulation Plant-RSGP) will be developed further due to consideration of receiving
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur from Hail & Ghasha to the new plant, via pipeline. This new plant
will be located to the East of the BOROUGE terminal-3 & to the west of RSHT-2 as depicted
in Fig.1.1 below.
Figure 1: New RSGP Site Location near Existing RSHT-2 Facility
1.2 Project Objective and Scope
The primary objective of the RSGP Development project at RSHT-2 is to accommodate
9,900 TPD of liquid sulphur received via pipeline from the Hail & Ghasha OPP. This involves
constructing new infrastructure for liquid sulphur receiving storage and transfer, sulphur
forming (Granulation), Granulated sulphur transfer and storage, stacking and reclaiming and
export to existing ship loaders. The project also includes implementing dust control systems,
sulphur remelting unit, utility systems and integration with existing facilities. The developed
facility aims to ensure efficient and environmentally sound sulphur processing, support
ADNOC's sulphur production, and maintain operational safety and reliability.

1.3 Purpose
The purpose of this Transportation Management Plan (TMP) is to establish a structured
framework and define the specific requirements for the safe operation, upkeep, and
maintenance of all heavy vehicles, light vehicles and mobile equipment within COMPANY
facilities/BOROUGE4 facilities/sites and for vehicles/equipment used solely for COMPANY
activities. This document applies to the CONTRACTOR and all Subcontractors working under
the CONTRACTOR’s direction throughout all phases of the EPC Project.
The objectives of this transportation management plan are to:
➢ Provide HSE guidelines for in-land transportation of equipment and materials.
➢ Define vehicle requirements and regulate access to ADNOC operational areas.
➢ Ensure all drivers are trained, competent, and compliant with safe driving practices.
➢ Specify journey management and route planning requirements.
➢ Establish emergency response and incident management measures for road transportation.
➢ Ensure compliance with Abu Dhabi Regulations and ADNOC standards for vehicle safety
and roadworthiness.
➢ Provide safe driving guidelines and signage to raise awareness.
➢ Promote employee adherence to vehicle access control procedures.

7.3 Hierarchy of Control
The following hierarchy of control shall be applied to eliminate or reduce road transport risks:
➢ Elimination - Avoid high-risk road transport activities where possible (e.g., use alternative
methods of material delivery, avoid travel during adverse weather or at night).
➢ Substitution - Replace higher-risk practices with safer alternatives (e.g., use buses instead
of multiple light vehicles; where possible).
➢ Engineering Controls - Implement vehicle design and technical solutions (e.g., IVMS,
speed limiters, seat belts, rollover protection, enhanced vehicle maintenance).
➢ Administrative Controls - Apply policies, procedures, and planning (e.g., Journey
Management Plans, driver rotations to reduce fatigue, defensive driving training, traffic
signage, road safety campaigns).
➢ Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) - Use protective measures where risks cannot be
otherwise controlled (e.g., seat belts, high-visibility clothing when exiting vehicles on roads).

7.6.10 Driving During Fog and Sandstrom
Among all weather conditions, fog is probably the most dangerous. If driving in foggy weather,
the driver must reduce his/her speed, turn on the low-beam headlights (or fog lights if available)
and increase their following distance. The driver must not use hazard-warning lights or
overtake. If visibility falls below 100m, the driver must not start driving or if he/she is already
driving, pull safely off the road and stop driving until they can see 100 m of clear visibility. It is
advisable to avoid driving during adverse weather conditions.
Visibility boards are required to be fixed in place 100m from all exits to allow the drivers to
confirm that they can see 100 m of clear visibility and help them to make an informed judgment
as to whether or not they should drive.
The following steps shall be followed while driving in fog:
➢ Be aware of local weather conditions. Before you drive and during your trip, check weather
forecasts and road reports. If there is weather warning or report of poor visibility, delay your
trip until conditions improve.
➢ Slow down gradually. If you encounter fog while driving adjust your speed accordingly.
Avoid sudden stops, stopping in the middle of the road, sudden changes in speed and
unnecessary overtaking.
➢ Allow more distance between vehicles. Maintain extra distance from the vehicles ahead of
you, as the extra distance can help to brake safely if required. Never rush or speed to get
out of the fog.
➢ Use fog lights and your low beams. The light from high beam headlights shall be reflected
by thick fog, so use low beams in thick fog.
➢ Turn on your front and rear defroster and wipers. The moisture in the air may continuously
collect on the windshield, making it more difficult to see. Adjust the defroster and windshield
wiper speed as necessary for maximum visibility.
➢ Do not drift. Be sure to stay in your lane and use the right-side pavement line as a reference.
➢ Do not turn on your hazard lights while driving in fog. Some people tend to turn on hazard
lights to make themselves more visible, but other drivers may panic thinking that you have
stopped and brake sharply, causing accidents. Hazard lights should only be used when the
vehicle is stopped for emergency purposes.
➢ Reduce distractions in your vehicle. Let your passengers know that your full attention is
required for driving.
➢ Do not stop on the road, If the fog is too dense to continue, pull completely off the road into
a safe position and turn on hazard lights while your vehicle is parked.
➢ Ask for help. Do not be afraid to ask your passengers to help keep an eye out for oncoming
cars and obstacles on the road.
➢ The same vision standards in fog conditions are applicable for sandstorm conditions.

7.6.11 Driving in Rain
Driving in the rain can be hazardous. The following steps shall be followed while driving in rain:
➢ Plan your journey in advance. Check the local road report in your area before starting your
journey. Leave earlier, plan more travel time, drive slowly, look for heavy traffic and to avoid
areas prone to flooding.
➢ Be especially careful when the rain first starts. When the roads are dry for a long period of
time, engine oil and grease build up on roads. When the first drops of rain start to fall, the
water mixes with this build-up making the roads incredibly slick.
➢ Always keep both hands on the steering wheel. Avoid distractions, such as cell phones,
radio etc. and maintain a 360-degree overview.
➢ Turn on your headlights even in light rain – to help you see and be seen.
➢ Turn on your front and rear defoggers to keep your windows clear.
➢ Slow down! It takes longer to stop or adjust in wet weather. Increase your crash avoidance
space and be in the speed limits or at a slower pace as required.
➢ Avoid cruise control, hard braking and turning sharply.
➢ Brake earlier and less forcefully, than you normally would. This increases the stopping
distance between you and the car in front and alert the driver behind you.
➢ Keep an eye out for pedestrians. People may become distracted by opening an umbrella or
rushing to get out of the wet and they may accidentally step into the road at the wrong time.
➢ Watch for intersections and beware of driving in the rain at night. Vehicles could skid at
intersections.
7.6.12 Driving in Hot Weather
When driving in hot weather, the heat has an impact on both the driver and the vehicle. The
driver shall keep an eye on the warning lights and gauges, especially the temperature gauge.
Due to the hot temperature, drivers may be fatigued faster than normal therefore drivers shall
keep their vehicle well-ventilated to avoid drowsiness. If drivers are dazzled by bright sunlight,
they shall slow down and if necessary, stop. The driver shall consider wearing sunglasses if
necessary.
7.6.13 Driving in Wind/Dust
Strong winds generally reduce your steering control. In certain cases, the wind may blow up
sand from deserts as well and may impact visibility. The best defensive driving technique for
windy conditions is to drive at lower speeds or if the wind is too strong, safely pull over until
winds subside.
In addition, drivers shall also be careful of any build-up of sand on the road. If drivers are
driving too fast on an approach to sand, the vehicle tyres can easily lose traction with the road,
leading to skids and loss of control of the vehicle. It is encouraged for drivers to slow down in
these instances and avoid any sand build-up on the roads.

10.8 APPENDIX - 08 Road Signs
Road traffic signs are an essential part of any traffic system. They provide rules everyone shall
obey and warn us about the hazards on the road ahead. Each sign shall have a distinctive
combination of shape, color and symbol.
All personnel driving any vehicle shall adhere to UAE national traffic and road sign codes in
ADNOC premises as well for communicating road safety signs.
Signs can be in the form of words or symbols on panels, road markings, beacons, bollards or
traffic lights. A sign shall give its message clearly and early enough to be seen, understood
and acted upon safely. Symbols are used as much as possible because they are standardized,
more easily recognized and understood.
Types of Road Safety Signs
TYPE OF SIGN DESCRIPTION
Warning It gives early warning of hazard.
Prohibitive It gives orders and prohibitions.
Regulatory It gives positive instructions or orders.
Direction It gives information, positive instructions and local directions.
Supplementary It is rectangular in shape and gives additional information.
