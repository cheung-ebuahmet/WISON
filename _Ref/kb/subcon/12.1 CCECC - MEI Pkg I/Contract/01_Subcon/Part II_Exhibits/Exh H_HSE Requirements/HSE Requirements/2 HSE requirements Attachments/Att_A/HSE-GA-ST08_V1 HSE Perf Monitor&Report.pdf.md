1. INTRODUCTION
The monitoring and reporting of Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) performance is a critical process
that allows ADNOC Executive Leadership Team to evaluate, endorse and make decisions on significant
matters related to effectiveness of ADNOC HSE Management System.
The data collected, analyzed and elevated through established corporate channels, in line with ADNOC
HSE Governance Framework [Ref. 10], is essential for strategic decision making required to achieve the
set HSE objectives and targets, established as part of commitment to ADNOC 100% HSE in alignment
with ADNOC 2030 Strategy.
Monitoring and Reporting is fundamental for:
(i) Measuring successful achievements against targets and plans;
(ii) Demonstrating compliance with ADNOC HSE Standards; and
(iii) Identifying systemic gaps and weaknesses requiring improvement.
ADNOC primary objective is to have reliable, relevant, consistent, transparent, accurate and complete
HSE data collected and pre-analyzed at Group Company/Affiliate level, before transmission and
consolidation / analysis by ADNOC Group HSE Function for presentation to ADNOC Executive
Leadership Team.
ADNOC commitment to provide best in class quality data to stakeholders such as IOGP, requires a robust
monitoring process to detect and correct errors and omissions.
2. PURPOSE
The purpose of this Standard is to provide a framework for recognition and consistent reporting of the
HSE impacts to People, Environment, Assets and/or Reputation across the ADNOC Group Companies
(GC’s), Affiliates, Business Directorates & Functions in HQ. This is to enable continual improvement in
HSE performance and appropriate benchmarking of HSE Performance Data.
The objective of this Standard is to provide performance reporting principles, methodology, instructions
and templates to ensure a consistent basis for reporting, monitoring and assessment of Health, Safety
and Environment (HSE) Performance Data across the ADNOC Group.
3. SCOPE
This Standard stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates &
Functions in ADNOC Head Quarter, Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors.
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood,
implemented and thoroughly monitored.
4. LAWS AND REGULATIONS
The relevant UAE legislations applicable to this Standard includes but not limited to:
(i) Federal Law No. 8 (1980) UAE Labour Law and its Amendments Chapter V: Safety,
Protection, and the Health and Social Care of the Employees; Article (91).
(ii) UAE Ministerial Order No. (32), 1982. Specifying Preventive Methods and Measures for
Protecting Workers against Work Hazards.
(iii) UAE Ministerial Order No. (37/2), 1982. Establishing the level of medical attention, the
employer is obliged to provide to his workers.
(iv) Federal Law No.: 24 of 1999 for the Protection and Development of the Environment.
Group Companies shall ensure that their activities comply with all relevant UAE Federal and Local laws
and Regulations at all times, including any that may be introduced after the publication of this Standard.

7.4. PERFORMANCE REPORTING PRINCIPLES
Reporting principles are broad concepts that form the basis upon which HSE reporting can be developed
and improved over the time. Sustainability and HSE accounting and reporting practices are evolving;
however, the principles listed below are derived from generally accepted accounting and reporting
policies. They are intended to underpin and guide data gathering and reporting to ensure that the reported
information fairly presents the ADNOC Group HSE performance.
(a) Relevance
Ensure that the HSE Key Performance Indicators appropriately reflect the ADNOC Group’s HSE
performance and serve the decision-making needs of users - both internal and external. The data
shall be meaningful and valuable to the user. The KPIs are listed in Appendix 1 to Appendix 13 of
this Standard.
(b) Consistency
Use consistent methodologies to allow for meaningful comparisons of data over time.
Transparently document any changes to the data, inventory boundary, methods, or any other
relevant factors in the time series.
(c) Transparency
Address all relevant issues in a factual and coherent manner, based on a clear audit trail. Disclose
any relevant assumptions and make appropriate references to the accounting and calculation
methodologies and data sources used.
(d) Accuracy
Ensure that the quantification of HSE data is systematically neither over nor under actual
performance, as far as can be judged, and that uncertainties are reduced to a level As Low As
Reasonably Practicable (ALARP). Achieve sufficient accuracy to enable users to make decisions
with reasonable confidence in the integrity of the reported information. The accuracy of HSE data
shall be enhanced through digitalization to reduce errors of omission and commission.
ADNOC Group to review and implement requirements for installation of HSE Data monitoring
systems where appropriate to enhance data accuracy.
(e) Completeness
All HSE data shall be included in a manner that is consistent with the definitions, scope and
boundaries of this Standard. Reported information shall be as complete as is reasonably
practicable while recognizing the inherent limitations of the reported data. Any specific exclusion
shall be disclosed and justified.
(f) Efficient
Ensure that HSE Performance Reporting templates are user friendly with inbuilt formulae as
applicable and no redundant information within different reports.

7.5. MANAGING HSE DATA
ADNOC Business Directorates / Group Companies / Affiliates shall incorporate and apply effective
responses to ensure reliable reporting. HSE data shall be accurate and complete to be useful. ADNOC
Group shall manage HSE data quality systematically as part of their HSE Management System. The HSE
data management shall ensure that:
(i) Appropriate monitoring, checks and review processes are in place to detect and correct
errors and omissions;
(ii) Data processing and review are assigned to ensure submission of accurate and complete
data; and
(iii) Definitions used and the scope of the data management and reporting are in accordance
with this Standard, for the whole reporting period.
7.5.1. INHERENT LIMITATIONS
HSE performance data shall be determined by using the most accurate methodology that is reasonably
practicable. There may be inherent limitations to the accuracy, precision and completeness of HSE data
requiring reasonable efforts and appropriate techniques to rectify. These limitations stem from the nature
of the data. Certain parameters rely on human dynamics and are affected by culture and personal
perception. Other parameters rely on complex multivariate measurements that require constant tuning
so that the data is inherently imprecise. Still others rely on estimation, which lacks accuracy and precision.
For cases where no direct measurement parameter is available ADNOC Group HSE Function shall be
consulted for getting direction on implementing suitable measuring mechanism. ADNOC Group
Company and Affiliates shall accordingly provide full information on the limitations associated with the
measurement for its resolution by ADNOC Group HSE Function on case by case basis.
7.5.2. RE-SUBMISSION & RE-STATEMENT
It is possible that some time after the closing of the HSE data submission, errors are detected. This could
be a calculation error, a wrong application of default factors, omission of a reporting entity, changes to
qualitative data such as root causes, Life Saving Rule linkage, description of incidents etc. In these cases,
a re-submission of data shall be considered.
Changes larger than 10% shall always be re-submitted to the Business Organization. Changes between
5% and 10% are subject to discussion with the Business Organization. Changes smaller than 5% can be
communicated during the next round of data submission, with an explanation of the occurrence, but
published data shall not be amended.
7.5.3. INHERENT CONTROLS FOR HSE DATA MANAGEMENT
(a) Segregation of Duties
Proper consideration shall be given to segregate the duties of those directly rewarded by improved
HSE performance (monetarily, job promotion, achieving targets, meeting dashboard objectives)
from those who collect, classify and report HSE data; a lack of such segregation may create a risk
of impaired objectivity. Where formal segregation of duties is not possible, the objectives can also
be achieved by internal implementing independent reviews. In addition, improved controls over
data quality can be achieved by having different individuals involved in the collecting, checking and
reporting of data.
(b) Managing Data Risk
Risk management is the process used that enables resource allocation and the delivery of
expected results. Business Process are required to apply the ADNOC Risk Management Standard
[Ref. 22] to ensure that they have fit-for-purpose controls in place for data reporting. Data risk is

the potential for material error/omission, which could impact our understanding of performance or

To minimize the risk of reporting unreliable data, the HSE Function Planning Team shall prepare a
data controls assurance plan at the beginning of the year. The plan will identify data risks, assess
if the risks are material and apply controls to address the risks during the year.
The HSE data shall:

(ii) Assess the likelihood and severity of the error/omission. A high data risk occurs when there

is 5-10% error/omission and low risk <5% error/omission.
(iii) Apply responses after mapping existing controls to identify gaps that need to be better
controlled and to show where unnecessary work on low risk areas could be eliminated.
(iv) Demonstrate the risk analysis, together with progress against a mitigation plan at the year-
end.
(c) Audit Trail
Each reported parameter shall have an audit trail in order to facilitate control and review of HSE
data. An audit trail is a clear (documented) record of definitions, assumptions, aggregation,
calculations and references that result in the final reported data. For each of these data process
steps, it shall be indicated whether relevant documentation exists. This documentation shall be
kept in the “HSE Audit Trail” file, or an indication shall be given as to where the document is filed
(e.g. IT system, a web site, other department etc.). In addition, responsibilities for internal control
and signing off shall be defined.
(d) Data Checks and Review
Data checks shall be defined and documented in an audit trail that includes a description of the
roles and responsibilities for those conducting the checks. The level of data checking will partly be
dictated by the complexity of the parameter, the nature of the data collection, management and
reporting process.
As a minimum the data checks shall include:
(i) Checks on application of definitions;
(ii) Checks on calculations;
(iii) Variance analysis against a target, a prediction or the value from the previous period
(iv) Trend analysis (comparison of data over time, taking production/events into account)
(v) Cross plotting trends against related parameter to check consistency
(vi) Crosschecks with other sources – Internal (e.g. financial records) or external (e.g.
independent measurements taken by the authorities);
(vii) ‘Sign-off’ procedures that data has been checked for accuracy and completeness.

7.6.4. ADNOC HSE AUDIT PROGRAM
HSE assurance within the ADNOC Group is being executed at two distinct levels in compliance with
ADNOC HSE Governance Framework [Ref.10]] and ADNOC Audit & Assurance Standard [Ref. 16], as
described below.
(a) ADNOC HSE Audits
(i) At a corporate level, where ADNOC monitors the corporate HSE performance and conducts
audits across ADNOC Group.
(ii) One of the prime purposes of these audits is identification of areas of improvement and
verification of Group Companies’ / Affiliates’ compliance with ADNOC HSEMS Expectations.
However, and equally important, these audits contribute to the corporate overview of
common gaps/weaknesses in HSE performance management, with a view to initiating
remedial corporate programs. In addition, the audits play an important role in the
identification and subsequent dissemination of corporate ‘best practice’.
(b) Group Companies / Affiliate HSE Audits
(i) Internally, within the Group Companies / Affiliates, where management monitors HSE
performance, initiates internal audits, self-assessments and subsequently reviews the audit
reports for weaknesses and strengths of ADNOC HSEMS implementation.
(ii) Group Companies / Affiliates are maintaining adequate numbers of qualified and competent
HSEMS auditors.
(iii) Group Companies / Affiliates’ audit team are multidisciplinary in order to carry out the
independent audits. To ensure compliance with ADNOC Audit & Assurance Standard [Ref.
16], Group Companies / Affiliates shall establish a procedure for qualification, experience,
competency level, approval and training-retraining programme to maintain auditor skills.

7.10. PERFORMANCE MONITORING
The sequential steps and activities defined in this Standard constitute the process that shall be followed
for an accurate, effective and efficient monitoring, measuring, analyzing and reporting on how successful
ADNOC Group is implementing the ADNOC HSEMS, to meet corporate objectives and targets.
ADNOC Group HSE shall be the custodian of the ADNOC-wide monitoring and reporting system, which
imperatively requires addressing regulatory compliance, internal HSE performance improvement, in
addition to benchmarking.
HSE Data shall be recorded for consistency and comparability using accessible input tools and be
organised for ease of retrieval, reporting, analysis or audit.
The tools, usually Enterprise Resource Planning applications (ERP’s) shall be selected to provide clarity
on HSE data ownership and accountability for confirming its completeness and consistency. Best practice
requires using a suitable/proven tools that can readily interface with other related applications as
mandated by ADNOC Group HSE Function.
The corporate monitoring and reporting system methodology to be reflected in procedures at Group
Companies and Affiliates level, shall:
(i) Be kept simple and relevant to encourage data reporting and avoid unnecessary complexity
and burden.
(ii) Clarify how required HSE data shall be collected and classified against clear definitions;
using accepted industry guidelines as deemed appropriate, to facilitate benchmarking and
reporting to external stakeholders, including IOGP.
(iii) Ensure low-risk events that occur on a more frequent basis do not dominate reporting at the
expense of a focus on high-risk events. [The former can be given prominence via indicators
related to such events, especially leading indicators].
(iv) Ensure reporting boundaries are carefully defined to ensure inclusion of all reportable events
and incidents, relevant to ADNOC HSEMS.
Annually, ADNOC Group HSE may conduct, as and when required a critical assessment of the monitoring
and reporting system that includes:
(i) An evaluation of the system practicality and effectiveness;
(ii) The completeness and suitability of the KPI’s;
(iii) The alignment of definitions with international guidelines and best practice;
(iv) The interlink and alignment with ADNOC Corporate Risk Matrix;
(v) The quality and effectiveness of HSE data analysis and trending;
(vi) The effectiveness of implementing critical improvements from lessons learned;
(vii) Timely and effective cascading and implementation of related ELT decisions.
All GC’s and Affiliates stakeholders shall be fully involved in any proposed recommendation to make
changes to the system because there can be multiple uses of established data and learnings.

7.12.1. CORPORATE LEARNING AND COMMUNICATION
ADNOC corporate learning process involves multiple communication mechanisms/tools for sharing the
Performance Data Analysis outcome identified as “lessons learned”.
ADNOC Group HSE Function shall determine the appropriate type of communication to be used
depending on the nature of the lessons learned and the recipients of the key learnings.
Alignment with best practices should cover, as a minimum, the below listed communication
mechanisms/tools:
(i) Performance Dashboard: communicating near reel time data through digital solution
helping Senior Management in continual monitoring the Performance trends for informed
decision making
(ii) Communication Packs: prepared presentations and supporting information to equip a
manager, supervisor, or team leader to reinforce applicable lessons more thoroughly than
they could in a standalone bulletin
(iii) HSEMS Update Process: communicating learnings to ADNOC HSEMS Custodian and
Administrators for any required update in the System based on Performance Analysis outcome
(iv) Workshops and Forum: sharing the key lesson learned across the Organization through
workshops and forum ensuring effective communication and continual improvement
(v) Alerts or Bulletins: briefly summarizing lessons from events or assessments that can be
widely disseminated through the workforce
(vi) Tool-Box Talks: onsite workgroup discussions to apply learnings in preparation for an
upcoming task
(vii) Training: updating specific topic course content & competency matrices to reflect the learning.
Gauging Effectiveness – ADNOC Group Companies and Affiliates shall establish mechanisms/tools to
track effectiveness of communication and identifying areas of improvement.

7.15. REPORTING TO INTERNATIONAL ORGANIZATIONS
ADNOC Group Companies and Affiliates shall report their annual performance statistics to ADNOC Group
HSE Function as per the reporting template adopted from the International Organizations (e.g.
International Association of Oil & Gas Producers (IOGP)). The Reporting Template adopted shall be
shared by ADNOC Group HSE Function to the ADNOC Group Companies and Affiliates.
The data serves two distinct purposes, namely:
(i) It permits the respective ADNOC Executive Leadership Team to monitor Group Company
performance and to highlight issues that may require additional attention and/or resources;
(ii) Provides input to the Annual ADNOC Group HSE Performance Report, which includes key
HSE results of all Group companies, and are presented as overall ADNOC Group statistics.
Group Company / Affiliates’ HSE results shall be verified for correctness and accuracy by the Corporate
HSE prior to reporting to ADNOC. Data reported annually to ADNOC comprises selected key
performance statistics, which are only part of the total performance data that shall be recorded by the
Group Companies.

addressing issues that require corporate overviews and approaches).

HSE Function for compilation and distribution to stakeholders as per established requirements.

Incident. Large companies with multiple substantial business units may report milestones per business
unit.

paid overtime but exclude leave, sickness and other absences.

be broken down into ADNOC Companies in a contracting/servicing role (e.g. ADNOC Drilling, ADNOC
