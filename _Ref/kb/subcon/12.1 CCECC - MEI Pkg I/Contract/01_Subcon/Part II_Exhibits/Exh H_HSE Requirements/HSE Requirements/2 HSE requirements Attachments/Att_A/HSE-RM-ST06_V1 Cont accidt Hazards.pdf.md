1. INTRODUCTION
The nature of the oil, gas and petrochemical industry means there is the potential for Major Accidents to
occur. On a worldwide basis, there have been instances where Major Accidents have occurred, leading
to substantial loss of life. The risks of such events happening within the ADNOC Group therefore need
to be properly assessed and, where necessary, controlled.
ADNOC Group must take all measures necessary to identify and prevent Major Accidents and limit their
potential consequences to people, assets, environment and or reputation at sites which they operate.
Prevention and mitigation must be based on reducing risks to broadly acceptable levels or to tolerable
level subject to risks being demonstrated to be As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP).
Under the self-regulatory regime for the oil industry in Abu Dhabi, ADNOC assumes the role of regulator
and uses ‘COMAH Reports’ (through the Health, Safety and Environmental Impact Assessment (HSEIA)
process) to evaluate the acceptability or otherwise, of proposed projects and existing Major Hazard Sites.
The COMAH approach is a systematic procedure for the identification, evaluation and documentation of
Major Accident Hazards (MAH) and the risk levels of new projects, existing facilities and operations. It
facilitates a preventative approach to risk management as appropriate risk control and mitigation
measures are then to be incorporated at an early stage in the design process or for existing operations.
The main value of the COMAH approach for existing facilities and operations lies in the evaluation and
documentation of Major Accident Hazards with subsequent focus on risks due to modifications/update
since last COMAH update, changes in codes and standards, control procedures, management of Health,
Safety and Environment (HSE) critical equipment & systems, HSE critical activities and task and required
competencies.
2. PURPOSE
The objective of this Standard is to define the approach to the Control of Major Accident Hazards (COMAH)
for all Major Hazard Sites in the ADNOC Group.
COMAH requires ADNOC Group to demonstrate that:
Major Accident Hazards in the design, construction, commissioning and operation (all phases of the
lifecycle) of their sites & facilities are identified and managed to risk levels which are either broadly
acceptable or are As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP) as defined in HSE-RM-ST01: HSE Risk
Management System [Ref. 1]. This includes hazards with ‘Major Accident Potential’ which may be
introduced within the Major Hazard Site boundary e.g. from marine, road transport and helicopter
operations, offsite or third parties.
Note: Detailed guidelines for the assessment of Hazards from road, marine and helicopter operations
outside Major Hazard Site boundaries are provided in separate HSE Standards and do not form part of
COMAH for inclusion within the HSEIA i.e.:
(i) Road Transport Operations Safety [Ref. 10]
(ii) Marine Operations Safety [Ref. 11]
(iii) Air Transport Operations Safety [Ref. 12]

7. REQUIREMENTS
The COMAH requirements are summarized as follows:
(i) The necessity for a COMAH report for all new projects (Greenfield), modifications (Brownfield) to
existing facilities (operating assets) is identified at the HSEIA screening [Ref. 2]. Where the need

projects/modifications. The COMAH Report must provide details of how Major Accident Hazards
will be managed throughout the project lifecycle (including operation phase) and preventive and
mitigation measures that are in place;
(ii) For existing facilities/operations, the COMAH Report is required to demonstrate that the risks
associated with Major Accident Hazards from operation of existing facilities are managed to
tolerable level with ALARP demonstration and required prevention and mitigation measures are in
place. The assessment shall take into account all operating facilities at given site and ongoing
projects which are in Engineering Procurement and Construction (EPC) phase. Integrated
approach shall be taken while carrying out required HSE studies to ensure completeness of
COMAH Report;
(iii) A COMAH Report is also required for Combined Phase operations. The Combined Phase
operations refer to special operations/projects that do not follow the standard project lifecycle.
Examples are drilling, well testing, infrastructure projects etc.;

7.1. COMAH PROCESS OVERVIEW
The production of a COMAH Report will assist ADNOC Group to identify any areas where action must be
taken to reduce risks and will be essential for operations, training and familiarisation purposes. The
COMAH Report provides the history on the management of HSE Critical Equipment and Systems
(HSECES) during design, build, commissioning and preparation to operate phases. The COMAH Report
will also provide ADNOC with a mechanism for ensuring best practice management of Major Accident
Hazards across the Group.
The COMAH Report is a tool for Operations and is always expected to focus on eliminating, reducing or
mitigating risks during the operations phase of the facility.

(ii) A demonstration that for all identified foreseeable and credible Major Accident Hazards and

either broadly acceptable region or tolerable region and demonstrated as low as reasonably
practicable (ALARP). This is achieved by carrying out Bow Tie Analysis (Qualitative Risk
Assessment), Quantitative Risk Assessment and other safety studies such as Fire and
Explosion Risk Assessment (FERA), Escape Evacuation & Rescue Assessment (EERA),
Emergency Equipment Survivability Assessment (ESSA), CFD assessments, etc. as

to ADNOC HSECES Management Standard [Ref. 6]);
(iv) A demonstration that Emergency Management System is in place and there is adequate
provision for full and safe escape & evacuation of all personnel from the facility in the event
of a Major Accident. That On-site and Off-site (where necessary) emergency response plans
have been prepared; that they have been communicated to all stakeholders; that they have
been tested and will continue to be tested periodically and that the necessary
training/exercising has and will continue to be provided. Refer to ADNOC Emergency
Response and Crisis Management Standard [Ref. 3];
(v) For projects – a plan for action/carry forward of recommendations to the next project phase

7.2. DESCRIPTIVE INFORMATION
COMAH report must contain detailed information of local environment and facilities being designed or
operated. The facility description is a useful document which provides internal and external stakeholders
with an introduction to the facility and an insight into the design and operation of the facility.
The facility description should include text, data, drawings, diagrams, schematics, photographs or any
other means of conveying information about the facility and activities to be conducted at the facility. The
facility description defines the intended range or scope of operation of the facility, the physical
arrangements of the facility, all of the activities on the facility, surrounding activities near the facility, and
the numbers of people involved in each type of activity. The facility description details the envelope or
range within which the GC is, or will, operate the facility and covers normal and emergency operations
anywhere within the set of conditions described.
The facility description should provide factual information regarding the physical layout, the controls and
proposed activities required to understand the major accident hazards that have been identified and
assessed in the safety studies, the arrangements for managing the risks of those MAHs and the
interactions between those risk control measures and the HSE management system. The information
should be sufficient to gain a full appreciation of the Major Accident Hazards and risk management
strategies from the safety studies. The facility description should highlight any novel or unusual conditions,
engineering solutions or technologies encountered or used at the facility.
Where COMAH report is part of HSEIA, reference may be made to relevant parts of the general
description as provided in the HSEIA Report [Ref. 2].
7.2.1. LEVEL OF DETAIL
The site/facility description should provide a document that contains important information but not include
too much detail that the document becomes quickly out of date and/or requires frequent revisions. The
focus of the facility description is on the design and operating philosophy and envelope.
The facility description should not replicate technical specifications and philosophies. COMAH report
must explain how the facility works and why particular safety design solutions were adopted. This means
the facility description must include, or describe, the design and operating philosophies for the facility,
and it must reference the design standards that have been used.
Group Company can provide information such as engineering drawings, maps, renderings, photographs,
specifications, technical descriptions, databases, etc. however adequate linkages should be given
between descriptions provided in the text of the facility description and the associated drawings,
photographs, maps, etc.

7.3. MAJOR ACCIDENT SCENARIOS
7.3.1. IDENTIFICATION OF MAJOR ACCIDENT HAZARDS
Major Accident Hazards are identified through hazard identification/hazard analysis such as HAZID,
ENVID and Hazards & Operability Study (HAZOP) workshops. Detailed list of hazards to be evaluated in
the HAZID and ENVID workshops are provided in the ADNOC HAZID/ENVID/OHID Standard, HSE-RM-
ST03 [Ref. 13]. HAZOP specifically deals with process hazards. HAZOP helps in identifying process
specific scenarios to be assessed as part of FERA and QRA studies and in identifying potential barriers
which may prevent, mitigate or otherwise control the hazard.
Major Accident Hazard is a hazard that has the potential to result in a ‘Major Accident’. Identified Hazards
from the HAZID/ ENVID/ OHID Register, HAZOP and supporting documents are screened and grouped
into Major Accident Hazards, Significant Environmental Impact, including Major Accident To The
Environment (MATTE), and High Occupational Health Risk.
All Major Accident Hazards shall be taken forward to COMAH Report. All Non-MAH hazards are
demonstrated to be managed through HSE management system as part of HSEIA process. Hazards
identified via ENVID and OHID process shall be taken forward to respective components of HSEIA i.e.
Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) and OHRA Respectively.
7.3.2. ASSESSMENT OF MAJOR ACCIDENT HAZARDS
The COMAH Report must demonstrate that detailed and systematic assessment of each Major Accident
Hazard is carried out. The potential likelihood, extent and severity of each identified scenario must be
determined at an appropriate level of detail required by nature of Major Accident Hazard. This is achieved
by using quantitative methodology such as Quantitative Risk Assessment (QRA) and associated safety
studies such as FERA, EERA, ESSA, CFD studies etc. Together these studies shall demonstrate that
facilities are designed and operated to manage the risk to As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP).
HSE studies shall be conducted as per the requirements illustrated in relevant ADNOC Standards.
The process of analysis and assessment of Major Accident Hazards must achieve five objectives:

7.4.2. BOW-TIES
The ‘Bow-Tie’ is a graphical display of the hazard’s potential threat and consequences, associated
barriers and how these barriers can fail (escalation factor). The MAH analysis is further presented through
Bow Tie method which is a Qualitative approach to identify additional appropriate controls (barriers) to
prevent the hazards and mitigate the potential consequences.
This exercise therefore helps in identification of key HSE Critical Equipment and System (HSECES) which
are in place and key HSE Critical Activities (HSECA) which are required to be carried out to ensure
management of Major Accident Hazard. It should be noted that Bow Tie is not the only means for
HSECES identification; in addition to bow-ties, documents such as PFD, P&ID, layout, engineering
deliverables, HSE Studies etc., shall also be reviewed to ensure all applicable HSECES are identified.
Therefore, all identified Major Accident Hazards must be subjected to comprehensive analysis using bow
ties. It is necessary that bow-ties are developed for each identified MAH which are grouped with similar
top event and are asset and location specific. Bow ties shall be developed in brainstorming workshop.
Bow Ties shall reflect the actual barriers which are in place. Where, barriers are changed prior to
finalization of typical project phase such as FEED, EPC, operation, Bow Ties shall be updated to reflect
these changes. For the EPC phase of the project, Bow ties conducted for EPC phase shall be updated
/validated for Operation phase.
All the safe guards and barriers identified in Bow ties shall be verified and confirmed as part of the
Commissioning check and PSSR to ensure that barriers are in place as per the design.
The detailed guidance and methodology on development of Bow Ties is presented in Appendix 1.

7.4.4. MANUAL OF PERMITTED OPERATION (MOPO)
MOPO is required to be developed for the operations phase of the project as part of the COMAH process.
The MOPO is a matrix, or set of matrices, that maps operational activities against foreseeable situations
that, if or when they arise, could compromise safe operating limits. These situations are commonly
identified from:
(i) The Threats and Escalation Factors identified as part of the Bow Tie assessments for Major
Accident Hazards; and
(ii) An assessment of other operations and activities that could contribute to the escalation of
an incident (e.g. continuing with hot work when fire pumps are unavailable).
Threats and Escalation Factors along with other situations that could compromise safe operating limits
are normally grouped under the following headings:

(i) Concurrent Operations, also referred to as SIMOPS (e.g. maintenance on critical elements
and/or production and/or construction and/or drilling and/or diving);
(ii) External influences (e.g. extreme weather, sea states, visibility); and
(iii) Inactive safeguards (e.g. Emergency Shut Down (ESD), fire-fighting systems) not available

The objective of a MOPO is to maintain an acceptable level of risk for the operations. The matrices shall
identify and differentiate between ‘stop’ conditions (i.e. operation not permitted) and what are ‘proceed

additional controls). Traffic lighting (i.e. red for “stop” and amber for “proceed with caution”) is a useful
tool to highlight the different conditions and should be adopted.
The MOPO shall be for permitted operations and not for prohibited operations, since the latter may lead
to the possible interpretation that anything not specifically prohibited is allowed.
MOPO shall be developed in a workshop for the projects. For modification projects where no changes
are seen in operating philosophy, MOPO can be retained from the existing facilities COMAH report.

7.5. EMERGENCY RESPONSE PLANS

A summary of the Emergency Response Plans must form part of the COMAH report though the ERP is

As part of the COMAH, review of Emergency Response Plans shall be carried out to demonstrate that

commissioning of the project.

shall be prepared, approved and rolled out prior to start of construction and mobilization of people.

Step 5: Identification of threat barriers: When effectively in place, threat barriers prevent the release of a

may often be common mode type failures and may concurrently affect the controls of more than one hazard.
Escalation factors typically cover many failure modes.
Example: A change in operating environment is an escalation factor that may invalidate systems in place to
manage many types of corrosion.

considered while identifying the escalation factors. These MAT are HSE Critical Integrity Activities and if not
carried out effectively, can lead to MAH.
Step 8: Identification of Escalation Factor Controls: Escalation Factor Controls are measures put into place
to mitigate the effects of Escalation Factors. Escalation Factor Controls are commonly referred to simply as
Escalation Controls.
Example: The Management of Change process is an escalation factor control for changes in operating
environment that could lead to increased corrosion.

APPENDIX 2 MANUAL OF PERMITTED OPERATIONS
The Manual of Permitted Operations (MOPO) is a tool to assist in planning and coordination of operations by
identifying safe operational limits and actions to take if/when situations arise which compromise operations
including:
 Concurrent Operations, also referred to as SIMOPS (e.g. maintenance on critical elements and/or
production and/or construction and/or drilling and/or diving);
 External influences (e.g. extreme weather, sea states, visibility); and
 Inactive safeguards (e.g. ESD, fire-fighting systems) not available or impaired.
The MOPO matrices shall identify and differentiate between:
 Conditions in which operations are not permitted (Stop);
 Conditions that can continue but it should be recognized that there is an increased risk and
additional control should be in place (Proceed with Caution); and
 Conditions that can continue with existing controls in place (Go).
Traffic lighting (i.e. red for “stop”, amber for “proceed with caution” and green for “go”) should be used to highlight
the different conditions.
The MOPO shall be for permitted operations and not for prohibited operations, since the latter may lead to the
possible interpretation that anything not specifically prohibited is allowed.
MOPO shall be developed in the workshop for the green field project or for existing facilities where substantial
changes in the operating philosophy is observed. For Brownfield project where no change are seen in operating
philosophy or current operation philosophy will be adopted, MOPO can be retained from the existing facilities
HSEIA. Where such information is not available, MOPO shall be developed.
The operations/maintenance/project team, whose members collectively are familiar with the Asset or activity
operation, the operating environment shall be part of the MOPO workshop.
A draft MOPO has been prepared based on following steps:
 Using the Bow-Ties, identify the Threats and Escalation Factors that may compromise the safe
operating limits;
 Identify other operations and activities that could compromise safe operating limits that should be
included in the MOPO;
 Develop the matrices of operations and activities against Threats and Escalation Factors under the
appropriate groupings (e.g. concurrent activities, external influences and inactive safeguards);
 Identify the ‘stops’ and ‘proceed with cautions’ using traffic lighting as appropriate; and
 Provide supporting guidance and explanatory notes as appropriate to assist Supervisors and Line
Managers if/when the situation arises.
These draft matrices are collectively reviewed during MOPO workshop for completeness and to ensure suitability
for use.
