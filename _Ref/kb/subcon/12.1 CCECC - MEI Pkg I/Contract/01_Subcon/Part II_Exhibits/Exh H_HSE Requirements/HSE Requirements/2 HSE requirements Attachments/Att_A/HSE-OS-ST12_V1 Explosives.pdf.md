1. INTRODUCTION
The use of explosives in the Oil and Gas industry is widespread in the exploration and production sectors;
ranging from seismic surveys to help map reservoir zones, to drilling operations to allows faster access
to oil reservoirs by perforating the casing or liner of drilled wells.
Serious incidents do unfortunately happen, such as the 6 fatalities recorded by the International
Association of Geophysical Contractors between 2000 and 2007 [See Appendix 1].
The handling of explosives as a specialist operation shall be considered a hazardous activity, which has
to be managed using a highly focused approach, in line with ADNOC aspiration of 100% HSE.
The use of explosives must also consider perceptional consequences when determining its use in areas
of acquisition. Third party perception is defined by exposure to information which is often incorrect and
biased to actuality where explosives are involved. It is therefore important to ensure a precautionary
principle when contemplating explosive use where exposure to third parties is likely. As a consequence
other seismic sources must be considered and determined as not suitable prior to the decision to use
explosive sources.
2. PURPOSE
The purpose of this document is to define the minimum requirements that shall be established and
maintained to ensure the highest safety standards are in place during all phases that involve the handling
of explosive materials; i.e. transportation, storage and use.
This Standard provides the necessary guidance to ensure all work related to explosives within the
ADNOC Group shall be undertaken under the supervision of qualified/competent persons; responsible
for establishing and enforcing site-specific procedures that include all necessary safety precautions.
3. SCOPE
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to the ADNOC Group and its
Contractors with regards to handling, storage and transportation of explosives.
ADNOC Group and its Contractors shall ensure that all requirements listed herein are fully understood,
implemented, complied with and monitored at all times.
4. LAWS AND REGULATIONS
UAE Legislations applicable to this Standard includes but not limited to:
(i) UAE Federal Law No. 3 of 2009 regarding Weapons, Ammunition and Explosives.

Standard.

7.1. GENERAL INFORMATION
7.1.1. OVERVIEW
Explosives are a combination of various substances and mixtures that produce a rapid exothermic
reaction when initiated. The explosive ingredients are either a molecular explosive where the fuels and
oxidizers are on the same molecule, such as with Trinitrotoluene (TNT) or a physical mixture of solid
and/or liquid oxidizers and fuels such as in emulsion explosives.
Explosive materials are routinely initiated by the shock effect of a detonator. They are sensitive to heat,
shock (impact), or friction so they shall be protected from these potential triggers to prevent premature,
unintended or accidental detonation.
Explosives used for seismic exploration are either of the dynamite-type (nitro-glycerine with oxidizing salts
and solid fuel components), non-nitro-glycerine explosives which are generally emulsions or water gels
and molecular types (Pentolite) which are combinations of ingredients that are individually explosive
materials, such as Pentaerythritol tetranitrate (PETN) and TNT. Emulsions and water gels are aqueous
explosives (water based) which contain fine mixtures of explosive ingredients which are either emulsified
to form a ‘mayonnaise’ like explosive or crossed linked to form a ‘gel’ like explosive.
7.1.2. SECURITY ASPECTS
In line with American Petroleum Institute Recommended Practice (API RP) 67 [Ref. 1], consistent security
measures shall be established and routinely applied, in line with UAE government laws and in
coordination with relevant government body.

7.2. CLASSIFICATION OF EXPLOSIVES AND HAZARD LABELING
Generally, explosives may be categorized by the speed of the chemical reaction. There are two
categories:
(i) High Explosives
(ii) Low Explosives
High explosives are materials where the chemical reaction through the material moves faster than the
speed of sound (this process is called detonate) whereas low explosives are materials where the chemical
reaction moves slower than the speed of sound (this is termed as deflagrate).
High explosives are further divided into two categories, which are primary and secondary explosives.
Primary explosives are sensitive and can detonate by ignition by a flame or spark. They are generally
used as detonators. Secondary explosives require a detonator to explode and is used as the main charge
of an explosion.

7.3. OTHER EXPLOSIVE MATERIALS
7.3.1. DETONATORS
These are devices containing an initiating or primary explosive that is used for initiating a detonation in
another explosive material. There are several types of detonators, which include but are not limited to:
• Fuse caps
• Electric detonators
• Non-electric detonators
• Electronic detonators
7.3.2. PYROTECHNIC DEVICES AND HAND-HELD EXPLOSIVE TOOLS
In general, the pyrotechnic devices and tools such as flares, signal cartridges, ammunition for nail guns,
etc. held in the quantities for normal operations are outside the scope of this Standard. Nonetheless, for
guidance purposes, the equipment shall only be operated by trained operators and in accordance with
the manufacturer’s recommendations and international regulations.
When not in use, the equipment shall be kept under lock and key and be under the control of a
Responsible Person. Records shall be kept of receipts and issues.
7.4. EXPLOSIVES SELECTION PROCESS
When using explosive materials in an operation, careful consideration shall be given to the product type,
characteristics and long-term implications in the event of a misfire. Different explosive and detonator types
are better suited to different requirements, work site conditions, and terrain. Examples of such explosives
are:
• Dynamite (nitroglycerine)
• Pentolite (PETN and TNT)
• Emulsions
• HMX
• HNS
• RDX
• Propellant (Chemical Cutter)
Each Group Company shall conduct the necessary assessment to determine the right type of explosive
and detonator for each particular operation.

All personnel involved in the use, handling and transportation of explosives shall be adequately trained
and qualified to be able to identify all specific hazards associated with those activities.
7.5.1. IDENTIFYING THE HAZARDS
The first step in managing risks associated with explosives is to identify all associated hazards, which
may potentially cause incidents and harm people.

Group companies shall ensure that their hazard registers include all identified site/project specific hazards,
which are associated with the use, handling and transportation of explosives.
The hazard register shall reference the specific controls that are required to reduce risk to acceptable
levels, i.e. ALARP (as low as reasonably practicable).
These hazard registers shall be kept continually updated, and double checked prior to mobilizing to site
or start of any new project involving the use of explosives.
7.5.2. ASSESSING THE RISKS
Following the identification of hazards related to the handling of explosives, Group Companies shall
ensure risk assessments are conducted for all the planned activities to:
(i) identify which workers will be at risk;
(ii) assess the likelihood of each hazard causing harm and determine the level of risk;
(iii) assess the effectiveness of existing control measures and decide for each remaining hazard
whether the risk is ALARP; and
(iv) Identify, as necessary, what additional control measures shall be implemented to achieve ALARP.
When assessing the likelihood of each hazard actually causing harm in a specific situation, the following
can be considered:
(i) How often and for how long will exposure to the explosives hazard occur?
(ii) If exposed to the hazard will the outcome be severe, moderate or mild?
(iii) What is the substrate being blasted?
(iv) What is the likelihood of damaging any assets?
(v) What are the surface coatings of the items being blasted? For example, do they contain lead or
other toxic metals?
(vi) What are the conditions under which the explosives handling activities are being carried out?
(vii) What are the skills, competence and experience levels of personnel?

7.5.3. CONTROLLING THE RISK
(i) Eliminating the risk
The use of explosive materials shall be strictly restricted to the specialist activities for which they
are absolutely required and have no substitute in Geophysical and Drilling Operations.
(ii) Minimizing the risk
1. Isolation: Separating the explosive materials handling hazard from personnel, shall be
implemented as far reasonably practicable at the work/well site. For example safe distances
delineated by barriers with warning signs.
2. Engineering controls: The engineering controls are the physical control measures that
minimise the explosive materials handling risk.
3. Administrative controls: Group companies shall consistently comply with the requirements
of this Standard, applicable laws & regulations, all the permit to work conditions while
applying prudent operating practices and procedures in the conduct of work involving the
handling of explosives.

7.5.4. MAINTAINING AND REVIEWING CONTROL MEASURES
Group companies shall ensure that the established control measures are strictly implemented and

(i) when an incident or near miss occurs because of a hazard the risk assessment failed to consider;
(ii) before making changes to approved method statements for executing activities at site; and
(iii) when there are changes to personnel assigned to carry out the planned work.
7.6. QUALIFICATIONS OF EXPLOSIVES HANDLERS

All personnel working with explosives materials shall be trained in the proper handling and use of these
products in compliance to local regulations and industry standards. The United Arab Emirates Ministry of
Interior requires workers in the explosives industry to have a license before being legally allowed to work.
Requirements to be qualified for working with explosives shall include, but not limited to:
• Obtaining a health certificate stating the absence of physical illness, mental illness, psychological
disorder, and fitness to perform duties from the Ministry of Health and the Health Authority of Abu
Dhabi;
• Providing a certificate or evidence of training in the handling and transport of explosives, with
relevant work experience covering the previous 24 months;
• Providing evidence of positive security check or no previous criminal records; and
• Undergoing a Basic First Aid training course.

7.7. SAFETY PRECAUTIONS DURING THE HANDLING OF EXPLOSIVES
7.7.1. GENERAL
The following general requirements which include but are not limited to the following, shall be
implemented:
(i) Radio silence is mandatory and the use of radio or any kind of radio transmitter when handling
explosives is strictly prohibited. This includes, cellular phones, Global Positioning System (GPS)
stations, Frequency Modulation (FM), Very High Frequency (VHF) radios, etc.
(ii) Use of tools that can generate sparks is prohibited.
(iii) Smoking in the vicinity of operations using explosives or near storage area is forbidden.
(iv) Use of explosives shall be avoided during night time, where possible. If necessary for operational
requirements, Group Companies shall ensure an adequate level of lighting, not less than 200 lux,
is provided, to enable:
1. charging operations to be carried out safely;
2. inspections to be done properly after charges are fired; and
3. any misfire to be dealt with in accordance with contractor procedures.
(v) Personnel shall ensure themselves to be grounded frequently by touching clean metal surfaces or
touching the ground with the bare hand. This shall be done immediately prior to handling detonators.
(vi) Detonators shall not be placed inside the pockets of the personnel’s clothes.
(vii) Detonators shall not be handled during sand or electrical storms. During such weather conditions,
all work shall cease and the explosives and detonators shall be left in sight but separated by a
distance of at least 25 m at all times.
(viii) Explosives and detonators shall not be left unattended.
(ix) All personnel shall assure that there is no unauthorized person or animals inside the blasting zone.
(x) Non-critical personnel shall maintain a safe distance while the hole is being loaded.
(xi) A charge is considered primed when it has been fitted with a detonator. The detonator shall be
inserted into the charge only when the charge is ready to be loaded, never before that.
(xii) A charge shall not be primed prior to verification of the hole. Any charges primed shall not be left
lying around the work site.
(xiii) All personnel shall wear the required PPE.
(xiv) All personnel shall report any incident that occurs during the operation.
(xv) Explosives shall not be armed within the vicinity of high-voltage power lines. If unavoidable, a risk
assessment shall be conducted to determine the minimum required safe distance and necessary
precautions taken to mitigate risks.
(xvi) The use of detonators which have higher stray voltage threshold shall be considered for each
explosive operation.
(xvii) In the event of sandstorms or bad weather, any explosives that are already in hole shall be left in
hole until the bad weather has passed. Explosives shall not be armed when bad weather is
approaching. If any explosive is armed but cannot be run in hole before the bad weather arrives, it
shall be disarmed and the detonator shall be moved to a safe area, inside a grounded shield.
(xviii) Any hot work shall not be performed in the vicinity of any explosive operation. If required, a
minimum safe distance shall be determined. Any other activities which may impact the explosion
operation shall also be treated in the same manner.
(xix) Detonator wires must be shunted while not in use or up to the time of arming.

(xx) Any cathodic protection system within the vicinity shall be turned off to avoid stray voltage.

7.7.2. MISFIRES
Explosive materials are expected to function as per manufacturer’s specifications if handled and stored
properly. Every shot point that is drilled and loaded is intended to detonate. The way explosives are
handled contributes significantly to the prevention of misfired charges.
To reduce the remote probability of a misfire, the following shall be implemented:
 Always follow manufacturers’ recommendations for safe use and limitations
 Never handle or use explosive materials unless qualified, trained and certified or under the direct
supervision of a person who is qualified, certified and trained
 Always be completely familiar with the required safe work procedures and regulations for the area
in which you are operating
 Never investigate the contents of a detonator
 Never pull wires, coupling device, plastic tubing, or detonating cord out of any detonator
Misfires shall be dealt with as promptly as possible to eliminate the potential hazard of any misfired hole
being accidentally initiated. A thorough risk assessment should be conducted to determine the safest,
most effective way to deal with the misfire.
Since misfires occur under varied conditions and are caused by different factors, it is impossible to offer
detailed instruction to cover every situation. Due to the potential hazards involved, only personnel who
are certified, trained and experienced in the use of explosive materials shall handle misfired charges. All
misfire scenarios shall be identified in the risk assessment conducted together with remedial actions for
each scenario identified. The results of the risk assessment shall be communicated to all relevant
personnel.

explosives, all personnel shall be located at least 23 m (75 ft.) from the equipment under
test.
(ii) Downhole Equipment
1. Primary High Explosives: Primary high explosives are only permitted in electric and
percussion detonators. Detonators or initiators shall not contain any exposed primary
explosive.

one of the following features:
 Direct Current (DC) resistance of not less than 50 ohms and a “no-fire” current of not
less than 200 milliamperes.
 A high-voltage exploding bridge wire (EBW) design.
 A high-voltage exploding foil initiator (EFI) design.
 Features providing protection substantially equivalent to that afforded by the features
described in items 2 and 3, and which have been validated by an independent,
recognized testing agency.
(iii) Downhole Firing Systems: Capacitive discharge systems shall incorporate a permanent bleed-off
resistor that should discharge the capacitor to below 50% of the minimum required firing voltage
within 60 seconds.

Explosives are very delicate and environmental factors such as temperature and humidity have an impact
on their performance and stability. Explosives shall be kept in accordance with suppliers’ requirements

General requirements of explosives storage shall include, but are not limited to:
 Never keeping primed explosives in storage.
 Only temporary storage shall be allowed at operating sites for the specific operations required.

 Detonators and explosives shall be stored separately and a minimum distance of 25 meters shall
be kept between them.
 Storing only in their original packaging.
 Keeping away from exposure to direct sunlight.
 Properly stacking and securing from any accidental impact.

7.9. TRANSPORTATION OF EXPLOSIVES
7.9.1. GENERAL REQUIREMENTS
The following are some general requirements with regards to explosive transportation:
(i) Any transport of explosives shall comply with UAE government regulations and Police
requirements dictating time and quantities allowed.
(ii) A journey management plan shall be in place before the start of the journey. The plan has to include,
but will not be limited to:
 Selection of safest and most secure route avoiding populated areas and industrial areas
 Safe rest stops
 Security escort (preferred with uniformed national law enforcement guard)
 Emergency response protocol in case of vehicle break down
(iii) The vehicle used to transport the explosives shall be fit-for-purpose and well-maintained. The
vehicle shall be equipped with emergency equipment such as a first aid kit and fire extinguishers.
(iv) Non-sparking material shall cover any parts of the vehicle which may come in contact with the
explosive load.
(v) The vehicle shall have at least one fire extinguisher in the vehicle along with a first aid box.
(vi) There shall be no radio equipment fixed inside the vehicle, unless they come equipped with
approved magazines with detonator segregation.
(vii) The vehicle shall be protected with at least one security guard. It can never be left unattended.
(viii) Vehicles carrying explosives must not stop to refuel or enter a workshop for services.
(ix) Explosives and detonators shall be transported in separate Faraday boxes, preferably placed in
separate vehicles altogether.
(x) Only authorized passengers trained in handling explosives may travel with together with the
explosives.
(xi) Transport operations are only allowed during the day. In the case of bad weather, operations are
to be ceased.
(xii) Any dangerous, spark-producing or flammable items shall not be transported with the explosives.
Smoking during loading and unloading, or anywhere near the vicinity of the transport is strictly
prohibited.

Each Threat, Hazard, and Aspect has been classified with a Risk Level. There are two Risk Level
classifications (RL1 and RL2) and they are defined as:
RL1 – Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health (IDLH), Life Critical, major environmental impact,
Process Safety, serious injury, explosion, dropped objects, loss of well control, loss of primary
containment, major property damage greater than $250,000.
RL2 – All remaining HSE and Service Delivery impacts not listed in RL1.

All Threats, Hazards, and Aspects are evaluated using the Five Fundamentals methodology to ensure
each identified risk is appropriately mitigated.

It is everyone's responsibility to "STOP WORK" when Unsafe Conditions or Conditions that do not
support product quality or delivery are recognized.

NOTE:  Risk Levels identified as "RL1" REQUIRE "STOP WORK" until controls are in place to reduce risk
or impact.

For "RL2" Risk Levels, if controls cannot be implemented the team must stop and revaluate task to
determine new controls that can be implemented.
