3. INTRODUCTION
Hydrogen Sulphide (H S) is an extremely toxic, corrosive and flammable gas, causing serious
2
incidents in the hydrocarbon industry every year. At low concentrations, Hydrogen Sulphide is
detectable by its characteristic rotten-egg odour, but at higher concentration (above 100 ppm)
smell cannot be relied to forewarn dangerous concentrations as H S rapidly paralyses the
2
sense of smell. Exposure to H S above 500 ppm for any length of time is considered
2
potentially lethal.
In order to compare H S risk, not only the H S concentration (in air) but also the H S (release)
2 2 2
pressure is important. Hence the definition of H S partial pressure should be the prime metric
2
to be used when performing H S risks and verify mitigation.
2
Hydrogen Sulphide identified as Major Accident Hazard (MAH) in GASCO operations. The
COMAH studies conducted in GASCO recommend a high degree of risk controls & recovery
preparedness measures for managing Hydrogen Sulphide in our operations. GASCO have
identified 10 Life Saving Rules (LSR), one of them specifically to cover H S and stipulates to
2
carry a Personal Gas Detector & Emergency Escape Breathing Device (EEBD) when entering
an H S zone.
2
This standard summarises OPERATIONAL requirements for safe handling and working with
Hydrogen Sulphide that are stated in ADNOC-COPV4-10, April 2014 on the management of
Hydrogen Sulphide and provide further guidance on how to implement the ADNOC COP in
GASCO operational sites.
4. PURPOSE
The purpose of the standard is to:
 Outline the hazards associated with Hydrogen Sulphide and to ensure that the risks
to people from Hydrogen Sulphide releases are prevented, controlled and mitigated
to a risk level that is As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP); and
 Provide a consistent and safe work practices in GASCO Plants and Pipeline
Divisions for handling and working with Hydrogen Sulphide.
5. SCOPE AND APPLICATION
This standard is applicable to all activities involving exposure to Hydrogen Sulphide, which are
performed by employees and contracting companies within GASCO premises. It applies to all,
Operation and Maintenance activities in H S classified zones.
2
This standard does not address any DESIGN practices. Please refer to GASCO DGS for the
design requirements that stated in ADNOC CoP V4-10.

8. HEALTH HAZARDS OF HYDROGEN SULPHIDE
The major effect of H S on humans is its ability to cause rapid damage to respiratory system
2
or sudden death due to accidental exposure. The unpleasant odour of H S can even be
2
detected at concentrations of 0.02 ppm, may not be detectable at concentrations above 100
ppm due to rapid loss of the sense of smell. Therefore, one should never trust his sense of
smell to detect H2S presence. Exposure to H S above 500 ppm for any length of time is
2
considered as potentially lethal. The health effects of H S is detailed as below.
2
8.1 Acute Exposure Effect of H2S
H S is highly toxic at higher concentrations. It is absorbed rapidly, interferes with cellular
2
respiration. At higher concentrations, H S starts to affect the central nervous system and can
2
cause accumulation of fluid in the lungs (pulmonary oedema).
At 50-150 ppm H S can start to knock out the sense of smell and this is one of the dangers of
2
the gas; operators cannot rely on the sense of smell. At higher concentration levels this effect
is immediate and H S cannot be smelt at all.
2
The following summarizes the effects of H S at defined concentrations as reported in the
2
various standards and Shell Health Guidance on Acute Toxicity values for Hydrogen Sulphide.

10.3 Isolation Requirements
The isolations practices for the mechanical isolation shall be in line with GASCO Mechanical
Isolation Standard (HSE-ST-035).
The equipment in sour service shall be positively isolated, gas freed and purged as
necessary. SCBA/ Airline Breathing apparatus shall always be worn when breaking any
system integrity until the system has been deemed safe.
A single isolation valve is not regarded as adequate for safety, when a vessel or pipe section
is to be opened. For general maintenance operations there must be a positive isolation in the
form of a blind flange or spade, or a removable spool piece.
For some operations such as filter cleaning, frequent removal and replacement of spades
can increase risk. A double block and bleed valve arrangement may be provided for such
situations.
For lethal service, double block and bleed should be supported by a pressure indicator
between the block valves. Care should be taken in the design of this indicator, since it must
withstand full process pressure, but must be able to detect low pressures arising from valve
leakage.
Bleed from double block and bleed isolation must be routed to a suitable location, with no
possibility of back flow of gas, as could arise for example by routing the bleed to a
pressurized flare header.
Double block and bleed using valves remote from the section to be isolated is not acceptable,
being too prone to error.
When a leak occurs at sections of pipe where there is only one block valve between the
location and a large hydrogen sulphide inventory. In this case the best overall risk reduction
may be to close the single isolation valve, and make any repair etc. using airline breathing air
supply, or SCBA equipped with full face mask, if the work is very short, as the second level of
protection. In this case care must be taken to ensure that the air supply is adequate, that the
person(s) carrying out the work are supported by a “buddy” who also has an airline or SCBA
air supply, and that there are no persons in the potential hazard zone.
Positive Isolation means incorporating the use of spades, spectacle blinds, or removable
spools and blind flanges. A formal written scheme of isolation must be prepared with
isolations documented on the Isolation Confirmation Certificate (ICC).

10.6 Sumps, Drains and API separator
Sumps and drains are frequent areas for accidents. Mostly the accidents can be prevented
by confined space restrictions. There are cases, however where H S can be emitted from
2
sumps and drains. The most important ones are admission of rich amine solutions to the
drains, admission of hot water (steam condensate) to drains, and gas release in to closed
drains due to level controller failure.
Amine solutions which are drained from process equipment should be collected, not
released to drains. This may be done by providing a closed sump or by draining to a
portable vessel.
Drainage of hot water to drains needs to be controlled procedurally (i.e., SOP).

2
arising from unavoidable emissions during the operations together with background
emissions from sources such as leaking valve stems or local venting.

18. SELECTION OF RPE FOR FACIAL HAIR WEARERS
A number of employees have facial hair which together with the facilities operating with
Hydrogen Sulphide in concentrations exceeding fatal concentration demands that RPE be
effective and available to all. It is essential that in selecting RPE that careful consideration
be made to those individuals who may use the selected RPE that the RPE is suitable and
offers their respiratory organs as much protection as possible.
Facial hair as well as other surface related facial characteristics such as scars, wrinkles or
jewellery can significantly affect the expected protection due to interference with the seal
against the cheeks and chin portions of the face. RPE incorporating a hood design with
fitting adjustment are more suitable for use by facial hair wearers. Facial hair refers to
unshaven hair which has not been shaved within a day period prior to commencing work
up to a full beard of no more than 20cm in length from the base of the chin.
18.1 Certifications and Selection
The general principles for selection, use and maintenance of RPE are outlined in ADNOC
Codes of Practice on Personal Protective Equipment and Provision of Fire Service
Equipment. These Codes of Practice refer specifically to ‘Tight Fitting’ RPE for clean
shaven use.
A specification of the Code of Practice requires manufacturers to provide all proper
marking and CE marking of the apparatus and application of certification under existing BS
EN standards 136 & 137. BS EN standard for inward leakage is superseded by ISO16900-
1. This standard shall be followed by manufacturers who shall be required to provide
certified test results of the inward leakage test having used test subjects with facial hair.
18.2 Selection Based on RPE Types
The primary purpose of the RPE is to form a barrier between the wearer’s respiratory tract
and the contaminated environment. There are two types of RPE:
 Tight fitting - Full or half face masks rely on a good seal around the forehead,
cheek bones and under the chin. RPE certified under BS EN136: 1998 meet
ADNOC requirement and all manufacturers currently stipulate that these are for
use by clean shaven wearers only.
 Loose fitting – these have a hood with integrated internal oral nasal masks (½
masks) or full face mask. The integrated oral nasal type only provides a seal
around the neck of the wearer, whereas the latter with integrated full face mask
provide two potential seals. A primary seal on the face and a secondary seal
around the neck of the wearer. This design is more suitable for wearers with
facial hair including full beards, it also operates effectively with clean shaven
wearers
18.3 RPE Adequacy
Any form of RPE can only be considered adequate if it has the capacity to reduce the
wearer’s exposure to a hazard to acceptable levels (e.g. to comply with occupational
exposure limit values). The adequacy assessment of RPE for facial hair wearers must
evaluate if the inward leakage is sufficiently limited to offer the required protection to
maintain the wearers supplied air below the recommended WEL for H S exposure.
2

APPENDIX B- CHARACTERISTICS OF HYDROGEN SULPHIDE
Hydrogen Sulphide is a colourless flammable gas, which burns with a pale-blue flame forming
Sulphur Dioxide and water vapour. It has a very offensive odour similar to that of rotten eggs,
which can be detected at concentrations between 0.0002 to 0.3 ppm.
The main characteristics of Hydrogen Sulphide are outlined below;
 Extremely toxic and colourless gas.
 At lower concentrations, it is detectable by its characteristic rotten-egg odour
 At higher concentrations it paralyses the sense of smell, therefore one should
not trust his sense of smell to detect a leak.
 Normally present as a gas and not as liquid.
 Flammable, auto-ignition temperature of 260° C (500° F) and explosive
(flammable) range by volume in the air: 4.3% - 46%.
 It is soluble in both water and hydrocarbon liquids. Pools of water /oil or sludge
at the bottom of the tank or pit may contain concentrations of H S and if agitated
2
or heated will release the gas.
 It is also corrosive and soluble in water and hydrocarbon liquids and can cause
severe stress cracking of steel and other metals.
 H S is heavier than air (specific gravity = 1.1895) and can collect in low lying
2
areas, especially pits and sumps which are closed in and have poor ventilation.
 It burns with a blue flame and gives off Sulphur dioxide (SO2), which is also a
toxic gas.
 It can form pyrophoric Iron Sulphide on internal surfaces of carbon steel
equipment containing H S. Pyrophoric Iron Sulphide can auto-ignite when
2
coming into contact with air.

Assumption Sr. No. 10
Subject: Leak Frequency Analysis
DNV GL proprietary software LEAK was used for the leak frequency calculation of process
equipment and process piping. The leak frequencies for the main process equipment items
are based on an analysis of the HSE hydrocarbon release database (HCRD) for the period of
1992-2010. The LEAK software frequency calculations are consistent with OGP 434-1
Guideline for leak frequency analysis.
Definition of Leak types (Full releases, Limited releases and Zero pressure and Total
releases) as per OGP 434-1 is presented below:
 Full releases: consistent with flow through the defined hole, beginning at the normal
operating pressure, and continuing until controlled by emergency shut-down and
blowdown (if present and operable) or inventory exhaustion.
 Limited releases: cases where the pressure is not zero but the quantity released is
much less than from a full release. This may be because the release is isolated locally
by human intervention (e.g. closing an inadvertently opened valve), or by a restriction
in the flow from the system inventory (e.g. releases of fluid accumulated between
pump shaft seals).
 Zero pressure releases: cases where pressure inside the leaking equipment is
virtually zero (0.01 barg or less). This may be because the equipment has a normal
operating pressure of zero (e.g. open drains), or because the equipment has been
depressurised for maintenance.
 Total Releases: This is the overall frequencies for all release types (Full, Limited
Zero) taken together.
As advised by GASCO, zero pressure leaks were ignored (discounted) during leak frequency
calculation to avoid overestimation of H S zones.
