1. ABOUT THIS MANUAL
1.1 INTRODUCTION
Incidents involving motor vehicles remain one of the most significant causes of injuries and
fatalities in our operations. The road transport hazard has been assessed as a high-risk (i.e.
RAM red) activity hence it is categorized as a Major Accident Hazard (MAH).
United Arab Emirates Federal Law Number 8 (1980), Article 91, requires employers to
provide workers with preventative equipment to protect workers against the dangers of
occupational accidents. All persons driving on ADNOC Group Company business, whether
employee or contractor, must do so within the terms of UAE Federal Law No 21 of 1995.
GASCO has developed this Road Transport Management Manual to ensure that a
management system is in place to control the risks associated with road transportation. All
employees and contractors must follow the requirements of this Manual and display the
correct attitude and behaviour, as driving on Company business directly reflects the
Company’s reputation.
GASCO has performed a full analysis of the risks associated with road transportation. This
is documented in the Road Transport COMAH Report (HSE-R-122).
This Manual replaces the GASCO Road Safety Standard (HSE-ST-006) and the GASCO
Fleet Management Manual.
1.2 PURPOSE & SCOPE
This Manual describes all of the requirements for the management of road transport safety
for all GASCO’s vehicles as well as contractors, and the controls in place for managing
company owned, leased or rented vehicles. These vehicles are:
 Light Vehicles
 Heavy Goods Vehicles
 Buses and Mini Buses
 COMAH Site Vehicles
 Trailers
See section 3.1 for details on types of vehicles covered by this Manual.
Any deviation(s) to requirements as specified in this Manual will need a detailed risk
assessment and approval from the HSE Division.
This Manual applies to all GASCO Plants, Pipelines, Projects and Head Office Divisions
and ALL CONTRACTORS as specified in section 1.4 and applies to the types of vehicles
listed in this Manual.

1.3 ROAD TRANSPORT POLICY
Road Transport Policy
GASCO is committed to:
 Protecting staff, contractors and third parties from harm arising from road
transport related activities.
GASCO shall:
 Implement and maintain a Road Transport Management system, which is
aligned to relevant UAE and ADNOC regulations, standards and procedures, to
attain the highest road safety standards.
 Demonstrate leadership and seek engagement in road transport safety through
collaborative relationships.
 Hold Line Management accountable for road safety performance, giving the
same importance to road transportation as any other business.
 Provide adequate training and promote participation of all employees in
improving road safety performance.
 Implement innovative solutions to improve road transportation safety.
 Conduct transportation activities in a manner designed to minimize the fuel
consumption and HSE risks to a level, which is As Low As Reasonably
Practicable (ALARP), which includes the consideration of other alternatives to
road transportation activities.
 Conduct periodic audits and reviews to verify compliance with this policy and
implement audit recommendations to improve our road transportation
management system.
This Road Transport Policy is available in Arabic and English. It is given to each employee
who is authorized to drive company vehicles upon completion of the GASCO Safe Driver
Training. It is also included in the GASCO Safe Driving Handbook and discussed and
explained in relevant training courses. The policy is also displayed in prominent locations,
such as notice boards and transport offices.

1.4 APPLICABILITY TO CONTRACTORS
The specifications in this Manual are applicable to all contractors and sub-contractors who
provide Primary and Secondary Logistical support services to GASCO. The specifications
are not applicable to services defined as Tertiary Logistics. The definition of these terms is
as follows:
Primary Logistics
 Refers to all logistics resources, irrespective of whether they are owned, leased,
chartered or assigned, which are operated by own staff and/or a third party, where
the scope of work is predominately (>50% of available time) for the direct provision
of logistics services to the company or any other third party, as directed by the
Company. The General Services Department directly controls these resources. All
the standards in this Manual are to be applied.
 Examples: Emirates Transport, AVIS, etc.
Secondary Logistics
 Refers to all logistics resources, either full time or on part time basis, which are
being provided as part of a contract scope of work, mainly intended for the provision
of services other than Primary Logistics. These resources are controlled by the
respective Division separately from the General Services Department. All the
standards in this Manual are to be applied.
 Example: EPC Contractors, contractors providing services such as Logistics,
Operations and Maintenance, shutdown, catering, etc.
Tertiary Logistics
 Refers to logistics resources whose services to the Company are only a minor part
of their business and whose business provides services to other companies within a
single logistical journey. The standards in this Manual do not need to be applied.
 Examples: DHL, Aramex, Catering Companies sub-suppliers (e.g. fish, bread,
vegetable), etc.

Modes of Contract
GASCO and contractors (aka partners) work under HSE Management Systems. Depending
on the maturity of the Contractor’s HSE Management System and/or the type of work
performed by the contractor, it will be (contractually) agreed under whose management
system the work will be conducted. There are 3 different Modes of contract:
Mode 1: GASCO HSE MS system applies, including any specific rules and requirements,
thus including this Manual. GASCO verifies overall effectiveness of the
Contractors HSE MS.
Mode 2: Contractors HSE MS system applies; bridging documents to GASCO HSE MS,
thus including this Manual. GASCO verifies overall effectiveness of the
Contractors HSE MS.
Mode 3: Contractors HSE MS system applies; following this Manual is not mandatory.
GASCO not obliged to verify overall effectiveness of the Contractors HSE MS.

2. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES (HSE CRITICAL TASKS)
GASCO carried out a Road Transport Bow-Tie workshop in May 2015 with the participation
of a multidisciplinary team of employees and contractors. A detailed risk assessment of the
road transport hazard was performed which is summarised in Road Transport COMAH
Report (HSE-R-122).
Workshop participants identified the threats and the barriers to prevent the release of the
hazard. The threats were then analysed in detail and the barriers were developed to reduce
the hazard to as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP). The consequences following the
loss of control of a vehicle were also analysed and recovery measures were identified to
mitigate these consequences.
HSE critical tasks were developed to ensure that these barriers and recovery measures
remain effective at all times. These tasks have been reworded as Roles and
Responsibilities and allocated to match those who are accountable for their delivery.

3. SAFETY STANDARDS OF VEHICLES
3.1 VEHICLE TYPES
This Manual is applicable to the following types of vehicles:
 Light Vehicles (saloon, pickup, 4X4,)
A light vehicle is any vehicle having a kerb weight of less than or equal to 4000 kg
and having eight passenger seats or less. Examples are saloon cars, pick-ups and
4x4 vehicles.
 Heavy Goods Vehicles
Any motor vehicle with a kerb weight of more than 4000 kg, which is designed to pull
a trailer or carry cargo. Examples are prime movers, tankers and vacuum trucks.
 Buses and Mini Buses
Buses: Passenger carrying vehicles normally seating driver plus more than 12
passengers.
Mini buses: Passenger carrying vehicles normally seating driver plus 8 to 12
passengers.
 COMAH Site Vehicles
Any vehicle that may enter an area where there is a likelihood of major accident
hazard from hydrocarbon gas release. Any vehicle entering Zone 2 area as per the
Dangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations 2002 (DSEAR)
which defines a Zone 2 as:
a) An area in which an explosive gas atmosphere is not likely to occur in normal
operation and, if it occurs, will only exist for a short time.
b) Explosive atmosphere for less than 10h/yr., but still sufficiently likely as to require
controls over ignition sources.
Each division should specify these hazardous areas and manage vehicle entry. An
example is any vehicle entering to Habshan Plant gate (second gate) will be
considered as COMAH Site vehicle. Any vehicle, entering ONLY the Main Gate (first
gate) will not be considered as COMAH Site vehicle.
 Trailers
A platform or a frame with wheels used to transport cargo that is towed by another
vehicle, which provides power.

4.4 TYRE MONITORING AND REPLACEMENT
Tires must be selected based on manufacturer recommendations. The condition of tires
has a significant effect on vehicle steering, the grip on the road, fuel consumption and
braking performance. Tires must be maintained at the correct pressure. Recommended
tyre pressures must be displayed on vehicles and drivers must be provided with the
means to check them.
It is the sole responsibility of the respective Transport Coordinators to inspect the tyre
tread depth of all vehicles under their custody on monthly basis and enter the information
in the TMS. Accordingly they have the right to request the end users to make their vehicles
available for tyre checks and replacement.
For pool vehicles within HQ and sites, daily inspections must take place prior to assigning a
vehicle for employees for long journeys.
Before long journeys, drivers shall assess the condition and pressure of tires. Tires shall be
replaced in the event of whichever occurs first:
 Off-Road vehicles (4 WD) or trucks - every 30,000 Kms
 All other vehicle - every 40,000 Kms
 2 years from the manufacture date
 Before tread depth reaches 2.3 mm (GASCO limit)
In case any tyre displays unsafe conditions such as cuts, damage or more than two reported
punctures, immediate replacement must be carried out for safety reasons.

6.5 REPORTING AN INCIDENT
All accidents, regardless of location, on or off site, on or off road, or whether reported by
the police, must be reported using the GASCO Incident Classification, Investigation and
Reporting Standard (HSE-ST-007).
Each driver should report the incident through his/her Division. E.g. If HSE Division driver
has a vehicle collision he/she should report to HSE Division and that Division will be the
Incident Owner of that incident.
If the incident occurs after working hours and results in serious injury or fatality it should be
reported as soon as possible to the driver’s Department Head. If the incident does not
results in serious injury it should still be reported as soon as possible but at least by the
first working day.
Depending on the risk assessment of the incident, an investigation team will be formed to
investigate and learn from the root causes to avoid reoccurrence. The learnings will be
shared with all sites and corrective action, if required, will be taken by site Management
and the HSE Department.
See also the GASCO Incident Management Standards, HSE-ST-007.

Desert driving requires special skills. No vehicle is allowed to be driven into the desert
unless the driver has received specific training, has a GASCO Safe Driving Document and
the vehicle is suitable.
Vehicles must not be taken into desert regions without appropriate safety precautions
including:
 The vehicle must be suitable for desert driving (e.g. all-wheel drive, appropriate tires
for soft sand that are inflated to a suitable pressure, roll over protection for the driver
and all passengers, appropriate type of seat belts and neck/head restraints and fitted
with a non-conductive flexible flag pole, a minimum of 3 metres high, to improve
visibility when traversing the crest of dunes).
 The vehicle must carry a desert survival box containing all equipment necessary for
desert survival including sufficient water for all persons on-board.
 The vehicle must be provided with communications systems and global satellite
positioning systems.
The driver must have special training for desert conditions. This special desert training for
drivers must include dealing safely with all foreseeable types of events such as:
 Checking equipment and vehicle for desert conditions prior to departure
 Desert hazards and how to avoid them
 Procedures for sandstorms
 What to do when stuck in soft sand
 Getting out of 'blow-holes'
 Vehicle overheating or breakdown
 Being lost
 Preventing the adverse effects of heat and sun, dehydration
 Dangers of animals, especially camels

8. JOURNEY MANAGEMENT
8.1 JOURNEY MANAGEMENT PLAN
In order to ensure that road transportation risks are as low as reasonably practicable a
journey management plan must be in place. Journey Management aims to minimize
exposure to road transport related risks and to ensure that proper controls are in place for
each journey.
The Site Transportation Officer and HQ General Service Division are to ensure that a
journey management system is established at site and at HQ respectively as per the
guidelines given below.
The journey management objectives are:
 Assuring the health and safety of all travellers and reducing the length of exposure
to road hazards
 Challenging the need for unnecessary journeys and only undertake the minimum
number of journeys necessary
 Maximizing the efficiency of each journey
 Ensuring that drivers are able to recover in a timely manner from any incident
 Monitoring journey performance
 Ensuring that drivers are fully aware of journey plans and any hazards
A good journey management system will contain the following activities:
 Appoint a Journey Manager
 Prepare a list of journeys not requiring separate approval
 Assess journey requests and approve journey by authorized person
 Prepare a Safe Journey Plan
 Confirm condition of the vehicle and implementation of control measures
 Execute journey and report as agreed
 Report upon arrival
 Evaluate journey plan and closeout Safe Journey Plan
 Conduct trend analysis and initiate improvements
Once the need for a journey has been established, then aspects of journey management

8.2 BLACK-SPOT SURVEY
The location in a road where the highest number of traffic accidents occurs is called a

shall identify the hazardous locations considering previous accidents, visibility, road
conditions, urban congestion and labour camps. These black spots should be mapped out
for each site by the HSE Department and by GS for pool vehicles driving to and from HQ.
If possible, these routes should be avoided. If not, each driver is to be trained to take extra
precautions while driving through these routes, during safety meetings and toolbox talks.

8.3 FATIGUE
Transport Coordinators or Line Managers are responsible for ensuring that professional
drivers have had sufficient rest before any journey is to be undertaken. Proper
accommodation must be provided when overnight stays are part of the journey requirement.
Transport Coordinators must ensure that drivers are fully aware of the risks and symptoms
of fatigue and they must assess the risks associated with driver fatigue that result from work
related journeys and commuting to/from a place of work.
Transport Coordinators are to implement good journey management principles to reduce
excessive driving hours by avoiding unnecessary journeys. They should plan work and
leave cycles so that drivers are well rested before long journeys and plan driver vacation
times so extra drivers are available during shutdown periods to avoid long driving hours.
Transport Coordinators are to encourage all drivers to report fatigue, medical and stress
conditions to supervisors during safety meetings toolbox talks.

8.5 LIMITED NIGHT TIME DRIVING
Driving during hours of darkness on GASCO business should be avoided if possible. No
night time travel is allowed between 22:00 to 04:00 to and from site tarmac roads unless the
Site Vice President has given written permission (standing instruction). This rule applies to
GASCO vehicles with exemptions as specified by the Site Vice Presidents.
Travel during hours of darkness off-road is not permitted unless there is an emergency or
the journey is authorized by the Vice President. A risk assessment must be conducted for
any night driving in the desert environment and all controls must be in place (vehicle checks,
desert survival kit, communication etc.) before the journey commences.
8.6 SPEED LIMITS
Speeding is one of the major causes of accidents. Drivers must obey the posted speed
limits. The drivers’ speed is monitored by IVMS, which is installed in all vehicles whereby
each driver has a unique identity to be able to drive the vehicle.
GASCO speed limits for driving outside populated areas are as follows (Ref: Life Saving
Rule No. 10):
 For a light vehicle, a maximum speed of no more than120 km/hr
 For an HGV, a maximum speed of 80 km/hr
 For buses and minibuses, a maximum speed of 100 km/hr
 For a vehicle driven in a desert environment, a maximum speed 60 km/hr
Note: Drivers of all types of vehicles are allowed to drive a maximum of 20 km/hr above the
posted speed limit for a limited time to facilitate overtaking. If this tolerance is
exceeded, the TMS will send a notification to the driver to highlight the breach and an
email will be sent to the Line Manager of the person breaching the limit.
Each site has reduced speed limits (between 25-40km/hr), which should be posted so as to
be clearly visible. It is imperative that all drivers obey these speed limits since any vehicle
impact with process equipment may result in a major accident.

8.7 USE OF MOBILE DEVICE WHILST DRIVING
In line with ADNOC COP-V4-06, drivers must neither initiate, nor answer a mobile telephone
call while driving a vehicle. This includes text messaging and web surfing as well as the use
of hands free devices.
Mobile telephones can be left switched on during a trip to alert the driver of any incoming
calls. The driver must safely leave the road; bring the vehicle to a complete stop in a safe
parking area before initiating or answering a call. This also applies to radios used for two-
way communication, including communication with base stations and any other network
enabled devices.
This applies to the driving of vehicles on company premises as well as on public roads.
Drivers must not use intermittent stops on the road (e.g. at traffic lights) as an opportunity
to make or answer mobile telephone calls.
The only exception to the above is for the use of two-way radios as part of convoy
management or for use during emergency situations. Radio use whilst driving in these
circumstances must be kept to the minimum necessary to communicate and control the
hazards and risks of the journey.
8.8 OCCUPANT RESTRAINTS
The use of seat belts by all occupants of cars, buses and other vehicles is mandatory. This
refers to drivers and all passengers both at the front and all subsequent seats. It is the
responsibility of the driver to ensure that passengers are wearing their seat belt in the correct
manner during the journey (Ref. Life Saving Rule No. 9).
Drivers must refuse to transport passengers who will not fasten their seatbelts in the correct
manner. Belts must of the lap/sash configuration incorporating automatic retraction and
deceleration activated emergency locking mechanisms – often referred to as inertia reels.
Where there are more than two seats in a row, lap belts are acceptable for centre seat
passengers.
The approved seating capacity of a vehicle must be equal to the number of available and
operational seats correctly fitted with working three-point seat belts in accordance with the
manufacturer guidelines. If a bus only has two-point seatbelts, passengers are allowed to
use these. Passengers are not permitted to travel using folding seats in buses.
The driver must check the seat belts to ensure that they are functioning correctly on a daily
basis. If a driver discovers that a seatbelt does not function correctly, he must ensure that
a passenger does not occupy that seat and that the deficiency is reported to his supervisor
as soon as possible.

8.9 DRIVING IN POOR WEATHER CONDITIONS
Visibility will be limited in bad weather conditions such as rain, fog, sandstorms and dust
etc. It can also make the roads very slippery. Drivers who drive during bad weather
conditions are cautioned to take extreme care to ensure the safety of driver and passengers
and to turn on their headlights (but not full beam) in any condition of reduced visibility.
Fog and sandstorm:
Among all weather conditions, fog is probably the most dangerous. If driving in foggy
weather, the driver must reduce his/her speed, turn on the low-beam headlights (or fog lights
if available) and increase their following distance. The driver must not use hazard-warning
lights or overtake. If visibility falls below 100m, the driver must not start driving or if he/she
is already driving, pull safely off the road and stop driving until they can see 100 m of clear
visibility. It is advisable to avoid driving during adverse weather conditions.
Visibility boards are required to be fixed in place 100m from all exits to allow the drivers to
confirm that they can see 100 m of clear visibility and help them to make an informed
judgment as to whether or not they should drive.
Drivers should be careful of any build-up of sand on the road. If drivers are driving too fast
on an approach to sand, the vehicle tires can easily lose traction with the road, leading to
skids and loss of control of the vehicle.
Rain:
The driver must slow down at the first sign of rain, or drizzle on the road. They should avoid
overtaking, and any sudden acceleration or harsh braking. This is when road surfaces are
most slippery because moisture mixes with oil and dust that has not been washed away.
The slippery roads are extremely risky as they can reduce traction and it is easy to lose
control of the vehicle. Drivers shall also increase their following distance, turn on the low-
beam headlights and use their windshield wipers.
Wind/Dust:
These conditions create additional problems for drivers. Wind generally reduces your
steering control. The best defensive driving techniques for windy conditions is driving at
slower speeds or safely pull over until winds subside.
Hot Weather:
When driving in hot weather, drivers should keep an eye on the warning lights and gauges,
especially the temperature gauge. The road surface may become soft affecting driver
steering and braking. Drivers should keep their vehicle well ventilated to avoid drowsiness.
If, drivers are dazzled by bright sunlight, they should slow down and if necessary, stop.
Drivers should be aware of possible fatigue caused by the heat; Headlights should be
switched on to low beam to increase vehicle visibility to other road users. Drivers should
wear sunglasses to avoid sun glare.

8.10 EMERGENCY RESPONSE
In case of break-down or accident:
There is a GASCO Road Transport Control Room Hotline (02 60 37090), available 24/7
to respond to emergency circumstances at HQ & Plant Divisions.
What to do if involved in an accident?
 Stop immediately and turn off the ignition.
 If there has been no injury, only minor vehicle damage, both vehicles can be driven,
and both drivers mutually agree, then the vehicles should be moved to the side of
the road to avoid holding up traffic.
 Switch on the vehicle’s hazard warning lights and warn oncoming traffic by using the
warning triangles (placed 50 - 100 m behind the vehicle).
 Wear the high visibility jacket provided.
 Move passengers to a place where they will be safe from other traffic.
 Stay at the scene but in a safe location.
 Assist any injured and give first aid if competent to do so.
 Telephone the Police (999) and, if necessary, the Ambulance Service (998).
 If you have been injured seek medical advice.
 Make as many written notes as possible. Record the details of any other driver(s)
involved in the accident - driver’s insurance, driving license and number plate details.
 Record details from any witnesses.
 Take photographs if possible.
 Only give statements to Police Officers and do not admit liability as this may
prejudice GASCO’s insurance. In special cases (minor damage, no injury) a Traffic
Police Officer may conduct an Amicable Settlement Report defining responsibility.
The GASCO driver may accept this.
 Report the accident to your GASCO line manager as soon as possible, but at least
within 24 hours.
 Site personnel shall follow the site FRP for reporting off site accidents.
What to do if involved in a breakdown?
 Move the car to a safe location (if possible).
 Switch on the vehicle’s hazard warning lights (if possible).
 Warn oncoming traffic by using the warning triangles.
 Wear the high visibility jacket provided.
 Stay in a safe location.
Contact the GASCO Road Transport Control Room Hot line and wait for advice. Transport
Coordinators shall coordinate with the relevant vendor for replacement of vehicle or provide
recovery services if necessary.

9. TRANSPORTATION OF HAZARDOUS MATERIALS
Vehicles transporting hazardous materials to and from GASCO sites (for example propane,
sulphur or chemicals) must comply with the UAE Federal Regulations for the Transportation
of Hazardous Goods.
Refer to the Chemical Management Standard HSE-ST-015 and Sulphur Transportation
Procedure for further details.
9.1 LOAD SECURING
The following applies for loads in any vehicles: saloons, 4x4, HGV, buses and tractors with
trailers.
It has been estimated that about 25% of accidents involving HGV can be attributable to
inadequate cargo securing. Cargo that is improperly secured can cause severe accidents
and lead to the loss of cargo, the loss of lives, the loss of vehicles, or cause environmental
hazards.
The driver of a vehicle is responsible for the safe loading of the vehicle. Where possible,
cargo or baggage must be carried in the designated cargo area of the vehicle, segregated
and restrained from interfering with the driver or passengers by a wire mesh netting or
similar restraint as a minimum. If personal belongings are to be carried within the
passenger compartment, they must be secured to prevent them becoming a safety hazard
to passengers and driver.
Any heavy items carried inside the cab of a pickup truck or cargo vehicle, including items
such as jacks or fire extinguishers must be firmly secured in a way to avoid injury to
personnel in the event of a crash. All loads transported in a pickup truck, utility truck or
other cargo vehicle must be fastened securely and must not exceed the vehicle
manufacturer specification or legal limit for the vehicle.
9.2 VEHICLE SIGNAGE
Placards designating the hazards associated with dangerous goods will conform to the
IMDG Code (International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code).
Vehicles carrying dangerous goods must be marked, front and rear, with a plain orange
coloured panel. The individual packages inside the vehicle must be marked with the
relevant UN number to identify the material or material type, a sign depicting the primary
hazard from the package contents (with the UN Classification as a number at the bottom
of the diamond), and any relevant secondary hazard signs.
Where packages are in a container, the primary and secondary hazard signs must also be
displayed on at least one side of the container.

12. APPENDICES
A) PROFESSIONAL DRIVER PRE-EMPLOYMENT & PERIODIC MEDICAL TESTS
(Ref. Document: ADNOC Codes of Practice: Occupational Health Guideline COP)
Periodicity
The examination should be carried out annually.
If age-based examination is being offered, then, it can be done in conjunction with the
periodic medical evaluation i.e.
 Age less than 40 – Every three years.
 Age 40 to 49 – Every two years.
 Age above 50 – Every year.
Examination
A full medical examination must be conducted with particular attention to cardiac fitness,
vision hearing and the Central Nervous System (CNS).
Fitness Standard
In addition to the standard fitness requirements the following criteria should be observed:
Note: Employees failing to maintain this standard or having any disease, which restricts the
ability of the candidate to perform the job, must not be allowed to be hired or continue as a
driver.
Vision
The visual acuity, using corrective lenses if necessary must not be less than 6/9 in the better
eye or 6/12 in the other eye. Also, the uncorrected acuity in each eye MUST be at least
3/60. (Monocular vision may be unacceptable).
 Peripheral vision 140° or >in each eye.
 Normal colour vision. (Ishihara test).
 No other significant eye disease.
Ear, Nose, Throat (ENT)
 No significant hearing loss.
 No vertigo.

Lungs
 No evidence of asthma due to possible exposure of individual to Hydrogen
Sulphide.
Cardio Vascular System (CVS)
 Normal stress E.C.G. on each examination.
 No evidence of previous complicated Myocardial infarction.
 No evidence of uncontrolled hypertension.
 No recent severe angina pectoris especially if provoked by driving.
 No history of coronary artery by-pass surgery or angioplasty.
 No history of sync opal attack and evidence of complete or second degree AV
block or symptomatic arrhythmias or bradycardia.
Central Nervous System (CNS)
 No fits, epilepsy or neurological condition that may impair consciousness, balance
or coordination such as intracranial hematomas, depressed skull fracture, stroke or

 No vertigo, giddiness, and ataxia.
 No sleep disorders.
Endocrine
 No evidence of uncontrolled diabetes mellitus (HbA1c <7).
Loco motor system
 No musculoskeletal disorders such as back disorders, restricted neck and limb
movements.
 Any condition causing muscle in coordination.
Mental disorder
 No significant psychosis or anxiety/depressive illness.
Medications
 No condition requiring on-going medication with possible side effect such as
drowsiness.
Drugs and Alcohol
 Any condition, which will result to frequent absenteeism from work.

H) PROFESSIONAL DRIVER EMPOWERMENT CONTRACT
GASCO Professional Driver Empowerment Contract
This driver is trained to drive safely to GASCO’s standards and agrees to obey all of the GASCO
safe driving rules.
This driver is empowered to refuse:
 To drive if any passenger is not wearing their seatbelt.
 To drive above sign posted speed limits.
 To drive if he is fatigued or otherwise unfit.
 To carry unsafe loads.
 To drive if serious defects in the vehicle are not rectified such as but not limited to poor
condition of brakes, speedometers, lights, tires.
 Passengers’ unsafe requests and report their unsafe behaviour to site HSE.
This driver commits to:
 Conduct daily vehicle checks.
 Follow the posted speed limit.
 Not use a mobile device when driving.
 Maintain a safe following distance from other road users.
 Only overtake in safe conditions.
 Stop driving when visibility falls below 100m.
 Implement emergency response.
 Lead a healthy lifestyle, including getting adequate rest.
