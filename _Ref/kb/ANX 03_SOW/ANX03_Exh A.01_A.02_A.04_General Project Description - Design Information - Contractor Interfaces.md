A.1 GENERAL PROJECT DESCRIPTION
A.1.1 BACKGROUND & INTRODUCTION
The Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 1 (RSHT-1) and Ruwais Sulphur Handling
Terminal 2 (RSHT-2) are owned by ADNOC and operated by ADNOC Gas and are
in Ruwais Industrial Area approximately 240 km west-southwest of Abu Dhabi City.
RSHT-1 is a multipurpose sulphur handling and processing facility designed to
receive liquid sulphur by trucks, pipeline and ships, produce granulated sulphur at a
capacity of 8,750 TPD, and export the granulated sulphur by ship from two marine
berths (Berth 1 and Berth 2) equipped with granulated sulphur ship loaders. In 2016,
LNG regasification facilities were added to the RSHT-1 including LNG unloading
arm installed and the FSRU is moored at Berth 2, rendered the existing Sulphur
ship loader at Berth 2 as out of service.
RSHT-2 facility is designed to handle bulk granulated sulphur coming from Shah
and Habshan plant via railcars and transported via new federal railway (Union
Railway) to Ruwais Terminal (SHT-2).
Originally, it was planned to enhance the existing capacity of RSHT-1 to receive
additional 9,900 TPD of liquid Sulphur from Hail & Ghasha via pipeline under RSHT-
1 Upgrade for Hail and Gasha Project for which FEED was performed by Worley
Parsons (FEED Consultant) in 2021. Subsequently, RFT Enquiry No. 6000066707
for RSHT-1 Upgrade for Hail & Ghasha was floated to the market for tendering.
However, based on ADNOC recent directives, a new Sulphur Granulation Plant will
be developed to handle liquid sulphur from Hail & Ghasha and to be integrated with
existing RSHT-2 for seamless operations at enhanced capacity & export of
granulated Sulphur. Accordingly, RFT Enquiry No. 6000066707 (Revised) was
issued for tendering. There is no FEED upgraded/performed for the new SGP for
the change in the original scope (i.e. shifting of the location to a new green field plot,
integration with the RSHT-2, sourcing of utilities and introduction of the new
interfaces).
This new plant will be located to the East of the existing BOROUGE-3 Plant & PP5
Areas, North of the under-construction BOROUGE-4 facilities, and to the West of
existing RSHT-2 as depicted in Figure 1 below.

A.1.2 SCOPE OF PROJECT
The overall scope of the PROJECT is divided into two broader scope elements,
namely Green Field Scope and Brown Field Scope as depicted in Figure 2.
Green Field Scope:
At the battery limits of the SGP within the Green Field scope, liquid Sulphur will be
received from the Hail & Ghasha OPP facilities through the heated pipeline system,
while granulated Sulphur will be transported to existing RSHT-2 for export and/or
storage in the existing Linear Storage Buildings.
The scope includes liquid sulphur receiving, liquid sulphur storage and transfer,
sulphur forming (granulation), granulated sulphur transfer and storage, stacking and
reclaiming, sulphur remelting, dust suppression and dust extraction, biocide
application, waste water collection & treatment system, evaporation pond, sewage
collection & treatment system.

Liquid sulphur is added to the drum space via a heated header pipe equipped with
fluid only type atomizing nozzles. Day vessel pumps head requirement is primarily
driven by the nozzle losses which require a header pressure in the range from 11.2
to 22.5 kg/cm2g (optimum operating pressure to be determined during
commissioning). Liquid sulphur is atomized through these spray nozzles, mounted
ona single header extending the length of the granulating drum interior, and drops
onto the falling ·curtain· of granules. The cooling and coating action are continuous
throughout the length of the drum, consuming the liquid sulphur while enlarging and
discharging granules.
Process water is introduced into the granulation drum through spray nozzles, also
mounted on a single header extending the interior length of the drum. The spray
nozzles are sized to provide the required water flow necessary to cool and solidify
the liquid sulphur.
Due to the granule cooling action and the fact that ambient air is drawn into the
system at the granulator drum feed hood inlet air vent, the inlet air gets heated and
humidified in the granulation drum and absorbs the water vapour.
An induced draft (ID) fan, located downstream of the drum draws atmospheric air
into and through the granulation drum. Then through a venturi style wet scrubber to
remove fine sulphur dust particles from the saturated air stream leaving the drum
while also maintaining the system under slight negative pressure.
The air stream exiting the drum contains water vapour from the evaporation
process and sulphur dust particles. The dust is separated from the exhaust air via
the venturi scrubber high air stream velocity and liquid medium washing. The
cleaner air is discharged via the exhaust fan to the vent stack.

GRANULATED SULPHUR TRANSFER AND STORAGE
While freshly formed sulphur granules are relatively strong compared to aged
granules (14 to 21 days old), they still need to be handled in a manner that
minimizes breakage and attrition through uncontrolled drop heights, and poor chute
designs.
A secondary benefit of good transfer design is that static electricity generation is
minimized and better earthed due to the high amount of contact with earthed chutes.
Granules created by the Ten granulators (N+1) are transported using one or both of
two granules collecting conveyors (N+1) each fitted with two belt scales. Each
conveyor delivers product granules to a new transfer tower. From here the granules
will be routed to new linear granulated sulphur storage building and further
transferred via new conveyors after integrating with existing RSHT-2 ship loaders
(in Berth-3 & 4) conveyors. Alternatively, option shall be provided to route the
granules to existing RSHT-2 west Linear storage. In case storage is not available,

STACKING AND RECLAIMING
Two combination traveling stacker reclaimers with each stacker rates of 600 tph &
reclaiming rates of 4000 tph shall be considered in the linear granulated sulphur
storage building. This allows stacking and reclaiming to occur simultaneously in the
linear storage building. This configuration to be kept similar & for easy integration
with existing RSHT-2 linear storage.
Sulphur granules are conveyed to the linear granulated sulphur storage building via
conveyors to the transfer chutes. The transfer chutes connect to the stacker boom
conveyors which in turn form the stockpiles inside these buildings.
To facilitate solid sulphur cooling, the granules are stacked in thin layers. The
stockpile in normal operation conditions will be no more than 20 meters peak height.
When the sulphur needs to be reclaimed, the scrapper reclaimers in the buildings
transfer stockpiled granules downstream to the existing ship loader(s).
SULPHUR REMELTING
The remelter unit will have a capacity of 30 TPD. All the waste sulphur, including
the drained liquid sulphur from filters and drain sumps in addition to granules lost
from spillage, which are conveyed with trolleys, are directed into the new remelter.
Sulphur for remelting is loaded into the remelter using a feed conveyor. Inside, the
remelter is sloped toward the pump to prevent migration of solids. A weir ensures
that the suction of the steam-jacketed remelter pump is completely flooded with
liquid sulphur. The pump delivers the liquid sulphur through a 500-m filter for
removal of large contaminants.
Remelting of solid sulphur is accomplished with Medium-Pressure (MP) steam that
heats up liquid sulphur with or without a heat exchanger depending on the
manufacturer and service duty. Liquid sulphur from remelter is transferred back to
liquid sulphur tanks inlet.
DUST CONTROL SYSTEM
At transfer chute in the conveyor system, stacker boom when the sulphur reaches
the end of a conveyor and to fall, dust is created. Majority of dust is created when
the product hits the conveyor below or any objects in between. Dust control is
required at expansion-related transfer points to meet the safe dust emission levels.
There are two (2) systems that to handle the generated dust in each transfer point:
1) Wet scrubber system.
2) Dust suppression system.

Dust Suppression System
Dust suppressant, a mixture of water and chemical to generate foam which is
sprayed onto the sulphur to reduce the surface tension of the sulphur molecules,
allowing water to adhere to them more easily. The moisture level on the sulphur
granules must not exceed 1% (at ship loading), or the sulphur will be off spec. To
control the moisture level, the amount of suppressant applied is controlled based on
the product mass flow rate that is calculated by DCS based on the conveyor speed
and conveyor weigh scale (if applicable). Suppression spray nozzles are controlled
flow such that nozzles only spray when sulphur is present.
Biocide Application System
Chemicals biocide injection are considered in Granule collecting Transfer tower.
Biocide chemical injection package shall essentially consist of chemical injection
pumps, chemical storage Tank, calibration pots, strainers, pulsation dampeners,
instruments, interconnecting tubing/ piping, valves, accessories, cables, junction
boxes, spray nozzles etc.
Biocide shall be added to mitigate bacteria growth associated with sulphur in
storage (linear storage building), specifically at the base of the stockpiles. Biocide
shall be added to the newly formed solid sulphur flow stream(s) from the granule
collecting conveyors.
Biocide application shall be in proportion to the solid sulphur flowrate. Two
application points are included namely the stationary portions of the transfer chutes
to transfer conveyors.
When direct ship loading of sulphur granules are considered instead of storing in
storage buildings, then biocide application is not required.

NEW STORM WATER / WASTE WATER EVAPORATION POND

wash water collection pits trough localized intermediate pits. Solid sulphur particles
present in the wash down water shall be allowed to settle in the inlet compartment

shall be removed periodically and disposed to company approved location.
A new storm water / wastewater evaporation pond is dedicated to new granulation

granulation area is sent to a new evaporation pond located in this area.
