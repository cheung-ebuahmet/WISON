A.2.8 
OPERATIONS AND MAINTENANCE PHILOSOPHY 
A.2.8.1 
INTRODUCTION 
The objective of the operating and maintenance philosophy contained in this section is 
to provide CONTRACTOR a guide for the design of the facility from an 
operation/maintenance viewpoint.  It serves to focus the design on simplicity, 
reliability, availability and minimum lifecycle costs for the facility within the agreed 
levels of manning. 
COMPANY requires all facilities to be designed and constructed with the proactive 
participation of suitably qualified and experienced Operations and Maintenance (O&M) 
personnel under circumstances where participation materially improves the design 
and meets COMPANY’s ultimate operability and maintenance objectives. 
This philosophy is not intended to provide an exhaustive index of relevant 
requirements but to guide the CONTRACTOR in detail design execution. 
A.2.8.2 
OPERATIONS 
A.2.8.2.1 
GENERAL 
The SYSTEMS shall be designed for optimum lifecycle cost using a minimum 
manning concept consistent with safe and efficient operating practice and the required 
availability.  This implies a philosophy of minimum complexity, maximum reliability, 
and unattended operation wherever possible. 
Spare equipment should be capable of isolation to allow maintenance or equipment 
removal without affecting production. 
The SYSTEMS will be designed for ease and safety of both the day-to-day operations 
and maintenance. 
A.2.8.2.2 
MANNING 
The system shall be operated in accordance with the operating shift system with 
control being from the respective control room, under the immediate charge of a shift 
supervisor. 
Operations staff will be skilled and all operations, maintenance, repairs, and 
equipment replacement should be possible using this workforce.   
VENDORS’ representatives shall be used where necessary for specialist services for 
particular critical items of equipment such as Scrapping Services etc. 
Operational, technical and maintenance manpower projections for INSTALLATION.  
These requirements are to be developed by CONTRACTOR in conjunction with 
COMPANY.

A.2.8.2.5 
ISOLATION 
Where any equipment is regularly isolated for routine maintenance entry, block, safe 
draining and venting facilities (where applicable) are necessary.  Where mechanical 
and electrical equipment requires isolation, positive means shall be provided that 
ensure all sources of energy can be locked out and tagged out, so as to provide a 
safe work envelope for personnel. 
Spare equipment should be capable of isolation to allow maintenance or equipment 
removal without affecting production. 
A.2.8.2.6 
ACCESS AND LAYOUT 
Layout of plant equipment must allow adequate access for all day-to-day 
operation/maintenance requirements and all major overhaul requirements.  Layout 
shall consider the proximity to existing facilities, vents, buildings, access roads and 
design accordingly.  Wind rose diagrams shall also be considered in deciding the 
layout. 
All routinely operated valves, local instruments, sample points, vents and drains, 
blinds and purge points should be accessible or operable from permanent decking or 
platforms.  
Accessibility shall be reviewed by operator at all phases of design.  Sufficient work 
space shall be provided around the equipment for maintenance. 
A.2.8.2.7 
START- UP AND SHUTDOWN

stage in the PROJECT.
