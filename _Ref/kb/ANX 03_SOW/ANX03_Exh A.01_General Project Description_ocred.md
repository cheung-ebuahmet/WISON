### General project description

## background & Introduction

The Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 1 (RSHT-1) and Ruwais Sulphur Handling Terminal 2 (RSHT-2) are owned by ADNOC and operated by ADNOC Gas and are in Ruwais Industrial Area approximately 240 km west-southwest of Abu Dhabi City.

RSHT-1 is a multipurpose sulphur handling and processing facility designed to receive liquid sulphur by trucks, pipeline and ships, produce granulated sulphur at a capacity of 8,750 TPD, and export the granulated sulphur by ship from two marine berths (Berth 1 and Berth 2) equipped with granulated sulphur ship loaders. In 2016, LNG regasification facilities were added to the RSHT-1 including LNG unloading arm installed and the FSRU is moored at Berth 2, rendered the existing Sulphur ship loader at Berth 2 as out of service.

RSHT-2 facility is designed to handle bulk granulated sulphur coming from Shah and Habshan plant via railcars and transported via new federal railway (Union Railway) to Ruwais Terminal (SHT-2).

Originally, it was planned to enhance the existing capacity of RSHT-1 to receive additional 9,900 TPD of liquid Sulphur from Hail & Ghasha via pipeline under RSHT-1 Upgrade for Hail and Gasha Project for which FEED was performed by Worley Parsons (FEED Consultant) in 2021. Subsequently, RFT Enquiry No. 6000066707 for RSHT-1 Upgrade for Hail & Ghasha was floated to the market for tendering.

However, based on ADNOC recent directives, a new Sulphur Granulation Plant will be developed to handle liquid sulphur from Hail & Ghasha and to be integrated with existing RSHT-2 for seamless operations at enhanced capacity & export of granulated Sulphur. Accordingly, RFT Enquiry No. 6000066707 (Revised) was issued for tendering. There is no FEED upgraded/performed for the new SGP for the change in the original scope (i.e. shifting of the location to a new green field plot, integration with the RSHT-2, sourcing of utilities and introduction of the new interfaces).

This new plant will be located to the East of the existing BOROUGE-3 Plant & PP5 Areas, North of the under-construction BOROUGE-4 facilities, and to the West of existing RSHT-2 as depicted in Figure 1 below.

## SCOPE OF PROJECT

The overall scope of the PROJECT is divided into two broader scope elements, namely Green Field Scope and Brown Field Scope as depicted in Figure 2.

Green Field Scope:

At the battery limits of the SGP within the Green Field scope, liquid Sulphur will be received from the Hail & Ghasha OPP facilities through the heated pipeline system, while granulated Sulphur will be transported to existing RSHT-2 for export and/or storage in the existing Linear Storage Buildings.

The scope includes liquid sulphur receiving, liquid sulphur storage and transfer, sulphur forming (granulation), granulated sulphur transfer and storage, stacking and reclaiming, sulphur remelting, dust suppression and dust extraction, biocide application, waste water collection & treatment system, evaporation pond, sewage collection & treatment system.

Brownfield Field Scope:

It includes integration of new facilities with the existing interfaces with RSHT-2, Borouge, GUP, etc. and associated tie-in’s and utilities such as electric power, steam, condensate, process water, potable water, nitrogen, firewater and Instrument and utility air.

LIQUID SULPHUR RECEIVING

The Hail and Ghasha liquid sulphur delivery pipelines, pipeline heating system and electrical power supply and the pipeline receiving system are not within the scope of this Project, apart from the continuation of the sulphur line inside the new SGP battery limit.

There is a separate Hail & Ghasha receiving station within the new SGP terminal fence line, where the pipelines terminate in a pig receival and loading station, with a small pit (under Hail & Ghasha project) to be used during initial pigging operation only for water draining/collection after hydrostatic pipeline testing.

The heating (Electrical Tracing) of the Sulphur Pipelines from HGD to new SGP Receiving Station interface point is excluded from the scope of this project.

This plant receives liquid sulphur from Hail and Ghasha only and the details are as below:

Two numbers of new 100% redundant 12” pipelines for transferring the liquid sulphur from the Hail & Ghasha OPP to 3 No’s liquid sulphur tanks in RSGP during normal operation.

Between OPP and new SGP the liquid sulphur pipelines are Skin Effect Electrical Tracing (SEET) heated. However, after the receiving station inside the new SGP boundaries the pipeline is changed to steam jacketed pipe to deliver a more uniform heat transfer to liquid sulphur.

LIQUID SULPHUR STORAGE AND TRANSFER

Three (3 No’s) new storage tanks are designed to handle 9,900 TPD of Hail & Ghasha with 5 days ullage time. The liquid sulphur from tanks will be pumped to three (3 No’s) liquid sulphur day vessels after passing through filters(A/B) provided at the inlet of each day vessel.

Four (4No’s) of liquid sulphur transfer pumps shall be installed, two to deliver sulphur to the granulator day vessels and the third to recirculate liquid sulphur to the tanks to condition and cool the sulphur and fourth pump will be standby.

Circulation between the tanks and delivery of sulphur to the granulation area day vessels are concurrent continuous operation.

The Tanks are insulated externally to maintain the temperature of the liquid sulphur in the tanks between 125 deg. C and 135 deg. C. The new tanks shall be in a containment area consisting of a concrete perimeter wall and storm water collection sump and pump.

Due to the cross connections in the supply to the three (3) new tanks from the pipeline and in the pump-suction headers from the tanks, the tanks will fill and empty together and will always be at approximately the same level unless one or more tanks are manually isolated for maintenance and inspection.

The liquid sulphur transfer pumps recirculate liquid sulphur back to any of the tanks or all tanks as required for liquid sulphur conditioning before sending to day vessels and as a pump protection minimum flow strategy. The capacity of the liquid sulphur transfer pump system design will be based on 10 granulators, operating simultaneously plus pump minimum re-circulated flow.

The re-circulated flow is required to accommodate fluctuations in demand from the day vessels and to allow for continuous operation of equipment and effective control of flow to the day vessels.

For each of the three liquid sulphur day vessels, there are two online filters can be switchable filters upstream of the vessels.

The three new tanks are sized to hold a total of three (5) days of liquid sulphur at the for-nameplate capacity of 9,900 t/d.

Because the tank roofs include perimeter natural ventilation nozzles and central discharge stack low levels (less than 10 ppm) of hydrogen-sulphide (H2S) can be present at the tank tops.  Means shall be provided to limit the access around the sulphur tanks and to the tops of the tanks. As a minimum, these means shall include locking gates in tank area and clearly displayed signage warning of the danger near the gates and around the tank area along with multi gas monitors worn as part of the PPE for all personnel at the terminal.

Also due to the natural ventilation sulphur flour accumulations occur at the discharge stacks.  Means to positively collect and remove sulphur flour from the tank vents shall be provided.  Dispersion of sulphur flour to the tank roofs and wall areas is not permitted.

SULPHUR FORMING (GRANULATION)

The day vessels hold vertical submersible pumps to pump liquid sulphur to a total of Ten (10 No’s) GXM1 granulators. 10 units is an N+1 installed redundancy strategy.

Total Three vessels along with pumps feed into sulphur granulation units.

Each granulator drum sulphur spray header has a dedicated pump and piping arrangement from the above grade heated and insulated day vessels.

One pump in each vessel is dedicated to each granulator.  The spare pump in each vessel can deliver to any of the granulators fed from the given day vessel.

The arrangement is such that any one granulator can be taken offline without impacting any other.

Liquid sulphur supplied to the granulators must be at a temperature between 125 deg. C and 130 deg. C and not contain particles larger than 500-µm.

RSHT-2 will have 10 installed Granulator Systems and future space provision provided for 2 No’s granulators is considered. Each Granulator system (Vendor Package) consists of the following equipment:

Inline Sulphur Filter

Granulator Drum (Including inlet hood and discharge hood)

Belt Conveyors (Granule collection conveyor & recycle Conveyor)

Vibrating Screen

Screen oversize chute Discharge Diverter Gate

Process Water Supply Filter

Process Water Tank

Process Water Pumps

Process Water Discharge Filters

Wet Scrubber (Including dust collection ducting and venturi style wet scrubber)

Scrubber Discharge Settlement Tank

Sludge Extraction Screw Conveyor

Scrubber Recycle Pump

Scrubber Exhaust Fan (Including stack)

Electrical, Instrumentation, Structural etc.

Liquid sulphur is added to the drum space via a heated header pipe equipped with fluid only type atomizing nozzles.  Day vessel pumps head requirement is primarily driven by the nozzle losses which require a header pressure in the range from 11.2 to 22.5 kg/cm2g (optimum operating pressure to be determined during commissioning). Liquid sulphur is atomized through these spray nozzles, mounted on a single header extending the length of the granulating drum interior, and drops onto the falling ‘curtain’ of granules. The cooling and coating action are continuous throughout the length of the drum, consuming the liquid sulphur while enlarging and discharging granules.

Process water is introduced into the granulation drum through spray nozzles, also mounted on a single header extending the interior length of the drum. The spray nozzles are sized to provide the required water flow necessary to cool and solidify the liquid sulphur.

Due to the granule cooling action and the fact that ambient air is drawn into the system at the granulator drum feed hood inlet air vent, the inlet air gets heated and humidified in the granulation drum and absorbs the water vapour.

An induced draft (ID) fan, located downstream of the drum draws atmospheric air into and through the granulation drum. Then through a venturi style wet scrubber to remove fine sulphur dust particles from the saturated air stream leaving the drum while also maintaining the system under slight negative pressure.

The air stream exiting the drum contains water vapour from the evaporation process and sulphur dust particles. The dust is separated from the exhaust air via the venturi scrubber high air stream velocity and liquid medium washing. The cleaner air is discharged via the exhaust fan to the vent stack.

Sulphur dust collected in the venturi water stream leaves the bottom of the scrubber and discharges into the settling section of the scrubber-water settling tank and is extracted as sulphur sludge containing up to 40% water by weight. The sludge is extracted from the tank using an inclined screw conveyor operating at low speed. The screw conveyor discharges to a three-sided concrete bund.

The scrubber water is recycled back to the venturi portion of scrubber. Process water is provided as make up water to prevent drop of liquid level of scrubber discharge tank.

The sulphur granules leave the granulator drum via the discharge hood bottom hopper and are fed onto the drum discharge conveyor which transfers the granules to a vibratory screen. The screen separates the smaller sulphur fines from the product sulphur and recycles the fines back to the granulator drum inlet to be further coated with liquid sulphur such that the recycled granules eventually pass out of the screen as right sized granules.

GRANULATED SULPHUR TRANSFER AND STORAGE

While freshly formed sulphur granules are relatively strong compared to aged granules (14 to 21 days old), they still need to be handled in a manner that minimizes breakage and attrition through uncontrolled drop heights, and poor chute designs.

A secondary benefit of good transfer design is that static electricity generation is minimized and better earthed due to the high amount of contact with earthed chutes.

Granules created by the Ten granulators (N+1) are transported using one or both of two granules collecting conveyors (N+1) each fitted with two belt scales. Each conveyor delivers product granules to a new transfer tower. From here the granules will be routed to new linear granulated sulphur storage building and further transferred via new conveyors after integrating with existing RSHT-2 ship loaders (in Berth-3 & 4) conveyors. Alternatively, option shall be provided to route the granules to existing RSHT-2 west Linear storage. In case storage is not available, then the granules shall be routed directly to ship loading through several downstream conveyors & Transfer towers.

Each of these processes are described below.

Transport Path to Linear Granulated Sulphur Storage Building and Direct Hit to Ship loading via conveyors.

New Linear storage building has the capacity of 165,000t (equal to 16.7 days of production with nameplate capacity of 9,900 t/d). Two stacker & reclaimers machines shall be provided inside linear storage. In case storage is not available, then the granules can be directly routed to ship loading through downstream conveyors.

Alternate Transport Path from granulators outlet conveyor to existing RSHT-2 west side Linear Storage.

STACKING AND RECLAIMING

Two combination traveling stacker reclaimers with each stacker rates of 600 tph & reclaiming rates of 4000 tph shall be considered in the linear granulated sulphur storage building. This allows stacking and reclaiming to occur simultaneously in the linear storage building. This configuration to be kept similar & for easy integration with existing RSHT-2 linear storage.

Sulphur granules are conveyed to the linear granulated sulphur storage building via conveyors to the transfer chutes. The transfer chutes connect to the stacker boom conveyors which in turn form the stockpiles inside these buildings.

To facilitate solid sulphur cooling, the granules are stacked in thin layers. The stockpile in normal operation conditions will be no more than 20 meters peak height.

When the sulphur needs to be reclaimed, the scrapper reclaimers in the buildings transfer stockpiled granules downstream to the existing ship loader(s).

SULPHUR REMELTING

The remelter unit will have a capacity of 30 TPD. All the waste sulphur, including the drained liquid sulphur from filters and drain sumps in addition to granules lost from spillage, which are conveyed with trolleys, are directed into the new remelter.

Sulphur for remelting is loaded into the remelter using a feed conveyor. Inside, the remelter is sloped toward the pump to prevent migration of solids. A weir ensures that the suction of the steam-jacketed remelter pump is completely flooded with liquid sulphur. The pump delivers the liquid sulphur through a 500-µm filter for removal of large contaminants.

Remelting of solid sulphur is accomplished with Medium-Pressure (MP) steam that heats up liquid sulphur with or without a heat exchanger depending on the manufacturer and service duty. Liquid sulphur from remelter is transferred back to liquid sulphur tanks inlet.

DUST CONTROL SYSTEM

At transfer chute in the conveyor system, stacker boom when the sulphur reaches the end of a conveyor and to fall, dust is created. Majority of dust is created when the product hits the conveyor below or any objects in between. Dust control is required at expansion-related transfer points to meet the safe dust emission levels.

There are two (2) systems that to handle the generated dust in each transfer point:

Wet scrubber system.

Dust suppression system.

Wet Scrubber System

In scrubber packages will extract dust from each transfer points at the transfer towers. The conveyor transitions are enclosed by a shroud. Air and dust are vented through duct to the nearest transfer point wet scrubber.  The air/dust mixture is pulled through a venturi scrubber by circulating water, and the dust-laden water is separated from the air in a cyclonic vessel. The clean air is vented through a fan to a stack, where it is dispersed in the atmosphere.

The water phase is drained by gravity to a conical tank where the clean water is decanted for recycle to the venturi. Make up process water provided for any liquid level drop in scrubber water tank. The solids layer is drained to a trolley containing a filter cloth. The solids are returned to the Remelter and the water layer is drained to drain system for pH adjustment and evaporation.

Dust Suppression System

Dust suppressant, a mixture of water and chemical to generate foam which is sprayed onto the sulphur to reduce the surface tension of the sulphur molecules, allowing water to adhere to them more easily. The moisture level on the sulphur granules must not exceed 1% (at ship loading), or the sulphur will be off spec. To control the moisture level, the amount of suppressant applied is controlled based on the product mass flow rate that is calculated by DCS based on the conveyor speed and conveyor weigh scale (if applicable). Suppression spray nozzles are controlled flow such that nozzles only spray when sulphur is present.

Biocide Application System

Chemicals biocide injection are considered in Granule collecting Transfer tower.

Biocide chemical injection package shall essentially consist of chemical injection pumps, chemical storage Tank, calibration pots, strainers, pulsation dampeners, instruments, interconnecting tubing/ piping, valves, accessories, cables, junction boxes, spray nozzles etc.

Biocide shall be added to mitigate bacteria growth associated with sulphur in storage (linear storage building), specifically at the base of the stockpiles. Biocide shall be added to the newly formed solid sulphur flow stream(s) from the granule collecting conveyors.

Biocide application shall be in proportion to the solid sulphur flowrate. Two application points are included namely the stationary portions of the transfer chutes to transfer conveyors.

When direct ship loading of sulphur granules are considered instead of storing in storage buildings, then biocide application is not required.

NEW STORM WATER / WASTE WATER EVAPORATION POND

Greenfield areas covering Granulation area, Tank Farm and associated utilities shall have independent drainage network which shall be connected to proposed Zero Discharge Settlement Pond through two compartment interceptor pit with RC cover with provision of manhole. Design principles of existing drainage philosophy shall be followed where possible. There is no overflow from Zero Discharge Settlement Pond is allowed and it shall hold the storm water /firewater from the project plot areas.

Wash water from the greenfield area shall be collected in the new wastewater treatment system consists of collection pit (1 No), disposal pumps (1W +1S) and caustic injection package (1 No.). After complete neutralization it shall be discharged to evaporation pond.

Area under the conveyors and transfer towers shall be paved and slope to the sump provided at regular intervals. Wash water is then taken out of paved areas to wash water collection pits trough localized intermediate pits. Solid sulphur particles present in the wash down water shall be allowed to settle in the inlet compartment of these pits sumps/pits as per process requirements. The wash water shall be allowed to overflow in the second compartment from there it shall be connected to vacuum pumping pits for emptying out using vacuum truck. These trenches, sumps /pits shall be observed periodically for the solid sulphur deposits. These deposits shall be removed periodically and disposed to company approved location.

A new storm water / wastewater evaporation pond is dedicated to new granulation area to achieve a zero-discharge arrangement for this portion of the terminal only. This is in line with best practices. Waste water from the new sulphur tanks and granulation area is sent to a new evaporation pond located in this area.
