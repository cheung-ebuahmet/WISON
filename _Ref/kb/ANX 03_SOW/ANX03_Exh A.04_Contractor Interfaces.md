A.4.1.1	Interface Philosophy

The key to successful management of interfaces is to have a well-defined structured procedure for managing interfaces. CONTRACTOR shall submit a detailed interface management procedure for COMPANY approval.

These procedures shall define the method for identifying interfaces, defining scopes and deliverables, assigning responsibilities, monitoring status, and follow-up and close-out of interfaces.  

The PROJECT interface philosophy shall include the following and is expected to:

Minimize the number of interfaces.

Eliminate duplicate effort.

Encourage the communication of interface information between different Contractors and COMPANY. Communication shall be cooperative, proactive, clear, accurate, timely, focused and consistent on resolving all interfaces issues.

Identify interface issues early to minimize impact to project cost and schedule.

Identify a party accountable for each interface as well as a responding party.

Assign a single responsible point of contact within each organization for each interface issue.

Assign personnel as contacts who shall be involved in and familiar with the interface issues.

Complete Interface Agreement Data Sheet (IFDS) for this PROJECT.  This includes defining the scope of work, responsible parties, due dates for deliverables, and providing drawings and documents related to the interface.   

Define the responsible parties for testing and pre-commissioning, QA/QC, final consolidation of the documents and as-built of the final installation in the Interface Agreement Data Sheet.

Ensure all organisations completely understand their respective responsibilities, activities and deliverables, and agree in writing on the plan and the schedule for resolution.

Coordinate the flow of information among each interface party and COMPANY.  

Identify and correct oversight, non-performance, and project schedule discrepancies in all activities.

Resolve and close out the Interface Agreement Data Sheet at each interface.

Notify all parties immediately if there are any changes to a closed interface.

COMPANY will validate and accept all interfaces between the Contractors prior to their becoming an official interface. This validation and acceptance will determine that they are complete and accurate and that unnecessary interfaces are not created by the Contractors. The following shall apply regarding the creation of interface:

Contractors shall identify an interface point.

The interface point shall be presented to COMPANY.  

COMPANY will review the information provided by the Contractors.  After review, COMPANY will either accept or reject the interface point as a valid interface.  

CONTRACTOR shall create and maintain UpToDate interface register which include as minimum interface parties, location, scope, status etc   

A.4.1.2          	Interface with COMPANY and Other Contractors

CONTRACTOR agrees to cooperate fully with COMPANY and/or Other Contractors performing such work and endeavour not to interfere with the performance of such work.

CONTRACTOR shall appoint an Interface Coordinator responsible for overseeing all interfaces between CONTRACTOR and COMPANY and/or Other Contractors. The Interface Coordinator shall be an experienced engineer familiar with all aspects of the WORK and capable of and authorized to communicate directly with COMPANY and Other Contractors etc. on interface related issues. Appointment of the Interface Coordinator is subject to approval of COMPANY. 

Within thirty (30) days of the EFFECTIVE DATE, CONTRACTOR shall prepare and submit for COMPANY’s information an Interface Plan covering all foreseeable interfaces during the course of execution of the WORK. CONTRACTOR’s execution plan shall address all such interfaces, allowing sufficient flexibility to accommodate changes in the known interfaces and additional interfaces identified during the execution of WORK. CONTRACTOR shall provide updates to the Interface Plan on a monthly basis. CONTRACTOR shall immediately inform COMPANY if there are any changes in CONTRACTOR’s planned execution of the WORK that have an impact on the work being or to be performed by others or if additional interfaces are identified by CONTRACTOR. 

CONTRACTOR shall communicate directly with Other Contractors etc. to review technical issues and exchange technical data related to interfaces and to reach mutual agreement on dates for interfaces. This may include visits by the Interface Coordinator and/or other CONTRACTOR personnel to the offices of Other Contractors. However, all formal communications shall be directed in writing to COMPANY. No communication from Other Contractors shall be considered an instruction or directive from COMPANY to CONTRACTOR.

CONTRACTOR shall provide in timely manner any and all information regarding the WORK as may be requested by COMPANY or any Other Contractor if such information is necessary for COMPANY or Other Contractor(s) to carry out their work in accordance with their schedule.  CONTRACTOR further agrees to request in writing from COMPANY or any Other Contractor, whichever is performing the work, in a timely manner any information about such work that may be required by CONTRACTOR to perform the WORK in accordance with the SCHEDULE.

CONTRACTOR shall develop interface drawings in sufficient detail to define the method and location of interfaces of the WORK and the work performed by the Other Contractors.

CONTRACTOR shall actively participate in monthly Interface Meetings with COMPANY and Other Contractors as determined by COMPANY during the design phase and the construction phase. CONTRACTOR shall host monthly Interface Meetings at its home office, and for the entire period of the meeting provide a meeting room and workspace for additional COMPANY personnel and personnel from Other Contractors attending the meetings. 

CONTRACTOR shall develop an Interdependency Schedule to detail and complement the Interface Plan and submit to COMPANY the first issue within thirty (30) days of the EFFECTIVE DATE. The first issue shall include the major Interdependent data.  Within ninety (90) days of the EFFECTIVE DATE, a subsequent issue of the Interdependency Schedule shall be submitted to include all interdependency issues.  The Interdependency Schedule shall identify the dates that the exchange of interface information is required by CONTRACTOR and each Other Contractor to allow CONTRACTOR’s  and the work of the Other Contractors to proceed without delay. Please refer to item 4.1.4 for further details.

Some portions for the INSTALLATIONS may be connected to and/or operate in conjunction with facilities being provided by Other Contractors.  CONTRACTOR’s SYSTEM co-ordination responsibility during the EPC design phase is defined in the discipline Interface Schedules and/or Philosophies listed in this EXHIBIT; CONTRACTORS responsibility during Construction and COMMISSIONING is defined in EXHIBIT A.4.7.

CONTRACTOR shall inform COMPANY immediately of any concerns or deficiencies that are discovered related to the Interconnecting   SYSTEMs.

COMPANY will assist CONTRACTOR in coordinating the interface issues with Other Contractors. CONTRACTOR shall remain fully responsible for the management of all interface issues.

Where necessary, COMPANY will facilitate interaction between CONTRACTOR and other contractors. In the event of conflict, COMPANY will assist CONTRACTOR to coordinate CONTRACTOR’s activities with those of other contractors and give necessary instructions/directions to resolve the issue.

A.4.1.3	Interface with existing facilities 

CONTRACTOR shall follow EXHIBIT B.21 when interfacing with existing systems at existing facilities.

CONTRACTOR shall in addition to following EXHIBIT B.21 do the following:

Tie-ins shall be allocated for each interface with an existing line and shall be shown on the respective system drawing (P&ID) and listed on the tie-in schedule.  It is the responsibility of CONTRACTOR to verify the tie-in points during the early phase and to ensure full compliance with all requirements of COMPANY’s specifications and with the specifications and work procedures at the existing location.  

CONTRACTOR shall be responsible for finalizing all details and exact locations for all tie-ins into EXISTING facilities and all interfaces into an existing or proposed new facility.

CONTRACTOR shall be responsible for the following and shall include all of this information in a work package that shall be issued to COMPANY before any physical work takes place:

Exact tie-in location and where necessary pipe routings

Verification of process conditions of the existing system, and operation and control

Checking suitability of existing system relieving devices

Reviewing the materials of construction of existing and new pipe-work to ensure compatibility 

Proximity of tie-ins and piping to hazardous equipment, including access, clearances shielding etc.

Method of isolation during tie-in operations

Extent of testing and COMMISSIONING

Decontamination procedures

Preparation, co-ordination and maintenance of tie-in responsibility matrix

Preparation of Method Statement for each tie-in and pipe routing for COMPANY acceptance.

Anything else the existing site and or COMPANY shall require to ensure that the tie-in is performed safely.  

COMPANY and the existing facility operators shall review and accept of all work and the work package before any physical work is started. COMPANY may require CONTRACTOR to make changes to the work package before any work is started. In the event such changes are required due to compliance with scope, safety and quality requirements, these changes will be carried out by CONTRACTOR without any additional cost. 

CONTRACTOR shall review detailed designs for each tie-in and pipe routing with COMPANY, the existing facility, and all related parties for compliance.  Tie-ins and piping shall be engineered and installed so as to minimise requirements for the shutdown of existing plant or piping system.  Where a shutdown is required, the timing must be co-ordinated with COMPANY and the EXISTING FACILITIES.

A.4.1.6	           Physical Interfaces

For all physical interface points CONTRACTORS shall provide sufficient material to complete the interface connection.  

CONTRACTORS shall use the same datum elevation and plan coordinate system and number system as established in the early design study.  Therefore, a common datum elevation and plan coordinate system and number system shall be used throughout the PROJECT by all CONTRACTORS.  The use of a common datum elevation and plan coordinate system and number system will allow checking of all interface points between CONTRACTORS to ensure that all physical interface points match.  

The plan coordinate system and number system and datum elevation point used are references only and available in early design documents (EXHIBIT C).

CONTRACTOR shall label all physical interface points on all drawings with the corresponding interface number.  The specific interface point shall be labeled on all drawings that contain that interface point. A note that lists all interface points on a drawing shall be placed in the reference notes section of that drawing identifying the appropriate interface number.

CONTRACTOR shall design, engineer, procure, install and provide commissioning services as required by AGREEMENT for all interfaces and any additional interfaces identified during the execution of the work by CONTRACTOR(S) and/or COMPANY. 

A.4.1.7           	Other Physical Interfaces

Every interface point or location to be identified and determined during early phase of EPC project. There could be additional interfaces that unfold during the development of the project including during the construction phase of the PROJECT. These new and unknown interfaces shall be determined by CONTRACTOR during the development of the PROJECT and shall be identified to COMPANY and other third parties/Contractors as required. 

CONTRACTOR shall engineer, procure, and install any material required to complete the new interfaces as necessary to ensure that the INSTALLATION operates in a safe and efficient matter and meets COMPANY objectives. In case of conflict, COMPANY shall decide which Contractor is responsible to carry out the work required for these unknown interfaces and same shall be implemented by the concerned contractor at no additional cost to COMPANY.    

CONTRACTOR shall work together with COMPANY and other Contractors as required to complete all unknown interfaces.
