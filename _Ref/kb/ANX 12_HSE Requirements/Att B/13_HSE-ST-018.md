2. INTRODUCTION 
A Confined Space is an enclosed or substantially enclosed volume where there is a 
reasonably foreseeable risk of serious injury to personnel entering the volume from hazardous 
substances or conditions within the volume or nearby.  These spaces may include, but are not 
limited to, vessels, tanks, pipes, ducts, valve pits, sumps, inspection pits, silos, trenches etc. 
ADNOC Code of Practice on non-routine operations (ADNOC CoPV4-05) classifies entry into 
a Confined Space as a non- routine activity. The Code of Practice demands ADNOC Group 
Companies to ensure hazards are identified, risks are assessed and appropriate control 
measures are implemented prior to carrying out any non-routine operations. HSE Impact 
Assessment studies conducted by GASCO have identified Confined Space Entry (CSE) as a 
high potential health and safety risk activity. The Company has adopted a risk based approach 
in the planning and execution of all site work which is controlled via Permit to Work (PTW) and 
Activity Risk Assessment (ARA) process. 
GASCO have established 10 Life Saving Rules (LSR) which are most likely to prevent injuries 
that could have resulted in loss of life. Rule No. 5 explicitly states, obtain authorisation before 
entering a Confined Space. 
This document defines the requirements and guidelines required for personnel to carryout 
Confined Space activities and sets out the main precautions needed to complete the activities 
in a Confined Space safely. 
3. PURPOSE 
  The purpose of this standard is to: 
 
Detail the requirements that applies to the preparation for safe entry and working 
environment to any enclosure or area categorised as a Confined Space; 
 
Establish safety and occupational health protection requirements to eliminate or reduce 
hazards of Confined Space entries thereby protecting the safety of employees involved; 
and 
 
Prevent or reduce consequences of incidents related to preparing and executing 
Confined Space Entry. 
 
4. SCOPE AND APPLICATION 
The standard applies to fully or partially enclosed spaces that are not designed and 
constructed for continuous human occupancy, have limited or restricted means for entry or 
exit, and where there is a risk of injury or health effect from hazardous substances or 
conditions. 
The standard is applicable to GASCO employees and contractors performing a task that 
requires entry into a Confined Space which often become the part of plant modifications, 
construction, inspection or maintenance work scope.

7. DEFINITION OF CONFINED SPACE 
A fully or partially enclosed space that is not designed and constructed for continuous human 
occupancy, have limited or restricted means for entry or exit, and where there is a reasonably 
foreseeable risk of injury or adverse health effect to personnel entering that space from 
hazardous substances or conditions present there or nearby. 
Examples of Confined Spaces are: 
 
Process vessels (separators, pressure vessels etc.), columns, column skirts, pipes, 
boilers, storage tanks, top of floating roof tanks, road/rail and tankers; and 
 
Spaces below ground such as drain pits, sewage pits and associated tunnels, trenches, 
wells, shafts etc. 
Other types of Confined Spaces are less obvious, for example: 
 
Rooms that become Confined Spaces because of the type of work being undertaken, 
e.g. Spray Painting….etc.   
 
Partially enclosed areas such as pits and excavations are considered to be Confined 
Spaces when they are deep enough for the breathing zone to be inside the pit (typically 
more than 1.5 meters) and the ventilation is insufficient to prevent possible build-up of 
hazardous vapours. When the width (narrowest side) of such an area is much less than 
the depth, ventilation will be insufficient.  
Note: In restricted areas, Confined Space entry certificate is required for working inside 
excavations, if the depth is more than 1.5 meter irrespective of the depth to width ratio. 
For unrestricted areas, Confined Space entry certificate is required if the depth is more 
than 1.5 meter and depth to width ratio is more than 2 (i.e., if Depth/ Width >2).  
Please refer Appendix E for more details for unrestricted areas. 
8. CONFINED SPACE HAZARDS 
Hazards that arise in Confined Spaces are due to the combination of confined nature of the 
place of work and the presence of substances or conditions, which taken together, increase 
the risk to the safety or health of people.  
The following are examples of hazards that may be encountered in Confined Space activities.   
Hazards associated with Confined Space are: 
 
Flammable Substances, which present a risk of fire or explosion 
 
Oxygen Enrichment, which may occur for example, if an oxygen cylinder (used when 
welding) leaks, or if the work is in close proximity to the discharge of an oxygen rich vent, 
such as from a nitrogen generator. Oxygen can render materials flammable that are 
otherwise non-flammable and will greatly increase the risk of fire or explosion. 
 
Oxygen deficiency, which may result from: 
 
Purging of the Confined Space with an inert gas to remove flammable or toxic 
gas, fume, vapour or aerosol. 
 
Naturally occurring biological processes that consume oxygen or can 
produce inert gas.

10.7 Gas Purging & Removal of Residues 
Purging is the displacement of the hazardous atmosphere of Plant or pipeline by an inert 
medium to remove potential hazards and allow work to proceed. Where flammable or toxic 
gases or vapours are present, or possibly present, it may be necessary to purge the gas or 
vapour from the Confined Space. This can be done with air or inert gas when the 
contaminants are non-flammable, but must be done first with inert gas only when there are 
flammable contaminants. Following purging, the atmosphere must be tested to check that 
purging has been effective and that it is safe to breath, before allowing people to enter. 
In order to fully purge equipment it is necessary to select the correct purge medium method, 
and: 
 
Control the purge rate. 
 
Carryout the purge for sufficient time or cycles. 
 
Take special care to deal with low points and dead legs.

purging with inert gas and the work cannot be carried out from a safe position outside the 
Confined Space, then a risk assessment must identify the standard of protection of all 
exposed persons. This may include the use of full breathing apparatus (e.g. use of airline 
manifold or SCBAs).

10.12 Access & Egress 
The access and exit to/from the Confined Space shall be unobstructed.   
Suitable means to prevent access must be in place when there is no need for anyone to be in 
Confined Space. The safe system of work must include a check that all persons have left the 
Confined Space prior to “boxing-up” operations, particularly where the Confined Space is 
extensive, complex or has multiple exits. 
Warning notices shall be posted and physical barriers shall be placed at the point of entry to 
prevent unauthorised access to the Confined Spaces. 
Size of Confined Space Openings 
Experience has shown that the minimum size of opening to allow access with full rescue 
facilities including self-contained breathing apparatus is 575mm diameter.  Good industry 
practice is to use a minimum manhole size of 600mm diameter. This minimum size must be 
incorporated into the design of all new plant.  In some cases the openings of Confined Spaces 
may need to be larger. 
Existing plant may have narrower openings and it will therefore be necessary to check that a 
person wearing suitable equipment can safely and readily pass through such openings.  
Choice of airline breathing apparatus in such cases offers a more compact alternative to 
bulkier self-contained apparatus.  Examples of commonly occurring narrower openings include 
tank containers and road tank wagons. 
The size and number of access and egress points must be assessed individually, dependent 
upon the activities carried out and the number of personnel involved.  Large-scale evacuations 
may need larger openings to prevent bottlenecks.  Top openings to vessels and tanks, etc., 
should be avoided, due to difficulties of access and rescue.  Bottom or low manholes are 
preferable, but may still require access platforms.  Where access and egress is difficult, 
temporary openings may be needed. 
In certain cases, for example sewers, pipes, culverts, small tunnels and shafts, where the 
critical entry dimensions extend over a significant length or height, different minimum size 
criteria apply. No person should normally enter extended spaces smaller than 0.9m high and 
0.6m wide.  Even this minimum size may be, in some circumstances, too small for reliance on 
a safe system of work alone and additional measures may be needed.  For example, if there is 
a long distance between access points, then structural alterations to improve access may be 
required.  
In the case of a shaft containing a ladder or step irons, a minimum of 900mm clear space must 
be available between the ladder/steps and the back of the shaft. 
The above restrictions on personnel entry into openings to Confined Spaces apply equally to 
restrictions within the Confined Space itself.

10.14 Special Precautions  
Petrol fuelled engines must never be used in Confined Spaces.  If Diesel fuelled internal 
combustion engines are to be used, then it should be located outside the Confined Space at a 
safe location where exhaust fumes cannot contaminate the atmosphere inside the Confined 
Space, either directly or through forced ventilation or breathing air systems. 
Pressurized gas cylinders (all types) must not be allowed inside Confined Space.  The use of 
Pipes and hoses for conveying Oxygen or Flammable gases into Confined Space for hot work 
(welding, gas cutting etc.) must be controlled to minimise the risk.  At the end of every work 
period, the supply valves for pipes and hoses must be securely closed, before the pipes and 
hoses are withdrawn to a well-ventilated location. 
Flammable and combustible materials must not be stored within Confined Space and any 
accumulation of flammable or combustible materials resulting from activities must be removed. 
Where it is necessary to store such materials in Confined Spaces, they must be kept in fire 
resistant containers.  
There shall be no radiation sources inside a Confined Space.  In certain cases radiation 
sources are used in certain types of level measuring instruments which shall be removed or 
shielded.

10.18 Limited Working Time & Number of People inside Confined Space 
There may be a need to limit the Time-Period that individuals are allowed to work in a 
Confined Space.  The duration of work and breaks inside Confined Space and number of 
people working inside will depend on the Risk Assessment considering the following:  
 
Breathing Apparatus are in use. 
 
Temperature / Humidity Conditions. 
 
Size of the Confined Space.  
 
Number of people working inside.  
 
Activity performed.  
 
Evacuation & Rescue Plan.  
Note: It is recommended that the maximum work limit for the continuous work inside 
confined space shall not exceed more than 2 hours. 
For any confined Space entry, a logging or tally system shall be necessary to check everyone 
in and out and to control duration of entry. 
11. TRAINING & COMPETENCY REQUIREMENTS 
Adequate training shall be provided for all personnel to be involved in Confined Space Entry, 
either as entrants, CSA, rescue team members or Authorised Gas Testers, to ensure that they 
possess the understanding, knowledge and skills necessary to safely perform their assigned 
duties.  
Personnel assigned for Confined Space Entry shall have correct level of training (particular to 
their roles), experience and knowledge. The maximum validity of the training is 3 years and 
thereafter refresher training is required. 
New personnel shall not be assigned to perform any tasks in the Confined Space, unless they 
are trained in their roles and accompanied by a competent person who is familiar with the 
hazards of Confined Space Entry. Entrants and rescue team members shall have adequate 
physical fitness to perform the activity. For contractors, medical fitness certificate (medical 
advice on an individual suitability) to be submitted.  
Training must cover: 
 
An awareness of the requirements of this standard and ADNOC –COPV4-05 
for Confined Space Entry. 
 
An understanding of the work to be undertaken, the hazards and necessary 
precautions. 
 
An understanding of the risk assessment and GASCO PTW System. 
 
How emergencies might arise and the need to follow prepared emergency 
arrangements. 
 
Training on SCBA, SABA & Physical Fitness requirements for entrants.

Training of Emergency Rescue Personnel 
Those likely to be involved in any emergency rescue must be trained for that purpose. The 
training needs for each individual will vary according to their designated role. It is important 
refresher training is organised and available on a regular basis. The maximum validity of the 
training is 3 years.  
When developing training requirements consideration must be given to: 
 
The likely causes of emergencies.  
 
Use of rescue equipment such as breathing apparatus, lifelines and where 
necessary, a knowledge of its construction and working. 
 
The checklist of procedures to be followed when donning and using breathing 
apparatus. 
 
Checking of correct functioning and/ or testing of emergency equipment. 
 
Identifying defects and dealing with malfunctions and failures of equipment 
during use. 
 
Relevant emergency procedures including how to initiate emergency 
response. 
 
Instructions on how to shutdown relevant process plant as appropriate. 
 
Resuscitation procedures and, where appropriate, the correct use of relevant 
ancillary equipment they may use. 
 
First aid and use of the first aid equipment provided. 
 
Use of fire-fighting equipment provided. 
 
Liaison with emergency specialists who may be called in including provision 
of relevant information about conditions and risks and provision of space so 
that they can carry out their tasks.  
 
Confined space & Work at height rescue techniques, including regular 
periodic rehearsals/ exercises. 
 
Training should be realistic and not just drill based and must be designed to 
test and enhance practice and familiarity with equipment. 
 
Training must also include the practical use of safety features and equipment.  It is essential that 
trainees are familiar with both equipment and procedures before engaging in a Confined Space
