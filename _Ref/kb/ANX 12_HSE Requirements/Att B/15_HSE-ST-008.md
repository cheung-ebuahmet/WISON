1. 
INTRODUCTION 
HAZID (HAZard IDentification), ENVID (ENVironmental IDentification) and OHID 
(Occupational Health IDentification) are techniques for early identification of potential 
hazards and threats. These risk assessment techniques involves assessments of 
identified hazards, risk evaluation of each hazard of a new project, modifications or an 
activity carried out as part of normal business (Operations), risk evaluation in terms of 
potential impact affecting people, assets, reputation or the environment  or within the 
vicinity. 
The outcome will be used for the development of Hazard & Effects register part of 
HSEIA Report or Project Risk assessment Report.  When major accident hazard is 
identified in Hazards and Effects Register, full HSEIA shall be performed as per 
ADNOC CoP. If no major accident hazards (as per ADNOC definition of MAH which 
correspond to 4 or 5 severity, or 3E   as per the Risk Assessment Matrix), no significant 
environmental impacts, and no extreme or high occupational health risks are 
identified a “De Minimis” status must be reported. It must be demonstrated that the “De 
Minimis” has been concluded following thorough screening of the Hazard and Effects 
Register. HSEIA Screening review should be performed and management of low and 
medium hazards should be done demonstrating why detailed HSEIA is not necessary. 
2. 
OBJECTIVES 
The primary objective of HAZID / ENVID / OHID study is to systematically identify all 
safety, environment and health hazards associated with the new project, operation or 
activity. A qualitative risk assessment for each identified hazards is conducted to risk 
rank the hazard and determine the focus of control measure applicability. This is done 
by the systematic identification of any hazard, which may have the potential to cause 
harm, followed by a review of whether adequate safe guards exist, or additional 
safeguards are required to mitigate the potential consequences to ALARP. 
The benefits of using HAZID / ENVID / OHID Review 
 
Full recognition of the importance and interdependence of all HSE aspects at the 
outset of the development. 
 
The potential to affect major changes to philosophy / design at very early stage 
before significant financial commitment are made. 
 
Identification of specific hazards and threats within a project life cycles. 
 
An opportunity to consider the HSE implication of alternative process design as 
part of the selection process between possible design options. 
 
Identification of all the environmental impact from the facility. This will focus the 
design effort on the minimization of such emissions as well as on compliance with 
Company and legal requirements. 
 
Identification of processes or tasks associated with the Project that might pose 
health risks to personnel and to establish an inventory of chemical, biological and 
physical agents that are potentially hazardous to health.  
3. 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID SCOPE 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID study is a technique used to systematically identify potential 
hazards and threats, which is the basis to form Hazard & Effects register and 
preparation of COMAH/EIA/OHID Report as part of HSEIA Report. The applicability of 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID study follows the same stages of HSEIA development in line 
with ADNOC HSE CoP requirement. It is applicable to all phases of the Project as well 
as to any Plant /Pipeline modification or major maintenance activity.

3.1 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID Stages 
Following ADNOC CoPV1-02 HSEIA requirements, there are four project phases 
where HSEIA report is required. 
a. 
Conceptual design and FEED. 
b. 
Engineering Procurement and Construction (EPC). 
c. 
Operation. 
d. 
Abandonment (De-commissioning, Demolition & Disposal). 
a. Conceptual design and FEED Phase 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID study of Conceptual design and FEED is best conducted 
during the first few weeks of developing the project concept and objectives in order 
to identify all the hazards associated with the development including the other three 
phases as an overview. 
b. Engineering Procurement and Construction (EPC) Phase 
EPC Phase HAZID / ENVID / OHID study identifies the hazard associated with 
detailed design, construction, pre-commissioning, commissioning (including 
performance test) and demobilization of contractor. In this phase the team can 
focus more effectively on components of the selected process, and review the 
engineering decisions on process technology, capacity, and sparing philosophy at 
operations concept. 
c. Operation Phase 
The operation HAZID / ENVID / OHID phase will identify the safety & environmental 
hazards associated with routine and non-routine operations, considering the 
modifications based on finalized construction. It is essential in this phase to review 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID finding of phases-I and 2. 
This phase is also appropriate to existing facility considering operation experience, 
maintenance findings and previous HAZID / ENVID / OHID studies 
recommendations. 
d. Abandonment (De-commissioning, Demolition & Disposal) Phase 
In this phase, the safety and environmental hazards associated with shutdown, 
decommissioning, removal, moth-balling, disposal and site remediation are 
identified.  
3.2 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID Duration & Timing 
a. Duration  
The duration of HAZID / ENVID / OHID study is governed by the location, size of 
the project, availability of information, project complexity technology. However the 
objective of HAZID / ENVID / OHID studies is to recognize and identify the issues 
rather than to propose solutions. A typical combined HAZID / ENVID/ OHID study 
would be 2 to 3 days per Project/package. 
b. Timing 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID study shall be held as soon as enough information is 
available of the Project. It is an integral part of HSEIA development.  
For conceptual and FEED phase is best conducted when facility schemes, location, 
population, and the costing/project economic information are available. Usually few 
weeks of project development and getting some feedback from shareholders. 
For EPC phase, HAZID / ENVID / OHID study is best conducted immediately after 
selecting the EPC contractor. It is also possible to conduct a separate

4. 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID METHODOLOGY 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID study is a brainstorming exercise, guided by the prepared 
checklist and taking benefit from the previous experience of the HAZID, ENVID and 
OHID study participants. 
4.1 
Break down of project nodes 
Many of the Hazards, environmental and occupational health issues are generic for a 
whole development and are not specific to any part of the plant, location or stage/ 
activity. The typical process is therefore to apply the HAZID / ENVID / OHID techniques 
to the whole project as a single entity, for which the study method is a combination of 
identification, analysis and brainstorming based on the hazards identified on the 
checklist. 
Once all global issues have been discussed, the team shall sharpen the focus of the 
study and the development may be divided into a number of clearly defined areas. The 
definition of a selection will usually be either a geographical location, for a development 
consisting of many sites, by activities, by chronological order of project execution, or 
by process group / financial area. The nodes to be defined by the chairman prior to the 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID review meeting and confirmed with team members during the 
study meeting.

project will establish a set of guidewords (in the form of checklist) to be applied in the 
HAZID/ENVID/OHID study meeting. A typical checklist is included in Appendix A as 
guideline for the chairman.

4.3 
Risk Ranking 
The intention of HAZID / ENVID / OHID study is to identify and assess the potential of 
each HSE Hazards by risk ranking. The risk ranking assess the likelihood and 
consequences of the hazard occurring. ADNOC HSE Risk Management Guideline 
(latest edition) to be used. 
For HAZID study the risks are evaluated using the qualitative ADNOC Risk 
Assessment Matrix as shown in Appendix B.  
For ENVID study risk is evaluated using 2 criteria’s (see Appendix C Planned 
significant Impacts using Environmental Impact severity matrix (Figure 2) and 
accidental significant impacts using Environmental risk potential matrix (Figure 3).  
For OHID study occupational health hazard are evaluated using modified version of 
the qualitative ADNOC occupational health risk matrix, shown in Appendix E. The 
definitions used in this matrix are explained in Tables 6a, 6b and 7. 
Note that the initial risk ranking is to assign a level conservatively assuming that no 
mitigation measures are in place. 
4.4 
Brain Storming 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID reviews give emphasis to the use of the checklist by the team 
for brainstorming. The team knowledge and experience are used to identify potential 
hazards. It is important that the list of hazards is used in a creative manner to allow for 
recognition of unusual hazards or specific combinations of factors that cause an HSE 
concern. 
It is the role of the Chairman to encourage the debate between participants, 
questioning input and ensure that everyone has an opportunity to contribute. The 
chairman will ask prompting questions to keep the meeting moving and to make sure 
review is completed on time. The team shall not try to solve any Engineering /Design 
problems but rather will raise actions to address these issues. 
5. 
RECORDING & REPORTING 
A HAZID / ENVID / OHID is used as a mean of initiating the process of compiling the 
hazard and effects register as part of  the HSEIA study  or for general Project Risk 
assessment activity.  
Recording of the risks and recommendations goes along with review discussion. Using 
the latest PHA Pro software (or Equivalent). HAZID discussion is recorded as per 
sample work sheet in Appendix F, and ENVID/OHID discussion is recorded in 
Environmental Aspect and Occupational Health spread sheet as per Appendices 
G&H, respectively.  
HAZID / ENVID / OHID Report structure to include study methodology, discussion (any 
assumption / exclusion), work sheets, recommendation, and participant list as per the 
format provided in Appendix I

6. 
MANAGEMENT OF RECOMMENDATIONS  
Recommendations identified during the HAZID / ENVID / OHID study shall be assigned 
by the team to individuals or disciplines for resolutions. The recommendations shall be 
defined, with clear directions for the project to develop an action and shall be identified 
on record sheet by the action party. 
All recommendations shall be entered in the project action tracking register. 
Closeout of HAZID / ENVID / OHID recommendations shall be via action tracking 
register, with approval of GASCO HSE Manager and clearly identifying the actions 
taken against each recommendation. HAZID ENVID OHID Close-out report shall be 
prepared when all actions are closed. 
7. 
HAZID / ENVID REVIEW TEAM 
7.1 
Team Composition 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID team should be kept comparatively small but the team should 
contain sufficient knowledge to recognize and identify all the HSE issues. A typical 
team would have four to six members maximum. 
The Project phase and nature would determine specialty of team required. The team 
should include the most experienced personnel available. The following disciplines 
shall be the full time team members: 
a. Chairman. 
b. Corporate HSE Representative.  
c. Operations Representatives. 
d. Contractor Representative. 
e. PMTC representative. 
The members may include one or more of the following staff: 
a. Engineering Manager. 
b. Construction Manager. 
c. Discipline Engineers ( Process, HSE, Piping, Instruments, Civil, Rotating & Static - 
As applicable). 
d. Commissioning Manager.  
Other engineering disciplines from COMPANY/ PMTC/ Contractor may be required to 
provide input to the hazard identification by attending the study meeting (Full time / 
Partial) as required. 
The key to a successful HAZID//ENVID/OHID is the correct combination of breadth 
and depth in the experience of the team. The levels of experience and knowledge 
required will mean that the team consist of senior personnel from within the COMPANY 
and Project organization.

8. 
HAZID / ENVID / OHID MEETINGS 
The HAZID / ENVID / OHID study meeting involves the following steps: 
a. Define the study scope including defining project aspect covered in the study, 
location and timing of the study. 
b. Select study team chairman and team members. 
c. The Chairman shall develop a set of guidewords in the format of checklist.  
d. Define the nodes in each facility, a node should not be too large or complicated. It 
should have typical boundaries and perform specific functions. Identify the hazard 
area, materials, activity, events or operations in each node for each project phase. 
E.g. Construction, pre-commissioning, commissioning, and operation. 
e. Ensure that all team members are familiar with the study scope and HAZID 
/ENVID/OHID methodology. A project representative shall introduce the team to 
the project facility and layout, including activity description / schedule. 
f. The chairman will prepare the checklist application to the studied node, and then 
ask the team to consider each guideword. 
g. In each case the team will analyze the guide word (helped by the example given in 
the checklist) to identify any hazard / Environmental impacts/ Health hazard and its 
potential effects which may be entered on the worksheets. Guide words to be 
challenged for comprehensiveness to add or remove. 
h. The brainstorming process will then be used to identify all the potential threats and 
hazards associated with the Project.  
i. 
The team will analyze the appropriate controls that are in place or required to 
prevent or mitigate each threat. 
j. 
Risk Rank each Hazard as per ADNOC Risk Matrix and assign the HSE priority 
(High, Medium, and Low). 
k. Determine each environmental impact effect during planned operations. 
l. 
Determine potential environmental impacts during Operation, abnormal operations 
and emergency situations and recommend necessary controls. 
m. Determine Potential Health Hazards associated with the Project and recommend 
necessary control measures.

the seriousness of harm is known as ‘Severity’.
