2. INTRODUCTION 
Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is defined as any piece of equipment that is designed to be 
worn for the protection of the health and safety of employees. It provides a barrier between the 
person at risk and the potential instrument of injury.  
It is GASCO policy to prevent injuries and occupational illnesses. One of the means of achieving 
this is to provide PPE to employees who may be exposed to a risk.  It should be noted that PPE 
may reduce the risk of harm or injury occurring to an individual but should only be used as a 
means of last resort. There is no substitute for engineering controls and/or safe systems of work 
to protect the workforce in general and reduce the need for PPE.

3. PURPOSE 
To define Company standards for identification, procurement, inspection, maintenance and 
usage of PPE, specifying what is required and providing guidance on how it will be implemented. 
The term PPE in this document includes both clothing as well as equipment.

This Standard applies to all staff, contractors, sub-contractors and visitors at GASCO assets, 
facilities, operations and projects.   
This Standard does not apply to diving equipment which is covered by Code of Practice on 
Diving Operations.

7. REQUIREMENTS 
 
7.1 Selection Criterion 
 
The PPE must be specific to the work activity, surrounding environment and overall risk to the 
personnel during normal, potential abnormal and downgraded situations. There are 2 categories 
of PPE: 
 
 Minimum PPE for protection of Head, Eye, Ear, Body and Foot. 
 Specific PPE for risk based applications such as Firefighting/ Emergency                       
response, Toxic gas environment, Chemical handling, Biological risk, Handling 
carcinogens, Hot and Cold environments and situations such as Work at height, 
Confined spaces, Exposure to radiation, High voltage electricity, Potential exposure to 
flash fires etc. 
 
The minimum required PPE to enter to any GASCO Restricted Pipeline/ Plant areas (as per PTW 
requirements) are: 
 
 
Safety Helmet  
 
Ear Protection  
 
Single Piece Fire Retardant Coverall 
 
Safety Shoes 
 
Safety Glasses (Clear Glasses for Night & Clear / Dark Glasses for Day) 
 
The following are examples of risk based PPEs and shall be worn as required: 
 
 
Personal H2S Detector- As per GASCO H2S Standard 
 
Emergency Escape Breathing Device / Escape Hood - As per GASCO H2S Standard 
 
Gloves 
 
PPE for the non-restricted areas shall be based on Activity Risk Assessment.  
Each site shall identify quantities and procurement of PPE in accordance with the site activities 
related to construction, commissioning, operation, maintenance and shutdown phases of the 
plant/pipeline including consumption rates. 
Pictorial Signboards shall be displayed at Sites/ Pipeline stations showing the mandatory PPE 
that must be worn, such as Helmet, Ear protection etc., to enter. Please refer to Appendix I for 
the PPE signboard template. PPE signboards shall also be displayed whenever there is a 
requirement of specific PPE. 
PPE selection shall be reviewed periodically based on LFIs, Audit recommendations, changes in 
work procedures/ materials and/or Equipment.  Heat stress, disposal of contaminated PPE, 
decontamination process etc. shall be considered while selecting PPE.

7.2 Compatibility of PPE 
Where there is more than one risk to health that requires the wearing of PPE, it is important that 
the PPE selected is compatible and continues to be effective against the risks.  For example, 
where a hard hat and a respirator are simultaneously required, it is important to check that both 
can be worn correctly and that both continue to give adequate protection from the risks for which 
they have been selected. 
7.3 Suitability & Selection of PPE 
PPE must be suitable for the degree of protection, which it is required to provide. In particular, 
PPE shall not be considered suitable unless: 
 It is appropriate for the risk(s) involved. 
 It is appropriate for the conditions at the place where exposure to risk may occur. 
 It takes account of ergonomic requirements and the state of health of the person wearing it. 
 It is capable of fitting the wearer correctly. 
 It is effective in preventing or controlling the risk(s) involved without increasing the overall 
risk(s). 
 
7.3.1 Selection process by PPE Selection committee: 
 
The PPE Selection committee shall be periodically evaluating the PPEs against the requirements 
in this Standard. PPE selection committee will be responsible for approving PPEs before 
procurement, after verifying the PPE specifications and quality/ test certificates issued by PPE 
Manufacturer/ Supplier. PPE selection committee shall be conducting re-evaluation of PPEs on 
annual basis or if any concerns raised during the PPE surveys (Refer section 12).   
The key issues to be considered when selecting appropriate PPE are: 
 Length of time for which the PPE will be worn. 
 The mobility, vision & communication required by the worker. 
 Physical effort required by the job and the methods of work. 
 Level of risks likely to be encountered. 
 SDS (Safety Data Sheet) of the chemical (if applicable).  
 Parts of the body to be protected. 
Personnel who will be required to use PPE shall be consulted prior to final selection of the PPE. 
(Sample PPE could be used for a trial period (e.g. two weeks) at different sites). This will provide 
useful input to the selection process from those who will know best what is involved during the 
job. The aim shall always be to choose appropriate PPE that will give the correct level of 
protection with the least discomfort to the user.

7.4 Procurement, Storage & Issue of PPE 
 
Sites shall establish system for administration of PPE. For procurement, the specifications for 
each types of PPE shall be applied. PPE stock shall be maintained in such a manner that the 
minimum 30 days stock inventory is always maintained with respect to the consumption rate. 
 
Sites shall ensure that appropriate storage is provided for PPE when it is not in use. The storage 
shall be adequate to protect the PPE from contamination, loss, or damage by harmful 
substances, damp or sunlight.  Where PPE becomes contaminated during use, it shall be stored 
separately from the uncontaminated PPE in a separate area, and suitably labelled.  If the PPE 
itself contains hazardous materials, it should be sealed in an appropriate container and clearly 
labelled. 
 
Please refer to Appendix A for PPE issue.  
 
7.5 Inspection, Maintenance & Replacement of PPE 
 
PPE shall be maintained in good condition as per the manufacturer’s recommendations. The 
level of inspection or maintenance with respect to PPE shall be determined by the type of 
equipment used and the conditions in which it is used. 
 
In certain circumstances, it may be more appropriate to provide a supply of disposable PPEs 
(e.g. single use coveralls) that can simply be discarded after use.  If disposable PPE is used, it is 
important that users know when & how it should be discarded and replaced. 
 
All damaged/ expired PPEs shall be replaced.  
 
7.6 Rejection of PPE 
 
PPE that is found defective or expired or not stored in hygienic conditions should be rejected. 
Records shall be maintained by the PPE Store Keeper for rejected PPEs. 
Please refer to section 12 for the Quality Assurance & obtaining user feedback for PPE.

8. PPE SPECIFICATIONS 
The following PPE specifications are applicable: 
8.1 Head Protection 
Where likelihood of risk is falling objects, loads or overhead equipment risks, the head protection 
must be worn in all areas designated as PPE mandatory. 
GASCO shall put in place measures to reduce or control at source any risk to the head. 
All personnel entering GASCO workplaces where a risk of head injury may exist shall wear 
Safety Helmets. This includes all GASCO operational facilities and construction areas. GASCO 
will ensure suitable head protection for everyone entering these areas, including visitors. This 
requirement applies equally to all plant facilities except offices, accommodation, messing or 
recreational areas, where there is no risk to personnel.  
Head protection should fit properly and be comfortable.  Personnel requiring head protection 
should be provided with instruction on its use and maintenance including, where appropriate, 
compatibility with other types of PPEs.

Inspection & Maintenance  
Head protection shall be maintained in good condition at all times.  In particular, it must:  
 Be stored, when not in use, in a safe suitable secure storage area.  It should not be 
stored in direct sunlight or in excessively hot, humid conditions. 
 Be visually inspected on regular basis for signs of damage or deterioration. 
 Have defective harness components replaced as required.  Harnesses from one design 
or make of helmet cannot normally be interchanged with those from another. 
 Have the sweatband regularly cleaned or replaced. 
Before head protection is reissued to another person (e.g. Visitors), it shall be inspected to 
ensure it is serviceable and thoroughly cleaned in accordance with the manufacturer’s 
instructions. The sweatband must always be cleaned or replaced. 
Replacement 
Exposure to heat or sunlight can make the shell go brittle.  GASCO shall consult suppliers and/or 
manufacturers data, or take specialist advice on Helmet material suitability and replacement 
frequency for the climate in UAE.  It is important to check for signs of damage before and after 
use, and if any is found, replace the Helmet. 
Head protection shall be replaced at intervals recommended by the manufacturer, irrespective of 
visible damage. It will also need replacing when the harness is damaged and cannot be replaced, 
or when the shell is damaged or it is suspected that its shock absorption or penetration 
resistance has deteriorated - for example when: 
 The shell has received a severe impact. 
 Deep scratches are visible. 
 The shell has any visible cracks. 
 Deterioration has occurred due to exposure to high temperatures/humidity. 
 Replace when UV - Indicator turns White from Red (if provided) or check for the 
manufacturing date (5 Years from the manufacturing date).

8.2 Eye & Face Protection 
Where a hazard exists such as air borne particles, flying objects during the use of lathes, grinding 
wheels, welding, chipping or the handling of chemicals or other dangerous materials, appropriate 
eye protection must be provided and worn.   
Eye protection is needed to protect against the hazards of impact (e.g. in work with power driven 
tools where chippings may fly off), splashes or liquid droplets during the handling of hazardous

8.3 Foot Protection 
All personnel entering in to Plant Operational Area shall have adequate safety foot wear. The 
type of footwear shall depends on Risk Assessment. 
GASCO shall provide instruction and training to personnel on selecting the right safety footwear 
for the job, and maintaining and replacing it as required. 
Types of Safety Footwear 
The following are examples of types of safety footwear: 
Safety Boots or Shoes are the most common type of safety footwear.  These normally have 
steel toecaps.  They may also have other safety features including slip resistant soles, steel 
midsoles and insulation against extremes of temperature. 
Rubber Boots protect against water and wet conditions and can be useful in jobs where the 
footwear needs to be washed.  They are usually made from rubber but are available in 
polyurethane and PVC, which have greater chemical resistance.  Rubber boots can be obtained 
with corrosion resistant steel toecaps, rot-proof insoles, steel midsoles, anklebone padding and 
cotton linings. Rubber boots with steel toe caps shall be used. 
Anti-Static Footwear or Static Dissipating Safety shoes (Marked as SD on the shoe as per 
ASTM F2413 standard) prevents the buildup of static electricity on the wearer.  It reduces the 
danger of igniting a flammable atmosphere and gives some protection against electric shock. 
Conductive Footwear (Marked as CD on the shoe, as per ASTM F2413 standard) also prevents 
the buildup of static electricity.  It is particularly suitable for handling sensitive components or 
substances.  It gives no protection against electric shock. 
Electric Hazard safety shoes are designed to impede (reduce significantly) the flow of electricity 
through the shoe and to ground, thereby reducing the possibility of electrocution. Electrical 
Hazard Safety Shoes will have an “EH” clearly visible on the ASTM label sewn inside the safety 
shoes. (ASTM F2413) 
Activities Requiring Safety Footwear for unrestricted/ other areas  
Safety footwear is mandatory to enter inside the restricted areas and Safety footwear shall be 
worn as per Risk assessment for all other areas. The following are examples of some activities 
involving risks to the feet and Safety footwear shall be worn in unrestricted areas also:  
Construction:  Work on building and demolition sites will usually require safety footwear to 
protect the feet from falling objects, objects dropped by the handler, tripping accidents and 
wheels running over feet; 
Mechanical and Manual Handling:  There may be a risk of objects falling on or crushing the 
front of the foot.  There may be a risk of a fall through slipping which could result in damage to 
the heel on impact; 
Electrical:  People who work where there are flammable atmospheres must wear anti-static 
footwear to help prevent ignitions due to static electricity; 
Thermal:  Work in hot conditions requires footwear with heat-resistant and insulating soles;

8.4 Hand Protection 
Hands are at risk of injury in many of the activities carried out in the industrial oil and gas sector.  
Cuts, abrasions or even amputations can occur in manual handling and construction work, while 
hands and skin are at risk of burns or skin disease where chemicals are handled.  Other dangers 
include electrical shock, and radioactive contamination.  GASCO shall put measures in place to 
protect against, or reduce these risks.  However, in certain circumstances, protective gloves will 
also need to be worn.  
Where hand protection is required, personnel must be provided with an appropriate type of safety 
glove, and given instruction on the selection, use and maintenance of the gloves.  They should 
also be trained to always wash hands after work. 
Processes & Activities Requiring Hand Protection 
The following processes and activities involve risk of injury to the hands, or are hazards for which 
hand protection may be necessary:   
Manual Handling:  Hands may be pierced by abrasive, sharp or pointed objects or damaged by 
impact when handling goods. However, when there is moving equipment, gloves should not be 
worn since the glove may become trapped in the moving equipment and draw the hand into the 
machinery. 
Construction and Outdoor Work:  Gloves can protect against disease spores that may 
seriously infect small cuts and abrasions. 
Hot and Cold Materials:  Gloves will also protect against hazards from handling hot or cold 
materials (e.g. as in cold stores) and work involving contact with naked flames or welding. 
Electricity:  Certain approved types of rubber gloves can protect against electric shock. 
Chemical:  There are many tasks where the hands may come into contact with toxic or corrosive 
substances. Gloves that are correctly selected and used can provide a barrier between the 
wearer's skin and the harmful substance, preventing local damage, or in some cases absorption 
through the skin. 
Radioactivity:  Danger from contamination when handling radioactive materials.  Impervious 
gloves can act as a barrier to radioactive liquids and dusts gaining access to the skin and from 
there into the body by ingestion.  They can also directly block alpha radiation.

Selecting Suitable Hand Protection 
Gloves or other hand protection should be capable of giving protection from hazards, be 
comfortable and fit the wearer well.  The choice should be made on the basis of compatibility with

When selecting gloves for chemical protection, reference shall also be made to chemical 
permeation and resistance data provided by manufacturers. 
The key features of safety gloves and their protective properties are: 
Penetration and Abrasion:  Gloves made from chain-mail or leather protect against penetration 
and abrasion.  Gloves made from knitted Kevlar will provide protection against cuts and gloves 
manufactured from Kevlar needle felt give good puncture resistance. 
Thermal Protection:  Depending upon their weight and construction, terrycloth gloves will 
provide protection against heat and cold.  Gloves manufactured from materials such as Kevlar, 
glass fiber and leather can be used to provide protection at higher temperatures. 
Fire Resistance:  Chromed leather gloves are fire retardant; (Only for a short period) 
Chemicals Protection:  Chemical protective gloves are available in a range of materials 
including natural rubber, neoprene, nitrile, butyl, PVA (Polyvinyl Acetate), PVC and Viton.  The 
degree of protection against chemical permeation depends on the glove material, its thickness 
and method of construction. Appropriate chemically resistant gloves must be worn when handling 
potentially harmful chemicals. 
General Use Gloves:  Rubber, plastic or knit fabric gloves are flexible, resist cuts and abrasions, 
repel liquids and offer a good grip.  Rubber gloves allow a sensitive touch and give a firm grip in 
water or wet conditions.  Cotton knit, leather or other general-purpose gloves are suitable for 
most other jobs.  General use gloves should only be used to protect against minimal (low risk) 
risks to health and safety. 
Absorption of Vibration: for the absorption of vibration due to direct hand contact with

8.5 Protective Clothing for the Body 
Where the hazard is fire, explosion, physical, chemical, thermal, electrical, sharp object 
penetration to the body etc. the required protective clothing shall be provided.  
Employees/ Contractors/ Vendors/ Visitors who have to work or visit in Restricted Pipeline/ Plant 
areas (as per PTW requirements) shall wear long sleeve fire retardant coveralls and Fire 
retardant Winter Jacket (during winter as required) that protect them against flash fires. This 
includes field based operation and maintenance crew and emergency responders.  
All other employees and contractors who are not working or visiting the Restricted Pipeline/ Plant 
areas (as per PTW requirements) can be provided with single piece 100% cotton coverall, based 
on Risk Assessment. 
Coveralls shall not contain polyester material due to static electricity properties. The coveralls 
shall provide full body cover including arms, legs and body. Loose clothing shall not be allowed 
particularly while working with rotating machines and at heights.  
 
Full protective suits fabricated from PVC, neoprene or rubber area shall protect against toxic 
vapours, liquids or toxic dusts and should be worn in conjunction with suitable gloves or gauntlets 
and footwear.  
There are many different types of protective clothing, including coveralls or aprons to protect 
against chemical splashes and spills, high visibility clothing for personnel working in areas where 
they must be seen to be safe, and life-jackets and buoyancy aids for work at sea.  Selection 
depends on the type of job being done, and the level of risk. 
Instruction and training shall be given on circumstances in which protective clothing should be 
worn, as well as on appropriate selection and maintenance.  It should be emphasized that 
personnel are compromising their own safety if they do not wear appropriate protective clothing 
in particular circumstances. 
Contractors (general workers) working in Major Shut Down, where the risk of fire is low may wear 
cotton coverall, if agreed in the Site Risk Assessment.

8.6 Hearing Protection 
Where noise is at a level in excess of 85dBA (8 hour TWA: Time Weighted Average), ear 
protection must be worn. Such protection shall meet ANSI S3:1974/ BS EN 352 criterion. The 
hearing protection can either be mounted on helmets or may be independent. 
While controlling noise at source is the best approach to minimizing noise exposure, effective 
hearing protection may also be required. GASCO shall provide hearing protection to personnel 
employed in facilities with high noise level environments, in accordance with the ADNOC Code of 
Practice on Occupational Health Risk Management: Physical Agents and the ADNOC Guideline 
on Hearing Conservation and Noise Control.  
Where personnel experience a daily personal noise exposure of greater than 100 dB (A), then 
double Hearing protection shall be provided (Ear plug +Ear defender).  Where area noise 
surveys indicate noise levels of greater than 85dB (A) - (8 hour-TWA), signs shall be posted to 
indicate that each high noise area has been designated as Hearing Protection Zone, and hearing 
protection should be worn in these areas.  Personnel working in a Hearing Protection Zone, or in 
an area where there is a likelihood of very loud impulsive noise, shall be provided with hearing 
protection and trained to use it. 
Information, instruction and training on the use of hearing protection shall include: 
 The duty of personnel to wear appropriate hearing protection in high noise level 
environments. 
 The damage that can occur to hearing through noise exposure. 
 How to fit hearing protection properly, and use it with other PPE. 
 The importance of using hearing protection at all times, so that its effectiveness is not 
compromised. 
 The reduction in effectiveness caused by long hair, earrings, hats etc., particularly if 
these become caught between the seal of an earmuff and the head. 
 
Types of Hearing Protection 
Hearing protection includes:  
 Earplugs which fit into the ear canal. 
 Semi-inserts or canal caps which cover over the entrance to the ear canal. 
 Earmuffs which completely cover the ear. 
 Enclosures (entire head). 
In a Hearing Protection Zone, hearing protectors shall be worn by anyone entering the area, 
whether or not they are undertaking work there.

Selecting Suitable Hearing Protection  
The selection of hearing protection depends on the level and type of noise.  The daily personal 
noise exposure of personnel must be reduced to below 85dB (A) Leq.   
Adequate/sufficient Noise Reduction Rating (NRR) of Hearing Protection devices shall be 
selected reference to level of personnel exposure. Subject Matter Expert (SME) advice (e.g. 
Industrial Hygienist) may be required for selection of appropriate NRR for particular exposure. 
Special consideration for Firefighter helmets shall be given while selection due to the design of 
these items as Ear muffs will not fit. 
Hearing protection should be hygienic, and should not interfere with other PPE.  For example, 
ear plugs should be chosen in circumstances where another item of PPE, such as such as safety 
glasses, would interfere with the seal of ear muffs and consequently reduce their effectiveness.  
Comfort is an important consideration.  Where possible, personnel should be offered a choice of 
appropriate hearing protection so they may select which type suits them best.  Disposable 
hearing protection may be preferable to re-usable in certain circumstances.

8.7 Fall Protection  
Work above 1.8 metre height require a fall protection. Only full body type safety harness to be 
used and body belts are not allowed as a part of a personal fall arrest system. Activities such as 
erection/ dismantling of scaffolding require to wear suitable personal fall protection (e.g., fall 
arrestor, safety harness), based on Activity Risk Assessment.  
Maximum deceleration distance for the shock absorber of safety harness shall be 3.5 feet (1.07 
meter).  
Personal fall arrest systems and components subjected to impact loading shall be immediately 
removed from service and shall not be used again for employee protection until inspected and 
determined by a competent person to be undamaged and suitable for reuse. Personal fall arrest 
systems shall be inspected prior to each use for wear, damage and other deterioration, and 
defective components shall be removed from service. 3rd party inspection shall be carried out in 
every 6 months and colour coded. Personal fall arrest systems shall not be attached to guardrail 
systems. When a personal fall arrest system is used at hoist areas, it shall be rigged to allow the 
movement of the employee only as far as the edge of the walking/working surface. 
Lanyards and vertical lifelines shall have a minimum breaking strength of 5,000 pounds (22.2 
kN). Personal fall arrest systems, when stopping a fall, shall limit maximum arresting force on an 
employee to 1,800 pounds (8 kN) when used with a body harness and shall be rigged such that 
an employee can neither free fall more than 6 feet (1.8 m), nor contact any lower level.

8.8 Respiratory Protective Equipment (RPE) 
Respiratory Protective Equipment (RPE) is designed to prevent or limit the exposure of persons 
to dangerous substances that may be inhaled. RPE should only be used when it is not 
reasonably practicable to control exposures to hazardous substances by other means, e.g. 
where work is to be carried out in a confined space, or during emergency conditions e.g. high 
H2S environments. 
GASCO shall select emergency RPE so that it is suitable for each circumstance, based on the 
Activity Risk Assessment.  Equipment shall be stored, maintained and used correctly, including 
provision of appropriate training for those who may need to use or maintain it. 
RPE equipment for emergency response falls within the definition of equipment requiring a 
performance standard.  In such cases, equipment shall be selected that fulfils the requirements 
of the performance standard.  Most manufacturers and suppliers of RPE provide information on 
the performance and use of their systems.  
All RPEs shall be CE marked and as a minimum the RPE must: 
 Reduce the wearer's exposure to toxic gases or hazardous particulates to as low as 
reasonably practicable, during escape from the hazard or during the work in the 
specified environment. 
 Provide clean air at a suitable rate during the whole period it is worn. 
 Fit the wearer correctly, especially the face-piece. 
 Be properly stored and maintained. 
 Not introduce additional hazards that might put the user's health and safety at risk. 
When developing performance standards, selecting or developing written schemes of 
examination for emergency RPE, GASCO shall take account of: 
 The type of hazardous material or materials to be protected against. 
 The maximum and likely concentration of toxic gas or particulates.  For example, 
asbestos fibre concentrations during uncontrolled stripping operations can exceed the 
level of protection provided by any commercially available RPE. 
 The need to protect eyes or other parts of the body, as well as respiration. 
 Sealing of the RPE. 
 The effect of facial hair on PPE performance and the requirement for and 
implementation of, a facial hair policy. 
 Use of qualitative or when necessary quantitative face fit testing. 
 Whether the equipment can be worn with glasses or jewellery. 
 The potential for oxygen deficiency when in use.

8.8.1 Types of Respiratory Protective Equipment 
There are three types of Respiratory Protective Equipment (RPE): 
 Atmosphere or Air Supplying Devices (e.g.: SCBA, Airline BA etc.).  
 Air Purifying Respirators (e.g. Mechanical filter cartridge, Chemical filter cartridge, Gas 
masks, PAPR - Powered Air Purifying Respirator etc.).  
 Combination of Air Purifying and Air Supplying. 
 
The Decision to Use RPE 
The control of exposure to substances hazardous to health should always, wherever possible, be 
achieved by means other than personal protective equipment (PPE). Where this is not 
practicable and residual inhalation exposure risks exist, suitable RPE shall be provided. 
The decision to use RPE needs to be based on outcome of the exposure risk assessment. On 
the basis of Risk Assessment, it can be decided which types of RPE are likely to provide the 
required level of protection. The decision on what type of RPE to be used for a particular 
circumstance shall be made by a suitably competent person, such as an Occupational Hygienist 
or a safety professional.

8.8.2 Fit Test 
People come in all shapes and sizes, so one particular size or type of RPE is unlikely to suit 
everyone.  The performance of face-pieces depends on achieving a good contact between the 
wearer's skin and the mask face seal.  Quantitative fit testing shall be carried out to ensure a 
good fit. The fit test is required for tight fitting type face masks. Fit test is not required for loose 
fitting type face pieces (Reference: HSE OC 282/ 28, www.hse.gov.uk). But, Loose-fitting face 
pieces (hoods, helmets, visors, suits etc.) cannot be used in negative pressure equipment that 
relies on the wearer’s lungpower to draw in air. 
 
All personnel who are required to use Respiratory protection (e.g. SCBA & SABA) for Hydrogen 
Sulphide atmospheres, including use of an emergency escape mask during an emergency 
must pass a fit test for all face masks types they may be using. The fit test must: 
 
Be carried out by a competent person.  
 
Quantitatively measure the fit factor must be greater than 2000:1 to a register 
pass. 
The quantitative fit test shall be carried out according to the appropriate European or equivalent 
standard for the relevant equipment such as: 
 EN 136:1998 Respiratory Protective Devices: Full-face Masks. 
 EN 140:1999 Respiratory Protective Devices: Half Masks and Quarter Masks. 
 EN 149:2001+ A1:2009 Respiratory Protective Devices: Filtering Half-masks to Protect 
against Particles. 
 EN 405:2001+A1:2009 Respiratory Protective Devices: Valve Filtering half-masks to 
protect against Gases or Gases and Particles. 
Repeat quantitative fit testing will be required if changing to a different model of RPE, or to a 
different sized face-piece, or there have been significant changes to the facial characteristics of 
the wearer, such as significant weight gain or loss. 
All emergency RPE shall be checked to see that it is clean and in good working order both prior 
to use and before it is returned to storage.  In particular check: 
 The condition of the head harness and the face-piece including seal and visor. 
 The condition of the inhalation and exhalation valves where fitted. 
 The condition of any threaded connections or seals. 
 The condition and type of filter(s); that they are "in-date" and fitted properly. 
 The airflow rate for power-assisted and powered respirators compared with the 
manufacturer's specifications. 
 Whether the RPE is complete and correctly assembled. 
 Additional tests in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

8.8.5 RPE Assessment for Facial Hair Suitability  
In selecting RPE for use by wearers with facial hair, four specific criteria must be met to assure 
the RPE is suitable: 
 
 Manufacturer formally recommends the RPE as suitable for use by wearers with facial 
hair. 
 The RPE bears the CE mark and has been tested against suitable BS EN certifications 
for the RPE design & operability for clean shaven wearers. 
 Manufacturer conduct tests as per ISO 16900-1: 2014 standard, using an accredited test 
centre. Test subjects must have facial hair as specified in below (RPE Inward Leakage 
Test) and that the results are verified by an independent party. 
 Manufacturer conduct additional Total Inward leakage laboratory test using H2S 
concentration limits specified by GASCO and that the results are verified by an 
independent party. 
 
Manufacturers promoting RPE as suitable for use by wearers with facial hair shall provide formal 
documentation to confirm that they are recommending the RPE as suitable for use with facial hair 
and that they have met all required testing criteria.  
RPE Inward Leakage Test 
The criterion for testing inward leakage shall be set against a range of facial hair growth length, 
including 1 and 3 day stubble, up to full beard of up to and no more than 20cm in length from the 
base of the chin. 
The most up to date standard (ISO16900-1), shall be used in the testing of RPE for facial hair 
wearers. In addition to the exercises stipulated in the testing standard further workplace type 
movements exercises shall be requested during the inward leakage test.  
The exercises requested must be designed to simulate the work activities of RPE users. These 
additional exercises will assist in determining the optimum performance of the RPE in the 
workplace in the form of a Simulated Workplace Protection Factor (SWPF).

temperatures of 5000 degrees F or greater in less than a second, creating an explosion 
equivalent to several sticks of dynamite. This can easily damage eyesight if Arc flash safety 
glasses are not used. 
Specialized safety eyewear, goggles and face shields are required to protect workers from arc 
flash, with the requirement of arc rating specific to the hazard present. The eye and face 
protection is required for all levels of arc flash hazards while entering into the Arc Flash 
Protection Boundary (AFPB) of any switchgear in a switchgear/substation room in order to 
perform any electrical task.

Two types of gloves are now commonly available for arc flash protection.

9. PPE FOR EMERGENCY PURPOSE 
9.1 Types of PPE for Emergency Response Activities 
PPE may be required to enable personnel to successfully escape the effects of an incident or to 
otherwise safely respond to an incident, including carrying out search and rescue activities.  Such 
PPE falls into one of the following four categories: 
 Devices to allow personnel to escape from toxic hazards. (Please refer RPE, 
Section:8.8) 
 Devices to allow personnel to undertake search and rescue and other necessary 
emergency response activity safely, where there is the potential for toxic hazard, 
including from combustion products. (Please refer RPE, Section: 8.8) 
 Clothing to protect personnel from the effects of fire during emergency response 
activities. (Please refer PPE for Fire Fighting, section 8.10) 
 Buoyancy aids (i.e. Life jackets for use in Ruwais). A lifejacket is a personal safety 
device, which shall be selected so that, when fully inflated (if an inflatable type), it 
provides sufficient buoyancy to turn and support an unconscious person face upwards 
within five seconds (ten seconds if automatically inflated).  The person's head should be 
supported with the mouth and nose well clear of the water. Other types of buoyancy aids 
can assist a conscious person in staying afloat.  However, they will not turn over an 
unconscious person from a face down position.  
 
9.2 Performance of Emergency Response PPE 
The ADNOC Code of Practice on ‘Identification and Integrity Assurance of HSE Critical 
Equipment and Systems’ requires ADNOC Group Companies to draw up performance standards 
for defined systems.  Emergency response PPE falls within this category and must have 
performance standards set.  
Emergency response PPE mentioned this section (Section 9.1) must be checked on a regular 
basis to ensure that the equipment is available and will perform as required. This must be 
accomplished and verified using a suitable written scheme of examination, as required by the 
ADNOC Code of Practice.
