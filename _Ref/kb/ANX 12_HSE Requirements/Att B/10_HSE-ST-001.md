 1.
 INTRODUCTION
Waste is defined as any material - solid, liquid or mixture - that is surplus to 
requirements. This GASCO STANDARD is consistent with the relevant ADNOC CoP 
V2-05 on Waste Management. This standard is intended to guide the application of 
standardized waste management practices in all GASCO facilities. 2.
 PURPOSE
The purpose of this STANDARD is to enable the generators of waste streams in 
GASCO to implement controlled disposal in compliance with ADNOC Codes of 
Practice.  
These goals provide the basis for an effective waste management process for each 
GASCO facility: 
 
Ensure compliance with the requirements of UAE Laws and Regulations 
 
Comply with ADNOC CoP‟s 
 
Progressively reduce waste, with particular attention to materials which could 
have an adverse impact on the environment 
 
Establish, implement and maintain a waste segregation scheme to facilitate reuse 
or recycling of waste materials 
 
Ensure that GASCO staff and contractors handle and dispose of waste in 
accordance with this STANDARD. 3.
 SCOPE AND APPLICATION
The standard is applicable to all Plants and Pipeline Divisions as well as Head 
Quarter and Al Rimal Club, where waste is generated. This includes normal 
operation, commissioning, maintenance and shutdown activities. This is also 
applicable to project activities executed by Contractors. 4.

 DOCUMENTCHANGE / CONTROL
The custodian of this document is GASCO Vice President HSE Division [VP (HSE)]. 
This document shall be reviewed by HSE Division as and when required but not less 
than once in three years.  
For suggesting any changes to this document, the „Document Change / Initiation 
Request‟ (DC/IR) Form shall be completed by the concerned party and provided to 
HSE Division. Amendment of document shall be agreed by VP (HSE). 
Amendment of this document shall be proposed by filling out DC/IR form and sending 
it to VP (HSE). 6.
 ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
It is the responsibility of the GASCO Operating Plant, Pipelines Division, Major 
Project or Technical Project Manager to implement a Waste Management Procedure 
(WMP) that complies with this STANDARD. The General Services (GS) division is 
responsible for the implementation of this standard to GASCO Head Quarter and Al 
Rimal Club waste management. 
This STANDARD is applicable to Contracting Companies working on behalf of 
GASCO and using the GASCO Health Safety and Environmental Management 
System (HSE MS).  Where a Contractor is using his own HSE MS, he is required to 
develop Waste Management Standard consistent with this STANDARD. 
In the case of Projects, GASCO Project Manager, or Technical Project Manager must 
ensure that this STANDARD (or the Contactor Standard) is implemented throughout 
the duration of the Project. 
6.1 
Reporting 
All waste generators must report an inventory of the waste they generate to HSE/E 
on a monthly basis through their monthly environmental performance reporting (refer 
section 7.1.1 for waste inventory). in addition, all waste generators must plan and 
implement a realistic long-term (5 years) waste reduction plan. HSE/E is to be 
updated on the status of the plan on an annual basis. 
It is the responsibility of HSE/E to compile, verify and submit all waste data annually 
to ADNOC through the Sustainability Performance Letter. 
In order to fulfill the requirements of BeAAT Long Term notifications, all waste 
generators must provide expected hazardous waste volume to HSE/E at least six 
months in advance to facilitate appropriate review, compilation and submission.

6.2 
Auditing 
A level 2 waste audit is performed by HQ HSE division once every two years to all 
waste generating facilities. The purpose of the audit is to identify areas for 
improvement and to ensure compliance with GASCO Waste Management Standard. 
It is expected that waste generating facilities will comply with the recommendations 
derived from the audit findings and close all action items identified. For effective 
waste management system, waste generating facilities ought to undertake a more 
frequent periodic internal audit as per applicable management systems (e.g. ISO 
14001: 2004). 7.

7.1.1 Inventorization  
In order to fulfill the requirements of Sustainability Performance Letter, waste 
inventory including type of waste generated, point of generation, classification and 
the quantities must be produced and submitted to HQ on a monthly basis. All 
GASCO Sites/Facilities/Projects must prepare a waste inventory to form the baseline 
from which improvements can be planned and future waste management 
performance assessed. The format for waste inventory is prescribed in Appendix 3 of 
ADNOC COP V2-05 and reproduced in Appendix-A. 
7.1.2 Classification  
In accordance with ADNOC COP V2-05, waste is classified into three categories.  
1) 
Domestic and municipal waste 
2) 
Non-hazardous industrial and commercial waste 
3) 
Hazardous waste 
 
Radioactive waste (a special category of hazardous waste) 
 
Medical Waste (a special category of hazardous waste) 
 
Domestic and Municipal Waste 
This includes all wastes which have chemical and physical characteristics similar to 
those of household waste such as garbage, paper, cardboard, plastic, cans, etc. 
Disposal of such waste is usually through a local municipal facility or by recycling. 
Non-Hazardous Industrial and Commercial Waste 
This includes any non-hazardous wastes that have physical and/or chemical 
characteristics, which are different from domestic waste such as construction wastes, 
glass, scrap metal, wood, used containers, tires etc. These materials generally pose 
little risk to the environment and can be disposed of at a local municipal facility after 
waste minimization options have been exhausted. 
Hazardous Waste 
GASCO defines hazardous waste in accordance with the Basel Convention as any 
waste that exhibits the characteristics listed below:   
 
Explosive 
 
Radioactive (including Natural Occurring Radioactive Material (NORM) or Low 
Specific Activity (LSA) scale) 
 
Ignitable or flammable liquids and solids 
 
Substances liable to spontaneous combustion 
 
Substances, which by interaction with water, are liable to become spontaneously 
flammable or give off flammable gasses 
 
Poisons with acute and chronic (delayed) toxicity 
 
Substances and preparations which release toxic gases, including those that are 
harmless on their own but release toxic gases upon contact with air or water

Radioactive Waste 
The management and control of radioactive waste must be in compliance with the 
requirements of Federal Law No (1) of 2002 „Regarding Organization & Monitoring of 
the Use of Radiation Resources and Protection‟ (3.2). This law prohibits the import, 
export, storage movement or disposal of radioactive wastes without prior consent by 
the Competent Authority.  
Medical Waste 
Medical waste classification and disposal requirements are defined in the UAE 
Regulation for Handling Materials, Hazardous Wastes and Medical Wastes, 2001 
(3.1).  This Regulation defines bio-hazardous waste from clinical source as follows: 
 
Any wastes made in whole or part of human tissue, animal tissue, blood or other 
body liquids, secretions, drugs or other pharmaceutical products, bandages, 
syringes, needles or other medical sharp objects. 
 
Any other wastes whether contagious, chemical or radioactive produced by 
medical activities, nursing, treatment, medical care, dental, veterinary or 
pharmaceutical or processed activities or others, tests, research works or study 
materials or sampling or storage of the same. 
7.1.3 Segregation 
This is the physical separation of waste materials according to their general physical 
and chemical nature. This is important from a safety point of view. Segregation also 
allows wastes of a similar type to be combined, thereby simplifying treatment and 
disposal arrangements. The principle of segregation is applicable to both hazardous 
and non-hazardous waste. Refer to ADNOC CoP for waste classifications. 
Failure to segregate waste could lead to cross contamination of hazard into the non-
hazardous waste streams resulting in costly remedial treatment. If hazardous waste 
is mixed with a non-hazardous waste, the resultant mixture waste must be 
considered hazardous and disposed off accordingly. 
Specific segregation requirements are listed in UAE Regulation for Handling 
Hazardous Materials, Hazardous Wastes and Medical Wastes, 2001 (3.1) and all 
GASCO facilities and their Contractors must ensure full compliance with this 
Regulation. The segregation requirement matrix for hazardous materials as provided 
in Schedule 1.3 of the Act is presented in Appendix-B and should be followed for 
segregation of hazardous wastes.

7.1.4 Minimization  
Each operating plant must strive to eliminate or minimize the volume of waste it 
generates and set a realistic target to achieve it. The principle of waste minimization 
is central to good waste management practice and must be incorporated into all 
aspects of plant operations. 
All waste generating facilities must develop a realistic waste reduction plan with 
specific targets (e.g. percentage reduction in total waste generation per unit 
production, percentage waste reused/ recycled, etc.)   
7.1.5 Treatment of Residue(s) 
Potential treatment options to destroy, detoxify and/or neutralize waste residues 
include processes such as: 
 
physical methods e.g. gravity separation, filtration, centrifugation, soil washing, 
solidification, stabilization, encapsulation. 
 
biological methods e.g. waste water treatment, composting, tank-based reactors, 
land farming via bio-remediation and/or phytoremediation. 
 
thermal methods e.g. destruction via incineration, burning, thermal desorption, 
detoxification. 
 
chemical reaction e.g. neutralization, stabilization, extraction, precipitation. 
7.1.6 Storage and Transport  
Waste materials generated from operational or other activities will normally require 
some form of temporary storage, pending reuse/recycling or transport to final 
disposal location. GASCO must ensure that suitable and sufficient temporary storage 
is provided to accommodate waste from its operations. The maximum retention 
time for temporary storage of a waste generated shall be 90 days. 
Waste at the temporary holding facility must be labeled properly to avoid confusions 
and accidents. Also, wherever feasible, original container of the material shall be 
used to store the waste. For labeling requirement refer to Appendix-C. 
Operating plants and Pipelines Division must provide a designated and fenced 
temporary waste storage area where wastes are stored pending transport to final 
disposal location.  
If Contractors are used to transport the waste, they must be licensed as Waste 
Carriers within the terms of the Regulation for Handling Hazardous Materials, 
Hazardous Wastes and Medical Wastes and comply with all relevant license 
conditions.  Similarly, if GASCO employees/vehicles are used to transport hazardous 
waste, they must comply fully with the requirements of these Regulations.  
All transport of Hazardous Waste must be covered under the Waste Transfer Note 
and be in accordance with the requirements of the BeAAT Waste Transfer Protocol 
(3.8). 
All hazardous wastes must be properly packed and labeled as specified in BeAAT 
Waste Transfer Protocol. For all other wastes, the packaging must be consistent with 
the nature of wastes. The labeling of waste should be as specified in Appendix C.

7.1.7 Disposal  
Disposal must be seen as the last resort and should be practiced only when the 
hierarchy of waste management methods has been fully evaluated (see Figure 1).  
All operating sites and Pipelines Division should be up-to-date on identification of the 
alternative disposal routes, consider dealing with organizations which are specialist in 
waste handling and make the open burning (incinerators) the last choice for waste 
disposal. Open burning is the worst disposal options and must be avoided where 
feasible. 
Any waste not sent to BeAAT, should be disposed off with transfer note. It is the duty 
of the approved waste transporter to obtain the final waste disposal certificate issued 
by the authorized receiving facility. A template of such certificate is presented in 
Appendix E. 
Hazardous waste generated in GASCO can only be disposed of at BeAAT facility in 
accordance with BeAAT Waste Transfer Protocol 3430-TEC-OM-001. Analysis of the 
waste must be conducted prior to sending it to BeAAT as per the Protocol. The 
approved waste transporter must obtain a copy of the consignment note with due 
acknowledgement of receipt by BeAAT. 8.
 WASTE MANAGEMENT PROCEDURES
All GASCO Sites/Facilities/Projects must prepare a Waste Management Procedure 
(WMP), which is consistent with the scheme defined above, and the nature and 
quantities of waste produced. The GS division should ensure appropriate WMPs 
exist for the management of waste generated at the HQ and Al Rimal Club.  
The WMP must take into account the specific environmental sensitivities of the area 
in which each Site/Facility/Project is located.  The approach to be adopted whilst 
preparing a WMP is outlined below. 
Step 1: Management Leadership and Commitment. The WMP must demonstrate 
visible management commitment and support for the WMP. Overall waste 
minimization goals and objectives must be defined. 
Minutes of Waste Management toolbox talks should be prepared and circulated to all 
site employees.  
Step 2: Roles and Responsibilities.  
HSE site representative shall:  
 
Ensure that all waste management activities carried out on site are in 
compliance with this standard and site specific Waste Management 
Procedure. 
 
Ensure that the procedure is kept current as specified in this standard and in 
the document control page. 
 
Identify ways of preventing and reducing waste production. 
 
Develop and implement a realistic waste reduction plan.

Step 3: Scope of the Procedure. The area covered by the WMP must be defined 
and the business(es) participating in it detailed. In defining the scope, consideration 
must be given to the existing environmental conditions and sensitivities. 
Step 4: Waste Inventorization and Classification. Each Site/Facility/ Project must 
identify all waste streams generated in the area from all sources. Each waste stream 
must be defined in terms of its classification, quantity produced and source. The 
WMP should classify waste as detailed in section 7.1.2. This will become the waste 
inventory. The physical, chemical and toxicological properties of the waste must also 
be recorded. The sources of all waste streams must be identified on a plot-plan of the 
facility. 
A centralized location should be made for maintaining records of waste generated 
and disposed off through various means e.g. recycling, reuse, treatment, disposal, 
etc. 
Step 5: Duty of Care. It is important that all GASCO operating plants and Pipelines 
Division follow the Duty of Care Regulations. 
These are mandatory and their requirements include: 
 
A system of signed Waste Transfer Notes.  
 
All those handling waste to keep records of waste received and transferred. 
 
The waste producer to describe the waste, the waste carrier and disposal site 
to be licensed to carry and accept the specific waste. 
Information is provided in the Waste Transfer Notes by the waste producer, carrier 
and disposal site. For details on the requirement of GASCO Waste Transfer Note, 
refer to ADNOC-COPV2-05, Waste Management or BeAAT Waste Transfer Protocol 
for all hazardous waste disposals. 
Step 6: Evaluation of Management and Disposal Options. For each waste stream, 
a list of potential management options must be compiled. Factors which must be 
taken into account in considering options include: environmental issues, location, 
engineering feasibility, economics and potential long term liability. For some types of 
waste (e.g. PCB, radioactive material), it may be necessary to consider long-term 
storage in specialized facilities if no other viable alternative exists. GASCO phased 
out the use of transformer oil containing PCBs and replaced them with synthetic oil, 
free of PCB traces. 
Step 7: Waste Minimization. Opportunities for waste elimination or minimization 
must be examined for each waste stream. 
Step 8: Selection of Preferred Option(s). Once the list of management options has 
been compiled, the preferred option for each waste stream can be selected. This 
must be based on a life cycle approach, which takes into account the HSE risks 
associated with storage, transport, treatment and disposal. 
Step 9: Procedure Implementation. The information collected in the above steps 
must be used to prepare the WMP. This WMP must be a concise document, suitable 
for use by personnel on the Site/Facility/Project. It must contain all relevant 
information on the preferred disposal option(s).

Step 10: Procedure Review and Update. Effective waste management is an 
ongoing process. The WMP must be reviewed annually (at least) to ensure that it is
