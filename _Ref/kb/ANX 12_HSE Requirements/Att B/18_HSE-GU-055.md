1. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
The term Pre-Start-Up Safety Review (PSSR) first received importance in the process 
industries with the introduction of the Process Safety Management (PSM) regulations, requires 
companies to conduct Pre-Start-Up safety reviews. 
 
It has been shown that as many as 60-70% of all incidents occur at start-up and shutdown. 
 
Pre-Start-Up Review is team based activity and an essential part of any process safety 
management program. That provide a last chance and a breathing space for everyone 
associated with a project or involved in operating modified facility, an opportunity to make sure 
that no unsafe acts or conditions have slipped through and the facility or equipment that they 
are about to start is safe and operable. 
 
Ultimately, PSSR will give plant managers the authority to refuse to accept responsibility for a 
plant or item of equipment that they judge to be unsafe. 
 
It shall be stressed here due care must be given to the PSSR activity. The temptation to bounce or 
carry out too quickly this important step must be avoided. 
 
 
2. 
PURPOSE 
 
The fundamental purpose of a Pre-Start-Up safety review (PSSR) is to help identify and ensure 
that the facility or any changes made to a facility or item of equipment meet the original design 
or operating intent.  
 
Neither the PSSR does aim to identify new hazards, nor is a last-minute hazards analysis. 
 
The PSSR shall confirm prior to the introduction a new hazardous materials or a modification 
to the current hazardous materials inventory to a process that: 
 
1. Construction of equipment is in accordance with design specifications; 
2. Safety, operating, maintenance and emergency procedure are in place and adequate. 
3. Appropriate safety reviews during engineering phase such as HAZID, ENVID, OHID, 
HAZOP, SIL, 3DModel review, HSEIA, SIMOPs, and PHSER have been carried out 
and recommendations and actions raised have been completed. 
4. Any modifications during construction phase have been properly controlled and 
documented. 
5. For modified processes, the Management of Change requirements have been met; 
contained in management of change process. 
 
6. Training of each operator and maintenance personnel involved in the operating process 
has been properly completed before starting.

3. 
SCOPE AND APPLICATION 
 
A formal PSSR shall be conducted for all projects, new facilities and for modified facilities when 
the modification requires a change in the process safety information (PCR modifications, In-
house changes, Vendor package changes, prior to restart after a major outage, prior to restart 
post an incident, Software changes in DCS/PLC etc...). 
 
For Plant Change Management (PCM), PSSR is required to be completed for any situation 
whereby the work that was completed has impacted the process safety information for the unit. 
Unless they have been exempted (refer to section 8), the PCM implementation focal point shall 
determine after coordinating with relevant disciplines whether a PSSR is needed or not.  
 
It should be noted that the Pre-Start-Up safety review is a verification check, independent of 
the Plant Change Management (PCM) process.

5. 
GENERAL 
 
Because of its role in creating the concept of prestart up safety reviews in the process 
industries, the OSHA process safety management (PSM) regulation standard requires that 
construction and equipment is in accordance with design specifications and that the facility’s 
procedures reflect the manner in which the facility is to be operated after the completion of the 
project or the process changes have been made. The PSSR shall check that safety, operating 
and emergency procedures for the new operation have been written down, and that they 
accurately describe what has to be done.

During the pressure of construction, there is sometimes a tendency to postpone some of the 
Hazard identification recommendations until ‘there is sufficient time’, or because such projects 
all too often fall behind schedule and/or run over budget. Consequently, creating pressure on 
the project team to eliminate or postpone the installation of any items that are not absolutely 
necessary for the start-up. If not controlled properly, this can lead to corner-cutting ⎯ either 
intentional or inadvertent ⎯ which may then jeopardize safety. 
 
It is required that new/modified facilities conduct a process hazards analysis such as HAZID, 
HAZOP…the PSSR team shall check that the Hazard identification was in fact carried out, and 
that its recommendations have, in fact, either resolved or implemented properly.  
 
Furthermore, PSSR is needed whenever process safety information is changed. Since virtually 
all changes result in updates to the facility documentation, particularly P&IDs, the effect of this 
requirement means that virtually all changes will have to be reviewed by a PSSR. 
5.1 
Plant Change Management (PCM) and PSSR 
 
Changes to a process must be thoroughly evaluated to fully assess their impact on HSE and 
integrity to determine the needed changes to operating procedures and practices. The 
following considerations must be addressed prior to any change: 
 
The technical basis for the proposed change,  
 
Impact of the change on employee safety and health,  
 
Modifications to operating procedures,  
 
Necessary time period for the change, and  
 
Authorization requirements for the proposed change.  
 
Moreover, the personnel who operate a process and maintenance whose job tasks will be 
affected by a change in the process must be informed of, and trained in, the change prior to 
Start-up of the process or the affected part of the process.  
In this regards, for further requirement, refer to PCM procedure (OEX-PR-026 - Plant Change 
Management Procedure) where categories of changes are defined.

5.4 
PSSR and Audit 
 
A PSSR has the form of an audit, but it is not a formal compliance audit. The purpose of the 
PSSR is to make sure that all necessary process safety work has been completed prior to 
Start-up, not to check the implementation of the process safety management systems 
themselves.

7. 
PSSR REQUIREMENTS 
 
There are four major situations which would cause the facility to initiate a Pre-Start-Up safety 
review prior to the introduction of hazardous materials or energy to the process: 
1. Construction of a new facility.  A new facility would consist of a facility constructed on 
a worksite where there are no other facilities or a facility subsequently constructed 
such that it is physically separate from and otherwise independent from existing 
facilities. 
 
2. Modify an existing facility where the modifications require a change in the process 
safety information and/or where the physical equipment has been changed or 
altered. 
3. Start-up after turnarounds or unit outages. 
4. Start-up after a production unit, major piece of equipment or pipeline have been 
mothballed or out of service for an extended period. 
 
It should be highlighted that some projects that are large in scope and size, or have multiple 
phases, may require a series of Pre-Start-Up Safety Reviews.

The following situations are examples that hold the completion of a formalized PSSR prior to 
operation of the equipment: 
 
Start-up of equipment after replacement in kind. 
 
Start-up of equipment after routine maintenance has been completed and there 
has been no change to process safety information. 
 
“Replacements in kind means an item (equipment, procedure, etc.) that meets the design 
specification of the item it is replacing. This can be an identical replacement or any other 
alternative specifically provided for in the design specification, as long as the alternative does 
not in any way adversely affect the use of the item or associated items”. 
Examples are: 
 
Equipment (vessels, heat exchangers, etc.) fabricated exactly to the original design and 
specification or existing approved design and located in the same position as the original. 
 
equipment and materials that are manufactured by an approved supplier to a standard 
specification, such as bolts, gaskets, flanges, steam traps, piping, insulation, structural steel. 
 
Instrumentation (transmitters, pressure and temperature gauges, etc.) of the same 
specification and range, and purchased from an approved supplier 
 
Electric motors, fuses, and circuit breakers of the same specification from an approved 
supplier. 
 
Replacement of piping with the same size, material, flange make-up, and routing as the 
existing. 
 
Temporary change – planned for a limited duration; such as, a trial or evaluation or by-pass of 
a safety system for maintenance. 
 
It should be noted that a PSSR is not always a paper-based check. It is sometimes feasible 
to test the new or modified system in the field before actually putting it into operation.  
For instance, an interlock system was upgraded in order to prevent such unauthorized over-
rides. Part of the PSSR is to have a knowledgeable and determined operator try to override 
the new interlock while the plant is shut down in order to see if he could ‘beat the system’.

7.4 
Post Outage Restart of Existing Systems 
 
PSSR shall be conducted on equipment that has been out of service for a planned unit or 
system outage or idle for an extended period of time prior to restart.  
Such a PSSR is called a Restart Safety Review, or RSSR, and they are carried out even if 
no (intentional) changes have been made to the equipment. The following are some of the 
reasons for conducting an RSSR: 
 
1. If a plant has been shut down for a long period of time, uncontrolled changes may 
have been made to it. For example, personnel from other departments may have 
cannibalized the shutdown equipment for spare parts. 
 
2. During the shutdown period the integrity of the plant may have deteriorated. 
Equipment and piping may have corroded, rotating equipment shafts may have 
bent, seals may have become degraded, pockets of corrosive materials may have 
formed at the base of storage tanks, or instruments contacts may have covertly 
failed. The RSSR will check that all necessary maintenance has been completed. 
 
3. During the shutdown period, external factors may have changed. For example, 
the quality of the streams feeding the unit may be different, or the utilities may 
have changed properties. 
 
4. If pipeline has been mothballed for longer duration, deterioration of its physical 
properties happened as per associated degradation mechanism. For example, 
Internal and external corrosion and coating defects due to third party inferences. 
 
On restart it is quite likely that new operations and maintenance personnel will have been 
assigned to the unit during the period of the shutdown. The RSSR will check that these 
people are properly trained, and that they are supported by updated documentation.

checklist, the team must determine if the status of the item is adequate to allow the new facility or 
the modified equipment to start safely.

the PSSR, they must identify the corrective action and the individual who is responsible for 
ensuring the issues are resolved as well as the appropriate due date for the resolution of the issue. 
The team must also determine whether the issues must be resolved prior to starting the equipment, 
process or not.
