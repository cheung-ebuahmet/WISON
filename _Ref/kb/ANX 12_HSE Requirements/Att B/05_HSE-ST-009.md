1. GASCO PERMIT TO WORK SYSTEM 
1.1 GASCO HSE Policy 
The GASCO HSE Policy includes the statement that “GASCO shall conduct activities in a manner 
designed to minimise HSE risk to a level which is As Low as Reasonably Practicable (ALARP). 
Amongst its activities, GASCO requires to carry out work, which may be in Operational or 
Non-Operational Areas of Plant facilities or Pipelines.  In order to manage the potential risks associated 
with this activity to ALARP, GASCO has developed a Safe System of Work, called the “GASCO Permit to 
Work (PTW) System”.  This System is described in this Standard. 
1.2 Legal Requirements 
The United Arab Emirates Law No. 8, for year 1980, on Conservation of Petroleum Resources Chapter V, 
Articles 91 & 92, and Ministerial Order No. 32 for year 1982, Protection of Employees against 
Occupational Hazards Article-1, state that: 
“Every employer shall provide adequate preventative equipment to protect workers against the dangers 
of employment accidents and occupational diseases that may occur during the work and also against 
fire hazards that may result from the use of machines and other equipment.  He shall also adopt all 
other preventative methods ordered by the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs.” 
“Every worker shall use the protective equipment and the clothing supplied to him for this purpose, shall 
comply with all the instructions given by the employer to protect him against hazards and shall not take 
any action liable to hamper compliance with such instructions.” 
“Every employer shall display detailed instructions in a conspicuous position at the work place indicating 
the measures to be taken to prevent fire and protect the workers against the hazards to which they may 
be exposed while performing their work.” 
The GASCO PTW system is a key part of the measures implemented by GASCO to comply with these 
legal requirements. 
1.3 Objective of the Permit To Work System 
The prime objective of the GASCO Permit To Work system is to provide a safe system of work for 
activities conducted at sites, on pipelines and on construction projects. It is designed to protect both 
personnel and assets and, by doing so, also the environment and GASCO’s reputation. 
It is the means of providing written instructions and authorisation to people carrying out work which 
may be potentially hazardous. The PTW system uses documents to define work, the associated hazards 
and the precautions to be taken for those hazards, and includes a system of authorised signatories for 
the preparation of the documents. It defines the roles and responsibilities of persons in the GASCO 
Organization, and those of Contractor management, with respect to the GASCO Permit to work system 
to achieve the safety at workplace.  
Controlling exposure to occupational hazards is the fundamental method of protecting workers. 
Traditionally, a hierarchy of controls has been used as a means of determining how to implement 
feasible and effective control solutions.

1.4 Purpose and Scope 
The purpose of the GASCO PTW System is to ensure that a safe working environment is achieved by 
providing management control over the various work activities, which may be potentially hazardous. 
The PTW System provides a formal and controlled process that identifies and communicates 
hazards and risks associated with planned work and ensures that appropriate precautions 
and control measures are implemented so that the work can proceed and be completed safely. 
It is important to note that a Permit to Work is not a permission to carry out hazardous work. It is an 
essential part of a procedure that provides instructions on how to carry out any potentially hazardous 
work safely and in a managed, controlled way.  
The PTW System has the following key features: 
 
It allows personnel to be aware of the potentially hazardous activities being undertaken under 
PTW by providing a systematic overview. The specific arrangement for the display of Permits 
allows identification of potentially conflicting work tasks, both on a geographical and time 
basis. 
 
It defines the controls required to prevent threats from releasing hazards during the work as 
well, as the recovery controls that will mitigate the effects should a hazard be released. 
 
It sets limits to the duration and extent of the work.

1.5 Custodianship of the PTW System 
The Principle Custodian, responsible for authorising all changes to the GASCO PTW System, is the Chief 
Executive Officer (CEO). 
The Custodian responsible for the development of the PTW system and the co-ordination across 
GASCO, is the HSE Vice-President (VP-HSE). 
Custodians responsible for the implementation of the PTW system, are the Plant and Pipeline 
Vice-Presidents (Asset Owners). 
1.6 Deviations from the PTW System 
Deviation from the PTW System may be warranted in special circumstances, but the safety of 
personnel, the protection of the environment and assets must not be jeopardised. In such potential 
cases, a dedicated risk assessment must be performed to evaluate the risk and put in place adequate 
controls to manage this risk. If anybody involved in the PTW System is unsure of a course of action, he 
must consult a higher authority and not take risks. 
Any planned deviations from the PTW System must be requested by the Plant or Pipeline Vice-
President, in writing, to the GASCO Technical Authority (Operations HSE Department Head) for review. 
Approved deviations are to be issued as a Supplementary PTW procedure, endorsed by VP- HSE and 
authorised by CEO and approved for issue by the Plant or Pipeline Vice-President for subsequent 
inclusion in all site copies of the PTW System standard. The Plant or Pipeline Vice-President may cancel 
deviations when no longer required. 
The approved deviations are to be detailed in the Deviation Register (refer Appendix VIII) and the 
approved Supplementary Procedures appended to each controlled copy of the PTW System standard 
(of that site).

1.7 Periodic review of PTW Standard  
There will be a formal periodic review of the PTW System led by the Operations HSE Department Head. 
This review will encompass but will not necessarily be limited to: 
 
Incidents relating to PTW violations and non-compliances 
 
PTW Audit results 
 
The review of proposals for change to the PTW System 
 
All deviations applied for 
 
PTW training course feedback 
 
Lateral learning opportunities from the Industry and sharing of Best Practice 
Proposals for change or improvement to the PTW System and/or standard can be made at any time. 
The proposal should be raised and documented on the standard PTW System Change Proposal Form 
(refer Appendix IX) This is to be discussed, agreed and countersigned by the Plant or Pipeline Vice-
President before being forwarded to the Operations HSE department head for review.  
The HSE Vice-President will endorse such proposals on a regular basis. Approved proposals will be 
incorporated in the Annual review of the PTW System standard. Originators will receive notification of 
the action taken on their proposal or reasons for rejection. 
1.8 Control of PTW System Standard 
Only controlled copies of the PTW System standard are to be used. The latest revision of the PTW 
standard is available on the GASCO HSE Division’s webpage.

2.  ORGANISATION 
2.1 Management of PTW System 
The GASCO PTW System is managed through the line under the principles of custodianship. 
2.1.1 Roles and Responsibilities 
Chief Executive Officer (CEO) 
CEO is the principal custodian of the PTW System and is responsible for: 
1. Authorizing all deviations to the PTW Standard. 
2. Periodic review of the implementation of PTW System. 
Vice President HSE (VP HSE) 
VP HSE is the custodian of the GASCO PTW standard and is responsible for: 
1. Providing advice in GASCO on all aspects of the PTW System. 
2. Endorsing all proposed changes to the PTW standard. 
3. Conducting Level 2 Audits of the PTW system. 
4. Periodic review of the PTW System and proposing any necessary changes to CEO 
for authorisation. 
Technical Authority (HSEO) 
Operations HSE Department Head (HSEO) is the Technical Authority (TA) of the GASCO PTW Standard 
and is responsible for: 
1. Reviewing any planned deviations from the PTW System requested by the Plant or Pipeline 
Vice-President. 
Plant and Pipeline Vice-Presidents 
The Plant or Pipeline Vice-President, who is the Asset Owner, is responsible for: 
1. The effective implementation and compliance to the PTW System on that Plant or Pipeline. 
2. Preparation of drawings are to identify Restricted Areas, Unrestricted Areas, Hazardous 
Areas (electrical classification zones) and H2S Zones. 
3. Maintaining a register of “Work in Restricted Areas not normally requiring a Permit”. 
4. Providing a comprehensive training programme for PICWS and Permit signatories and that 
competence standards are both established and maintained as defined in the standard. 
5. Appointing persons who are required to sign Permits, with their responsibilities and limits of 
authority (for area) clearly defined in writing. 
6. Ensuring that the planning and administration of permitry is properly coordinated. 
7. Ensuring that the audit programme for the PTW System is carried out as per plan and that 
corrective actions resulting from audit findings are carried out. 
8. Ensuring that Activity Risk Assessments (ARA) and the associated Activity Sequence Control 
(ASC) for activities in the High Risk categories are reviewed and approved. 
9. Requesting any deviations from the standard.

Department Head (Sites) 
The Senior Representative of a Department, who is the Asset Holder, is responsible and accountable to 
the Asset Owner for the integrity and safe condition of the systems and equipment allocated, under 
the PTW System, their duties include, but are not limited to: 
1. Assisting the Plant or Pipeline Vice-President with the implementation and administration of 
work under the PTW System. 
2.  Deputising for the Plant or Pipeline Vice-President as the Asset Owner when authorised and 
appointed in writing to do so. 
3. Reviewing and issuing Permits requiring the higher level authorisation of the Asset Holder. 
4. Reviewing and approving Activity Risk Assessments within his sphere of responsibility. 
5. Implementing the layout of the Permit Control Facility as stated in the standard. 
6. Providing visible support for the implementation of PTW tools by taking part in monitoring or 
auditing of the PTW system. 
7. Identifying key personnel to participate in PTW Auditing as team leader and as team members. 
Site HSE Department Head 
The Site HSE Department Head fulfils an important advisory role to the Operations and Engineering 
Line. Their responsibilities include: 
1. Provision of expert advice to the Operations and Maintenance/Engineering Line for jobs being 
executed under a work permit, particularly when the risk is judged high or medium using the 
Risk Assessment Matrix (RAM). 
2. Provision of resource, including training and HSE personnel so that the HSE controls identified 
in permitry can be monitored and assured. 
3. Support to the Plant or Pipeline Vice-President in maintaining standards, and monitoring 
adherence to standards, by monitoring and follow-up. 
4. Maintaining records of all daily monitoring and auditing of PTW system, and the updating of 
status in the Remedial Action Plan. 
5. Acting as focal point for all communications relating the PTW and maintaining the register 
of any approved deviations from the PTW System. 
6. Keeping a register, for PTW purposes, of all personnel trained and authorised by the Plant 
or Pipeline Vice-President. 
7. Appointing a person from the HSE department for collating the daily monitoring reports, 
recording the findings of daily monitoring reports. 
8. Reporting out to the Plant or Pipeline Vice-President on the current level of effectiveness of 
the PTW System, outlining the key opportunities for improvement. 
Section Heads (Sites) 
The Section Heads are responsible for: 
1. Participating in monitoring & auditing of the PTW system and ensuring that personnel (Senior 
Engineers, Engineers, Supervisors, etc.) who are involved in the PTW process contribute to the 
daily monitoring and auditing of the PTW system. 
2. Providing daily monitoring sheet to HSE department for recording and analysing the results.

2.2 Competencies and Responsibilities of PTW Signatories 
2.2.1 Originator 
Competence 
The Originator must demonstrate to the Plant/Pipeline Vice-President (or his nominated deputy) that 
he is competent to: 
1. Adequately describe any work to be undertaken on the Permit and on Supporting Permits. 
2. Participate in risk assessments for any work he may originate.  
3. Identify those risk assessments which require higher level signature. 
4. Updating Activity Risk Assessment (ARA) when required. 
5. Signing the work permit agreeing with the statement on BOX 6 of the Work Permit. 
6. Identify what supporting permits/certificates are included in the PTW System and explain when 
they are required  
7. Explain the PTW process after origination. 
Responsibilities 
1. Describing on the Permit and on Supporting Permits the work to be undertaken, the hazards 
associated with the work, and the necessary controls in boxes 1 to 6 inclusive. 
2. Where the work is complex, it may be necessary to support the permitry with a Maintenance 
or Engineering Method Statement, or Activity Sequence Control form (ASC) or both. 
In such cases the Originator should include these in the permitry package. 
3. Discussing and agreeing the work scope, equipment to be used and permitry details with the 
Authorised Maintenance/Engineering Authority who will then present the permitry at the daily 
PTW Meeting for validation by the Asset Operator. 
Note: Maintenance/Engineering Authority may act as Permit Originator in some cases. 
2.2.2 Maintenance/Engineering Reviewer 
Competence 
The Maintenance/Engineering Reviewer must demonstrate to the Plant/Pipeline Vice-President (or 
his nominated deputy) that he is competent to: 
1. Review and approve various work scopes defined by the Permit Originator on work permits 
and other permitry.  
2. Participate in risk assessments. 
3. Identify those risk assessments which require higher level signature. 
4. Explain the purpose of the Daily PTW Planning Meeting. 
5. Explain the PTW process after origination. 
Responsibilities 
1. Reviewing and approving various work scope defined by the Permit Originator. 
2. Agreeing with the associated risk assessment.

2.2.4 Issuer (Asset Operator or, for Higher Risk Work, Asset Holder) 
Competence 
The Issuer must demonstrate to the Plant/Pipeline Vice-President (or his nominated deputy) that he 
is competent to: 
1. Explain the outcome of risk assessments for work which he authorizes by issuing 
a work permit. 
2. Identify those risk assessments which require higher level signature. 
3. Explain the PTW process before and after issuing the permit, including shift change. 
4. Communicate the necessary information to PICWS.

3. FRAMEWORK OF THE PTW SYSTEM 
3.1 Introduction 
For the purposes of this Permit to Work System, work can be described as an activity that comprise 
one or more tasks. It is implicit that the work has been agreed as necessary, having been through 
the planning stage of the work management process. The Permit to Work System is concerned with 
authorising and managing this work. 
The issue of a Permit does not, by itself, make a job safe. Those preparing for the work can only achieve 
that, together with those supervising and executing the work. In addition to the PTW System other 
precautions may need to be taken, e.g. in relation to Plant Operations and Worksite Preparation 
phases. All levels in the organisation must clearly understand both the PTW System and their roles 
within it. 
Any Permit to Work System relies on 6 important components (pillars) to make it successful; these 
are: 
1. Compliance - Do people do what they are supposed to do (minimum requirement), and do 
they do it because they believe it helps them stay safe? 
2. Hazard/Situational awareness - Would people actually recognise a hazard when they refer 
one (awareness), and/or would they accept it (“It won’t happen to me” or “I don’t have time 
to do the proper thing”)? 
3. Planning - Does the system provide the conditions, tools and time and resources to plan and 
coordinate work effectively, with full involvement of Production operations and 
Maintenance disciplines? 
4. Competence - Do the people know and understand how to use the PTW process/system 
and, most importantly, their HSE critical roles and responsibilities? Do they understand why 
the system is used, what each step adds and what the ‘real’ requirements are for each step 
are?  
5. Communication - Do people actually talk to each other during the preparation of the permit 
(or do they prepare in isolation sitting at their desk or behind a computer screen) and do 
they visit the worksite before and during the work? 
6. Supervision - Do supervisors go outside (are they given/ taking the time) and do they check 
and verify risk controls and compliance to PTW requirements, as well as coach and train on 
PTW application when they are out there

3.3.7 Work which requires Interface Procedures 
Interface procedures apply whenever there is a risk of interference or mutual risk between two parties 
working within close vicinity of each other. They exist to ensure that all parties are aware of each 
other’s activities and that the risks and potential hazardous interactions have been jointly identified 
and communicated, and that satisfactory control measure have also been jointly identified and 
implemented to allow the work activity to continue safely. 
PTW Interface procedures (refer 3.3.7.1 to 3.3.7.5) apply for all work being undertaken within the 
following designated interface areas: 
 
Within 30 m of the boundary between two different GASCO Area Authorities who are 
responsible for a section of the plant within the responsibility of the same Asset Operator 
 
Within 30 m of the boundary between two different GASCO Asset Operators, e.g. Habshan 
Plant/Facility having different Shift Controllers 
 
Within 30 m of an ADNOC Group Company administered area, facility or OPCO’s pipeline 
 
Where the work is conducted within the designated boundary of an ADNOC Group Company 
facility, pipeline corridor or administered area by GASCO or GASCO contractors 
 
Where the work is conducted within the designated boundary of an GASCO administered area, 
facility or pipeline corridor by an ADNOC group company or their contractors

Where the work is conducted within the designated boundary of a shared PTW Interface Area

3.4 Risk Management Process for Work 
There are, in general, three types of work activity: 
1. Work activities that have had a Risk Assessment previously completed and recorded 
in procedures or in Maximo 
2. Work activities that are new or have not been risk assessed, and 
3. Work activities that can be covered by individual’s competence and training 
In all cases, the initial Risk Assessment should be undertaken prior to the origination of the Work 
Permit and any supporting Permits or Certificates (refer section 4). It is the responsibility of the 
Maintenance/Engineering Authority to initiate the process and record the Risk Assessment 
The following sections describe, for each of these types of activities, the steps that must be taken 
in order to ensure the integrity of the risk assessment process. 
3.4.1 Introduction to the Activity Risk Assessment (ARA) Process 
Whenever work has to be undertaken by GASCO, Third Parties or Contractors on GASCO sites,  
or in the immediate vicinity of GASCO pipelines, it is necessary to make a suitable and sufficient 
assessment of the risks to the health and safety of employees and others, for the purpose of 
identifying the necessary control measures. 
Practically this involves four basic steps, which are to: 
 
Identify the possible hazards associated with the work activity 
 
Assess the consequences of the hazardous event(s) and the likelihood of their happening, 
i.e. evaluating the risks arising from them 
 
Introduce control measures (precautions) to prevent or reduce the risks to a level “As Low 
As Reasonably Practicable” (ALARP).  Refer 3.4.1.4 for further explanation and guidance on 
demonstrating ALARP. 
 
Specify recovery measures in case the controls fail. 
The process of identifying the hazards, evaluating risks and applying controls and specifying 
any recovery measures will be referred to as “Activity Risk Assessment” (ARA). This is shown 
diagrammatically in Figure 3.5 below.

3.4.1.1 Identify the Hazards 
One of the most important aspects when conducting a risk assessment is accurately identifying 
the potential hazards in the workplace. 
The ideal starting point is to walk around the workplace and think about any potential hazards, 
e.g. what is it about the activities, processes or substances used that could injure personnel 
or harm their health. 
There are some hazards with a recognised risk of harm, for example working at height, working with 
chemicals, machinery, and asbestos. However, when you work in a place every day it is easy to 
overlook some hazards, and that is why it is important to conduct a site visit when identifying potential 
hazards.

3.4.1.2 Assessing the Risks 
Assessing risk involves considering how likely it is that each hazard could cause harm, by considering 
the threats which may lead to the release of the hazard, what the consequences could be, 
and, in some cases, the potential for the event to escalate. 
The extent of any risk assessment will depend on the nature of the risks, the complexity associated 
with the activity, and on whether the risks are well understood. The ARA in the workplace can be as 
basic as deciding how to go about doing a task safely for simple routine jobs. 
GASCO has developed a Risk Assessment Matrix (RAM) as a tool to help categorise the risk, based on 
its consequences and the probability of the potential consequence occurring (refer Appendix III).  
3.4.1.3 Controlling the Risks 
Controlling risk involves eliminating the hazard wherever possible, or applying measures to prevent 
the hazardous event and/or the harm from happening.

3.4.1.4 Demonstrating ALARP 
ALARP means to reduce a risk to a level, which is as low as reasonably practicable. This involves 
balancing reduction in risk against the time, trouble, difficulty and cost of achieving it. This level 
(ALARP) represents the point, objectively assessed, at which the time, trouble, difficulty and cost 
of further reduction measures becomes disproportionate to the additional risk reduction obtained. 
A key concept in identifying the ALARP level is to ensure that all possible options for risk reduction 
have been identified. It is often the case that only the obvious or easy options are considered. 
It is important, however, to look at all options even if at first they appear difficult or costly. 
The Risk Assessment Matrix (RAM), (refer Appendix III) is a tool that provides a standard approach 
to the qualitative assessment of risk. All activities, which fall in the yellow (medium) or red (high) 
sectors of the RAM, require documented evidence that they have been reduced to ALARP. 
An Activity Risk Assessment (ARA) form is provided for this purpose (refer Appendix V). 
Note: Specific guidance on the completion of the ARA form is given in 3.4.3, Stage 6, and Appendix 
IV. 
3.4.2 Performing an ARA for Previously Assessed Activities 
Most activities that are undertaken are not new, they are routine, or common tasks that have 
previously been risk assessed and/or are covered by existing procedures. They have probably 
been developed through established good practice and with due consideration for the hazards 
that may be present. 
Such activities do not need a new risk assessment each time they are carried out, but do require that 
the assessment is subject to periodic review, primarily to identify if anything has changed that would 
affect the outcome of the assessment. 
Some questions, which should be asked, are, for example: 
 
Are the location specific hazards captured? 
 
Have the persons doing the activity changed and are any new persons competent? 
 
Have new hazards been introduced? 
 
Are new tools being used or new procedures introduced? 
 
Is it for a High risk activity?

review should be recorded. All ARAs being used for Medium / Low risk activities should be reviewed

4.7 Completion of Excavation Certificate

4.7.1 Safe Work Practices in Excavations 
Due to the potential presence of Hydrogen Sulphide (heavier than air) or hydrocarbons, a Confined 
Space Entry Certificate must be issued for all excavations over 1.5 metres deep, irrespective of depth 
to width ratio, within Restricted Areas where these gases are present, or are likely to be present. 
Oxygen, Hydrogen Sulphide and combustible gas testing must be conducted as part of the Confined 
Space Entry Certificate conditions. 
In addition, a Confined Space Entry Certificate is required in all areas for excavations over 1.5 metres, 
having an excavation depth to excavation width ratio of more than 2.0 or when the access and egress 
are limited requiring further controls. 
Road Excavations: Traffic flow for emergency access shall be maintained at all times (alternative 
arrangements may be required). During road crossing excavations, a responsible person shall be 
delegated to control traffic. In the case of road blocking, the Asset Operator should be consulted as to 
possible alternative access routes. 
Use of Mechanical Excavators: Mechanical excavators shall not be used when the presence of 
underground pipes, cables, vessels or structures are known, or expected within 3 metres of the 
excavation, where the risks have been adequately assessed, mechanical excavations can be 
undertaken to a distance of 1 metre from pipework/structures. 
In addition, Risk management must include marking and exposing the pipework/structure to be 
excavated, suitably protecting it from damage where appropriate and providing close and competent 
supervision. The identified risks and action to be taken must be discussed and communicated during 
the Toolbox Talk.

5. WORKSITE PREPARATIONS 
5.1 Introduction 
Once the hazards associated with a particular job have been identified, it will be necessary to consider 
how they can be separated or isolated from the equipment or plant to be worked on. Flammable, 
toxic, pressurised, high temperature, or low temperature fluids will normally have to be removed from 
the plant / equipment before working on it. Inadequate worksite preparations on vessels, tanks and 
systems which have held flammable materials may lead to serious accidents  
An essential ingredient of any Safe System of Work is the highest level of integrity of the isolation 
practices. Whilst the Work Permit identifies the isolation requirements it does not provide the specific 
isolation details. These are to be documented and appended to Permits as per existing, Plant specific, 
Isolation and re-instatement practices.  
All Sites must ensure that mechanical Isolation and re-instatement practices are controlled in 
accordance with GASCO Mechanical Isolation Standard (HSE-ST-035).  
5.1.1 Identify Isolation Requirements 
When work is to be undertaken which requires isolation of equipment, a formal written scheme of 
isolation must be prepared with isolations documented on the Isolation Confirmation Certificate. 
The isolations must provide control of all hazardous energy sources within the defined system and/or 
equipment isolation boundaries including electrical power, process fluids, and hydraulic, pneumatic, 
thermal, chemical and mechanical systems. Where appropriate (e.g. where there are no tag numbers 
or where the isolation is complex), a P&ID showing the isolations must accompany with the isolation 
Confirmation Certificate (ICC). 
 The various levels of process isolation are represented in the Hierarchy of Controls, given below: 
First: Remove equipment from the sources of hazards, or create an air gap, or physical break that 
prevents the hazard from contacting people.  
Second: Isolate equipment from hazards by using a solid physical barrier. Blinding is the minimum 
isolation requirement for confined space entry and for activities covered by hot work.  
Third: Move to the safe position and lock movable devices, electrical circuit breakers and valves that 
isolate hazards. When applying this control for valves:  
 
Two valves are to be closed and a drain opened between them (Double-Block and Bleed); or  
 
Use a Single Valve, verify that it provides isolation, and provide at least one additional control 
(such as personal protective equipment).  
 
Where the Single Valve is the barrier to an acute toxic substance, a pressure greater than 4 
bar, a material above its flash point or a material above 60°C (Celsius) this method of control 
can only be used by installing a blind. 
The isolations shall be considered in the order of this controls hierarchy, air gapping being the most 
stringent. The minimum control that shall be applied is the least stringent isolation that reduces the 
risk to ALARP.  The determination of ALARP is done through risk assessment and evaluation of the 
controls by a competent multidisciplinary team with at least a maintenance and an operations 
discipline involved in this assessment

7. TOOLBOX TALKS 
7.1 Introduction 
This Section supports the Activity Risk Assessment Process described in Section 3.4 and should be read 
whilst referring to the Work Permit (Figure 4.1 & 4.2). 
One of the last steps in the Activity Risk Assessment (ARA) Process is to discuss the Work Permit, and 
any supporting Permits or Certificates, with the persons who will actually be carrying out the work, 
and therefore be directly exposed to possible release of hazards. This is achieved by holding a “Toolbox 
Talk”. 
Toolbox Talks are an essential element of safe working practices in GASCO’s sites for both GASCO 
and contractor staff. The purpose of the talk is to discuss with the working party: 
 
What the work activities will be; 
 
What hazards have been identified that will require controls during the course of the work; 
 
What those controls are and how the work party will be affected. 
The work party may, by virtue of their experience identify further hazards or may be able to suggest 
alternative, or improved, controls. Where this is the case, the hazards and additional precautions 
section of the Permit should, if necessary, be modified and initialled after consultation and agreement 
with the validating and issuing authorities. 
The Originator of the Permit should formally capture this feedback by updating the ARA, which can 
then be utilised the next time the work is undertaken. 
7.2 Format of the Toolbox Talk 
Where there is a perceived higher risk, the Permit Originator should stipulate the requirement for a 
formal record of the Toolbox Talk. In such cases, the Toolbox Talk is to be recorded on the Toolbox 
Talk Record Form on the back of the PTW (refer figure 7.1) 
The Toolbox Talk Record Form provides an effective communication means for ensuring the work 
party are: 
 
Aware of the work scope; 
 
Aware of the hazards; 
 
Knowledgeable as to the methods/procedures to be adopted; 
 
Aware of the PTW precautions/controls; 
 
Aware of any constraints; 
 
Aware of what tools and equipment are to be used; 
 
Aware of Environmental considerations; 
 
Aware of any potentially conflicting activities; 
 
Able to clarify any issues and provide comments.

9) Does the PICWS understand the requirement to provide a Toolbox Talk to any new people

10) Are there any Language/Literacy Issues? 
11) Does everyone know what to do in the event of a site alarm or emergency? 
 
If the answer to any of these questions is NO, then the safety of people is at risk and must be 
addressed.

8. PERMIT CONTROL FACILITY AND ADMINISTRATION OF PTW SYSTEM 
8.1 Objective 
A fundamental requirement of any PTW System is that it provides a suitable display of permitry on the 
facility, in a central location, to allow staff an overview of current status. It is also required that all 
closed out permitry be archived in a systematic fashion for easy reference and follow-up auditing 
purposes. 
This section contains the detailed procedure for the handling of Work Permits, supporting Permits and 
Certificates, and other related documents as they pass between signatories and are administered 
through the Permit Control Facility (PCF). 
8.2 Permit Control Facility Requirements 
8.2.1 Purpose of the Permit Control Facility 
The Permit Control Facility (PCF) will be provided in a central point of the Plant or Pipeline facility 
through which all Permits pass each time their status changes, i.e. when Authorised, Endorsed, 
Suspended or Closed. 
This enables a check to be made that the work specified does not conflict with any ongoing Permit 
work. It also allows a continuous display of work in progress to be maintained. 
It can, in addition, provide the venue for the daily Permit-to-Work (PTW) meeting whereby all work 
requirements will be discussed and agreed 
8.2.2 Layout and Location of the PCF 
The size and layout of the PCF will depend upon the complexity and configuration of the facility. For a 
small and less complex facility it may be a section of the Control Room, whilst for a more complex 
facility, if space permits, it may merit a separate room. 
The PCF should ideally be located in, or in close proximity to, the Control Room or other central facility. 
Where this is impractical, the location should be selected carefully to ensure that it is readily accessible 
to users, and will not cause excessive additional journeys for personnel obtaining signatures. 
A PCF has to be of sufficient size to house, in one location, all the information displays boards, permit 
racks, and all the relevant files and data that are required to be stored, for the Asset under the 
authority and control of the Asset Operator. Where there is more than one Asset, with several Asset 
Operators (e.g. Habshan), it is permissible to have separate PCF facilities, providing the Operation 
Coordinator (Asset Holder) can maintain an overview. 
8.2.3 Facilities within the PCF 
Permit Rack: As a minimum, there are to be conventional racks for the display of paper copies of all 
Permits from all departments of the Plant or Pipeline. It is to be arranged in such a way that Permit 
location and status are readily visible. This enables staff to readily check which equipment is under 
maintenance and is not available for operation.

8.3.2 Segregation of Permits in Permit Display Racks 
The Permit Control Facility (PCF) is to provide a Permit Rack system, suitable for the display of 
permitry, sub-divided by Area of the Plant or Pipeline facility. 
The Permit Rack arrangement should allow for the further segregation and visual presentation of 
permitry status within the following sections (refer also diagrammatic representation, figure 8.1): 
8.3.2.1 VALIDATED Section 
After validation, by the Maintenance/Engineering Authority and the Asset Operator (normally the 
Operations Coordinator), the Work Permit, together with its additional supporting certificates are 
placed in the 'VALIDATED' section of the Permit Display Rack. 
Operations staff is now aware that planned work in the Operations area has been approved but has 
not yet started. 
The Shift Controller shall, prior to arranging for any worksite preparations, check the Permit against 
other Permits displayed in the PCF for conflicting activities. If any conflicts are identified, he is to advise 
the Originator and Operations Coordinator who will agree either to: 
 
Withdraw the new Work Permit, or, 
 
Arrange for other Permits to be Suspended or Cancelled, dependent upon relative priorities. 
Once this step is completed the Shift Controller may authorise Worksite preparations to proceed. He 
is to ensure that any other supporting documentation (e.g. isolation/blind lists) is retained in the 
Permit Rack along with the associated permitry. 
8.3.2.2 ENDORSED Section 
When the worksite preparations and checks for conflict are complete, the permit is issued by the Asset 
Operator (Shift Controller) and accepted by the Maintenance/Engineering Authority who then takes 
the top, original (white) copy to the worksite for Area Endorsement. 
The 2nd (yellow) is transferred to the “ENDORSED” section of the Permit display rack 
Operations staff is now aware that the planned work is actually proceeding in the Operations Area. 
At the start of each new shift period, prior to each subsequent endorsement of the Work Permit, the 
Asset Operator will again check for conflict with any other Work Permit or ongoing activity prior to 
releasing the Permit. 
Note: It is permissible for the Assistant Shift Controller to carry out subsequent endorsements of 
permitry on the agreement of the Shift Controller who is the principle Asset Operator 
8.3.2.3 SUSPENDED Section 
At the end of the Maintenance/Engineering work period the original top copy, is returned to the PCF 
and, together with the other copy, is transferred to the 
“SUSPENDED” section of the Permit Display Rack. The permitry is to be arranged with the top original 
(white) copy on top. 
Operations staff are now aware that work which was in progress will/has been suspended but is to 
continue and isolations will/may be in place.

9. PTW SYSTEM AUDITING 
9.1 Permit to Work Monitoring and Auditing Tools 
The main objective of these PTW tools is to periodically review and monitor the effectiveness of the 
PTW System. The two tools are designed to monitor, review/ record, trend and feedback the 
effectiveness of the PTW system in the respective plant/pipeline division. 
1. Daily Monitoring Tool  
2. Auditing Tool 
The PTW tools will measure the effectiveness of the PTW system by focusing on the six (6) behaviour 
pillars and the eight (8) step PTW process from permit planning through to permit closure. 
9.2 Daily Monitoring Tool  
The characteristics of daily monitoring tool are described below: 
 
Closed (Yes/No) type questions 
 
Focusing on implementation (daily) 
 
Needs representative (>70) sample for accuracy of data 
9.2.1 How to use the Daily Monitoring Tool 
The monitoring tool focuses on the daily implementation of permits. The following steps will describe 
how to use this tool and how to navigate within the tool (refer Appendix VI):  
1) Go to the print sheet tab and print out the monitoring questionnaire form; 
2) Hand out the questionnaire with form to the nominated person to assess the selected permit, 
e.g. hand out to the Area Authority, Shift Controller, Operation Coordinator, Maintenance 
Authority etc.; 
3) The nominated person returns the completed form to HSE Department focal point who loads 
the data into the Data Input sheet; 
4) Fill in the date, name of Assessor, Permit number, location, brief work description and risk 
level.  The question responses Yes / No are recorded in the relevant row, adding any 
comments on any areas of non-compliance; 
5) The HSE Department focal point (appointed by HSE department head) enters the data in the 
spreadsheet; 
6) It is deemed that about 70 permit forms are required to get to reliable data; 
7) Assess the spider diagrams along the 6 behavioural pillars and the 8 step process;  
8) Discuss the results, findings and areas for improvement in your daily / weekly or PTW or 
Management Meetings.

10.3 Control of Work in the Vicinity of Pipelines 
In Pipeline Division, 30 metres either side of a designated pipeline, pipeline corridor and related 
facilities [Sectionalizing Valve (SV), Customer Receipt Station (CRS), Manifold (MF)] is defined as a 
Restricted Area. The intent is that no work shall take place within 30 metres of any GASCO Pipeline 
containing hazardous material, without approval and implementation of controls for work being 
applied. 
These controls and approval of work are applied depending on the distance from pipeline and facilities 
as follows: 
Permit to Work (PTW) 
 
The area within 30 m from above ground facilities such as manifolds, SVs, CRS & blow-off 
points. 
 
Area within 5 meters from the fence or bottom of pipeline berm (when there is no fencing) 
throughout the corridor. 
Authorisation to Work (ATW) (For OPN Contractors & third Parties)/EPC-PTW 
 
ATWs are issued to OPN Contractors and third parties’ viz. municipality, telecom & other 
authorities for any activities, from 5 m of the fence or bottom of pipeline berm (when there is 
no fencing) to 25 m from the PTW application zone, throughout the pipeline corridor.

Confined Space 
A place which is substantially enclosed (though not always entirely), 
with restricted means of entry or exit, and where serious injury can

might not be adequately ventilated.

H2S Zone 
An area of the operational plant or pipeline, within a restricted area, where 
there is potential for, high levels of H2S to be present in the process stream. 
Four Zones: Green, Yellow, Amber and Red are defined, each with its own 
specific PPE & access control requirements, as specified within ADNOC CoP  
Red Zone is considered to exist if there is a significant risk of H2S release, 
which could be rapidly lethal. The Toxic LSIR level for Red Zones is 1E-3 per 
year. 
Amber Zone is considered to exist where persons affected would have time 
to don PPE carried ready for use, but would not have time to reach the 
nearest PPE location.  The Toxic LSIR level for Amber Zones is 1E-4 per year. 
Yellow Zone is considered to exist if there is a possibility of H2S release, 
which could be rapidly injurious.  The Toxic LSIR level for Yellow Zones is 1E-
5 per year.  
Green Zone is considered to exist where H2S concentrations above the 8 
hour TWA limit is foreseeable, but the area is not in an otherwise classified 
zone.  The Toxic LSIR level for Green Zones is 1E-6 per year.V4-10.

APPENDIX - II : TERMS OF REFERENCE FOR  DAILY PTW PLANNING MEETING 
Daily PTW Meeting Objective: 
1) To provide a daily overview of all ongoing and planned work under the control of the Permit to Work 
System and to identify any potential conflicting activities 
2) For Operations to review and agree new Permits, including any Activity Risk Assessments previously 
undertaken, time scales and resource requirements (Note that the daily PTW meeting is not the 
forum for undertaking the ARA) 
3) To provide an effective handover mechanism to duty shift teams through the recording 
of work that is to be undertaken in the Operations Area 
4) To provide a Maintenance/Engineering and Operations interface for Permit to Work requirements 
Attendees: 
As a minimum, the daily PTW Meeting will be attended, by the Operations Coordinator,  
who 
will 
normally 
be 
the 
Chair 
Person, 
Shift 
Controller 
(as 
available) 
and 
appointed 
Maintenance/Engineering staff. Further attendees, for example Technical Support and/or HSE, will be 
present if requested by the Operations or Maintenance/Engineering attendees, when it is recognised that 
further expertise or advice is relevant. 
Terms of Reference: 
1) The meeting will be held daily, taking into account weekend working 
2) Key input mechanisms to the daily PTW Meeting will be work details/status of ongoing work and any 
new activities. Any preparatory requirements for new activities should be highlighted 
3) As far as is possible/practicable, all new Permits should be processed through the daily PTW meeting, 
unless it is HSE or production critical and cannot wait. In such cases, Permits may be processed 
outside of the PTW Meeting, but the Maintenance/Engineering Authority and the Asset Operator 
must still adhere to the same review and validation process 
4) Permitry must be submitted with adequate advance warning to allow Operations the required 
timescale for detailing and recording of isolation procedures and for equipment preparation 
A check must be made to ensure that there is no forerefern conflict, both geographically (i.e. across other 
work boundaries) or on a time basis (activities which may start later).This is to be done by the Asset 
Operator. 
At weekends, or where permitry is not submitted via the daily PTW Meeting, the Asset Operator 
and Authorised Maintenance/Engineering Authority must undertake the process of reviewing and validating 
of permitry.Once the permitry has been discussed at the daily PTW Meeting and controls agreed, the Permit 
can be VALIDATED, that is, accepted by the Asset Operator as an agreed piece of work that has been risk 
assessed and is to be undertaken in an area controlled by Operations (or for Unrestricted Areas the 
appointed Asset Operator). 
 
Note: Pipelines Division may hold the meeting at a lesser frequency, as workload requires but not less than 
weekly
