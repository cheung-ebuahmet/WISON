3. INTRODUCTION 
Hydrogen Sulphide (H2S) is an extremely toxic, corrosive and flammable gas, causing serious 
incidents in the hydrocarbon industry every year. At low concentrations, Hydrogen Sulphide is 
detectable by its characteristic rotten-egg odour, but at higher concentration (above 100 ppm) 
smell cannot be relied to forewarn dangerous concentrations as H2S rapidly paralyses the 
sense of smell. Exposure to H2S above 500 ppm for any length of time is considered 
potentially lethal.  
 
In order to compare H2S risk, not only the H2S concentration (in air) but also the H2S (release) 
pressure is important. Hence the definition of H2S partial pressure should be the prime metric 
to be used when performing H2S risks and verify mitigation.  
 
Hydrogen Sulphide identified as Major Accident Hazard (MAH) in GASCO operations. The 
COMAH studies conducted in GASCO recommend a high degree of risk controls & recovery 
preparedness measures for managing Hydrogen Sulphide in our operations. GASCO have 
identified 10 Life Saving Rules (LSR), one of them specifically to cover H2S and stipulates to 
carry a Personal Gas Detector & Emergency Escape Breathing Device (EEBD) when entering 
an H2S zone.   
   
This standard summarises OPERATIONAL requirements for safe handling and working with 
Hydrogen Sulphide that are stated in ADNOC-COPV4-10, April 2014 on the management of 
Hydrogen Sulphide and provide further guidance on how to implement the ADNOC COP in 
GASCO operational sites. 
4. PURPOSE 
The purpose of the standard is to: 
 
Outline the hazards associated with Hydrogen Sulphide and to ensure that the risks 
to people from Hydrogen Sulphide releases are prevented, controlled and mitigated 
to a risk level that is As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP); and 
 
Provide a consistent and safe work practices in GASCO Plants and Pipeline 
Divisions for handling and working with Hydrogen Sulphide. 
5. SCOPE AND APPLICATION 
This standard is applicable to all activities involving exposure to Hydrogen Sulphide, which are 
performed by employees and contracting companies within GASCO premises. It applies to all, 
Operation and Maintenance activities in H2S classified zones. 
 
This standard does not address any DESIGN practices.  Please refer to GASCO DGS for the 
design requirements that stated in ADNOC CoP V4-10.

8. HEALTH HAZARDS OF HYDROGEN SULPHIDE 
The major effect of H2S on humans is its ability to cause rapid damage to respiratory system 
or sudden death due to accidental exposure. The unpleasant odour of H2S can even be 
detected at concentrations of 0.02 ppm, may not be detectable at concentrations above 100 
ppm due to rapid loss of the sense of smell. Therefore, one should never trust his sense of 
smell to detect H2S presence. Exposure to H2S above 500 ppm for any length of time is 
considered as potentially lethal. The health effects of H2S is detailed as below. 
 
8.1 Acute Exposure Effect of H2S 
H2S is highly toxic at higher concentrations. It is absorbed rapidly, interferes with cellular 
respiration. At higher concentrations, H2S starts to affect the central nervous system and can 
cause accumulation of fluid in the lungs (pulmonary oedema). 
 
At 50-150 ppm H2S can start to knock out the sense of smell and this is one of the dangers of 
the gas; operators cannot rely on the sense of smell. At higher concentration levels this effect 
is immediate and H2S cannot be smelt at all.

various standards and Shell Health Guidance on Acute Toxicity values for Hydrogen Sulphide.

100 ppm for 30 minutes exposure is a different level defined by a separate organization; the

escape. 
650 ppm is defined to be the concentration at which 1% of the exposed population will suffer 
fatality (or incapacitation leading to a fatality) if they are exposed to H2S for less than 10 
minutes. This means that exposure to 650ppm, in an emergency, could be tolerated for a very 
short duration e.g. 75 seconds where RPE should be donned quickly. 
Above 1000 ppm the effects of H2S can be instantaneous; immediate knock-down. Rapid 
rescue and resuscitation are the only chance of survival. This level is in similar range to the 
50% fatality probability for 10 minutes. 
8.2 Long Term Exposure Effect of H2S 
At low H2S concentrations, the gas has an irritating effect on the eyes and membranes of the 
nose, throat and lungs. The H2S can be detected by smell in the parts-per-billion range. At 0.1 
parts per million, the H2S has a pungent and unpleasant odor of rotten eggs. However at this 
concentration it is not harmful to health.

9. HYDROGEN SULPHIDE ZONES, ENTRY & WORKING REQUIREMENTS 
GASCO Plants and selected locations at Pipeline division (Valve stations, Compressor 
Stations, Pigging Stations etc.) are classified as H2S zones in accordance to the potential 
threat from hydrogen sulphide based on the precautions needed to allow people a good 
chance to escape in the event of an accidental release of hydrogen sulphide. 
Four Zones: Green, Yellow, Amber and Red are defined, each with its own specific PPE 
requirement (or no requirement). The criteria for defining the extent of each zone is logically 
derived from the PPE requirements using the Time To Protect principle (see ADNOC CoP 
V4-10, Section 2.8.3) together with the ADNOC Risk Matrix and The ALARP Principle 
presented in the ADNOC CoP V5-06, HSE Risk Management. 
One of the key risk metrics while establishing these zones and determining the hazard level is 
to use H2S Partial Pressure calculation.  
 
 
 
 
Partial Pressure H2S = Absolute Pressure x  H2S %. 
Employees shall be aware and understand H2S Zone classification of their facility and 
appropriate level of controls that applies to each zone for access and for performing activities. 
The H2S Zone classification shall be reviewed when; 
 
New facilities added to the plant; and  
 
Any major process modification takes place. 
 
H2S zones may be declassified during major shutdowns or a major overhaul of equipment 
provided that: 
 
The equipment has been depressurised, positively isolated, degassed and 
ventilated and tested for the presence of Hydrogen Sulphide; 
 
It has been determined that H2S cannot be generated within equipment or 
piping (e.g.: by chemical reaction); and  
 
The area is not overlapped by H2S zone arising from other equipment/ plant 
which is live. 
For H2S Zoning calculations, exposure calculations, dispersion calculation etc. please refer to 
ADNOC COP V4-10. 
An assumption register used by GASCO sites for calculating H2S zones is attached in 
Appendix F.

9.3 Yellow Zone 
YELLOW 
Definition 
Yellow Zones are the areas where the risks of fatal concentrations of H2S are 
low enough, so that personnel can reach to designated Emergency Escape 
Breathing Device (EEBD) locations and don the mask before being overcome. 
Toxic LSIR level is 1E-5 per year. In Yellow zones, there is in principle sufficient 
time to protect staff and thus don EEBD in case of H2S release.

 
Warning signs shall be posted at appropriate locations ( E.g.: Entrance 
Points) 
 
Number of personnel entering or present in Yellow zone, must not 
exceed the number of Emergency Escape Breathing Devices available 
at that time. 
 
No Assembly points shall be designated in Yellow Zone. 
Note: Assembly points which are located in Yellow Zones can be used, if they 
are equipped with H2S detectors and local alarms to provide advance warning 
to people so that they can move to an another safer area.

10. SAFE WORKSITE PRACTICES 
10.1 Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) and Standard Maintenance Practices 
(SMP) 
SOPs and SMPs shall be developed for, but not limited to, all HSE critical activities as per 
HSEIA to avoid H2S hazards during Operations or Maintenance. 
10.2 Permit to Work system 
GASCO plants and facilities where a Hydrogen Sulphide hazard exist must follow GASCO 
Permit to Work system (HSE-ST-009)  to carry out any activity in the H2S Zones. PTW 
monitoring tool should be utilized to conduct daily checks on compliance with PTW system.  
10.3 Isolation Requirements  
The isolations practices for the mechanical isolation shall be in line with GASCO Mechanical 
Isolation Standard (HSE-ST-035). 
The equipment in sour service shall be positively isolated, gas freed and purged as 
necessary.  SCBA/ Airline Breathing apparatus shall always be worn when breaking any 
system integrity until the system has been deemed safe. 
A single isolation valve is not regarded as adequate for safety, when a vessel or pipe section 
is to be opened. For general maintenance operations there must be a positive isolation in the 
form of a blind flange or spade, or a removable spool piece. 
For some operations such as filter cleaning, frequent removal and replacement of spades 
can increase risk. A double block and bleed valve arrangement may be provided for such 
situations. 
For lethal service, double block and bleed should be supported by a pressure indicator 
between the block valves. Care should be taken in the design of this indicator, since it must 
withstand full process pressure, but must be able to detect low pressures arising from valve 
leakage. 
Bleed from double block and bleed isolation must be routed to a suitable location, with no 
possibility of back flow of gas, as could arise for example by routing the bleed to a 
pressurized flare header. 
Double block and bleed using valves remote from the section to be isolated is not acceptable, 
being too prone to error. 
When a leak occurs at sections of pipe where there is only one block valve between the 
location and a large hydrogen sulphide inventory. In this case the best overall risk reduction 
may be to close the single isolation valve, and make any repair etc. using airline breathing air 
supply, or SCBA equipped with full face mask, if the work is very short, as the second level of 
protection. In this case care must be taken to ensure that the air supply is adequate, that the 
person(s) carrying out the work are supported by a “buddy” who also has an airline or SCBA 
air supply, and that there are no persons in the potential hazard zone. 
Positive Isolation means incorporating the use of spades, spectacle blinds, or removable 
spools and blind flanges.  A formal written scheme of isolation must be prepared with 
isolations documented on the Isolation Confirmation Certificate (ICC).

10.6 Sumps, Drains and API separator 
Sumps and drains are frequent areas for accidents. Mostly the accidents can be prevented 
by confined space restrictions. There are cases, however where H2S can be emitted from 
sumps and drains. The most important ones are admission of rich amine solutions to the 
drains, admission of hot water (steam condensate) to drains, and gas release in to closed 
drains due to level controller failure.  
Amine solutions which are drained from process equipment should be collected, not 
released to drains.  This may be done by providing a closed sump or by draining to a 
portable vessel.  
Drainage of hot water to drains needs to be controlled procedurally (i.e., SOP).   
10.7 Surveys 
Each Plant Division must carry out surveys of operations such as sampling and venting to 
determine the requirement for Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA) and any 
limitations on the time personnel can be present to avoid exceeding the allowable Time 
Weighted Average (TWA) dose. These surveys must take into account H2S concentration 
arising from unavoidable emissions during the operations together with background 
emissions from sources such as leaking valve stems or local venting.

11. HYDROGEN SULPHIDE DETECTION 
The purpose of H2S detection is to alert personnel on the presence of H2S at their Plant or 
work location. The H2S detection methods are by:    
 
Personal H2S Detectors 
 
Fixed H2S Detectors 
 
Portable Multi-Gas Detectors  
11.1 Personal H2S Detectors 
Personnel including temporary workers and visitors must be provided with personal H2S 
detector which will alarm when H2S concentration at or exceeding the toxic gas warning 
level of 10 ppm. Personal hydrogen sulphide detectors must have the following minimum 
specification: 
 
Cannot be switched off either deliberately or accidentally; and  
 
Alarm (Visual, Audible & Vibrating) at the toxic gas warning level in such a way 
as to be recognisable under all foreseeable conditions including in high noise 
areas. 
 
Detect H2S level as low as 5 ppm and up to 100 ppm. Historical data should be 
downloadable with the duration of each event for monitoring the occupational 
health surveillance. A minimum data of 20 events shall be available for download. 
 
Shall be intrinsically safe as required by the Hazardous Area Classification.  
 
Shall be capable to operate in ambient temperature range: 0 to +50 0 Celsius.  
 
Shall have adequate protection (Ingress Protection) for dust & water. 
If H2S personal detectors activated, it should be returned to HSE Department for testing 
and evaluation. Bump testing is compulsory on a frequent basis (minimum monthly or as 
per manufacturer recommendation).  
11.2 Fixed H2S Detectors 
Fixed H2S detectors shall be installed within plants and pipeline stations, between boundary 
with non-Plant area and beyond boundary fence as per QRA dispersion contours / H2S 
Zoning study and Area risk assessment study recommendations. 
Alarm from fixed detectors must be set at 10 ppm for warning (HIGH) and at the toxic gas 
emergency level of 15 ppm for immediate evacuation (HIGH-HIGH). Fire and Gas mapping 
is the tool to determine the locations and reliability of the fixed H2S detectors. 
11.3 Portable multi-gas detectors  
Portable multi gas detectors shall be used as per risk assessment requirements or 
wherever fixed gas detectors are not adequate. Multi gas detectors shall be calibrated as 
per manufacturer recommendations. It should have an alarm level of minimum +15 dB 
more than the noise level of the location, where it used. Alarm from the detector must be



 
Appropriate provision for explosion prevention and consequence mitigations must

 
Appropriate ventilation and PPEs are provided for the solid Sulphur storage

17.4 Minute Ventilation Rate (MVR) 
Air supply and replacement air cylinders will be required for long duration search and 
rescue emergencies hence the amount of air required needs to be considered during 
emergency planning.   
The amount of air breathed by a person is measured as Minute Ventilation Rate. The 
standard MVR for an adult is most commonly quoted as 5 to 8 litres/ minute when resting, 
but this value can raise up to 19 when moving and up to 30 when exercising.  
The MVR used for determining the capacity of EEBD, SCBA, design of Airline fed BA air 
supplies and for safe havens is by standard 35 litres/ minute.

18. SELECTION OF RPE FOR FACIAL HAIR WEARERS 
A number of employees have facial hair which together with the facilities operating with 
Hydrogen Sulphide in concentrations exceeding fatal concentration demands that RPE be 
effective and available to all. It is essential that in selecting RPE that careful consideration 
be made to those individuals who may use the selected RPE that the RPE is suitable and 
offers their respiratory organs as much protection as possible. 
Facial hair as well as other surface related facial characteristics such as scars, wrinkles or 
jewellery can significantly affect the expected protection due to interference with the seal 
against the cheeks and chin portions of the face. RPE incorporating a hood design with 
fitting adjustment are more suitable for use by facial hair wearers. Facial hair refers to 
unshaven hair which has not been shaved within a day period prior to commencing work 
up to a full beard of no more than 20cm in length from the base of the chin. 
18.1 Certifications and Selection 
The general principles for selection, use and maintenance of RPE are outlined in ADNOC 
Codes of Practice on Personal Protective Equipment and Provision of Fire Service 
Equipment. These Codes of Practice refer specifically to ‘Tight Fitting’ RPE for clean 
shaven use.  
A specification of the Code of Practice requires manufacturers to provide all proper 
marking and CE marking of the apparatus and application of certification under existing BS 
EN standards 136 & 137. BS EN standard for inward leakage is superseded by ISO16900-
1. This standard shall be followed by manufacturers who shall be required to provide 
certified test results of the inward leakage test having used test subjects with facial hair. 
18.2 Selection Based on RPE Types 
The primary purpose of the RPE is to form a barrier between the wearer’s respiratory tract 
and the contaminated environment. There are two types of RPE: 
 
Tight fitting - Full or half face masks rely on a good seal around the forehead, 
cheek bones and under the chin. RPE certified under BS EN136: 1998 meet 
ADNOC requirement and all manufacturers currently stipulate that these are for 
use by clean shaven wearers only. 
 
Loose fitting – these have a hood with integrated internal oral nasal masks (½ 
masks) or full face mask. The integrated oral nasal type only provides a seal 
around the neck of the wearer, whereas the latter with integrated full face mask 
provide two potential seals. A primary seal on the face and a secondary seal 
around the neck of the wearer. This design is more suitable for wearers with 
facial hair including full beards, it also operates effectively with clean shaven 
wearers 
18.3 RPE Adequacy 
Any form of RPE can only be considered adequate if it has the capacity to reduce the 
wearer’s exposure to a hazard to acceptable levels (e.g. to comply with occupational 
exposure limit values). The adequacy assessment of RPE for facial hair wearers must 
evaluate if the inward leakage is sufficiently limited to offer the required protection to 
maintain the wearers supplied air below the recommended WEL for H2S exposure.

Refer ADNOC TGN on selecting RPE for Facial Hair Wearers for RPE protection and

19.  EYE-GLASSES (PRESCRIPTION GLASSES) 
Wearing eye-glasses can be incompatible with some forms of respiratory protection. 
Personnel which may need to wear respiratory protection and who normally wear glasses, 
either must be provided with suitable respiratory protection which is compatible with 
wearing eye-glasses, or must be given suitable respiratory protection containing 
prescription lenses. 
If neither of these is practicable, such personnel must not carry out activities which may 
require the use of respiratory protection, including for emergency escape. 
20. TOXIC GAS REFUGES (TGR)  
Each site shall evaluate the need for TGRs based on toxic gas release scenarios as 
described and mapped on the site QRA report. Preference shall be given to converting the 
existing buildings such as IES, Substations, Operator shelters etc. into TGRs. The Facility 
Response Plan shall have a reference on the use of these TGRs. 
For plants where large no. of personnel are expected to work in amber zone, a temporary 
toxic gas refuge shall be made during the entire span of the activity, based on the risk 
assessment. 
21. AUDITING OF H2S  
Each Plant and Pipeline sites should develop dedicated H2S Management Audit plans 
which should cover all different H2S zones int different Areas. These audits shall be 
conducted to review the implementation of this standard at sites. 
A Self-Assessment compliance tool is introduced in ALL new HSE standards. Its primary 
purpose is to verify the level of compliance of this Standard and where identified gaps can 
be addressed. The adoption of such a tool by ALL Sites is going to assist in delivering 
consistency in HSE compliance to this Standard. This self-compliance assessment shall be 
conducted once in a year, as a minimum.  
A self-assessment compliance tool is attached in Appendix E. 
22. ENFORCEMENT 
Personnel who   do not adhere to the requirements of this standard are not only putting 
their own lives at risk, but also those of their colleagues, emergency service and others 
working at site. Personnel removed from any GASCO facility for violation of this standard 
must be prohibited from entering all GASCO facilities.  Organisations, groups, contractors 
or individual personnel who repeatedly fail to meet the requirements of this Standard must 
not be considered for future projects, contracts or employment across the GASCO 
facilities.

APPENDIX B- CHARACTERISTICS OF HYDROGEN SULPHIDE 
Hydrogen Sulphide is a colourless flammable gas, which burns with a pale-blue flame forming 
Sulphur Dioxide and water vapour.  It has a very offensive odour similar to that of rotten eggs, 
which can be detected at concentrations between 0.0002 to 0.3 ppm.   
 
The main characteristics of Hydrogen Sulphide are outlined below; 
 
Extremely toxic and colourless gas. 
 
At lower concentrations, it is detectable by its characteristic rotten-egg odour 
 
At higher concentrations it paralyses the sense of smell, therefore one should 
not trust his sense of smell to detect a leak. 
 
Normally present as a gas and not as liquid. 
 
Flammable, auto-ignition temperature of 260° C (500° F) and explosive 
(flammable) range by volume in the air: 4.3% - 46%. 
 
It is soluble in both water and hydrocarbon liquids. Pools of water /oil or sludge 
at the bottom of the tank or pit may contain concentrations of H2S and if agitated 
or heated will release the gas. 
 
It is also corrosive and soluble in water and hydrocarbon liquids and can cause 
severe stress cracking of steel and other metals. 
 
H2S is heavier than air (specific gravity = 1.1895) and can collect in low lying 
areas, especially pits and sumps which are closed in and have poor ventilation. 
 
It burns with a blue flame and gives off Sulphur dioxide (SO2), which is also a 
toxic gas.  
 
It can form pyrophoric Iron Sulphide on internal surfaces of carbon steel 
equipment containing H2S. Pyrophoric Iron Sulphide can auto-ignite when 
coming into contact with air.

Subject: Release direction, height and location 
a) Release direction: The release direction determines the initial direction of the plume and 
also what proportion of the initial velocity is used. The following figure demonstrates 
release directions {(Horizontal, Vertical, Angled from horizontal (+450 and -450) and 
Downward release} will be considered for modelling purpose. The release directions were 
agreed with GASCO, HQ after discussion and review of sample results of selected failure 
cases for plant.  
Figure 2:  Release directions  
 
 The letter “X” represents the direction of release from the release point.  
The directional event frequency distribution will be calculated by distributing failure case 
event frequency amongst all release directions equally by using following calculation basis. 
1) Horizontal, Angled from horizontal (+450 and -450) directional event frequency  
= {[Event frequency]/ [Total number of direction]}*2 
2) Vertical & Downward directional event frequency distribution  
= [Event frequency]/[Total number of direction] 
Note: - For the equipment located at average height of 1m, downward and angled from 
horizontal (-450) direction release directions will not be considered as agreed with GASCO 
during the evaluation of review of sample results of selected failure cases for plant.  In this 
case their directional event frequencies will be equally distributed proportionately amongst 
horizontal, vertical and angled from Horizontal (+450) directional frequencies.

b) Release height: The release height for all equipment except vessels (i.e. column, drum,

Subject: Ignition probability 
The probability of ignition of gas/liquid releases is one of the key input parameters within the 
QRA. 
DNV GL proposes to use OGP ignition probabilities database for execution of H2S Zoning 
study which provide estimates of the probabilities of hydrocarbon releases igniting to result in 
an explosion and/or a sustained fire. OGP data base estimates the ignition probabilities 
based on 28 mathematical functions drawn from the UKOOPA look-up correlation which 
relates ignition probabilities in air to release rate for typical scenarios both onshore and 
offshore.  
The OGP database overall (immediate + delayed) ignition probabilities for varying release 
rates is shown in following figure. These curves represent “total” ignition probability.  
This split i.e. delayed ignition & immediate ignition probability is supplied within the 
PHASTRISK ignition model.

Subject: Leak Frequency Analysis  
DNV GL proprietary software LEAK was used for the leak frequency calculation of process 
equipment and process piping. The leak frequencies for the main process equipment items 
are based on an analysis of the HSE hydrocarbon release database (HCRD) for the period of 
1992-2010. The LEAK software frequency calculations are consistent with OGP 434-1 
Guideline for leak frequency analysis. 
 
Definition of Leak types (Full releases, Limited releases and Zero pressure and Total 
releases) as per OGP 434-1 is presented below: 
 Full releases: consistent with flow through the defined hole, beginning at the normal 
operating pressure, and continuing until controlled by emergency shut-down and 
blowdown (if present and operable) or inventory exhaustion.  
 Limited releases: cases where the pressure is not zero but the quantity released is 
much less than from a full release. This may be because the release is isolated locally 
by human intervention (e.g. closing an inadvertently opened valve), or by a restriction 
in the flow from the system inventory (e.g. releases of fluid accumulated between 
pump shaft seals).  
 Zero pressure releases: cases where pressure inside the leaking equipment is 
virtually zero (0.01 barg or less). This may be because the equipment has a normal 
operating pressure of zero (e.g. open drains), or because the equipment has been 
depressurised for maintenance.  
 Total Releases:  This is the overall frequencies for all release types (Full, Limited 
Zero) taken together. 
As advised by GASCO, zero pressure leaks were ignored (discounted) during leak frequency 
calculation to avoid overestimation of H2S zones.
