1. 
INTRODUCTION 
Pollution Prevention and Control (PPC) is a term used to describe a regulatory system for control of 
pollution from certain industrial processes and activities, which is based on an operator demonstrating 
that: 
 
 
The Best Available Techniques (BAT) are being, or will be, used to prevent and minimize pollution 
from the installation; and  
 
No pollution takes place that causes significant adverse impact on the receiving environment.   
Pollution Prevention embraces a wide scope of activities including that: 
 
 
All appropriate preventative measures are taken against pollution; 
 
Waste production is avoided or, where waste is produced, it is recovered or where this is 
technically or economically impossible, it is disposed of while avoiding or reducing impacts on the 
environment; 
 
Energy is used efficiently; 
 
The necessary measures are taken to prevent environmental accidents and limit their 
consequences; 
 
The necessary measures are taken upon definitive cessation of activities to avoid any pollution 
risk and return the site of operation to a satisfactory state. 
ADNOC is committed towards PPC implementation is accordance with the National Strategy and 
international best practice.  
 
Appendix 1 presents the types of processes or facilities that would normally be required to demonstrate 
their conformance with BAT through a preparation of the PPC Compliance Report (see Section 7.2). PPC 
Compliance Reports must be prepared by the Group Companies and submitted to ADNOC Group HSE 
Function once every 5 years. 
2. 
PURPOSE 
This PPC Standard provides environmental performance requirements for all ADNOC Group facilities.  
Facilities that have or will commence operations prior to this date will be required to meet the relevant 
Federal laws outlined in Appendix 4 of this standard, so long as in doing so the operators can demonstrate 
the relevant Federal EQS is not exceeded.  
 
The benefits of applying new performance standards consistent with BAT are as follows: 
 
 
Operators do not need to articulate their case for a particular BAT solution to prevent or control 
pollution. Applying the new performance standard by proxy infers an operator has applied a 
technology / technique consistent with BAT; 
 
The new performance standards have been achieved for some time internationally, hence 
technology needed to meet these standards should not infer excessive cost, additional safety 
concerns, or considerable cross media effects;  
 
Having all ADNOC GCs applying the same standards presents a ‘level playing field’ across group 
companies with regards to environmental performance; and 
 
The new performance standards will allow ADNOC to fulfil its objective of minimizing harm to the 
environment and communities within the UAE.

REQUIREMENTS  
7.1. 
OVERVIEW OF PPC PROCESS REQUIREMENTS  
PPC is a regulatory approach to controlling the environmental impacts of certain industrial sources, based 
on the prevention of pollution or, where this is not possible or practicable, the minimization of pollution 
using the BAT.  BAT can be summarized as follows: 
 
Best: 
Means in relation to techniques, the most effective in achieving a high general level of 
protection of the environment as a whole. 
Available: 
Means those techniques which have been developed on a scale which allows 
implementation in the relevant industrial sector, under economically and technically 
viable conditions, taking into consideration the cost and advantages, providing they are 
reasonably accessible to the operator. 
Techniques: 
Includes both the technology used and the way in which the installation is designed, 
built, operated and decommissioned. 
 
PPC takes an integrated approach to management of environmental impacts.  This means that emissions 
to air, water and land, together with a number of other environmental factors, must be considered together, 
so that a high level of environmental protection is achieved overall.  
 
Whilst the concept of PPC is now firmly based on modern environmental regulatory processes in many 
parts of the world, its application requires significant expertise in many disciplines, both within Group 
Company (GC) HSE teams as well as ADNOC itself in its capacity as the regulatory body.   
 
Applying the PPC principle, in simple terms, promotes the concept of adopting emission limits, discharge 
standards or environmental quality standards (EQSs) that are normally lower than Federal limits by 
capitalising on modern advancements in control technology, where this technology is industry tested, cost 
effective, and does not create significant alternative pollution streams as a result of its operation (i.e. 
cross media effects).   
 
Group Companies shall ensure that their activities comply with all relevant Federal and Local laws and 
regulations at all times. This infers, as a minimum, all group companies establish their compliance with 
Federal Laws, presenting the findings in the cyclic 5-year PPC Report, using testing methods and models 
described in ADNOC Standards including ADNOC Environmental Performance Monitoring Standard, 
HSE-EN-ST05 and ADNOC Air Dispersion Modelling Techniques Standard, HSE-EN-ST07. It is the 
responsibility of the Group Companies to demonstrate the compliance of their units against the applicable 
Federal and other regulatory obligations. 
7.1.1. BACKGROUND TO BAT 
The basic approach to PPC involves identifying options, assessing the environmental effects and 
considering economics.  It differs from other regulatory approaches in that the environmental 
requirements are defined at the facility level, rather than on a general basis. 
 
PPC requires an operator to demonstrate that the BAT have been used to control discharges of pollutants 
to all environmental media and that there are no significant residual environmental impacts. 
 
BAT covers both the plant and how it is operated.  In particular, operation is taken to include: 
 
 
Management; 
 
Management systems; 
 
Staff numbers;

7.1.2. ADDRESSING EXISTING FACILITIES 
ADNOC operates a diverse and significant range of oil and gas assets in the Abu Dhabi Emirate. It’s 
earliest assets in operation today were established over 40 years ago. It is inevitable that older assets 
could not have capitalized on modern BAT at inception, and therefore attaining an operational standing 
in line with modern BAT requires retrofitting pollution control technology. This normally requires far greater 
investment for its successful implementation when compared to integrating BAT into new projects.

a number of challenges: 
 
 
Costs, both Operational Expenditure (OPEX) and Capital Expenditure (CAPEX) for retrofitting 
abatement. In some instances, retrofitting abatement may not be possible to achieve a

that the gas phase is only flared. Where possible, priority shall be given to connections to nearby existing 
facility. The combustion of the oil phase is prohibited within ADNOC. Provisions shall be made for the 
collection of liquids arising from the process which may include crude or produced water. For activities 
near populated areas, potential impacts to the surrounding community shall be assessed during the EIA 
process. 
7.4.2. ONSHORE DRILLING WASTE 
Preference should be given to water-based drilling muds were possible. In the event specific geological 
conditions determine the need for non-aqueous, the fluid shall be selected based on the disposal method 
selected. All of the disposal methods have specific requirements and limitations, and these, as well as 
the characteristics of the drilling waste, need to be considered when selecting a method. The disposal 
methods addressed include: 
 
 
Storage of drilling waste on a well site, on a pipeline right-of-way, or at a remote site; 
 
Landspray, landspray while drilling, disposal onto public lands, and pump-off of drilling waste onto 
surface soils, typically occurring outside the well site boundaries; 
 
Landspread and mix-bury-cover of drilling wastes into subsoils, occurring on a well site, on a 
pipeline right-of-way, or at a remote site; 
 
Biodegradation of drilling wastes, which includes land treatment or biodegradation in a contained 
system, occurring on a well site or at a remote site; 
 
Subsurface disposal of drilling waste down a well that is in the process of being drilled; and 
 
Sending drilling waste to approved waste management facilities and the use of mobile thermal 
treatment units to manage drilling waste. 
7.4.3. UNCONVENTIONAL DRILLING

environment. Key considerations presented below. 
(a) 
Effective underground water protection 


isolation; 
 
Produce detailed cement bond log or cement evaluation log for each well that shows the 
adequacy of cementing; and 
 
Avoid the use of diesel or benzene when passing through potable aquifers. 
(b) 
Effective soil and groundwater management 
 
Ensure sufficient containment is available for produced water; 
 
Ensure sufficient containment is available for drill cuttings; 
 
Use of smooth double lined pits that are regularly inspected and maintained;

7.6. 
ACCIDENTS & THEIR CONSEQUENCES 
As a general principle, PPC requires that necessary measures must be taken to prevent accidents that 
may have environmental consequences, and to limit those consequences.  Accident in this context 
includes any abnormal operation which may increase emissions. 
 
Some installations falling within the scope of this Standard will also be subject to control under the 
COMAH regime [Ref. 10].  In such cases, the site COMAH Report may provide the necessary 
documentation to satisfy the accident provisions of PPC.  However, it is possible that potential accident 
scenarios may be too small to fall within the scope of COMAH, but may still have significant environmental 
consequences.  Small-scale accidents that may have significant environmental consequences must 
therefore also be considered.

7.8. 
EXEMPTIONS 
7.8.1. NEW FACILITIES 
In the event that the design of a facility, equipment or process cannot comply with the ADNOC PPC 
requirements, an application for exemption must be submitted to ADNOC GHSE. The application must 
be supported with an independent technical study demonstrating that there is no potential for significant 
impacts to the receiving environment arising from the proposed exemption. It must be noted that Federal 
requirements shall be met at all times and no exemption may be sought for relaxation of Federal

7.8.2. EXISTING FACILITIES

Standards or other applicable regulation, an application for exemption must be submitted to ADNOC 
GHSE. The GC shall develop an action plan to support the application. The purpose of the action plan is 
to provide a framework of progression towards compliance. The action plan shall demonstrate the current 
conditions, the extent of non-compliance, the age of specific equipment/process and remaining useful life, 
key actions to be taken and key action dates. Action plans may be supported by independent 
environmental studies such as surveys or quantitative modelling.

INTERNATIONAL STANDARDS  
INTRODUCTION 
This appendix provides information on relevant international environmental standards and guidelines, which 
Group Companies must take into account when preparing PPC Compliance Reports, in accordance with the 
ADNOC Standard on Pollution Prevention and Control.  These international environmental standards should also 
be taken into account during preparation of Environmental Impact Statements, in accordance with ADNOC EIA 
Standard, HSE-EN-ST01 [Ref.4]. 
It is important to distinguish between environmental standards, which regulate the quantity of a particular pollutant 
permitted to be discharged to the environment, and standards which are designed to control environmental quality 
(ambient standards).  The former are generally referred to as source or emission standards, whilst the latter are 
typically called environmental quality standards/criteria.  Environmental Quality Standards (EQS) may be further
