1. 
INTRODUCTION 
Reporting on environmental performance is an essential element of each Group Company’s (GCs) Health, 
Safety and Environmental Management System (HSEMS) and Abu Dhabi National Oil Companies 
(ADNOC’s) Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) assurance process. It provides the mechanism for 
checking that environmental policies and corporate targets are being achieved and also enables 
corrective actions to be focused on areas where they are most needed. It also provides key information 
on environmental performance to ADNOC and GC Senior Management, including information on the 
overall goal of continual improvement.  
 
In order for environmental reporting to be of value, source data must be accurate and collected 
systematically, including appropriate quality assurance (QA) procedures. The purpose of this Standard is 
to establish the requirements for such systematic collection and reporting on environmental performance, 
throughout the ADNOC Group. The environmental performance data collected by Group Companies, 
together with health and safety performance data will be used to compile the annual Group–wide HSE 
Status Report and ADNOC Performance Report. 
 
In a wider context, there has been a growing trend in the oil and gas industry in recent years for more 
transparency in its environmental affairs, including the sharing and publishing of information on 
environmental affairs. ADNOC fully supports these initiatives and participates in environmental data 
sharing initiatives through e.g. the International Association of Oil and Gas Producers (OGP) 
Environmental Performance Indicators survey, Statistics Centre of Abu Dhabi (SCAD) and Environment 
Agency – Abu Dhabi (EAD) Emissions Inventory. Adherence to the requirements of this Standard will 
enable ADNOC to continue to support this global initiative. 
2. 
PURPOSE 
This Standard presents the procedures for conducting environmental monitoring for Group Companies 
and describes the thresholds, where necessary, for undertaking monitoring. This Standard also sets out 
the format and content of the environmental reporting as well as the expected frequencies. Environmental 
reporting is the means by which pollutant loading and regulatory compliance is communicated. 
  
Environmental monitoring and reporting forms an integral part of a Group Companies Pollution, 
Prevention and Control (PPC) approach, and hence this standard should be read in conjunction with the 
ADNOC Pollution, Prevention and Control (PPC) Standard, HSE-EN-ST02 [Ref. 1]. 
3. 
SCOPE 
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates & 
Functions at headquarters (HQ), Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors.  
 
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood, 
implemented and thoroughly monitored.

7.1.1. AIR QUALITY 
(a) 
Combustion and Process Emissions – Stack Testing Requirements  
ADNOC is currently reviewing its air emissions monitoring strategy. The below requirements should 
be verified with ADNOC prior to the implementation of a new stack testing regime. Group 
Companies shall, at a minimum, measure for the applicable parameters that are regulated in by 
Federal Law and ADNOC on an annual basis where emissions have the potential to exceed 50 
tonnes of criteria pollutant per annum.  
 
New facilities shall undertake initial performance testing of all point sources within 60 days of the 
facility achieving the normal production rate or within 180 days of initial start-up, whichever is earlier. 
The parameters to be determined shall be determined based on the potential to emit more than 50 
tonnes of pollutant per year. 
 
All emission testing shall be conducted by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 
17025 accredited and reported in accordance with methods and procedures that are approved by 
the US EPA or European Standards (EN). All Performance Guarantee tests including other stack 
testing shall be conducted as per technical supplier specifications under conditions of maximum 
designated use (max. design capacity) of the affected source. Operations during periods of 
shutdowns, start-up, and malfunctions shall not constitute representative conditions for the purpose 
of the emission tests. 
 
Group Companies shall notify ADNOC at least fourteen (14) days prior to any emission testing 
required ADNOC may elect to witness all or part of the required emission test. The electronic raw 
data (or hard copy) shall be initialled by all parties at the end of each run. The operator of a facility 
shall, within 60 days of conducting any required emission testing, submit a written report to ADNOC. 
The report shall contain all applicable information as given below: 
 
 
Analytical Data - Pollutants, moisture content, oxygen content; 
 
Physical Data - Flow rate, stack emission velocity, temperature, pressure, stack dimensions, 
isokinetic flow conditions; 
 
Process operating conditions at time of testing; 
 
One example calculation for each method; 
 
Emission results with raw data (electronic or hard copy) - emission data in same unit as the 
relevant emission standard in ADNOC PPC Standard, HSE-EN-ST02, mass emission data 
and concentration emission data; and 
 
Calibration. 
All Group Companies shall undertake additional emission tests deemed necessary by ADNOC to 
demonstrate the facility's current compliance status. ADNOC may waive or reduce the frequency 
of the emission testing requirements GC can demonstrate to the satisfaction of ADNOC that the 
source is consistently in compliance with the applicable standards.

For US EPA Method 21, a portable instrument is used to detect volatile organic compound (VOC) 
leaks from individual sources. The instrument detector type is not specified, but it must meet the 
specifications and performance criteria contained in Section 6.0 of the standard. A leak definition 
concentration based on a reference compound is specified in each applicable regulation. This 
method is intended to locate and classify leaks only and is not to be used as a direct measure of 
mass emission rate from individual sources. 
 
SLDAR consist of a hybrid methodology where an IR camera will be used for the initial detection 
and the later leak quantification will be measured with an FID. SLDAR technology (using IR camera 
joint with FID) has been approved by the US EPA as an alternative to traditional Method 21 LDAR 
(using just FID). The main advantages of the optical method (using IR camera) are the speed in 
finding leaks, efficiency and reliability as well as the safest sampling conditions, due to the greatest 
possible distance for the realization of remote sensing, which in some cases can be of to 50 m, 
thus avoiding risks arising from work so high, hot surfaces, confined or inaccessible, spaces etc.. 
Advancements in IR camera technology have resulted in a small, lightweight, passive IR camera 
that is able to make large hydrocarbon leaks (that is, normally leaks greater than 5,000 ppm visible 
to the camera operator. As remote sensing does not allow today's mass quantification of leaks, the 
joint use of an FID or toxic vapor analyser (TVA) that measures the concentration of the identified 
leakage following the procedures in Method 21 of the US EPA is usually recommended.

of new facilities to determine compliance with the relevant environmental noise standards presented in 
ADNOC PPC Standard, HSE-EN-ST02.

7.1.4. MARINE SEDIMENT 
Monitoring of marine sediment should be undertaken during the five-year EIA lifecycle to determine 
whether there has been any perceivable change to the receiving environment. The relevant parameters 
to be monitored must be determined based on the nature of marine discharges arising from a facility and 
the area of influence. The parameters should be compared with the sediment standards outlined in 
ADNOC PPC Standard, HSE-EN-ST02.  
7.1.5. SOIL 
Soil quality monitoring is not required during normal operations of a facility. Soil may require monitoring 
after spill events or in areas where there are discharges to soil. The relevant standards outlined in ADNOC 
PPC Standard, HSE-EN-ST02 shall be used. ADNOC may request collection and analysis of soil samples 
by Group Companies. Areas of potential contamination should be monitored at least once in every two 
years.  
7.1.6. GROUNDWATER 
Group Companies shall install an adequate number of permanent groundwater monitoring wells prior to 
facility operation. These wells shall be located so that representative samples of the groundwater that 
may be impacted by the proposed facility operations can be obtained. The number of wells, design and 
installation of any groundwater monitoring wells shall be presented in the EIA process. 
 
Group Companies with groundwater monitoring wells installed shall monitor the groundwater quality 
annually and report the results to ADNOC. The specific parameters to be monitored and reported shall 
be approved by ADNOC. The facility shall provide the ADNOC 14 working days prior notice to monitoring 
and ADNOC may elect to witness all or part of the required groundwater sampling and collected samples 
may also be shared with ADNOC. 
 
Group Companies shall conduct baseline groundwater monitoring and sample analyses prior to operation 
of their facility and shall submit a comprehensive baseline groundwater monitoring report to ADNOC. The 
specific parameters to be monitored and reported shall be determined during the EIA for the Project.
