1. 
INTRODUCTION 
Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC), as part of its Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) policy, 
commits to ensure that a safe working environment is maintained in all areas and that all personnel who 
undertake use, storage, handling, transport and disposal of hazardous substances, are fully aware of the 
hazards involved and have the competence, training and awareness to undertake such activities. 
 
Different chemical products are used regularly throughout the Oil and Gas industry for a variety of 
purposes. The range of these products is huge and includes as typical examples: fuels, lubricants, acids, 
paints, solvents, corrosion inhibitors, biocides, cleaning agents and powders. Many of these chemical 6 
products, which are toxic to humans and/or can seriously affect the environment, are considered to be 
hazardous substances. They can cause damage when they enter the human body by ingestion, direct 
skin or eye contact, absorption through the skin and inhalation of gas, vapor, mist or dust. They can also 
have an adverse effect on animal and plant life. 
 
This Standard describes the hazards and necessary control measures for limiting the effects with respect 
to health and safety of personnel, assets and/or environment due to the use, storage, handling, transport 
and disposal of hazardous substances within the ADNOC Group. 
 
2. 
PURPOSE 
The purpose of this Standard is to establish and maintain systems of work that ensure protecting the 
health and safety of ADNOC employees and its contractors from the harmful effects of substances 
hazardous to health. This shall be achieved by: 
 
(i) 
Ensuring employees are trained in the use and storage of hazardous materials; 
(ii) 
Enabling them to be fully aware of the risks involved and precautions to be taken; and 
(iii) 
Ensuring assessments are made to determine the risk to health and the environment. 
3. 
SCOPE 
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates & 
Functions in ADNOC Head Quarter, Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors. 
 
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood, 
implemented and thoroughly monitored.

7.1.1. IDENTIFICATION OF HAZARDOUS SUBSTANCES 
Hazardous substances are materials with the potential to cause harm to human health and the 
environment. Hazardous substances shall assess and classify the substances in line with ADNOC 
Chemical Hazards Standard [Ref. 16] and international model regulations (e.g. Globally Harmonized 
System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals), laboratory analysis, expert judgement, and/or 
weight of evidence to determine if materials create a: [Ref. 1] 
 
(i) 
Physical Hazard, for e.g.; 
1. 
Flammable Solid/Liquid/Gas; 
2. 
Oxidizing Solid/Liquid/Gas;  
3. 
Corrosive to metal; and 
4. 
Pyrophoric Solid/Liquids. 
(ii) 
Health Hazard, for e.g.; 
1. 
Acute Toxicity; 
2. 
Carcinogen; 
3. 
Skin Sensitizer; and 
4. 
Target organ toxicant – repeated/single exposure. 
(iii) 
Environmental Hazard, for e.g.; 
1. 
Hazardous to the aquatic environment –acute/chronic; and 
2. 
Hazardous to the ozone layer. 
(iv) 
Other hazard. For e.g.; 
1. 
Cryogenic; and 
2. 
Radioactive. 
Hazardous Substances can exist in various forms such as Biological Agents, Dust, Fumes, Gases, 
Liquids, Mist, Fog, Smoke, Vapor and Chemical Agents. Details on the forms and classification of 
hazardous substances are provided in the ADNOC Chemical Hazards Standard [Ref. 16]. 
 
The hazard communication system shall be in line with the ADNOC Hazard Communication Standard. 
[Ref. 21]. 
 
Exposure to hazardous substances in the workplace can lead to a variety of short term and long term 
health effects such as poisoning, skin rashes and disorder of the lung, kidney and liver. Potential exposure 
to hazardous substances may cause harm to human health and the environment; therefore, it is important 
that ADNOC employees and its contractors know the risks that could result from exposure and how to 
minimize the risks.

7.1.3. HIERARCHY OF CONTROLS 
The hierarchy of controls is a systematic approach to managing safety in the workplace by providing a 
structure to select the most effective control measures to eliminate or reduce the risk of certain hazards 
that have been identified as being caused by the activities. While undertaking a risk assessment for the 
activity, the following hierarchy of controls shall be adopted. (Refer to Figure 7.1.1)

(a) 
Elimination 
This includes removing the hazard from the workplace. This is the most effective control measure 
and shall always be considered before any other control measure. For example, redesign the job 
or substitute a substance so that the hazard is removed or eliminated. If eliminating the hazard is 
not reasonably practicable, then substitution, isolation, engineering and procedural control 
measures, or a combination of these control measures to minimize the risk shall be considered. 
For example, find alternative process that removes the need for the hazardous substances or 
chemical or eliminate an activity that increases the risks. 
 
(b) 
Substitution:  
Minimize the risk by substituting or replacing a hazard or hazardous work practice with a safer one. 
For example: 
 
(i) 
Find a less dangerous material to use. Use detergent instead of chlorinated solvent for 
cleaning. Use water-based chemicals instead of solvent-based, use less hazardous 
substances where compatible to the task; and 
(ii) 
Use safer form (e.g. pellet v/s powder) or process, paint with a brush instead of spraying, 
purchase a substance in a safer form. 
(c) 
Isolate/Separate 
Minimize the risk by isolating or separating the hazard or hazardous work practice from personnel. 
For example: 
 
(i) 
Consider separation from other dangerous goods; 
(ii) 
Isolate the process to one room with restricted access or use appropriate barriers/screens 
to separate substances; and 
(iii) 
Distance workers from substances/processes through the use of remote controls. 
(d) 
Engineer 
This includes engineering the control measures to minimize risk. For example:

(i) 
Designing buildings and plant to relieve and redirect pressure and flame in the event that an 
explosion occurs; 
(ii)

7.2.2. KEEPING HAZARDOUS SUBSTANCES STABLE 
It shall be ensured, so far as is reasonably practicable that hazardous substances do not become 
unstable, decompose or change so as to create a hazard different to the hazard originally created by the 
hazardous chemical or significantly increase the risk associated with any hazard in relation to the 
hazardous chemical.  
 
Some hazardous substances are inherently unstable or highly reactive, or they can become unstable 
under certain conditions during use, often in a deliberate process. This is mainly for hazardous 
substances that are dangerous goods, however other hazardous substances may also present a risk if 
stability is not maintained. The following shall be ensured for keeping hazardous chemical stable: 
 
(i) 
Follow manufacturer’s recommendation or instructions on the SDS; 
(ii) 
Maintain specified proportions of ingredients, goods and other components that constitute 
the hazardous substances, for example phlegmatizers, diluents, solvents, wetting agents, 
desensitisers, inhibitors and/or other adulterants; 
(iii) 
Include a stabilising ingredient where appropriate; 
(iv) 
Keep the hazardous substances within any control temperature range where necessary; and

7.2.3. IMPACT PROTECTION 
To prevent damage from the movement of the structure or plant including any attached pipe work or 
equipment, ensure that structures or plant used for the storage or handling of hazardous substances are 
appropriately located and fixed to stable foundations. 
 
Measures required for preventing or controlling impact normally depends on the nature of risks. Impact 
protection measures are necessary for: 
 
(i) 
Structures containing large quantities of hazardous substances; 
(ii) 
Plant and equipment including storage and process vessels, associated pipe work, pumps 
and controls; 
(iii) 
Storage areas (including transit storage) for packages, Intermediate Bulk Containers (IBCs) 
and associated shelves and racks; and 
(iv) 
Exposed parts of the fire protection systems. 
7.2.4. CONTAINING SPILLS 
It shall be ensured, so far as is reasonably practicable that where there is a risk of a spill or leak of a 
hazardous substance in a solid or liquid form, provision is made in each part of the workplace where a 
hazardous substance is used, handled, stored or generated for a spill containment system that contains 
within the workplace any spill or leak of a hazardous chemical and any resulting effluent. 
 
When a spill, leak or accidental release of hazardous substances occurs, appropriate actions shall be 
taken to contain the hazardous substances within the workplace (Refer Table 7.7.1). 
 
Any spill containment system shall be large enough to ensure that all spills can be held safely until cleaned 
up. Factors to be considered when designing a spill containment system includes but not limited to the 
following:  
 
(i) 
The nature of the hazardous substances (whether liquid or solid);  
(ii) 
The quantity of the hazardous substances; 
(iii) 
The size of the largest container or reasonably foreseeable largest spill; 
(iv) 
The potential impact if the hazardous substances escape to the environment; 
(v) 
Whether it is necessary to provide for the management of firewater at an incident; 
(vi) 
A separate spill containment is provided for incompatible goods; 
(vii) 
The materials used to construct the containment system, as well as any materials used for 
absorption, are compatible with the hazardous substances; 
(viii) Other materials in the vicinity that will prevent contamination of groundwater or soil; and  
(ix) 
The system’s integrity will be maintained in any reasonably foreseeable incident.

The Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (GHS) is the unified single 
system adopted under the auspices of the United Nations to address the classification of chemicals, 
labels, Safety Data Sheets (SDS). 
 
The United Nations Economic and Social Council’s Sub-Committee of experts on the Globally 
Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (UNSCEGHS) is responsible for 
maintaining the GHS.  
 
UNSCEGHS provides guidance as needs arise and maintains the stability of the GHS by ensuring it is 
regularly revised and updated to reflect national, regional and international experiences in implementing 
requirements into national, regional and international laws, as well as experiences of those doing the 
classification and labelling. [Ref. 1]

(c)

7.4. 
PROCUREMENT  
Before the purchase of any hazardous substance, it shall be subject to a review of its suitability for use 
in the context of the HSE risks under normal and emergency conditions. 
 
Prior to the requesting of any hazardous substances, the SDS shall be reviewed. The procurement 
request shall also be reviewed and approved. Any additional information required to facilitate the review 
and approval of the new substances shall be furnished.  
 
The review shall include consideration of current SDS information and any other relevant data, including 
quantity of material to be used, potential exposure routes and risks, the specific PPEs to be used while 
handling the substances and to ensure that these PPEs are available at site as soon as the substances 
reaches the warehouse. The review shall be carried out by competent person. 
 
Additionally, it shall address potential issues related to the storage, handling, use and disposal of 
hazardous substances. The main focus shall be on localized impacts with the objective of eliminating or 
minimizing the hazards. 
 
If the result of the review shows that there are unacceptable HSE risks associated with the substance, 
the requester shall be notified in writing that the chemical is not to be ordered. 
 
If the product is acceptable then the Purchase Order shall be raised, which shall be accompanied by a 
copy of the SDS and shall be passed for processing by the relevant procurement or purchasing authority.

(iii)

(iv)

(v)

7.7.5. TYPES OF STORAGE 
(a) 
Rack Storage  
In small warehouses, racks shall be used in which the transport or individual containers are placed 
in storage or removed by hand. Racks and walk-through racks shall also be used for order picking 
of smaller packaging units. Handling of chemicals above head-height shall be banned for safety 
reasons. Steps and ladders or storage retrieval machines shall be used in the case of higher racks. 
Fragile packages shall be protected against falling down. This can be achieved with upturned shelf 
edges, small trays, special shelf inserts and spacing away from the shelf edge. 
 
The storage facilities of larger warehouses shall be divided into pallet, mobile-rack, walk-through, 
drive-through and high-bay stores. Individual shelving units shall be formed into shelving aisles. 
These aisles shall consist of single or double shelves. Push-through protectors shall be provided 
in the case of safety spaces of less than 0.1 m between the pallets on double shelves. . 
Pallet storage shall be carried out by industrial trucks (fork-lift trucks) or storage retrieval machines. 
When placing pallets into storage, attention shall be paid that: 
(i) 
The contours of the pallet units are retained (no overhang by packaging slipping out of place); 
(ii) 
The pallet weight does not exceed specifications for field loads; and 
(iii) 
Specific features for individual shelving points or levels such as restricted height or width 
because of hindrances (e.g. sprinkler pipes or ventilation shafts) are observed. 
The pallets shall be placed straight and central on the shelving beams or shelving areas. Opened 
pallets (commissioning pallets) should preferably be stored on the lowest shelving level and not on 
upper shelving levels. If shelves are being serviced by industrial trucks (except track-bound), the 
corner areas shall be protected by collision guards. The collision guard, at least 0.3 m high, shall 
not be connected to the shelf and shall have black-yellow marking. The maximum permitted shelf 
loads shall be clearly marked on the storage shelves. Any damage to shelf supports or beams 
during storage or removal shall be reported immediately so that the extent of the damage can be 
assessed without delay. If necessary, loads shall be removed from the shelf immediately and the 
damage parts replaced. 
 
(b) 
Block Storage and Line Storage 
Unlike shelf storage, pallets in block warehouses shall be placed straight on the floor. Block storage 
is suitable for large quantities of identical products as access is only available to the pallets at the 
front. This consists of rows of blocks standing next to each other with aisles in between. Permitted 
carrying loads, vertical loads and stacking heights shall not be exceeded when stacking pallets and 
stack-nest containers. Packaging for the transportation of hazardous substances (with the 
exception of sacks, non-stackable combination and large packaging) shall be tested for a stacking 
height of 3 m. 
 
If remaining below the maximum permitted gross mass, the stacking height shall amount to a 
maximum of 6:1 in ratio of height to narrow side of the ground area. The angle of the warehouse 
floor and the wind load if storing outdoors shall be taken into account when stacking.

shall only be stackable if the load is stable and has a horizontal resting surface. If the stack has an

Hazardous Materials in Tanks 
The following list presents some of the special requirements for storing hazardous materials in 
tanks: 
 
(i) 
When choosing the tanks, it is important to consider the characteristics of the hazardous 
materials to be handled. In addition, precautionary measures shall be taken to prevent 
corrosion; 
(ii) 
Secondary containment shall be provided to prevent spillage of hazardous materials into the

(iii)

(b) 
Storing Compressed Gas Cylinders

(c) 
Storing Flammable Materials  
The following list presents some of the special requirements for storing flammable materials: 
(i) 
A warning sign indicating the presence of flammable materials shall be posted outside the 
storage of flammable materials; 
(ii) 
To help minimize the risk of fire, explosion, or another type of incident, proper segregation

incompatible with flammable substances shall be separated as per the compatibility charts. 
For example, flammable materials shall not be stored near oxidizing materials to avoid 
reactions that might cause fire; 
(iii) 
A stock inventory list containing each flammable material stored shall be maintained and 
regularly updated. The inventory list includes the types and quantities of flammable materials 
stored; 
(iv) 
When designing the storage area, the building shall be constructed of a fire-resistant material 
to ensure the containment of fire in case an incident should occur; 
(v) 
Flammable materials shall be stored in a shaded and well-ventilated place that is away from 
any source of ignition; 
(vi) 
Highly flammable materials shall be stored in a cold store; 
(vii) 
A fire extinguishing system that is adequate enough to extinguish small fires shall be 
installed; 
(viii) Fire exit doors shall be rated as fire resistant;

7.7.9. VENTILATION  
(a) 
Ventilation for Flammable Storage 
(i) 
If the storage area is located inside a building, a dedicated room with proper ventilation shall 
be used. It shall be positioned away from other rooms and main work areas or constructed 
from fire-resistant material and be well-ventilated using mechanical ventilation to an outdoor 
area; 
(ii) 
Where flammables are stored within a chemical storage cabinet, any ventilation system shall 
exert fumes to the outside atmosphere and away from any ignition sources or places where 
people are likely to congregate. A suitable mechanical fan shall be constructed of non-
sparking blades and shrouds and be capable of dispersing flammable vapours or gases; 
(iii) 
Ventilation ducting of a flammable cabinet shall provide at least the same level of fire 
protection as the cabinet walls and be resistant to vapours, fumes or dust in the cabinet. 
Steel piping is recommended for ventilation ducting of a cabinet containing flammable 
chemicals; 
(iv) 
PVC ventilation piping shall not be used for a flammables cabinet as it could not withstand 
the excessive temperatures resulting from a fire; and 
(v) 
Flammables vapours are denser than air so the vapours can accumulate at floor level. The 
extraction system shall ensure that the vapours at low level are also extracted. 
(b) 
Ventilation for Toxic Chemicals 
(i) 
Where toxic substances are stored within a chemical storage unit, the ventilation system 
shall be able to sufficiently ensure that the concentration of any toxic substance within the 
breathing zone of any person using the cabinet is as low as possible; 
(ii) 
The cabinet shall be vented to the external atmosphere, i.e. outside the building, in a way 
that allows safe dispersal of vapours, fumes or dusts without entering the room in which the 
cabinet is situated; and 
(iii) 
The circular vents that are installed in the sides of some cabinets can allow toxic or 
flammable vapours to escape into the adjoining area. Therefore, they shall be kept securely 
closed and ensure that they cannot be breached by any pressure that may build up within 
the cabinet. 
(c) 
Ventilation for Corrosive Chemicals 
Where corrosive chemicals are stored in a chemical storage cabinet, the mechanical fan shall be 
constructed of corrosive resistant blades and shrouds and be capable of dispersing corrosive 
vapours, gases, or mists. The ventilation ducting shall provide at least the same level of corrosion 
protection as the cabinet walls and be resistant to any vapours or fumes which may be exerted 
form the chemicals stored in the cabinet.

7.11.1. EMERGENCY SHOWER AND EYE WASH STATIONS 
Emergency eyewash and shower units shall be designed to rinse contaminants from a user’s eyes, face 
or body. As such they are a form of first aid equipment to be used in the event of an incident. However, 
they shall not be considered a substitute for primary protective devices (including eye and face protection 
and protective clothing) or for the use of procedures for the safe handling of hazardous substances. 
 
Eyewash and shower stations must be in accessible locations that require no more than 10 seconds to 
reach. The average distance travelled by most able-bodied persons in 10 seconds is approximately 55 
feet (i.e. approximately 17 meters) [Ref. 8]. Special consideration should be given to employees with 
disabilities, and for work areas with highly injurious materials. The emergency shower shall be located 
on the same level as the hazard and the path of travel shall be free of obstructions that may inhibit its 
immediate use.

materials, suitable facilities for quick drenching or flushing of the eyes and body shall be provided within 
the work area for immediate emergency use. SDS indicate the requirements and same shall be followed.

All equipment shall be inspected annually to ensure the device conforms to installation requirements. 
Annual testing is necessary to ensure the equipment functions properly and to ensure any changes in 
the area have not affected the safe use and operation of the equipment. At a minimum, the equipment 
needs to perform according to the manufacturer’s instructions. This includes functional aspects such as 
placement of the spray heads, shower handle and the equipment itself in relation to the hazard. Site 
evaluation and testing results should be recorded and maintained. [Ref. 11]
