1. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
ADNOC HSE Policy states, “the ADNOC Group is committed to managing risk through effective controls 
and minimising impacts to our business”. 
The ADNOC Risk Assessment and Management framework aims to ensure that Risks are identified, 
analysed, evaluated and treated in a consistent manner and assigned to respective management tier for 
implementation, follow-up and closure. 
Any technical changes made or proposed to be made to the original design basis of an operating plant 
or facility shall be managed in such a way that increased risk is identified, assessed and managed to 
ALARP. 
ADNOC Facilities are designed with best Engineering Principles and Practices according to the ADNOC 
HSE Management System, which requires the design to demonstrate that all Risks are ALARP. However, 
there are instances or events which demand to make changes to the design basis of existing facilities, 
either proactive changes proposed to improve safety or performance, or reactive changes required to 
respond to increased risk introduced by external circumstances or events. A technical change proposal 
is considered whenever there is an opportunity for improving plant HSE and asset integrity; energy and 
resources conservation; reliability, availability and maintainability and operability; profitability or changes 
in product specifications.  
A downgraded situation (DGS) is declared whenever an anomaly, event or situation has been identified 
in an operating plant that temporarily raises the level of risk and thus requires effective mitigations and 
approvals to be in place in order to continue operating until a permanent solution is implemented or the 
situation is resolved.

2. 
PURPOSE 
 
The purpose of the Management of Downgraded Situations standard is to provide instruction on the 
evaluation and mitigation of risk for all occurrences of a downgraded situation, and to ensure risk controls 
are authorized, applied, monitored and removed in a correct and controlled manner. 
The standard sets out a recommended Work Process for the safe, standardized and robust management 
of downgraded situations, as required to ensure risks associated with exposure to hazardous energy 
sources are as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP). 
If anybody involved in the downgraded situations work process is unsure of a course of action, a higher 
authority or management shall be consulted rather than taking risks. 
3. 
SCOPE  
 
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to all ADNOC Group Companies, 
employees, contractors, facilities (including interface areas), activities, services and products without 
exception.  
The scope applies to ADNOC operated production and manufacturing plant, facilities, wells and pipelines. 
Non-critical utilities (e.g. low pressure steam) are excluded from the scope. 
This document sets out minimum requirements that shall be followed across ADNOC Group Companies. 
However, each Group Company may enforce stricter procedures meeting or exceeding the requirements 
of this Standard. 
Contractors shall be responsible for ensuring that all activities performed within ADNOC operating 
facilities in the course of carrying out their contractual requirements are managed in accordance with the 
requirements of this document and reported, when applicable, to the contract holder. 
 
4. 
LAWS AND REGULATIONS 
 
Currently, there are no specific UAE laws applicable to HSE Risk Management. However, laws on 
protection of people and the environment are relevant in that the consequences of a hazard may result 
in either adverse effects on people or environmental impacts. Relevant legislations include: 
 
(i) 
Federal Law No. 8 (1980) UAE Labour Law and its Amendments Chapter V:  Safety, 
Protection, and the Health and Social Care of the Employees; Article (91). 
(ii) 
UAE Ministerial Order No. (32), 1982. Specifying Preventive Methods and Measures for 
Protecting Workers against Work Hazards. 
(iii) 
UAE Ministerial Order No. (37/2), 1982. Establishing the level of medical attention, the 
employer is obliged to provide to his workers. 
(iv) 
Federal Law No.: 24 of 1999 for the Protection and Development of the Environment. 
 
Group Companies shall ensure that their activities comply with all relevant Federal and Local laws and 
regulations at all times, including any that may be introduced after the publication of this Standard.

DOWNGRADED SITUATIONS FRAMEWORK 
A Downgraded Situation is declared when an anomaly, event or situation has been identified in an 
operating plant that temporarily raises the level of risk and thus requires effective mitigations and 
approvals to be in place in order to continue operating until a permanent solution is implemented or the 
situation is resolved.   
Downgraded Situations are an integral part of Management of Change. The downgraded situation 
represents a change to the design basis for operating the plant, as a result of an unintended external 
factor. It is thus reactive, and the objective is to manage the increased risk effectively until the design 
basis is re-established, or a permanent Change has been implemented and as-built. A proactive change 
or modification proposal, on the other hand, is proposed by staff members in order to improve some 
aspect of plant safety or performance. The emphasis is to ensure that the proposed change is justified 
either economically or for HSE reasons, and that the design and execution process is technically 
approved to meet all required standards. Proactive plant change proposals follow a different procedure 
defined in HSE-OS-ST30 “Management of Technical Change’.   
Temporary changes that are required to facilitate an activity (e.g. maintenance) which is managed by the 
Permit To Work (PTW) System are not considered as DGS. Any isolations, defeats or changes to LO/LC 
valve status covered by the PTW process are controlled by separate procedures under the HSE Work 
Management System (WMS), and are therefore excluded for DGS.

Cumulative Risk ASSESSMENT PROCESS 
The objective of the Cumulative Risk Assessment process is to assess the overall risk presented by the 
sum of all open and registered DGS’s. 
The process should take place at least every quarter (every 3 months), or as and when the VP-
Operations requests based on his judgement of possible interaction between a number of DGS’s. 
It is required because whereas each individual DGS is controlled by an agreed Risk Control Plan, it could 
be that there are so many DGS’s on one particular process system, or in one particular area of the plant, 
that the overall cumulative risk is no longer ALARP and requires a different type of risk mitigation. For 
example, take the main flare header process system: If there are one or two DGS’s registered on this 
system, each can be well controlled and monitored. However, if there are suddenly 5 DGS’s, on the same 
flare system, spread far apart around the plant, the overall integrity of the system is compromised and a 
different approach to risk mitigation may be required. It may even be required to schedule a plant 
shutdown at short notice in order to eliminate the cumulative risk. 
The Cumulative Risk Assessment process is a facilitated meeting comprised of multi-discipline subject 
matter experts from operations, HSE, maintenance and engineering. It is designed to look at all open, 
registered DGS’s by process system and by geographic area (i.e. on a plot plan), to assess if there is 
any cumulative risk that needs to be re-assessed, with a modified Risk Control plan in place. 
The detailed procedure for holding a Cumulative Risk Assessment (CRA) is shown in Appendix 5.

7.10. DEVIATIONS FROM THE DGS MANAGEMENT REQUIREMENTS

circumstances, but the safety of personnel, the protection of the environment and assets must not be 
jeopardized. In such cases, a dedicated risk assessment shall be performed to evaluate the risk and put 
in place adequate controls to manage this risk.  
Asset Owner shall request deviations from the Downgraded Situations requirements along with the risk 
assessment, in writing, to the Group Company Corporate HSE Vice President (or Head) for review. Group 
Company Corporate HSE VP shall accordingly endorse the request for deviation and share it with 
ADNOC Group HSE Function for their review.  
ADNOC Group HSE Manager shall approve deviations from this Standard, for issue by the Asset Owner, 
for subsequent inclusion in all site copies of the Downgraded Situations Management Procedure, as an 
addendum. The Asset Owner may cancel deviations when no longer required and update the addendum 
in the procedure accordingly.

Appendix 5 Cumulative Risk Assessment Procedure 
 
Objective: 
The objective of the Cumulative Risk Assessment process is to assess the overall risk presented by the 
sum of all open and registered DGS’s within a defined operating plant or facility, and to specify and follow 
up any recommendations for maintaining control of the overall cumulative risk. 
Initiate the CRA meeting 
The CRA meeting should be initiated by the Asset Custodian either: 
 
Every quarter 
 
Earlier if directed by the Asset owner based on a particularly high level of open DGSs. 
The Asset Custodian shall appoint a workshop facilitator from any of the following disciplines. Note: it is 
preferable to train and appoint a single Asset CRA facilitator and define the role within this person’s job 
description. 
 
Operations 
 
Process Safety Engineer 
 
HSE advisor 
 
Operating integrity Engineer 
The workshop facilitator shall arrange the meeting room and send invites to the following required 
meeting attendees: 
 
Process Safety SME  
 
Asset Custodian 
 
Operations 
 
Maintenance Manager 
 
HSE Advisor 
 
Discipline Engineer (or SME) Mechanical or Pipelines (as appropriate)  
 
Discipline engineer Inspection/ Corrosion 
 
Discipline Engineer (or SME) Electrical 
 
Discipline Engineer (or SME) Control & Automation / Instrumentation 
 
Process Engineer 
Note:  All meeting attendees are expected to have a detailed working knowledge of the asset and the 
processes. 
Plan and Prepare the CRA Meeting 
The following are required to be prepared prior to the meeting: 
 
Print out of current DGS register (one for each attendee) 
 
Slides showing all DGSs grouped by process system and by location 
 
Plot plans of plant or facility indicating locations of open DGSs 
 
Process flow schemes of the plant or facility 
 
Copies of ADNOC 6x6 risk matrix (one for each attendee) 
 
Minutes or report from the previously conducted CRA as and when available

Conduct the CRA Meeting 
The meeting will be facilitated by the appointed CRA facilitator. 
The facilitator will present each process system which contains more than one DGS (using the pre-
prepared slides) 
Attendees will consider each DGS in the process system in detail and answer the following questions: 
 
Is there any potential interaction or interference between several DGSs within the same process 
system? 
 
Is the overall integrity of the process system impacted by the sum of all the DGSs? 
 
Is there an increasing trend in the number of DGSs? (with reference to previous CRA meeting) 
 
Are there other abnormal operational constraints on that system e.g. maintenance deferrals, 
temporary or permanent MOC’s ? 
 
Is there a requirement for any additional Risk control activities above what is already in place? 
If the answer to any questions is yes, then an additional risk mitigation activity shall be recorded, with a 
time frequency and responsible action party. 
The same process should be repeated based on the plot plans, with the following questions answered 
for each defined zone or area of the plant: 
 
Is there any potential interaction or interference between several DGSs within the same location 
on the plant? 
 
Is the overall integrity of each plant or zone impacted by the sum of all the DGSs within it? 
 
Is there a requirement for any additional Risk control activities within the zone above what is 
already in place? 
If the answer to any questions is yes, then an additional risk mitigation activity shall be recorder, with a 
time frequency and responsible action party. 
Document and Follow up 
The discussion and any resulting actions should be minuted in a formal document and sent to the Asset 
Owner for review and approval. 
Any new or modified Risk Control plans shall be implemented and monitored within the DGS 
management process. 
The Minutes of the CRA meeting should be signed and dated by the Asset Owner after the following 
statement: 
“The risks represented by all the Downgraded Situations present on the Plant have been 
assessed, and the combined Risk Control plans are sufficient and effective in controlling the risk 
to ALARP.  Approval is given to continue operating the plant in its current known state of 
integrity”
