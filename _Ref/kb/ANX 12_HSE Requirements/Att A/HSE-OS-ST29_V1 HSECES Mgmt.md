1. 
INTRODUCTION 
Effective management of integrity of Health, Safety and Environment Critical Equipment and Systems 
(HSECES) is paramount for the safe, reliable and efficient operation of process facilities. It is a key 
element of the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC) HSE Risk Management Framework. This 
assures that all HSECES are managed effectively across ADNOC and Group Companies in a manner 
which ensures that they continue to perform their intended functions throughout their lifecycle.  
 
ADNOC, as part of its HSE and Asset Integrity commitment, strives to ensure that all HSECES across 
ADNOC and Group Companies, involved in prevention of, and protection from, major accidents are fully 
maintained; and processes & resources are in place for their identification, the development of 
Performance Standards, their performance assurance and verification, and Management of Change 
(MOC). This HSECES Management Standard is a part of the suite of Risk Management Standards under 
the HSE Management System. 
 
Asset Integrity is defined as the ability of an asset to perform its required function effectively and efficiently 
for a time period equal to or greater than its intended life with all its risks As Low As Reasonably 
Practicable (ALARP). As such, asset integrity encompasses various different elements, including Integrity 
Management, Operating Integrity, Maintenance Management, Well Integrity, and Inspection and 
Corrosion Management.  
 
HSECES Technical Integrity is defined as the ability of an HSECES to perform its intended HSE-critical 
function(s) effectively and efficiently as defined in the associated Performance Standard, for a time period 
equal or greater than its intended life.  
 
Figure 1.1 below shows the relationship between Technical Integrity, Risk Management and Asset 
Integrity; all under the overall governance of HSE Management System. This illustrates that the technical 
integrity of HSECES is where the requirements of risk management and asset integrity combine to 
achieve the necessary requirements. That is, by aligning asset integrity management with the 
requirements of HSECES defined by risk management, the effective and compliant implementation of 
HSECES integrity assurance and verification can be realised throughout the entire asset life cycle.

Technical Integrity should not be confused with Process Safety. Process Safety includes Design Integrity, 
Technical Integrity and Operating Integrity and addresses both hard and soft integrity barriers. This 
Standard covers Technical Integrity of HSECES only. Design integrity relates to the project design 
aspects and phases. 
 
This Standard addresses management of technical integrity of HSECES, the measures necessary for its 
definition, implementation and sustainment by assurance and verification throughout the facility life cycle 
(from design, fabrication, construction, installation, commissioning, operation, maintenance, modification 
and repair to decommissioning and abandonment). 
 
This Standard shall be used in conjunction with other ADNOC HSE standards such as ADNOC HSE 
Impact Assessment (HSEIA) Standard [Ref. 4], ADNOC Control of Major Accident Hazards (COMAH) 
Standard [Ref. 8], ADNOC Critical HSE Roles & Competence Standard [Ref. 9] and integrity management 
standards [Ref.11, 12, 13, 14 and 15]. 
 
Throughout this standard, the following words, when used in the context of actions by ADNOC Group 
companies and others (contractors), have specific meanings as follows: 
 
(i) 
“shall” indicates a requirement

(iii)

2. 
PURPOSE 
The purpose of this Standard is to provide a standardized process for effective management of HSECES 
technical integrity. It stipulates the mandatory requirements to ensure that HSECES are initially suitable 
and that they are maintained in a condition so that they will continue to perform their required function 
throughout the asset life cycle. 
 
3. 
SCOPE 
This Standard is applicable to ADNOC Group [Directorates & Functions (in ADNOC Headquarter), Group 
Companies and Affiliates] and its Contractors. [For temporary and portable equipment refer to Section 
7.6.3(d)]. 
 
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood, 
implemented and thoroughly monitored. 
 
It covers all ADNOC assets owned, operated and managed by ADNOC and Group Companies throughout 
the asset lifecycle.  
 
This standard excludes the following; conditional upon the applicable ADNOC entities applying and being 
able to demonstrate an equivalent process of managing major accidents for approval by ADNOC HSE: 
 
(i) 
Assets and entities which are either not owned and not operated by ADNOC Group and that 
are governed by other government authorities and comply with their own set of HSE 
regulations and requirements, such as but not limited to the following: 
1. 
Aviation transport facilities e.g. airports in ADNOC administered islands such as Das 
Island airport, Zirku Island airport, etc., fixed wing aircrafts and helicopters and 
support systems  
2. 
Marine transport facilities e.g. marine ports in ADNOC administered islands such as 
Das Island port, Zirku Island port, etc., ships, Navigational Aids (NAVAIDS) and 
support systems.

QRA 
Quantitative Risk Assessment; It is a formal and systematic 
approach of estimating the likelihood and consequences of 
hazardous events, and expressing the results quantitatively as risk 
to people, the environment, asset or reputation.  
 
A structured approach to assessing the potential for incidents and 
expressing this potential numerically. In QRA statistical values are 
derived for potential loss of life and damage to resources and 
environment. Note: These values must never be interpreted as 
unavoidable and acceptable losses. 
 
It must always be recognised that the calculated fatality (or loss) 
figures are based on experience, statistical failure and incident rates 
representing an average historical quality of management. Incident 
investigations usually show that these ‘historical’ incidents were, 
with the benefit of hindsight, quite preventable.  
 
QRA is a tool which helps to translate this hindsight into foresight 
(planning) in order to assist management in deciding the best 
approach and show ways and means (e.g. improved engineering, 
procedures, supervision, etc.) to prevent the potential incidents from 
happening. QRA is not to be used to justify or encourage risk taking

7.3. 
HSECES IDENTIFICATION AT CATEGORY LEVEL 
7.3.1. HSECES AND BOW-TIES 
The ‘Bow-Tie’ is a qualitative graphical display of the hazard, its potential threats and consequences, 
associated barriers and how those barriers can fail (escalation factor). The major accidents are analysed 
using the bow-tie method to identify hardware and soft barriers for each of the threats, consequences 
and escalation factors and their controls. The barriers that keep the threats from becoming top events 
(The release of the Hazard, the first consequence, e.g. loss of containment) are prevention barriers (left 
side of bow-tie) while those that mitigate or recover from the effects of the consequences are protection 
(mitigation or recovery) barriers and are on the right side of the bow-tie. 
 
The HSECES identification process starts with the HSECES List from COMAH, developed in line with 
ADNOC Corporate HSECES Barrier Model and ADNOC COMAH Standard. All the physical (hardware) 
barriers on the left and right sides of the bow-tie diagram are HSECES. 
 
7.3.2. HSECES LIST 
A list of HSECES shall be assembled from the information generated by the major accident identification 
process. This list shall be periodically updated when the COMAH is revised. The register should contain 
a record of the HSECES identification process and record the reasons for the assignment as HSECES 
for all designated equipment/ systems. In some cases, it may be appropriate to also record the reasons 
for not identifying a system or equipment as HSECES. 
 
7.4.

in order to comply with the HSECES technical integrity management requirements, it is mandatory that

one function (e.g. a process system instrument may belong to ESD/ IPF, Ignition Control (certified

to be recognized within CMMS. This allows the maintenance, inspection and testing strategies and 
procedures/ plans to be aligned with the appropriate requirements of each HSECES Performance

7.5. 
PERFORMANCE STANDARDS 
A Performance Standard is a statement, which can be expressed in qualitative or quantitative terms, of 
the performance, required of a system, or item of equipment, and which is used as the basis for the 
assurance and verification of HSECES throughout the lifecycle of the installation. Performance Standards 
are live documents and define the necessary performance required of the HSECES under normal and 
emergency conditions for major accidents management. Establishing and maintaining the performance 
of an HSECES throughout its lifecycle is the main purpose of ADNOC’s HSECES management standard. 
 
This section provides clear guidelines for developing HSECES Performance Standards in order to 
facilitate their effective implementation across ADNOC and Group Companies in a consistent manner.  
 
ADNOC and Group Companies shall prepare detailed HSECES Performance Standards for the project

7.6.4. HSECES ASSURANCE IN DECOMMISSIONING AND DISPOSAL 
At the end of the facility production and economic life, the facility will be decommissioned then equipment, 
plant and structures dismantled. During this period the major accident scenarios are likely to change 
continually, requiring further reviews and revisions of risk control measures. Thus, a detailed plan for 
HSECES assurance shall be developed including decommissioning and dismantling.  
 
The aim of HSECES assurance in decommissioning and dismantling is to assure the continued suitability 
of the HSECES during such operations. Major accidents shall be identified for the entire abandonment 
process in the Abandonment and Decommissioning HSEIA.  
 
Given the nature of the decommissioning and dismantling activities, this may give rise to change in the 
HSECESs boundary and or impair their performance i.e. effectiveness of risk control measures (or 
prevention and protection barriers to major accident events) has been reduced. In such circumstances, 
ADNOC and Group Companies must consider enhancing other risk control measures to ensure the risk 
is As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP). 
 
Normally the decommissioning and dismantling processes will be split into phases or stages. Prior to the 
commencement of each phase, risk assessments for the remaining major accidents and associated 
HSECESs should be carried out in order to ensure integrity throughout that particular phase. The 
remaining HSECES shall be commented upon by the Independent Competent Person (ICP) and changes 
to the HSECES list shall be recorded. The availability of HSECESs throughout each of the phases shall 
form part of the TIV activities to be carried out.

maintenance, inspection, testing or repair activities. Deferral of maintenance, inspection or testing 
of HSECES is needed when scheduled task cannot be carried out at the defined date in the plan.

(b) 
HSECES Degradation 
Ageing and deterioration of HSECES is an important factor in increased risk of loss of containment 
and other critical failures resulting in MAH events or failure to control MAH consequences.  
 
Ageing equipment is equipment for which there is evidence or likelihood of significant deterioration 
and damage taking place since new, or for which there is insufficient information and knowledge 
available to know the extent to which this possibility exists. The significance of deterioration and 
damage relates to the potential effect on the equipment’s functionality, availability, reliability and 
survivability against the effects on an MAH. Ageing is not about how old the equipment is; it is 
about its condition and performance, and how that is changing over time. It is not directly related 
to chronological age. There are many examples of very old plant remaining fully fit for purpose, 
and of recent plant showing evidence of accelerated or early ageing, e.g. due to corrosion, fatigue 
or erosion failures. All types of equipment can be susceptible to ageing mechanisms. 
 
(c) 
Temporary Defeat of HSECES 
A temporary defeat, bypass or isolation occurs when an HSECES is taken out of service (isolated 
or shutdown) while the equipment or facility remains in operation.

intended function as a result of any of the following: 
 
(i)

(ii)

(v)

7.8.4. SIMULTANEOUS AND COMBINED OPERATIONS  
This section (also refer to Section 7.9) addresses the HSECES Technical Integrity requirements related 
to SIMOPS/COMOPS activities such as; mobilisation of a well services vessel to an offshore installation, 
close proximity to or physical connection with a construction support barge and/or LQ vessel, 
maintenance campaign at any otherwise operational asset, etc. Typically, this is concerned with offshore 
facilities but consideration in relation to onshore facilities also needs to be considered as appropriate. 
 
The ADNOC or ADNOC Group Company Operations or Project Team shall, prior to mobilising a 
contractor to a facility which has existing HSECES it will need to have HSECES identified in relation of 
any physical equipment used to support execution of the scope of work taking into account the hazards

systems, fire water systems, crew living quarters, etc. In addition, the impact on the existing ADNOC 
facility’s HSECES will need to be assessed – examples of such impacts are; temporary blockage of 
escape/evacuation routes, heavier lifts that existing facilities are protected against, etc. This should be 
part of the process of identifying, assessing and demonstrating the acceptability of incremental risks 
during the SIMOPS/COMOPS programme of work.  
 
All of the requirements of Technical Integrity management, as defined throughout this document shall 
apply to all HSECES identified on such 3rd party facilities, as described above (e.g. a barge/vessel). In 
practice, this means that the owner/operator of such vessels/rigs will need to comply with this standard’s 
requirements as a normal part of their operations and not just during such a mobilisation in order that

equipment that may be considered as HSECES when on location at an ADNOC facility.
