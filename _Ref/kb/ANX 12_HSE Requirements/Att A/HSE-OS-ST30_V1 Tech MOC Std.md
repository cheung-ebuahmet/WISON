1. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
ADNOC HSE Policy states that “the ADNOC Group is committed to managing risk through effective 
controls and minimising impacts to our business”. 
The ADNOC Risk Assessment and Management framework aims to ensure that Risks are identified, 
analysed, evaluated and treated in a consistent manner and assigned to respective management tier for 
implementation, follow-up and closure. 
The ADNOC Integrated Management System (IMS) defines the following types of change that require to 
be managed in accordance with the ADNOC Risk assessment and Management Framework: 
 
Administrative (Process) changes: Changes to, or deviations from, requirements in a 
Governance Document (policy, standard, process, procedure, etc.) or non-material change to an 
operational procedure or specification 
 
Organisational (People) changes: Permanent change in an organizational structure or change 
in personnel with specific knowledge or experience. 
 
Capital Project changes: changes to the project scope (Statement of Requirements - SOR) or 
the approved design occurring during the ‘Define’ and ‘Execute’ stages of the project. 
 
Technical (Plant) changes: Physical change to an existing operated facility (Plant, Equipment, 
Materials), operational process change or deviation from documented design limits or 
requirements. 
This Standard relates to Technical (Plant) Changes as mentioned in Section 3 (Scope). The other three 
types of change are subject to separate Management standards.  
Any technical changes made or proposed to be made to the original design basis of an operating plant 
or facility shall be managed in such a way that increased risk is identified, assessed and managed to 
ALARP. 
ADNOC Facilities are designed with best Engineering Principles and Practices according to the ADNOC 
HSE Management System, which requires the design to demonstrate that all Risks are ALARP. However, 
there are instances or events which demand to make changes to the design basis of existing facilities, 
either proactive changes proposed to improve safety or performance, or reactive changes required to 
respond to increased risk introduced by external circumstances or events.  
A technical change proposal is considered whenever there is an opportunity for improving plant HSE and 
asset integrity; energy and resources conservation; reliability, availability and maintainability and 
operability; profitability or changes in product specifications.  
A downgraded situation (DGS) is declared whenever an anomaly, event or situation has been identified 
in an operating plant that temporarily raises the level of risk and thus requires effective mitigations and 
approvals to be in place in order to continue operating until a permanent solution is implemented or the 
situation is resolved [Refer to ADNOC DGS Standard (HSE-OP-ST31)].  
ADNOC Technical MOC Standard (HSE-OP-ST30) will be maintained jointly by ADNOC Group HSE 
Function and ADNOC Production Function, Operational Excellence Department.

2. 
PURPOSE 
The purpose of this standard is to set forth requirements for Management of Technical Change Proposals 
(MOC) as they apply to ADNOC Group. It provides an outline of the business process that needs to be 
in place to ensure changes to the facility/system/procedures, etc. do not introduce unforeseen or 
unacceptable HSE risks. 
It defines the roles and responsibilities of the various parties within ADNOC and the ADNOC Group of 
Companies who have responsibilities for Technical MOC, the common framework and control 
procedures through which preparation and approval of MOCs are managed across ADNOC Group. 
 
3. 
SCOPE  
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates & 
Functions in ADNOC Head Quarter, Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors. 
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood, 
implemented and thoroughly monitored. 
This document provides a structure and methodology to assess the impact of change and put into place 
appropriate controls to ensure that the risks inherent in changes are managed to meet ADNOC HSE 
objectives.

This document specifies the requirements for management of changes to: 
 
Plant, facilities and pipelines – adding, removing or changing plant hardware, materials or 
equipment, including operational wells, plant controls (software and hardware modifications), 
changes in feedstock, technology, product, chemicals and catalysts 
 
Processes - changing the work processes or procedures required to operate the plant. 
This document addresses change requests that are below the threshold for initiating a capital project. 
Any major changes that require significant engineering design resources and project management 
should be initiated as capital projects and follow the Value Assurance Process (VAP) (reference ADNOC 
VAP Manual doc no. AHQTEOGPRMAN001R0119). 
This standard is applicable equally to both permanent and temporary change proposals. 
The following is excluded from the scope: 
 
People changes – including changes to the safety critical roles / positions required to operate 
and support the facilities.  Refer to the MOOC. 
 
Management of Organization Change (MOOC) is excluded from the scope and will be managed 
through ADNOC Organizational Management of Change Procedure (IMS Procedure) via 
processes established by ADNOC Human Capital & Administration (HC&A) Division. This 
Standard however stipulates requirements applicable to changes to Safety Critical Roles / 
Positions as defined by ADNOC Critical HSE Roles & Competencies Standard (HSE-GA-ST03). 
 
Situations which are covered under HSE standard: Management of Downgraded Situations  
(HSE-OS-ST31) 
 
Situations covered under Temporary Defeat of HSECES (WMS) as defined in ADNOC Defeat 
Management Standard (HSE-OS-ST05) 
 
Replacements in Kind - this includes situations where an item is replaced with an identical item, 
usually in the course of routine maintenance, where it is a direct like-for-like replacement. Refer 
Appendix-9 for specific examples. 
 
Changes with significant cost or complexity that can better be managed as capital projects via 
the VAP (reference ADNOC VAP Manual doc no. AHQTEOGPRMAN001R0119)  
 
Changes or modifications made during the project development phase of the Asset lifecycle (i.e. 
prior to Project to Asset handover), except where brownfield projects require temporary changes 
to the existing facilities in order to optimize production, safety or economics. Changes to Project 
will be managed through ADNOC Projects Management of Change Procedure (IMS Procedure) 
via processes established by ADNOC Project Management & Construction Department. 
 
Routine adjustments to process parameters within safe operating limits (SOL) as per approved 
Operating Manuals and Alarms and Trip List engineering documents. 
 
Scheduled revision and up-dates to documentation and procedures unless the planned changes 
have potential to impact risk, in which case they shall be processed through this MoC procedure.

4. 
LAWS AND REGULATIONS 
Currently, there are no specific UAE laws applicable to HSE Risk Management. However, laws on 
protection of the environment and people are relevant in that the consequences of a hazard may result 
in either adverse environmental impacts or effects on people. Relevant legislations include: 
 
(i) Federal Law No. 8 (1980) UAE Labour Law and its Amendments Chapter V:  Safety, Protection, and 
the Health and Social Care of the Employees; Article (91). 
(ii) UAE Ministerial Order No. (32), 1982. Specifying Preventive Methods and Measures for Protecting 
Workers against Work Hazards. 
(iii) UAE Ministerial Order No. (37/2), 1982. Establishing the level of medical attention the employer is 
obliged to provide to his workers. 
(iv) Federal Law No.: 24 of 1999 for the Protection and Development of the Environment. 
ADNOC Group Companies must ensure, that their activities comply with all relevant Federal and Local 
laws and regulations at all times, including any that may be introduced after the publication of this 
Standard.

7.1. 
MANAGEMENT OF TECHNICAL CHANGE WORK PROCESS 
The Management of Technical Change Process (hereafter shortened to the Technical MOC process) is 
established to manage change proposals (except for “replacement-in-kind”) to physical processes, 
chemicals, technology, equipment, procedures, and facilities that could potentially introduce new hazards 
or risks to an operation or activity. It ensures that the following considerations are addressed prior to any 
change: 
 
The technical basis and justification for the proposed change, 
 
The potential impact of the change, 
 
The required updates to operating or maintenance procedures, or any As-built documents 
 
Necessary time period for the change, and 
 
Approval requirements for the proposed change. 
A Technical MOC is used to formally evaluate, document, and authorize a physical, or process change 
affecting the integrity of the facility or operating activity. 
If the answer to any of these questions is “Yes” a Technical MOC is required: 
 
Is this a change or deviation to operating design limits? 
 
Is this a change or deviation to process equipment specifications or use? 
 
Is this a change or deviation to the composition or use of process substances? 
 
Is this a change or deviation to process design or facility layout? 
 
Is this a material (significant) change to a Critical Document (more than editorial)? 
The MOC standard stipulates a review and authorization process for evaluating proposed changes to 
facility design, operations, or activities prior to implementation to help verify that no unforeseen hazards 
are introduced, that the change is economically justified and that the risk of existing hazards to 
employees, the public, or the environment is not unknowingly increased.  
In addition, it includes steps to help ensure that potentially affected personnel are notified of the change 
and those pertinent documents, such as procedures, drawings and Process Safety Information (PSI), 
are kept up–to-date. 
The MOC process covers the operating phase of the assets and all disciplines. It ensures that risks 
caused by the change are identified, mitigated and accepted prior to implementing the change.  
Appendix 1 provides the Technical MOC Process Flow. Appendix 2 provides helpful guidance on 
deciding what constitutes a change. Appendix 9 provides examples of “Replacement in Kind vs. Change.”

 
Proposed changes to feed rates or compositions, catalysts, chemicals, or extended run times 
(Note: reacting to an enforced change caused by external factors is DGS) 
 
Alarm set points and software modifications

7.2.2. 
TEMPORARY CHANGE 
The same as above, except the change is proposed for a temporary period, not more than 12 months,

7.2.3. 
EMERGENCY CHANGE 
Emergency changes are those required in a situation where the time required for following the normal 
MOC steps could result in an unacceptable safety hazard, a significant environmental or security incident, 
or extreme financial losses. This shall only be applied in High/ Medium risk situations.  An initial risk 
assessment using ADNOC Risk Matrix shall be performed prior to raising the Change Request (CR).  
Justification and risk control plans shall be documented. 
Emergency change requests require a fast-track process to effect the change, and may also require DGS 
to be implemented during the time it takes to complete the change request. 
These include CRs raised on an emergency basis, initiated during weekends or the middle of the night 
due to an urgent plant situation such as to bring the plant back to a safe state. 
Emergency changes by their nature sometimes do not allow sufficient time to comply with requirements 
of this Standard before the change is implemented. However, requirements outlined in this Standard shall 
be applied retrospectively to ensure that the need for any additional safeguards is identified and 
documentation is updated.



 
Any learnings from the implementation of the change are captured and disseminated to others who

delay, misunderstanding, cost and potential HSE and Integrity issues. 
The MOC Coordinator shall consolidate the PIR and report the findings to the MOC Committee for final 
closeout approval.

7.5. 
TEMPORARY CHANGES 
A temporary change is a change that is implemented on a short-term basis (equal to or less than one 
year) with the intention of returning to the original design basis. The temporary change must have a 
specific expiry period (e.g. number of days from implementation date). Changes beyond this period 
should be designed and processed as permanent. If required to return to a different operating condition, 
a new MOC must be generated. 
All temporary changes must be evaluated using the same considerations as permanent changes. 
7.5.1. 
EXTENDING THE TEMPORARY CLOSURE DATE 
If a temporary change requires an engineering project or project planning process to be returned to 
normal operation, the change may be extended for two (2) years or until the next scheduled turnaround, 
if this is required to return to normal operation. This determination is acceptable if both of the following is 
in place: 
 Written engineering appraisal that validates that sufficient safeguards are in place and that stated 
safeguards and proposed temporary changes will maintain their integrity for the duration of the 
proposed temporary change. 
 MOC approval includes management level review and approval.  
To extend a temporary change, Temporary Extension Form (Appendix 6) shall be used. 
7.5.2. TEMPORARY CHANGES MADE PERMANENT 
If the temporary change is going to become a permanent change, the MOC process must be re-started 
and the change reviewed as a permanent change to the asset, e.g. Temporary Defeat and DGSs which 
are intended to make permanent will pass through the same protocol as of permanent change. 
Note:  The MOC for the permanent installation will not replace and close the temporary MOC until the 
PSSR has been completed through acceptance for start-up.

 
On-call personnel shall be on-site for emergency changes to technically evaluate the situation.

7.7. 
PHASED OR MAJOR PROJECTS  
Phased projects may have a substantial time lag between the initial connection to an active process and

generated for each phased portion of the project. Alternatively, multiple MOC packages can be generated 
to efficiently manage each phased portion of the project.  
For projects that involve more than one unit, a MOC request must be initiated for each unit affected.

shall be established for each step. 
Group companies shall, at their own discretion, define these requirement to manages such changes for

7.9. 
MOC CANCELATION 
During the course of any MOC, there may be concerns raised, or the project may be cancelled during 
the review process, that would make it necessary to cancel the MOC. The MOC Coordinator shall be 
notified in writing of the request for cancelation including a brief explanation of why the MOC is being 
cancelled. MOC shall be cancelled in following two ways: 
 MOCs prior to Management approval: MOC Coordinator shall submit the MOC to MOC Committee 
for formal cancelation.  
 MOCs after Management approval: MOC Coordinator shall submit the MOC to Facility/Division 
Manager for formal cancelation.  
 MOCs after HIRA: MOC can be cancelled based on high risk or major operability issues identified 
during HIRA stage. A recommendation to cancel MOC shall be initiated by Process Safety Function 
by submitting a HIRA report to MOC Coordinator. MOC Coordinator shall submit the MOC to 
Facility/Division Manager for formal cancelation. 
The MOC Coordinator subsequently notifies the originator about the cancelation of the MOC.

Proposals to change the numbers of Safety Critical Roles/Positions, their organizational design, their role 
description or competency requirements must consider requirements of this Standard, in order to ensure 
that resulting changes are managed and implemented in a manner that assures continued safe operations.  
Any permanent organizational changes including organization restructuring, staff reduction, transfers, etc. 
shall be undertaken under ADNOC Organizational Management of Change Procedure (IMS Procedure) via 
processes established by Human Capital & Administration (HC&A) Division. In such cases, a dedicated 
study shall be performed to identify and address potential process safety and HSE impacts arising from 
these changes.   
Temporary organizational changes (lasting longer than 90 days) and permanent changes (associated 
increase/decrease of duties performed by the existing positions) shall be implemented considering the 
requirements described in this Standard. 
Positions affected by this Standard include: 
 
Positions performing HSE critical activities and HSE critical integrity activities (refer to ADNOC 
HSECES Standard). 
 
Positions in operations, including supervisory and engineering positions with direct responsibility in 
operations 
 
Positions with HSE and process safety responsibilities (this includes positions with responsibility in 
HSE and process safety programs and personnel whose job functions are part of the implementation 
of HSE and process safety programs) 
 
Positions on emergency response teams 
 
Positions in maintenance, including supervisory positions, which can affect maintenance of a process.  
This includes resident daily maintenance contractors.

 
Substantive changes in responsibility of a position (for the purposes of this procedure, a substantive

management, reporting or record keeping of HSE/process safety related tasks 
 
Temporary changes associated with turnaround preparation   
Organizational changes to HSE Critical Roles / Positions shall be implemented in accordance with ADNOC 
Organizational MOC Procedure (IMS) with the following specific requirements: 
 
Technical reviews shall focus on concerns associated with work quality, competency requirements, 
conflict of interest, HSE and other work requirements for a position under review; 
 
Engineering stage may include workload analysis to ensure that new task will not affect quality and 
timeliness of HSE critical activities performed by a position (i.e. additional administrative work affecting 
field supervision, etc.).  
 
Engineering stage may include development /revision of work instructions/SOP, revision of job 
descriptions, establishing temporary reporting lines, training need analysis, etc. 
 
Implementation stage may include trainings, work under the assigned mentor and competency 
assessment before actual commencement of new assignment.  
 
All Organizational changes subject to this Standard shall be made on a non-emergency basis.      
Some specific examples of changes requiring the use of this Standard:   
 
Assigning additional collateral duties to operations, maintenance, Process Safety, HSE or emergency 
response personnel. 
 
Consolidation within department, such as changes in number of field operator for operating zones or 
areas. 
 
Re-organization of the Emergency Response Teams. 
 
Moving job duties from one department, such as Operations, to another, such as HSE or Process 
Safety.  
 
Personnel changes, including changes in staffing levels, staff experience, or contracting strategy that 
directly affect Process safety of the facilities. 
 
Policy changes or cost optimization initiatives (such as budget cutting) that impact Safety Critical roles. 
Organization Changes not Covered by this Standard: 
 
Organisational changes that do not affect Process Safety.  
 
Routine organizational changes such as staff promotions, appointments and rotation of staff within 
approved organization charts (both vertical and horizontal) unless such activities involve / lead to 
changes described above.
