1. 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1. 
HAZID STUDY 
Hazard Identification (HAZID) is a technique for early identification of potential hazards and threats 
associated with a proposed activity, with a view to eliminate or reduce the hazards at the earliest 
practicable stage of a development or venture. Early identification of hazards facilitates early 
implementation of risk reduction measures, thus reducing the impacts on cost and schedule compared to 
later implementation of risk reduction measures. The HAZID is a team-based brainstorming technique, 
normally guided by Hazard Guidewords and taking benefit from the collective knowledge and experience 
of the workshop team to identify potential Health, Safety and Environmental (HSE) hazards. 
 
1.1.1. OBJECTIVES OF HAZID 
The HAZID Study shall be undertaken with following objectives: 
 
(i) 
Systematically identify and analyze the potential hazards of the facility; 
(ii) 
Identify causes/threats associated with each top event; 
(iii) 
Identify all consequences of the hazards including Major Accident Hazard (MAH); 
(iv) 
Identify preventive/mitigation safeguards (controls) that are in place;  
(v) 
Identify additional  risk reduction measures required, if any, to eliminate the hazard, prevent or 
mitigate the consequences; and 
(vi) 
Provide input to Management in its efforts to manage risks. 
1.1.2. ENVID STUDY 
An Environmental Impact Identification (ENVID) technique is a team-based brainstorming approach used 
to identify environmental aspects and impacts associated with the project phases. Both planned and 
unplanned 
(emergency/accidental) 
environmental 
hazards/aspects, 
their 
impacts 
and 
their 
duration/likelihood shall be identified in ENVID.  
 
Planned impacts are those which result from routine (planned) activities performed either on continuous 
or abnormal activities performed on intermittent basis for operational and maintenance purpose. 
Scenarios for accidental (unplanned) events (such as loss of containment etc.) and their environmental 
effects/impacts that were identified in HAZID shall also be further discussed in detail during ENVID 
session.  
 
The brainstorming shall be a workshop based activity to identify and examine all reasonably possible 
sources of environmental impact which in turn shall be used to eliminate or reduce the impact, through 
controls, mitigation and management of the impact. The ENVID should ultimately be viewed as the key 
interface tool between the Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) and the Environmental Engineering 
processes with regard to a project or development. 
 
1.1.3. OBJECTIVES OF ENVID 
(a) 
The ENVID Study shall be undertaken with following objectives: 
(i) 
Systematically identify activities and analyse the planned and unplanned environmental 
aspects of the facility; 
(ii) 
Identify all aspects of environment and evaluate the  impacts associated with all activities;  
(iii) 
Identify control and monitoring measures that are in place;  
(iv) 
Identify additional measures required, if any, to prevent or mitigate the impact to the 
allowable environmental standards; and

OHID STUDY 
The Occupational Health Identification (OHID) study objective is to identify the occupational health 
hazards. OHID is synonymous with Qualitative OHRA; requirements for conducting Qualitative OHRA is

2. 
PURPOSE 
The purpose of this Standard is to provide an overall understanding of the HAZID and ENVID processes 
and their application within the ADNOC Group. This Standard establishes the consistent requirements 
for planning, conducting and documenting HAZID and ENVID Studies within ADNOC Group. 
 
All HAZID and ENVID studies carried out for ADNOC Group shall be in accordance with the requirements 
of this Standard. 
 
Requirements for carrying out HAZID and ENVID studies at various stages of a project are provided in 
the ADNOC HSE Impact Assessment (HSEIA) Standard, HSE-RM-ST02 [Ref. 1].

SCOPE

ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood, 
implemented and thoroughly monitored.

DESCRIPTIONS 
QRA  
Quantitative Risk Assessment; It is a formal and systematic approach 
of estimating the likelihood and consequences of hazardous events, 
and expressing the results quantitatively as risk to people, the 
environment, asset or reputation.   
 
A structured approach to assessing the potential for incidents and 
expressing this potential numerically. In QRA statistical values are 
derived for potential loss of life and damage to resources and 
environment. Note: These values must never be interpreted as 
unavoidable and acceptable losses. 
 
It must always be recognised that the calculated fatality (or loss) 
figures are based on experience, statistical failure and incident rates 
representing an average historical quality of management. Incident 
investigations usually show that these ‘historical’ incidents were, with 
the benefit of hindsight, quite preventable.  
 
QRA is a tool which helps to translate this hindsight into foresight 
(planning) in order to assist management in deciding the best 
approach and show ways and means (e.g. improved engineering, 
procedures, supervision, etc.) to prevent the potential incidents from 
happening. QRA is not to be used to justify or encourage risk taking. 
Risk 
Risk is the product of the measure of the likelihood of occurrence of 
an undesired event and the potential adverse consequences which 
the event may have upon: 
 
• 
Health and Safety of People – fatality, injury, irreversible health 
impact or chronic ill health or harm to physical or psychological 
health 
• 
Environment - water, air, soil, animals, plants and social 
• 
Reputation - employees and third parties.  This includes the 
liabilities arising from injuries and property damage to third parties 
including the cross liabilities that may arise between the 
interdependent ADNOC Group Companies 
• 
Financial - damage to property (assets) or loss of production 
• 
Legal - Legal impacts due to breach of law, breach of contract etc.  
 
Risk = Severity (Consequence) x Likelihood (Frequency)

REQUIREMENTS  
7.1. 
HAZID/ENVID THROUGH PROJECT/FACILITY LIFECYCLE 
HAZID/ENVID study is an integral part of the project activity. HAZID/ENVID studies shall be included in 
the Project Schedule on the critical path, with the timing determined by the availability of the required 
documentation with adequate details. 
 
The type of HAZID/ENVID studies required during the asset lifecycle: 
 
(i) 
Concept Stage and FEED Stage HAZID/ENVID – This should include information on the proposed, 
technical and facility schemes, locations of the development, the quality of the infrastructure and 
details about the population and previous land use. In terms of the proposed development, several 
options may have been under consideration and some information on costing and project 
economics should be available. 
(ii) 
Detailed Engineering Stage HAZID/ENVID – The design stage work would have progressed to the 
point where a Process Flow Scheme (PFS) has been developed and plot layout, hazardous 
inventories, utility requirements etc. are available to comprehensively assess the operational 
hazards.  
(iii) 
Construction Stage HAZID/ENVID – The project stage work would have progressed to the point 
where construction, installation and pre-commissioning requirements are available. 
(iv) 
Operation Stage HAZID/ENVID – HAZID/ENVID is required to be updated if there has been 
changes to the operation of the facility. 
(v) 
Decommissioning/Demolition Stage HAZID/ENVID – This should include the considerations of 
shutdown, decommissioning, and/or demolition of facility. 
Note: If preliminary plot plan and Process Flow Diagrams (PFDs) are available, a HAZID/ENVID study 
could be conducted at Pre-Concept Stage of the project.

7.2.2. PREPARATION PHASE 
The Preparation Phase typically includes the following activities: 
(i) 
Identifying and locating supporting data and information;  
(ii) 
Project management preparations;  
(iii) 
Consensus on HAZID/ENVID guidewords to be used during the study; and 
(iv) 
Development of Terms of Reference. 
(a) 
Facilities 
The meeting room should be appropriate for the HAZID/ENVID techniques that are to be adopted, 
e.g. curtains/blinds may be required if an overhead projector is to be used, and there should be 
adequate wall space to display the drawings. The appropriate hardware (computer, overhead 
projector and printer) should be made available during the HAZID/ENVID workshop. It is also 
important that adequate refreshments are provided as this helps with maintaining concentration 
and in building a team spirit. 
(b) 
Terms of Reference (TOR) 
TOR shall be developed for each study. This HAZID/ENVID Standard ensures consistency across 
studies. However, each study shall have separate terms of reference defining the key elements of 
specific study including, but not limited to: 
(i) 
The scope and exclusions including physical or boundary limits;  
(ii) 
The team members; 
(iii) 
Reference documentation; 
(iv) 
Tentative schedule. 
TOR shall be issued and approved by SME from ADNOC Group prior to the HAZID/ENVID 
workshop. 
 
7.2.3. EXAMINATION PHASE 
The Examination Phase begins with identification of all hazards with respect to the Plant Area/Node to 
be examined. For example: 
 
(i) 
Plant areas may be broken down into smaller nodes as necessary, e.g. Hydrocarbon and Non-
Hydrocarbon areas, different facilities such as processing and wellhead, etc. 
(ii) 
A separate Node/System to be considered for each phase (i.e. Detail Engineering, Construction, 
etc. 
(iii) 
Similar sections or steps may be grouped together to facilitate assessment. 
The HAZID/ENVID guidewords are then applied to each of the hazard source, identifying causes, their 
consequences and risk ranking (Refer to Section 7.3). A search for potential hazards are carried out in a 
systematic manner. All the hazards, threats, consequences, controls or indications are investigated and 
recorded. 
 
Note: It is recommended to prepare draft pre-populated HAZID/ENVID worksheet and share it with team 
members prior to workshop for an efficient HAZID proceeding.  However, during workshop each of the 
guideword/hazard shall be discussed in detail and any additional inputs to be captured accordingly.

7.6. 
HAZID/ENVID RECOMMENDATIONS 
The purpose of the HAZID/ENVID study is to discover potential hazards of the facility. It is generally not 
within its scope to try to solve the complex problems and stipulate detailed actions. A prescriptive 
recommendation may be counter-productive as it may inhibit the engineer from considering an alternative, 
and perhaps better solution. While wording recommendations, avoid using words such as “may”, “ensure”, 
“assure”, “consider”, “if necessary”, “if feasible”, etc.,  which may lead to a sense of ambiguity to the 
recommendation. 
 
(i) 
Closed (Simple) Actions 
It is preferable for the team to agree and recommend simple actions, which can be defined as 
‘closed’, because they do not ask the responding engineer to consider options. The team should 
be of enough experience and seniority in order to make design decisions for the project. The team 
should therefore be able to take decisions while not spending undue time developing solutions to 
complex problems.

information obtained during the deliberations of actions to be taken. The reasons for modification, referral,
