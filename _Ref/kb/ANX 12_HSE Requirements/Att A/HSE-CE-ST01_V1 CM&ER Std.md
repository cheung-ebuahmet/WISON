1. 
INTRODUCTION 
This standard supersedes the ADNOC Code of Practice on Incident and Crisis Management. 
This standard provides the basis for understanding, developing and implementing effective emergency response 
(ER) and crisis management (CM) within ADNOC Group. It describes the key principles of the ER and CM 
process and sets minimum expectations for the preparation for, response to, and learnings from the following:  
(i) 
Operational and enterprise incidents 
(ii) 
Business disruptions. 
For the purpose of this document, where it is not required to distinguish between ER, CM and business continuity 
(BC), the term crisis management shall be used as a collective term for these response and recovery 
management activities. 
2. 
PURPOSE 
The purpose of this standard is to create a well-prepared organization that is resilient to a multitude of risks and 
hazards associated with activities of ADNOC Group.  
3. 
SCOPE 
This standard includes requirements for the following: 
(i) 
Development of mitigations and response plans based on risks 
(ii) 
Development of response capability to support the plans 
(iii) 
Exercising and validation 
(iv) 
Accounting for personnel. 
This standard applies to all ADNOC Group companies, ADNOC HQ, ADNOC operational units and SPC affiliates 
hereinafter called ADNOC Entities. 
4. 
LAWS AND REGULATIONS 
In the event of a conflict between this standard and applicable legal and regulatory requirements, the applicable 
legal and regulatory requirements shall be followed. If this document sets a higher standard, it shall be followed 
as long as full compliance with applicable legal and regulatory requirements is also achieved.

An initial over-response: 
1. 
Is based on initial incident potential assessment that assumes credible worst case 
2. 
Prevents an inadequate response caused by lack of detailed incident information. 
Once the initial response is underway, an assessment should be conducted based on accurate incident 
information to consider whether the level of response is appropriate. Periodic re-assessment allows the 
response to be managed effectively. 
Finally, it is important to clearly confirm that response teams are standing down so that all stakeholders 
are aware that the response phase has ended. 
(c) 
ADNOC Group shall use the tiered response structure to organize and manage response 
operations directed at events, regardless of nature, size, severity and location. 
The level or response required to address an individual event should be defined by the ability of the 
response teams and availability of appropriate resources to mitigate the negative effects of the event.  
The terms that may be used to define an event should be as follows: 
(i) 
Incident is an event, series of events or set of circumstances that interrupts normal 
operating procedures and has the potential to precipitate an emergency or crisis 
(ii) 
Emergency is an unforeseen combination of circumstances that does the following if not 
controlled, contained or eliminated immediately: 
 
Disrupts normal operating conditions 
 
Poses an actual or a potential threat to people, environment and assets. 
(iii) 
Crisis is an event, series of events or set of circumstances that threaten to fundamentally 
affect or alter the way ADNOC chooses to do business. 
Where it is not required to distinguish between these categories of events, the term incident may be used 
as a collective term. 
(d) 
ADNOC Group shall adhere to a consistent practice of establishing the right level of readiness as 
described below: 
1. Identify incident scenarios utilizing the ADNOC Entity risk register, the output of the ADNOC 
Entity’s major accident risk assessment and other relevant information 
 
2. Implement and maintain crisis management plans to manage the scenarios identified  
 
3. Provide access to trained personnel, resources, medical emergency and other facilities needed 
to implement and execute the crisis management plans 
 
4. Validate the plans and resources through exercising them at defined intervals. Review the 
plans at least annually to reflect changes in risks or organization, and implement identified 
improvements 
 
5. Implement, maintain and exercise a documented process for accounting for personnel during 
and after an emergency evacuation.

7.2. 
SCENARIO IDENTIFICATION 
ADNOC Entity shall follow standard approach in the identification of risk events as defined in the relevant 
standard. The risk events which require emergency response action as a mitigation measure to eliminate 
or reduce the severity of the potential consequences shall be called incident scenarios. 
7.3. 
PLAN 
(a) 
ADNOC Entity shall have crisis management plans in place. The output from organisation’s HSE 
and ERM risk registers shall be consulted for developing these plans. The plans shall include 
procedures from initiation to response and recovery. At a facility level these plans shall include 
arrangements for evacuation and, where needed, for initial shelter-in-place 
(b) 
The plans shall include roles and responsibilities within the response organization and procedures 
for notification and activation, and communication with key stakeholders within and/or outside 
ADNOC Group. At operational facilities the procedures shall also include incident management 
guides (IMG) (see Appendix 2) with pre-defined recommended actions for each incident scenario 
(c) 
The plans shall be reviewed and updated on a regular basis with a minimum frequency of once a 
year or when there is a material change to either risk profile, organisation or response strategy that 
may have an impact on the response capability. 
For consistency purposes the following plan titles should be used: 
(i) 
Emergency Response Plan (ERP) – at a facility level or at a level of operational area 
combining more than one facility, describes roles, responsibilities and processes that an 
Incident Management Team (IMT) should follow when activated, including procedures for 
notification and activation, and communication with other stakeholders in order to support 
emergency response on site. ERP shall include IMGs for all incident scenarios. For more 
details regarding the content of an ERP refer to Appendix 1. 
(ii) 
Emergency Support Plan (ESP) – at an operational area level combining more than one 
facility or at an ADNOC Entity level, describes the roles, responsibilities and processes that 
an Emergency Support Team (EST) should follow when activated, including interaction with 
the ADNOC Entity Crisis Management Team (CMT). The ESP may also include IMGs for 
scenarios that are beyond control of the facility IMT, such as sub-sea and onshore pipeline 
incidents, helicopter ditching en route to/from an offshore site, road traffic collision outside 
the onshore facility boundaries. This plan is designed to ensure the ADNOC Entity EST 
receives appropriate notification and mobilises to support the IMT by: 
 
providing required internal and external resources 
 
developing response strategy 
 
notifying internal and external stakeholders 
 
undertaking various other support functions related to the emergency. 
(iii) 
Crisis Management Plan (CMP) – at an ADNOC Entity level, describes the roles, 
responsibilities and processes that the CMT should follow when activated. The purpose of 
this plan is to ensure that the CMT receives appropriate notification and mobilises in line with 
ADNOC response philosophy in order to:  
 
support the overall response

7.3.1 
BUSINESS CONTINUITY PLAN 
ADNOC Entity shall have Business Continuity Plans (BCP) in place. 
BCP will be designed to:  
 
Define appropriate scope by setting business objectives an ADNOC Entity must achieve 
during a disruption in order to deliver time-critical products and/or services 
 
Identify time-critical business processes that deliver the defined objectives 
 
Identify the supporting assets that deliver and support time-critical processes. These are 
people, places, physical resources, supplies and suppliers 
 
Work with those parts of an ADNOC Entity that have responsibility for recovering 
supporting assets such as IT, procurement and offices to gain the necessary 
understanding of the strategies in place and the consequences on business activities 
should they fail 
 
Agree what contingencies need to be in place, organize the implementation and document 
them in plans so they are ready for use during a disruption. 
It is prudent to treat any event that leads to the activation of a part or all of a BCP as a potential 
crisis.

7.4.1 
TIERED RESPONSE STRUCTURE 
The tiered response structure (refer to Figure 1) shall comprise of the following teams: 
(c) 
Incident Management Team (IMT) shall perform response operations at a facility or an operational 
area level.  
IMT may cover one or multiple facilities, which can be located remotely. There may be more than one 
IMT within an ADNOC Entity. The primary role of the IMT shall be to: 
 
 
Verify safety of responders 
 
Manage incident impacts 
 
Direct tactical response to the incident 
 
Brief the Emergency Support Team or Crisis Management Team. 
 
The primary objective of IMT is to prevent, eliminate or minimize negative effects of an incident on site 
as soon as possible.   
 
IMT structure should be based on the Incident Command System. IMT will consist of operational 
personnel, representatives of support functions, such as logistics, HSE and security, and on-scene 
tactical response team(s) (TRT) of specially trained auxiliaries or professional emergency responders, 
such as fire and rescue, oil spill and medical response personnel. IMT shall be led by Incident 
Commander (IC), a senior leader of the affected facility or of the operational area combining more than 
one facility. The TRT will be led by On-scene Commander (OC).  
 
The size and exact composition of IMT will depend on the severity and duration of the event (refer to 
APPENDIX 3). IMT may expand or contract based on the needs of an individual response. In case of 
IMT expansion it may relocate to an alternative venue away of the incident scene, the initial IMT may 
perform as a TRT and initial IC will assume the role of OC. The large size IMT in a long lasting response 
to a crisis event should be able to perform actions beyond standard IMT capacity and may include 
functions more relevant to higher-level teams within the response structure, like HR, Legal and 
Communications.

7.4.2. MUTUAL AID 
Large-scale, complex or long-running event can create a situation where the capacity of an ADNOC Entity 
to respond using only its personnel is or may be exceeded. When an incident potential assessment 
indicates that this may occur, the ability to access trained response personnel swiftly from other ADNOC 
Entities is important to management of the incident. The method by which ADNOC Group manages this 
need is through the following: 
 
Adoption of the mutual aid concept 
 
Establishment and maintenance of Mutual Response Team (MRT).   
 
(a) 
MRT shall consist of trained and experienced response personnel, primarily IMT members, who 
have been: 
 
Nominated by ADNOC Entities 
 
Approved for participation by ADNOC Group VP, C&RM. 
 
Mutual aid is the concept of placing the needs of ADNOC Group above the needs of an ADNOC Entity, 
resulting in internal mutual support among ADNOC Entities. In practice, mutual aid consists of providing 
ADNOC Entity resources to another ADNOC Entity, but only in so far as the provision of those resources 
does not compromise safe and compliant operations at the contributing ADNOC Entity. 
MRT activation may result in the deployment of single or multiple responders with specialized skills or, in 
rare cases, the full MRT. The scale of activation is determined in consultation between the responding 
ADNOC Entity and ADNOC Group VP, C&RM. 
 
(b) 
On request, ADNOC Entity shall: 
 
Support participation of ADNOC Entity employees in MRT 
 
Provide additional trained response personnel when the combined capacity of the following 
is exceeded: 
 Local response team (mainly IMT and EST) 
 MRT. 
 
Provide response equipment if needed by another ADNOC Entity in its response to an 
incident. 
 
ADNOC Entities will engage with the government, partners and other third parties to secure provision of 
sufficient resource to maintain their level of readiness and to identify own resource that can be allocated 
to these parties to support their response. These arrangements should not compromise ADNOC’s or

APPENDIX NO. 1 - EMERGENCY RESPONSE PLAN 
 
Purpose 
 
Emergency planning is part of an overall strategy for preventing and minimising the effects of major 
accidents to people and the environment. There are three basic parts of this strategy:  
 
Identification – incident scenarios should be identified based on risk and major accident hazards 
registers and include any reasonably foreseeable risk events which are not recognised within 
risk and major accident hazards registers 
 
Prevention and control – appropriate controls should be applied based on an assessment of the 
hazards, risks and possible consequences  
 
Mitigation – even with all controls in place, major accidents will never be totally eliminated so the 
effects of any that do occur should be kept as small as possible. Emergency planning is one of 
the key steps to achieving this. 
 
Objectives 
 
Each operational facility, programmed operational activity delivered outside established facilities, offices 
and other facilities owned, operated and permanently occupied by an ADNOC entity, shall have an ERP. 
The objectives of ERP are as follows: 
 
Contain and control incidents so as to minimise the effects, and to limit damage to people, the 
environment, assets and reputation 
 
Implement the measures necessary to protect people and the environment from the effects of 
major accidents 
 
Communicate the necessary information to internal and external stakeholders, including 
communities, emergency services and authorities concerned in the area 
 
Provide for the restoration and clean-up of the environment following a major accident. 
 
ERP should contain all the information that those responding to an incident may reasonably expect. The 
procedures that it contains for individual responders should be simple and straightforward, should not be 
contradictory, and should enable maximum flexibility of response, relative to the size of the facility.  
 
Scope 
 
While developing an ERP, the scope of its application should be clearly defined, including geographic 
coordinates and parameters for unfenced locations (pipeline corridors, exploration activities, etc.), plant 
units and buildings within fenced areas of established facilities, battery limits (areas of accountabilities 
with another party clearly divided) and duration (temporary activities carried out outside established 
facilities which are limited by time). ERP should also consider interdependencies with facilities outside 
the defined scope.  
 
Response organisation 
 
ERP should clearly describe the response organisation designated for managing the on-site response, 
including IMT and TRT(s) and roles and responsibilities of members of these teams, and how it fits into 
the wider entity tiered response structure. ERP should identify designated response resources, including

graphic format (notification flowchart).

addition to this information documented in ERP, the up-to-date duty call-out lists and emergency phone 
directories should be available to the response personnel. Where appropriate, ERP should also define 
maximum time limit within which the duty response teams should convene in their designated response 
centres. 
 
Incident Management Guides 
 
Based on the risk and major accident hazards registers, ERP should cover the range of incident scenarios 
that can be realistically anticipated. To achieve this each identified incident scenario should have a 
dedicated IMG with a logical sequence of predefined recommended actions for the response personnel. 
While developing an IMG, consideration should be given to the following factors: 
 
quantity of hazardous materials that could be released as a result of an accident (including smoke 
effluent from fires), the rate of release, the effect of explosions, the effect of thermal radiation 
from fires 
 
interdependencies with other facilities outside the scope of the ERP 
 
availability of sufficient response resources (personnel and equipment) on site (e.g., water for 
cooling, fire hoses and sufficient trained fire and rescue personnel)

To familiarise personnel with:
1. Muster/assembly point location and evacuation 
routes
2. Audio/visual signals which summon persons
to these muster stations                        
3. Headcount
4. Communication whilst at muster/assemply points
5. Location & use of life saving equipment.
OFFSHORE: LIFEBOAT DRILL 
To familiarise all personnel with:-
1.  Location of and route to designated lifeboat.
2. Method of accounting for personnel in lifeboat
3. Audio/visual signals associated with lifeboat
evacuation
4. Lifeboat loading technique - prelaunch and use
of essential equipment.
ALTERNATIVE EVACUATION
To familiarise personnel with:-
1. Routes to the helideck and liferaft locations
2. Escape to sea locations
3. Additional survival equipment locations
4. Can be practiced with another exercise.
MAN OVERBOARD
Maintain competence of personnel in actions to be 
taken during such incidents including standby vessel 
participation.

plans (IMGs), procedures 
2. Evaluate training effectiveness
3. Test response equipment
4. Apply lessons learned.
