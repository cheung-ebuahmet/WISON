1. 
INTRODUCTION 
High Pressure (HP) water jetting operating at 680 to 1700 bar and Ultra High Pressure (UHP) water jetting 
operating above 1700 bar is a process using a stream of pressurized water to remove material, coatings 
or contamination and debris from the surface of a workpiece or material substrate including cutting. It can 
also be used on vessel internals, in cutting applications as well as underwater. 
 
High Pressure Water Jetting (HPWJ) systems consist of an energy source like an electric motor or internal 
combustion engine, a pump, control mechanism, hoses, pipes, nozzles and various other components 
necessary for the equipment to function as a system. 
 
Exposure to high pressure waterjet has the greatest potential for causing serious injury or death. Some 
common hazards and risks include the water jet piercing the skin, being hit by flying debris and exposure 
to noise. Other hazards associated with high pressure water jetting include working in confined spaces, 
fall hazards, respiratory and eye hazards, electric shock and potential exposure to hazardous chemicals. 
 
2. 
PURPOSE 
The purpose of this Standard is to provide an overall understanding of use of HPWJ system and its 
application within the ADNOC Group. This Standard establishes a consistent requirement for personnel 
involved in the activities which may require the use of HPWJ within the ADNOC Group. 
 
All works related to the use of HPWJ system carried out for ADNOC Group shall be in accordance with 
the requirements of this Standard. 
 
3. 
SCOPE 
This document stipulates the mandatory requirements applicable to ADNOC Group (Directorates & 
Functions in ADNOC Head Quarter, Group Companies and Affiliates) and its Contractors.  
 
ADNOC Group and Contractors shall ensure that all expectations listed herein are fully understood, 
implemented and thoroughly monitored.

7.1. 
OVERVIEW 
Water jetting systems generate a very high pressure jet by forcing water through a small bore nozzle. 
The nozzle is mounted on the end of a jetting lance/gun which is held by the operator. The water is 
supplied via a hose from a jetting unit which has a source of water, a high pressure pump and a prime 
mover (e.g. a diesel engine). The water jet has very high energy and the potential to cause serious 
injury/death or damage. It has a wide variety of applications in the offshore, marine, construction, oil & 
gas and petrochemical industries, etc. For example, removing marine growth, scale, rust and old 
paintwork from offshore structures and ships; scabbling and cutting concrete; cleaning tube bundles, heat 
exchangers and boiler tubes, removing graffiti, paint, rust, cement, grease and oil from any surface. The

7.1.1. SELECTION OF HPWJ 
There are two types of HPWJ, namely High Pressure (HP) and Ultra High Pressure (UHP) water jetting. 
HP water jetting operates at lower pressures (680-1700bar) from UHP water jetting (>1700 bar) as shown 
in Table 7.1.1. Generally the higher pressure generates greater cutting efficiency. With current available 
pumps UHP jetting is achieved at reduced flow rates, typically 20 litres per minute.

7.2. 
HPWJ TOOLS AND EQUIPMENT  
HPWJ system consists of several primary components: a high pressure pump, supply and working hoses, 
fittings, a control gun and a nozzle assembly. ADNOC Group and its Contractors shall ensure the 
following for HPWJ: 
 
(i) 
Water jetting equipment and attachments shall only be used in accordance with 
manufacturer’s recommendation; and 
(ii) 
No water jetting equipment shall be modified without the approval of the manufacturer; 
(iii) 
HPWJ systems shall be maintained by a competent person in accordance with the 
manufacturer’s recommendation. Records shall be kept of service, repairs and maintenance. 
7.2.1. HIGH PRESSURE PUMP 
A typical high pressure pump is a positive displacement plunger type pump with three or more plungers 
[Refer to Figure 7.2.1]. The high pressure pump is divided into two distinct sections the power end which 
houses the crankshaft and connecting rods; and the fluid end which houses the plungers and valves. 
Every high pressure pump shall be equipped with a pressure gauge to indicate system pressure. The 
gauge shall have a scale range of at least 50% above the maximum operating pressure of the high 
pressure pump. The high pressure shall be equipped with at least one and preferably two pressure relief 
devices. 
 
The pump unit shall be maintained in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendation. This shall 
include daily pre-operational checks but not limited to the following items as applicable: 
 
(i) 
Engine and drive unit - lubricating oil, water, and hydraulic fluid and fuel levels, ignition 
controlled source; 
(ii) 
Pump unit - lubricating oil, water filters, drive belts, gauges and gearbox oil levels; 
(iii) 
Condition of guards, shields and safety interlocks; 
(iv) 
Electric leads and connectors; and 
(v) 
Condition of water filters in accordance with the pump manufacturer’s recommendation.

7.3. 
HAZARDS ASSOCIATED WITH HPWJ 
HPWJ equipment and processes present a variety of hazards to the workers. The list of hazards 
associated to HPWJ are but not limited to the following: 
 
(i) 
Waterjets: Exposure to high pressure waterjet has the greatest potential for causing serious 
injury or death. The speed of high pressure water is powerful enough to slice through solid 
materials or to damage any part of the human body. Even minor injuries can be fatal as 
micro-organisms can be injected into the body through injury site, along with air, water or 
debris. Debris propelled by water jets can injure eyes, skin and body parts upon impact. 
(ii) 
High Velocity Impact: Debris propelled by waterjets can injure eyes, skin, and body parts 
upon impact. Waterjet tools and equipment may be subject to inadvertent movement, with 
the potential to cause harm to workers. The high velocity impact may also lead to the 
damage of valuable assets. 
(iii) 
Back thrust or reaction force: There is a possibility of back thrusts created by high-pressure 
jet stream which can easily put a person off balance there by leading to potential injury. It is 
recommended that a person shall not withstand a back thrust of more than one third of the 
body weight which has potential to cause fatigue. 
(iv) 
Noise: The noise hazard may arise due to breakdown of work object, hitting of drops of water 
to the solid surface of the work object, flow turbulence induced by collision of the jet and 
object, jet noise etc. ADNOC Physical Hazards Standard [Ref. 9] shall be referred for 
hazards associated to noise. 
(v) 
Chemical Exposure: Contact with hazardous chemicals is an everyday risk in the high 
pressure washing. The purpose of the activity is to clean or remove chemical substances 
and by-products. Workers not adequately protected through procedures, position, or PPE 
are likely to be exposed to substances that may cause significant acute injury or long-term 
health effects. ADNOC Chemical Hazards Standard [Ref. 10] shall be referred for chemical 
exposure. 
(vi) 
Musculoskeletal Injury: Personnel working with HPWJ system are often required to work in 
awkward positions or in confined spaces, to lift heavy tools or materials, and to work with 
high push/pull (reaction) force leading to potential musculoskeletal injury including hand-arm 
vibration syndrome. ADNOC Manual Handling Standard [Ref. 15] and ADNOC Ergonomics 
Hazards Standard [Ref. 11] shall be referred for activity related to lifting of heavy tools or 
materials. 
(vii) 
Machinery and equipment: Mechanical hazards are also usually associated with power 
transmission parts consisting of belts, pulleys, chains, sprockets, gears, shafts, couplings 
etc. Proper safeguarding of power transmission parts within and around a high pressure 
pump shall be essential. 
(viii) Confined Spaces and Stored Materials: Confined spaces such as tanks, hoppers, boilers, 
etc. pose hazards to the personnel which may contain toxic gases, risk of entrapment,

RISK MANAGEMENT PROCESS

7.3] that could potentially cause harm to people, environment and/or assets. These shall be identified by: 
 
(i) 
Conducting a walk through assessment of the workplace; 
(ii) 
Observing the work and talking to workers about how water jetting is carried out; 
(iii) 
Inspecting plant and equipment used during the activity;  
(iv) 
Reading product labels, Safety Data Sheets (SDS) and manufacturer’s instruction manuals; 
and 
(v) 
Reviewing incident reports. 
 
7.4.2. RISK ASSESSMENT 
Risk assessment shall be ensured for the activity, which helps to: 
 
(i) 
Identify which workers are at risk;  
(ii) 
Determine what sources and processes are causing the risks; 
(iii) 
Identify what kind of control measures shall be implemented; and 
(iv) 
Assess the effectiveness of existing control measures.  
The likelihood of each hazard actually causing harm in a specific situation shall be assessed. The 
following shall be used for the assessment: 
 
(i) 
How often and for how long will exposure to the hazard occur? 
(ii) 
If exposed to the hazard, how severe the outcome be based on Risk Assessment Matrix? 
(iii) 
What is the substrate being blasted? 
(iv) 
What is the likelihood of damaging the assets due to the pressure exerted and type of nozzle 
selected?  
(v) 
What are the surface coatings/chemical content of the items being blasted? For example, 
do they toxic materials or reactive chemicals? 
(vi) 
What are the conditions under which HPWJ activity being carried out? For example, are they 
carried out in a confined space? 
(vii) 
What are the skills, competence and experience of the personnel?

(b) 
Heat Exchanger Cleaning 
The most significant risks from cleaning a heat exchanger are: 
 
(i) 
The nozzle exiting the tube being cleaned while still under pressure; 
(ii) 
The tube becoming pressurized and forcing the nozzle out at the operator end; 
(iii) 
The tube becoming pressurized and ejecting a plug of scale or steel blocking a faulty tube; 
and 
(iv) 
The nozzle and hose assembly being damaged when exiting the tube. 
Possible control measures for these hazards include: 
 
(i) 
Using chemicals to clean tubes; 
(ii) 
Using mechanical or automated lance handling equipment; 
(iii) 
Installing mechanical shields over the end of the tube bundle opposite to the end being 
worked on; 
(iv) 
Limiting the distance the hose can enter a tube; 
(v) 
Using nozzle extensions preventing it and hose crimps from fouling on the tube face when 
being pulled back into the tube; and 
(vi) 
Using anti-withdrawal devices to prevent inadvertent or hydraulic force ejection of the flexible 
lance and nozzle. 
(c) 
Vessel Cleaning 
Vessel while cleaning using HPWJ is a high risk activity. The need for the person to enter a vessel 
during cleaning shall be eliminated so far as is reasonably practicable. Tank cleaning heads can 
be used instead. These can be mounted in a variety of ways, for example by being hung from 
cables or mounted on a rigid fixture. The most important aspect of their activity is to check the arms 
are not bent and the nozzles are balanced and in good condition. If there are problems in either of 
these areas, the reaction forces on the head will be unbalanced which could possibly swing about 
uncontrolled inside the vessel. 
 
(d) 
Drain Cleaning 
It is operated at considerably lower pressures than needed for most high pressure water cleaning 
activities, however the hazards are of same magnitude and appropriate control measures shall be 
taken.
